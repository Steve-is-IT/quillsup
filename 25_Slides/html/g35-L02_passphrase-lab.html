Step 1 (1 min): Meet the Guess-Machine Draw a robot on the board. Say: “This is our pretend Guess-Machine. It never gets tired. In our lab, it makes 1,000 guesses every second. (Real computers can be much faster, so this is a gentle model.) It tries every possible lock one by one.”

Step 2 (2 min): Build the Crack-Timer table together Before class, copy this table (R-2.2) onto the board or a poster and cover the last column with sticky notes. (Drawing it live eats 3 to 4 minutes.) In class, reveal it row by row with a drumroll while the class says the multiplication aloud. For each new “slot,” the number of possibilities multiplies by the number of choices. | Lock type | Choices per slot | How many possible locks | Time for Guess-Machine (1,000 guesses per second) | |---|---|---|---| | 1 digit PIN | 10 | 10 | less than 1 second | | 2 digit PIN | 10 x 10 | 100 | 0.1 second | | 4 digit PIN | 10 x 10 x 10 x 10 | 10,000 | 10 seconds | | 1 word from our 36-word list | 36 | 36 | 0.04 second | | 2 words | 36 x 36 | 1,296 | about 1 second | | 3 words | 36 x 36 x 36 | 46,656 | about 47 seconds | | 4 words | 36 x 36 x 36 x 36 | 1,679,616 | about 28 minutes | | 5 words | 36 to the 5th | 60,466,176 | about 17 hours | | 6 words | 36 to the 6th | 2,176,782,336 | about 25 days | | 7 words | 36 to the 7th | 78,364,164,096 | about 2.5 years | Say: “Look at what happened. Each new word multiplied the lock by 36. Not plus 36, TIMES 36. That's why length wins.” (Teacher note: for a 3rd-grade class, fill the first two columns together and use the time column as “wow” numbers. For 4th-5th, ask students to multiply 46,656 x 36 on paper to check 4 words.)

Timer for these steps: 3 min. Press T to start.

Step 3 (3 min): Roll your practice passphrase Each student rolls two dice to pick a word from the list: first die = row, second die = column. Roll three times to build a 3-word passphrase, written on the worksheet. Say: “Random is stronger than clever. Rolling the dice means a guesser cannot predict your pet's name or your favorite team.” Remind: “This is a PRACTICE passphrase. Never use a class practice passphrase for a real account, and never tell it to friends. At home, you and a grown-up make a real one.”

Step 4 (2 min): Do the math Students write the number of possible 3-word passphrases from the 36-word list (46,656) and how long the Guess-Machine would need (about 47 seconds). Then: “Now imagine the list had 7,776 words instead of 36, like real dice-word lists do. Four words from that list means over 3 quadrillion possibilities.” (7,776 to the 4th is about 3.66 quadrillion [RECOMPUTED 2026-10-05: 3,656,158,440,062,976]. Even at one billion guesses per second, that would take over a month.) “A real long passphrase of four or five random words is very hard to guess.” (Note: real-world guess speeds vary hugely; this is a teaching model, so do not present these times as real-world facts. All times above were recomputed on 2026-10-05 and match.)

Timer for these steps: 5 min. Press T to start.

Takeaway (2 minutes together with the exit ticket). Do the hand signals as you say each word.

“Long and random beats short and clever. And your passphrase is private: share it only with a parent or guardian. If anyone asks for your password, Pause, Check, Tell.” Practiced behavior: say aloud “I only share my passphrase with my grown-up.”

Hackles line: If someone asks for your password, your hackles should go up. That is your gut check. A false alarm is fine. Say it out loud: Hackles up!

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes. The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

Energizer (30 seconds): “Human Guess-Machine.”: A volunteer becomes the Guess-Machine (robot walk, “BEEP BOOP”) and tries to guess a classmate's secret number (one digit, then two digits) while the class counts the guesses aloud. Everyone feels why more digits take longer.

Game variant: “Password Doctor Speed Round.”: Use the pairs on Activity Book page 7. Read a pair aloud; students stand for the first choice and sit for the second. Fast, silly, instant feedback (“kettle river orbit” or “Max2015”?).

Chant (stomp-clap): “Long and random, that's the way! / Dice pick words that guesses can't say. / Short and clever? Not for me! / Longer's stronger: one, two, three!”

Silly voice / puppet moment: Give the Guess-Machine a flat robot voice: “BEEP. I AM GUESS-MACHINE. I TRY 'PASSWORD.' NOPE. I TRY 'DRAGON1.' BEEP-BEEP-BEEP, GOT IT!” Hackle's quills pop higher as the timer column climbs: “Forty-seven seconds?! Hackles UP!”

Level-Up Challenge: Beat the Machine. How long would our pretend Guess-Machine need for an 8-word passphrase from the 36-word list? (36 to the 8th is about 2.8 trillion, so about 89 years at 1,000 guesses a second. Just a classroom model.) Spiral: “Who may know your real passphrase? Look at your Trusted Hand from Lesson 1.”
