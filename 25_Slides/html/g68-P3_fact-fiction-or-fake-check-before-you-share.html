Step 1 (1 min): Read the case file and show the Check Strip > CF-P3: The Closing Rumor > Tavi is twelve. A post says, “SCHOOL CLOSED TOMORROW because of a danger. Tell everyone!” It has a scary photo. Friends are forwarding it. Tavi's heart is pounding and Tavi's thumb hovers over “share.” Write the Check Strip on the board. Four questions always: WHO made it? PROOF: what shows it is true? WHO ELSE says so (a school, a trusted news source, an official)? FEELING: is it trying to scare, anger or rush me? Two more when they fit: LOOK CLOSER (a picture or video: when was it taken, who took it, has it been edited?) and DATA (a number or chart: who is it about, when, who collected it, one source or several?).

Step 2 (4 min): Claim Check (groups) Each group gets three of the six Claim Cards (RP3), so the class covers all six. Cards (all made up): (1) a school-closing rumor with a scary photo; (2) a “miracle cure” from an influencer; (3) a screenshot of a “private message” about a classmate; (4) a chatbot answer stated very confidently; (5) a chart saying “Our school's reading scores doubled!” whose bars start at 90, so a small gap looks huge; (6) a photo “from today” of a flooded street that is actually from years ago. For each card the group names the first Check Strip question to ask, what good evidence would look like, and a decision: SHARE, CHECK FIRST, DO NOT SHARE or TELL AN ADULT.

Timer for these steps: 5 min. Press T to start.

Step 3 (2 min): Debrief by card One group per card gives its decision and reason. Use the Answer Key. For card 6, if you have time, show on the board how a reverse image search works in words only (“you upload the picture and the search shows where else it appeared and how old it is”), or demonstrate with a harmless sample picture. For card 5, redraw the chart on the board with the bars starting at zero.

Step 4 (1 min): Share Pause rule and when to tell Students write their own one-line Share Pause rule: “If I cannot say who made it and who else says so, I do not pass it on.” SAY: “If a post is spreading rumors about a person, scaring people, or asking for money or personal information, it is more than a wrong fact. Use the report button in the app, and tell an adult. If a rumor targets a classmate, treat it like the pile-on in Lesson 9. For AI-made fakes, see Lesson 8.”

Takeaway (2 minutes together with the exit ticket). Do the hand signals as you say each word.

“Pause before you share. Check who made it, the proof and who else says so. Tell an adult if it is hurting someone or scaring people. It is never your fault if you shared something false by mistake. You fix it by telling people and not passing it on.”

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes. The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

2-minute hook variation: Two Truths and a Rumor about the school mascot (all invented): the class votes which is the rumor and says what they would check. No real people, no news.

Debate or game version: Fact Detective Relay: each group has 60 seconds per card to name the first question and the decision; the class gives a thumbs-up for a good reason, not for speed. Debate option: “Is sharing something false by mistake as bad as making it up?” Draw out: the effect can be the same, so the fix is the same.

Peer-teaching or choice option: Choice menu: (a) write a one-sentence private correction to a friend who shared a false post, (b) draw the Check Strip as a poster, (c) invent a Claim Card for the class set (made-up claim only).

Level-Up Challenge: Family Four Questions: try the four Check Strip questions on one post with a parent or caregiver this week and bring one sentence about what you found. Private.

Say-it-back line (post it): “Who made it? What is the proof? Who else says so? How is it trying to make me feel?”

Timing and extended block: If time is short, use four Claim Cards (1, 3, 5, 6) and keep the Share Pause rule. Extended block (30 min): add a Source Hunt with a library book or a school-approved site, finding a second source for one made-up claim.
