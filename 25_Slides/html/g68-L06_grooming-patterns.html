Hackles up? When someone online asks you to keep secrets or pulls you away from others, trust that feeling. It is never your fault, and you can tell someone. Today's case file is about a pattern. First, an important truth: most people you meet online and offline are fine, and most friendships are good. Today we are learning to spot the unusual pattern that can show up when someone is trying to build a relationship that is not safe. This could be an adult, an older teen, or someone your own age. It could be someone you know or someone you met in a game.

Step 1 (1 min): Read the case file > CF-6: The Great Teammate > Luz is twelve and loves a team game. A player on her team, “Skye99,” is friendly, funny, and great at the game. Over a few weeks, Skye99 gives Luz free in-game items, compliments her, and says things like “You are so mature for your age.” Skye99 suggests, “Let's chat somewhere more private, it is easier.” Later, Skye99 says, “Don't tell your parents about me. They would not understand,” and “Your friends are just jealous.” One day, Skye99 asks for something that makes Luz's stomach drop, a private photo “just for me.” Luz feels confused and trapped, because Skye99 has been so nice. (Teacher note: stop at “private photo.” Do not add detail. If students ask what the request is, say “a request that crosses a line about privacy and the body. We do not need the details to know it is a red flag.”)

Step 2 (4 min): Timeline Strip Sort (groups of 4-5) Each group gets 12 strips from the case file (R6) and four stage labels: - - TRUST: kindness, attention, gifts, shared interests, compliments. - - SECRECY: “don't tell,” “this is just between us.” - - ISOLATION: moving to a private space; “your friends and family do not get you”; making the young person feel only this person understands. - - ESCALATION: requests that cross lines (privacy, body, meeting up), pressure, guilt, threats. Groups place each strip under a stage. Then they draw a star on the earliest strip where Luz could have told an adult, and a double star on any strip that should tell Luz “tell right now.”

Timer for these steps: 5 min. Press T to start.

Step 3 (2 min): Debrief: pattern, not single event Take two groups' answers. Reveal the answer guide (Teacher Answer Key). SAY: “Notice that no single strip proves anything. A free gift can be a kind gesture. Being told you are mature can be a nice compliment. It is the pattern, the way trust turns into secrecy, then into pulling you away from other people, then into requests that cross lines, that tells you something is wrong. And here is the key: the earlier you tell, the easier it is to stop. But if you are further along, it is not too late. You are not in trouble at any stage.”

Step 4 (1 min): Safe and Unsafe Secrets Read three or four Safe or Unsafe Secret statements aloud (R6). Students thumbs up (safe, like a surprise party) or thumbs sideways (check it, a secret that gets someone hurt or that you were told to hide from trusted adults). SAY: “A surprise is a secret that ends in something happy and does not hurt anyone. A secret that makes your stomach drop, or that you are told to hide from parents or trusted adults, is a secret to share. A safe adult or friend will never ask you to keep your relationship secret from your family.”

Timer for these steps: 3 min. Press T to start.

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes (calm lesson: keep it quiet, no scoring or competition). The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

2-minute hook variation: Safe Mentor (90 seconds): before any case file, ask pairs, “What makes a coach, teammate, or older friend safe to be around?” List answers on the board (respects no, welcomes your other friends, okay with your family knowing them). Keep the list up; the pattern strips will be compared to it. This starts from what is good, which keeps the tone calm. All Analyst Rules, the Pass card, and the counselor heads-up stay exactly as written above.

Debate or game version: By design, no competition, timer race, scoring, or voting on this lesson. Calm alternative: Silent Gallery. After sorting, groups leave their strip order on the table and walk silently to one other table, then say one thing they noticed was the same or different. No grading. Optional debate (non-personal): “Which is more useful: noticing one message, or noticing a pattern?”

Peer-teaching or choice option: Choice menu (pick one, Pass always allowed): (a) write a five-point Safe Adult Checklist, (b) write the one-sentence Tell-3 you would say to a friend, (c) be the group Recorder or Observer. Peer-teaching is limited to the Safe or Unsafe Secret cards; students never re-tell or act out the case.

Level-Up Challenge: Helper Poster: with counselor approval, design a general poster for the school showing the four stages and the Help Ladder in plain words, with no case details. Or write a two-line message a friend could send a friend who seems to have a 'secret' online friend: “I am here. Want to go tell someone together?”

Say-it-back line (post it): “A surprise ends happy. A secret that squeezes your stomach gets shared.”

Timing and extended block: Use eight strips (Differentiate) if short. Extended block (30 min): add a second pass of the Safe or Unsafe Secret cards and a Tell-3 rehearsal in triads. Keep the spacing rule: not adjacent to Lessons 7 and 8.
