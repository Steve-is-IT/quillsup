Hook, read aloud (about 2 minutes). Slide shows the main lines; the full script is here.

Hackles up? If something about an image or rumor feels wrong, trust that. False alarms are fine, and it is never the fault of the person it targets. Today's lesson is about fake pictures. Photo editing is not new. What is new is that some apps and AI tools can make a very convincing fake picture of a real person in seconds. Some are advertised to create fake private-looking pictures of someone, which can be used to embarrass or hurt them. Those pictures are fake. The harm is real. This lesson has no images and no graphic details. You can use your Pass card or the Quiet Corner at any time. And the most important sentence: if someone makes a fake picture of you, it is never your fault. You did nothing wrong.

Step 1 (1 min): Read the case file > CF-8: The Fake That Wasn't Hers > Nia is a seventh grader. Someone used an app that alters photos to make a fake picture of Nia, using her face from a school photo. It is made to look private and humiliating. It lands in a class group chat. Nia did nothing; the picture is fake. Three classmates see it. Cam laughs and forwards it. Ari feels uncomfortable but freezes and says nothing. Idris feels sick and wants to help but does not know how.

Step 2 (1 min): Real or fake, the harm is the same SAY: “Notice that it does not matter whether the picture is real or fake. The hurt, the shame, and the whispering are the same. That is why people who make or share these pictures can face serious consequences at school, and in many places under the law. A federal law called the TAKE IT DOWN Act, signed in May 2025, treats sharing intimate images without consent, including AI-made fakes, as a crime, and it requires covered platforms (websites, apps and online services) to remove reported images within 48 hours. Many states have laws too (they vary; check yours with your counselor or administrator). We do not need legal details. The point is: this is not a prank.”

Step 3 (4 min): The Upstander Four (groups of 4-5) Introduce the four moves: - - STOP the spread. Do not open, forward, comment on, like, or screenshot the picture. If you receive it, do not pass it on. - - SUPPORT the target. Reach out privately: “I am sorry this happened. It is not your fault. I am here.” - - TELL an adult. Use the Help Ladder. Say: “Something harmful is going around and I think someone needs help.” - - REPORT it. In the app or platform, and through an adult; adults can report to the school, law enforcement, or NCMEC as needed. Each group takes a Bystander Card (Cam, Ari, or Idris; assign two groups to each if needed) and uses the Move Cards to write: - - One line the character could send to Nia privately. - - One line to the group chat (for example: “Not okay. I am not sharing this and I am reporting it.”). - - One adult to tell and the first sentence (a Tell-3 line). - - What to report where.

Step 4 (2 min): If it is you, and if you made or shared one SAY: “If a fake picture of you ever appears, use the same steps: STOP, SAVE the words and links, not the picture, TELL, REPORT. NCMEC's free tool Take It Down can help remove images of someone under 18. If you have forwarded a fake picture, or if you were thinking of making or sharing one as a joke, it is not too late to stop. Stop sharing, tell a trusted adult, and take responsibility. A joke can become a crime, and it can hurt someone deeply.”

Timer for these steps: 6 min. Press T to start.

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes (calm lesson: keep it quiet, no scoring or competition). The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

2-minute hook variation: Alarm, Not Firefighter (20 seconds, after the opening statements): “If you see smoke, you are not the firefighter. You pull the alarm and you point. Bystanders are not responsible for fixing it. They are responsible for not feeding it and for getting help.” Keep every opening safeguard and the Pass option as written.

Debate or game version: By design, no competition or scoring on this lesson. Calm alternative: Four Corners. Post STOP, SUPPORT, TELL, and REPORT in corners. Students walk silently to the move they would find hardest to do first, then talk in that corner about what would make it easier. Nobody has to speak to the whole class. Optional structured debate (non-personal): “Is 'it was just a joke' ever a fair excuse?”

Peer-teaching or choice option: Choice: groups write about the character in the third person (“Cam could...”), so nobody acts out forwarding a harmful image. Students may choose Ari or Idris cards, or the Observer or Recorder role. Teach-back is limited to the Reporting Quick Card.

Level-Up Challenge: Upstander Card: make a one-page card for a younger student with the four moves in plain words (extension in Differentiate). Or apply the four moves to a fake audio clip or fake screenshot, using only invented examples.

Say-it-back line (post it): “Do not pass it on. Go to the person, then go to an adult.”

Timing and extended block: Extended block (30 min): add a rehearsal of the private message to Nia in pairs and a walk through the Reporting Quick Card. Use the same materials and add no new sensitive content.
