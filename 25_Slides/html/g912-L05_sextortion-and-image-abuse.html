REMINDER (teacher only): any student may pass or step out at any time without explaining. Opt-out families and the alternate activity are in your Teacher Guide. Keep the counselor reachable. If a student discloses something, use the disclosure box for this lesson in your Teacher Guide.

Step 1 (2 min): Read the pattern Hand out the Scenario Card (R5). Read it aloud, or have a student read it (ask for a volunteer, never assign). The card is fictional and has no explicit content. It follows “Rowan, 16” who is contacted by “Riley,” who appears to be the same age. Ask students to underline the pattern markers: fast closeness, flattery, a push to move to a different private app, “you first” or “I'll send one if you do”, sudden change of tone, a deadline, a payment demand in gift cards or similar, and a threat to share with followers or school, plus “do not tell anyone.” Say: “That is the script. Notice it does not need to be sophisticated. It needs speed and shame.”

Step 2 (3 min): Fill in the drill On W5, pairs write exactly what Rowan should do at each step. Use these guides when you circulate, and reveal at the end: - - STOP: Stop replying. Do not pay (paying usually leads to more demands, not to the end of it). Do not send more. Do not delete the account or the conversation. Do not try to “out-trick” the person. - - SAVE: Save the details: the username and profile link, the messages containing threats and the payment demand, dates and times, payment methods named. Do not copy, forward, or re-send any nude image of a minor to anyone, even to “prove” it happened. Leave the original content in place; adults will handle it. - - TELL: Tell a trusted person tonight, in any order: a friend you trust, a family member, a counselor, a coach, a teacher. If one adult does not react well, try another. Say: “I need help with something that happened online.” - - REPORT: Report the account on the platform. Report to NCMEC using the CyberTipline (report.cybertip.org, 1-800-843-5678). Use Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) to help get images removed if they were made or taken when you were under 18; it works by creating a digital fingerprint on your device, not by uploading the image. For people 18 or older, StopNCII.org offers a similar service. With an adult, contact local law enforcement or the FBI (tips.fbi.gov). In an emergency, call 911.

Timer for these steps: 5 min. Press T to start.

Step 3 (2 min): Practice the first sentence of TELL Pairs take turns saying out loud, then hearing the response. Student A reads: “Something happened online and I need help. Step 4 (1 min): The AI version Say: “If the image is fake, made by AI, the plan is exactly the same. A fake image can still be used to threaten. Creating or sharing a sexual fake of a person is abuse, and it can be against federal law and many state laws. The federal TAKE IT DOWN Act, signed in May 2025, makes publishing non-consensual intimate images, including AI fakes, a federal crime and requires covered platforms to remove reported content within 48 hours after a valid request; the platform duty took effect May 19, 2026.” Hand out the Helpline Card. Say: “This card is yours. Put it somewhere you will find it.”

Takeaway (2 minutes together with the exit ticket). Do the hand signals as you say each word.

“Pause the moment the speed, flattery, or secrecy begins. Check whether this person is who they say they are, and notice that you cannot. Tell, tonight. The drill is Stop, Save, Tell, Report. If you remember one thing: telling is the way out, not the way in trouble. Add to your Defense Plan: 'The one adult I would tell first is ____, and my first sentence is ____.'”

Hackles line: Speed, flattery, and secrecy are exactly what hackles are for. Trusting them is enough reason to stop and tell, tonight; being unsure it is “serious enough” is not a reason to wait.

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes (calm lesson: keep it quiet, no scoring or competition). The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

2-minute hook variation: Fire Drill Idea (20 seconds, added after the three up-front statements and never replacing them): “We practice a fire drill so nobody has to think when there is smoke. This is a drill for one specific kind of smoke.” All parent pre-notice, counselor, opt-out, and Step-Out safeguards stay exactly as written.

Debate or game version: By design, no competition, scoring, or ranked activity on this lesson. Calm alternative: Myth or Plan. The teacher reads a statement and the class shows thumbs up (plan) or sideways (myth): “Paying will make it stop” (myth), “If it is AI-made it is no big deal” (myth), “Deleting everything solves it” (myth), “Telling someone tonight is the way out” (plan). The teacher gives each answer; no one is asked to say what they have seen.

Peer-teaching or choice option: Choice of role in Step 3: Student A, Student B, or Observer with the checklist for the whole lesson. Students may write the TELL sentence in their own words or home language. Optional counselor-reviewed project: the shame-free message or counselor-door poster from Differentiate.

Level-Up Challenge: Write It Better: draft the three sentences a school could send to all students explaining STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT without shame, using no scenario detail. Or translate the Drill Card for a family (reviewed by a qualified translator).

Say-it-back line (post it): “Telling is the way out. Stop, save details, tell, report. Tonight.”

Timing and extended block: Fifteen minutes is the minimum for this lesson; the Extended block (30 to 45 min) is strongly recommended. If you must use 15, do not cut Step 3 (the TELL practice) or Step 4. Wording tip for Q1 in Discuss: ask 'what signals show up in the story, and why are they hard to notice in the moment?' so no student hears 'you should have known.'
