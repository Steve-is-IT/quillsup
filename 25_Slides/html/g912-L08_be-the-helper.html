Hook, read aloud (about 2 minutes). Slide shows the main lines; the full script is here.

When teens have a problem online, who do they tell first? Usually a friend, not an adult. That makes you the first responder, and nobody trained you. That is a lot to carry. So today you get a simple way to be useful without being a hero, because the worst outcome is a friend who is alone with a heavy secret and a friend who is carrying it by themselves.

Step 1 (2 min): Teach the model Present the Helper Card: - - HEAR: Listen first. Say: “Thank you for telling me.” “That sounds really hard.” “That is not your fault.” Do not say: “Why did you...?” “That is silly.” “Just ignore it.” - - HELP: Offer small, safe things: stay with them, help them save details (not images), help them find a trusted adult, help them report. Do not investigate, confront the other person, post a “call-out”, or forward screenshots. - - HAND OFF: Bring in a trusted adult, and offer to go with them: “I care about you too much to keep this just between us. I will go with you.” If they ask you to promise secrecy, you can say: “I will stay with you the whole way, and I cannot carry this alone.” For self-harm, immediate danger, or someone being hurt, tell an adult right away, even if they ask you not to. - - Then show the Escalation Ladder: Green (stress, a fight, a rough day: listen, check in later); Amber (threats, blackmail, harassment, controlling behavior, an image shared without consent: bring in an adult within 24 hours; use the Response Drill); Red (talk of self-harm or suicide, immediate danger, abuse, a weapon: tell an adult now; call or text 988 or call 911).

Step 2 (3 min): Round 1 Each Friend draws a card and reads it aloud to the Helper. The Helper practices Hear and Help, and says the Hand off sentence. The Observer ticks off the checklist. Cards (R8): F1 “Can't sleep. Someone I met in a game chat has a photo of me and says they will send it to everyone if I do not pay.” (Amber.) F2 “I do not want to be here anymore.” (Red.) F3 “My partner got mad I was out with friends and took my phone. I am fine. Do not tell anyone.” (Amber.) F4 “Someone made a fake of me and it is in a group chat.” (Amber.) F5 “The group chat is piling on me and I want to delete everything.” (Green or amber.) F6 “I clicked a link and entered my password. My parents will kill me.” (Green.)

Step 3 (3 min): Round 2 Rotate roles; use a new card. - - Circulate. Listen for the Hand off sentence. Remind groups that the Helper does not need to solve the problem and should not promise secrecy. If a Friend card is F2, make sure the Helper asks directly (“Are you thinking about hurting yourself?”), stays, and brings an adult now or calls or texts 988.

Timer for these steps: 8 min. Press T to start.

Takeaway (2 minutes together with the exit ticket). Do the hand signals as you say each word.

“Pause: do not react with blame. Check how big this is on the Ladder. Tell: hand off to a trusted adult, and look after yourself, too. You do not have to carry this alone. Add to your Defense Plan: 'If a friend needs help, my Hand off adult is ____ and my sentence is ____.'”

Hackles line: Noticing that a friend's message or behavior feels off is the first step of helping. Trust it, and hand off to a trusted adult.

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes. The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

2-minute hook variation: Alarm, Not Firefighter (20 seconds): “If you see smoke, you are not the firefighter. You pull the alarm and you point. Your job is to hear, help a little, and hand off.” Ask pairs: “Who do teens tell first?” (Usually a friend.)

Debate or game version: Calm Sort: add four extra low-weight cards the class sorts as Green, Amber, or Red: “My friend failed a test and is upset” (Green); “My friend's account got hacked” (Green or Amber); “A friend says everyone in the group chat stopped replying to them” (Green or Amber); “A friend says they feel like a burden” (Red: ask directly, stay, bring in an adult). Debate option: “When is it loyal to break a promise to keep a secret?” Draw out: when someone's safety is at risk.

Peer-teaching or choice option: Choice of cards: Friend may read F5 or F6 (lighter) instead of F1 to F4. The teacher may read F2 aloud as a whole-class demo so no student acts out a person in crisis; students then practice the Helper response. Observer or Recorder is always available. Helpers may write their Hand-off sentence in a text format, in person, or as a note.

Level-Up Challenge: Three Registers: write your Hand-off sentence as a text, as something you say in person, and as a note. Or draft the Helper guide for grades 6-8 from Differentiate, with no real cases.

Say-it-back line (post it): “Hear. Help. Hand off. Do not carry it alone.”

Timing and extended block: Safety note on F1: an active threat with a deadline (payment demand, 'one hour') means tell an adult tonight, not 'within 24 hours.' Say this at the Escalation Ladder step. Extended block: add a third round where the Helper uses a text message.
