Big idea

Social media affects sleep, mood, self-image, relationships and attention, for better and for worse, and research is still working out how much and for whom. Compulsive use is a pattern you can notice and change, and help exists.

Practice: Map the effects of a feature or habit across four domains, choose one adjustment, and know when to tell someone.

Step 1 (1 min): Four domains and one word Name the domains: Sleep, Mood and self-image, Relationships, Attention and control. Say: “'Addiction' is a word people use when something feels impossible to cut down even though it is causing harm. Whether a person has a health condition is something a doctor or counselor decides. Today we look at patterns and options.”

Step 2 (4 min): Fill in the map Each group gets one Domain Vignette (fictional, four or five sentences) and fills in the Impact Map (W-P2): (a) the effect: what changed; (b) what makes it worse: design features, comparison, cyberbullying, late-night use; (c) one adjustment the character could try; (d) the signal that means “talk to someone”, and to whom. The vignettes avoid self-harm detail. The Mood vignette ends with a character who “stops answering friends and says nothing will get better”; the group marks it GET HELP TODAY and names an adult and 988.

Timer for these steps: 5 min. Press T to start.

Step 4 (1 min): Two-Week Experiment Each student privately picks one adjustment (no phone in bed; mute a category of notifications; check feeds at set times; unfollow accounts that leave you feeling worse; one app-free evening; track which push notifications you actually needed and turn some off, which is the state guide's own home activity, p.6) and one person to check in with. Add the Defense Plan line DP-P2: “My well-being setting is ___.” Results stay private and are never collected.

REMINDER (teacher only): any student may pass or step out at any time without explaining. Opt-out families and the alternate activity are in your Teacher Guide. Keep the counselor reachable. If a student discloses something, use the disclosure box for this lesson in your Teacher Guide.

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes (calm lesson: keep it quiet, no scoring or competition). The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

2-minute hook variation: Quiet Count (60 seconds, silent): “Think of one app you could not put down last month. Do not say which. Which of the four domains did it touch most?” Nobody shares. All counselor and disclosure steps stay as written.

Debate or game version: Calm: Map Swap. Groups trade completed maps and add one adjustment the first group missed. Debate option (non-personal): “Should apps default to a quiet hour for teens?”

Peer-teaching or choice option: Choice of domain, or write a three-sentence caring check-in a friend could send. Nobody reports their own habits.

Level-Up Challenge: Design brief: a one-page proposal for a feature that supports well-being (a stopping point, a quiet hour) for a made-up app.

Say-it-back line (post it): “Notice. Adjust. Tell someone if it does not shift.”

Timing and extended block: If time is short, give two vignettes and keep Steps 3 and 4. Extended block (30 to 40 min): add a second pass on the Help Routes card (counselor, doctor, 988, a friend) and a Question Box read-out with the counselor in the room. Cross-teach with P4 (compounding effects) and Lesson 8.
