Hook, read aloud (about 2 minutes). Slide shows the main lines; the full script is here.

Raise your hand if you have ever gotten stuck on a really hard video-game level. Keep it up if you asked someone for help. Ha, almost everyone! Here is a secret: pros ask for help all the time. Today, we are going to practice a super-power that every good hacker uses. It is called telling someone. Let me tell you about our friend Kenji. Kenji was playing a game when a message popped up: 'Hi! I'll give you 500 free coins if you click here.' He felt a little tingle in his tummy, a feeling that said 'hmm.' He did not click.

Step 1 (1 min): Introduce the rungs Point to the poster and fill in the rungs as you read: - - Rung 1: Tell a friend you trust. A friend can listen, can come with you, and can help you find a grown-up. A friend's most important job: “I'll go with you to tell a grown-up.” - - Rung 2: Tell a trusted adult. A parent or guardian, a teacher, the counselor, a coach, a relative, a neighbor you know well. Hold up your Trusted Hand: five fingers, five people. - - Rung 3: Report it. If the problem happened in a game or app, grown-ups can press the report or block button. Some problems can be reported to helpers whose job is keeping kids safe (the school counselor, the principal, or helplines your grown-up can call). Say: “If a rung does not work, because the adult was busy or didn't listen, you keep climbing until someone helps. Never stop at the first no.”

Step 2 (1 min): Fill the Trusted Hand Hand out the Trusted Hand worksheet (Activity Book p. 1). Students write or draw five trusted adults, one per finger. Say: “At least one should be at school and one at home. If you only can think of two, that's fine. We'll add more.”

Step 3 (1 min): Learn three sentence starters Read each aloud and have the class repeat in a normal voice: - - “I need help with something online.” - - “Something happened and I'm not sure what to do.” - - “I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something.”

Timer for these steps: 3 min. Press T to start.

Step 4 (4 min): Triad role-play Form groups of three (a Teller, a Listener and an Observer). Hand each triad one Ladder Scenario Card (R-1.1). The Teller reads the card, picks the rung, and says the first sentence. The Listener practices the Good Grown-up Reply: “Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together.” The Observer gives one star (“what went well”) and one wish (“next time try...”). After 90 seconds, rotate roles with a new card. Do two rounds.

Step 5 (1 min): Share Ask two volunteers to demonstrate in front of the class. Applaud the Teller, not the story.

Timer for these steps: 5 min. Press T to start.

Takeaway (2 minutes together with the exit ticket). Do the hand signals as you say each word.

“Pause, check, tell. If something online feels wrong, you do not have to figure it out alone. Climb the Ladder until someone helps. Let's do the Hackle Beat together (Part 2): Hackles up? Gut check! Pause! (palm) Check! (magnifier) Tell! (hand on chest). Trust your hackles!”

Hackles line: When something online feels off, your hackles go up. That uh-oh feeling is a gut check. Climb the Ladder even if it turns out to be a false alarm. False alarms are fine, and telling is never your fault.

Pick ONE boost inside your 15 minutes. The rest are in the Teacher Guide for another day.

Energizer (30 seconds): “Ladder Climb.”: Everyone stands and mimes climbing three rungs with big knee lifts, chanting “Friend! Grown-up! Report!” On the top rung shout “Keep climbing!” and climb again.

Game variant: “First Sentence Speed-Dating.”: Two rows face each other. Every 20 seconds one row shifts and each new pair says a different first sentence in a different voice (robot, whisper, news anchor, pirate). Rehearsal is the point: the sentence gets easier to say every time.

Chant (any beat): “Rung one: a friend. Rung two: a grown-up. / Rung three: report it, and don't give up! / If a rung won't help you, don't stop there: / Keep climbing, keep climbing, till someone cares!”

Silly voice / puppet moment: Hackle is the “Ladder Coach” with a whistle and a sports-announcer voice during role-plays: “And Kenji tells Aunt Lin on the FIRST try! The crowd goes wild!” Applaud the telling, never the story.

Level-Up Challenge: Ladder for a Friend. Write what you would say if a friend told YOU something scary (“I'm glad you told me. Let's go tell a grown-up together.”). Then teach the Ladder to a younger reading buddy in a one-minute mini-lesson.
