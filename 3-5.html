Hands-On Hackers

Ten hands-on lessons for children aged 8 to 10. Each one is a mini-lab or a rehearsal: students crack a code, time a password guess, sort scams and practise what to say out loud.

The promise to students, repeated in every lesson: you are never in trouble for telling, and it is never your fault.

This page is for the grown-up. Children do not need to visit this site.

II.A three-step start

Teach it this week

  1. Read two things first

    Read the Teacher Primer (about 10 minutes) and the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Check your school's mandatory-reporting policy.

  2. Get Lesson 1 ready

    Read the card for Lesson 1 and print the card sets, one per group of 4 or 5. Tape the posters to the wall for the whole unit. Send the family letter.

  3. Teach Lesson 1

    Teach Lesson 1, The Help Ladder. It takes 15 minutes. Read the exit tickets the same day and send the family card home.

After that, teach one lesson a week for 10 weeks. Each Teacher Guide has its own pacing guide, and that one wins.

IV.A note for the adult who teaches

Sensitive lessons and the counselor heads-up

Students may disclose something during or after any lesson, and these lessons make it likelier. That means the lesson is working. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit marks two 3-5 lessons as sensitive.

Sensitive lessons, grades 3-5
LessonWhy it is sensitive
Lesson 6. Game Chat Red FlagsGrooming warning signs, secrecy, pressure
Lesson 1. The Help LadderRole-play of telling about worries

Teachers and counselors still report under their own policy and state law. Hackles does not change that.

Before you teach one of these lessons

  1. Tell your school counselor and an administrator which weeks you are teaching. The Teacher Guide names Lessons 1, 6, 7 and 10 as the likeliest to prompt disclosures. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit has the message.
  2. Know your school's mandatory-reporting procedure and who your designated contact is.
  3. Send the family letter and offer an opt-out, with an alternate activity and no penalty to the student.
  4. Read the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Have a calm place and a next step ready, so you will not be improvising.

V.The family piece

For families of ages 8 to 10

A family card and a reply slip come home with each lesson. Lesson 9 asks the family to choose a code word together.

Three questions to try at home

  1. What are the three rungs of the Help Ladder?
  2. What would be a good family code word? When would we use it?
  3. What is one thing you would want me to know about your online world?

These come from the ten conversation starters for ages 8-10 in the Family Engagement Pack. The pack also has the letters, the family code word guide and a page on what to say if your child tells you something scary.

If your child shares a mistake, begin with “Thank you for telling me.”

If a child is in danger nowCall your local emergency number. This site cannot respond to emergencies.

VI.Practice and play

Lab tools and games that fit

All six Lab tools fit this age, and three share a name with a lesson. They run offline in a browser. They use JavaScript, send nothing anywhere and store nothing.

Hackles Lab

  • Help Ladder Trail Grades K-8A team trail game for the whole routine, with the Help Ladder and the Next-Rung Rule.
  • Passphrase Lab Grades 3-12Build practice passphrases and compare how long each would take to guess.
  • Spot the Fishy K-12Notice the clues that make a message feel fishy, and sort real from fishy.
  • Secret Code Lab Grades 3-8Scramble and unscramble messages picked from a list. Nothing personal can be typed in.
  • Data Detective Grades 3-8See how small clues add up to a made-up person.
  • Hackles or Not Grades 2-8Practise the whole routine on short situations: notice, then choose Pause, Check or Tell.
