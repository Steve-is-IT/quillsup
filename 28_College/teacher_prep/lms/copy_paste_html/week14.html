Week 14: Capstone Studio: Designing the Family or Community Workshop

Academy module: M7. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 7: Partner with families and communities: explain the program plainly, respond to concern with L.E.A.R.N., and plan and deliver a workshop with a counselor partner.
  • CLO 4: Design, teach and refine a skills-based 15-minute Hackles lesson (Hook 2, Activity 8, Discuss 3, Takeaway 2) and a five-lesson unit that spirals across a grade band and maps to standards.

Quiz M8: Measure, Reflect, Grow (self-check, 10 items)

Mastery is 8 of 10. Answers and rationales are inside each item. Copy these into your LMS quiz tool or use the QTI file.

  1. What are Module 8's three questions to ask about teaching?

    • A. Is it graded? Is it published? Is it paid?
    • B. Did they learn? Would they act? Is it safe to tell?
    • C. Was it fun? Was it long? Was it cheap?
    • D. Who won? Who lost? Who left?
    Show answer

    Answer B. The third is the most important and hardest to measure.

  2. What is the Tell-Readiness indicator?

    • A. The share of students who give the best answer on the two or three core 'would you tell' items
    • B. The number of disclosures a teacher receives
    • C. The grade on the final exam
    • D. The number of students in class
    Show answer

    Answer A. It is the Assessment Pack's most meaningful number. Report it as a change in survey answers.

  3. Pre: 11 of 24 chose the best answer. Post: 17 of 22. What is the change in percentage points, rounded?

    • A. About +31 points (46 percent to 77 percent)
    • B. About +17 points
    • C. About +55 points
    • D. About +6 points
    Show answer

    Answer A. 11/24 is about 46 percent and 17/22 is about 77 percent. The kit's honest summary adds that class sizes differed slightly.

  4. Core items pre: 26 of 72; post: 51 of 72. What is the Tell-Readiness change?

    • A. From 36 percent to 71 percent, +35 percentage points
    • B. +71 points
    • C. +51 points
    • D. +25 points
    Show answer

    Answer A. 26/72 is about 36 percent and 51/72 is about 71 percent.

  5. Why may disclosures go up after lessons, according to the Assessment Pack?

    • A. Because the lessons cause more harm
    • B. Students feel safer to tell, which can be a sign the program is working; more or fewer disclosures does not map neatly to more or less harm
    • C. Because the survey is faulty
    • D. Because teachers invent them
    Show answer

    Answer B. Do not treat an increase in disclosures as a failure.

  6. Why does the kit say that with 24 students small changes may be noise?

    • A. One child is about 4 percentage points, so small shifts can come from a few students or chance
    • B. Surveys are never accurate
    • C. Percentages cannot be computed for 24
    • D. Statistics require 100 students
    Show answer

    Answer A. Treat results as signals, not proof.

  7. What can a pre/post survey without a comparison group not show?

    • A. Cause, real-world behavior, honest answers under pressure, long-term effects
    • B. How many students are in class
    • C. Which items were skipped
    • D. Whether the survey printed
    Show answer

    Answer A. Changes may reflect maturation, other lessons, news, repeated testing or chance.

  8. Matched pairs: 15 up, 7 same, 2 down among 24. Which statement fits the kit's example?

    • A. 'Everyone improved.'
    • B. 'Two students are failing.'
    • C. 'Most students (15 of 24) improved. A few (2) went down. We look at those who did not move and offer more practice.'
    • D. 'The program caused improvement in 15 students.'
    Show answer

    Answer C. Report counts and offer additional practice.

  9. Scores on the post survey went down. What does Scenario 8.A suggest?

    • A. Hide the results
    • B. Blame the students
    • C. Drop the program
    • D. Look at the answers before panicking; students may be more honest after learning, or the post survey may be harder; ask students safely what would make telling easier
    Show answer

    Answer D. A drop can occur by chance and is worth examining only if it appears across several classes.

  10. A teacher plans to add a survey item asking students whether they have ever been bullied online. What does the kit say?

    • A. It is fine if no names are used
    • B. It is fine if anonymous
    • C. Keep student surveys to knowledge, skills and confidence; items about a student's own experiences can fall under PPRA protected areas and need counsel, parental review and consent [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
    • D. It is fine if the teacher gives it in class
    Show answer

    Answer C. The kit warns that a survey on protected areas given by a school official is treated as required and needs written parental consent; an opt-out is not enough [VERIFIED 2026-10-05; re-verify].
