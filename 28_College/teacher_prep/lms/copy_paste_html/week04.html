Week 4: How Young People Really Live Online

Academy module: M3. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 3: Open conversations about learners' digital lives with curiosity instead of judgment, using the four patterns (Pressure, Secrecy, The Ask, The Move) and the curiosity script.
  • CLO 1: Describe the main online risks facing K-12 learners in calm, accurate language, and judge a statistic or claim by tracing it to its primary source and saying what it can and cannot show.

Quiz M2: The Cybersecurity You Need (self-check, 10 items)

Mastery is 8 of 10. Answers and rationales are inside each item. Copy these into your LMS quiz tool or use the QTI file.

  1. What does Module 2 say about a login code a service sends you?

    • A. It is safe to share if the person sounds friendly
    • B. A moderator may need it to fix your account
    • C. A code is a key: nobody who is really helping you will ask you to read it to them
    • D. Support staff may ask for it to verify you
    Show answer

    Answer C. Even a code from a text message can be tricked out of you. The rule holds for support, friends and moderators.

  2. Why does Module 2 advise turning on 2-step verification for email first?

    • A. Email can reset many other accounts, so it is the master key
    • B. Email accounts do not have passwords
    • C. Email stores the most photos
    • D. Email is the only account that can be hacked
    Show answer

    Answer A. Protect the account that can unlock the others.

  3. What is a passkey?

    • A. A code texted to your phone
    • B. A password manager's master phrase
    • C. A cryptographic key on your device unlocked by fingerprint, face or screen lock, replacing a typed password
    • D. A longer password written on paper
    Show answer

    Answer C. Nothing to type and nothing to hand over in a phishing message. Support varies by service, and recovery needs planning.

  4. Why can a passkey resist a phishing message?

    • A. It expires every hour
    • B. There is nothing to type or hand over, and the key only works for the real site on your device
    • C. It is stored in plain text where fake sites cannot see it
    • D. It is a very long password
    Show answer

    Answer B. The kit's description: nothing to copy and nothing to reuse. NIST treats such authenticators as phishing resistant.

  5. Which are the five hackles-raisers taught for phishing and scams?

    • A. Spelling, color, logo, font and length
    • B. Urgency, secrecy, too good to be true, the ask, and does not add up
    • C. Speed, price, brand, color and sound
    • D. Sender, subject, date, size and language
    Show answer

    Answer B. None of the five depends on technology, so they travel across apps.

  6. A message says, 'Your account will be deleted in 1 hour. Click to confirm your password.' Which hackles-raisers are present?

    • A. Secrecy only
    • B. Urgency and the ask
    • C. None of them
    • D. Too good to be true only
    Show answer

    Answer B. A deadline is urgency and a request to confirm a password is the ask. Pause and check another way.

  7. Why does the kit say 'Never share personal information online' is not enough on its own?

    • A. It is a complete safety plan
    • B. It is illegal advice
    • C. It is vague and cannot be followed perfectly, so it should be paired with 'who is asking, why, and tell a trusted adult'
    • D. It applies only to adults
    Show answer

    Answer C. The kit frames a better rule: who is asking, why, and do I trust them, plus telling.

  8. What does Demo 7 teach about updates?

    • A. Updates make old holes larger
    • B. Updates only add new features
    • C. Updates are optional for security
    • D. An update is a fix for a known hole, and the race is between the fix reaching devices and someone misusing the hole
    Show answer

    Answer D. Software has mistakes; some are holes. Every copy needs the fix.

  9. The kit says 'at least 15 characters (CISA recommends 16 or more)'. Why are both numbers there?

    • A. Both numbers are guesses
    • B. 15 is for children and 16 is for adults
    • C. The two agencies disagree and the kit picked one at random
    • D. NIST sets 15 as the minimum for a password used alone, while CISA recommends at least 16 characters or 5 to 7 unrelated words
    Show answer

    Answer D. Round 1 corrected the earlier rule to reflect both sources.

  10. A slide says 'MFA blocks over 99 percent of attacks, says Microsoft.' How should it be handled?

    • A. Raise it to 100 percent for emphasis
    • B. Delete all mention of MFA
    • C. Present it as a proven national statistic
    • D. Label it as a vendor claim (99.9 percent of account compromise attacks, 2019, citing a SANS white paper), indicative not exact, and note CISA gives no percentage
    Show answer

    Answer D. Round 1 corrected the wording and kept the caution.
