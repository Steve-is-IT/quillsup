Week 8: Responding to Disclosures I: Duty, the First 10 Minutes and Care

Academy module: M6. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Content note. This week practices the first minutes after a student discloses something hard, using fictional, non-graphic cards only. You choose how you take part: Teacher, Observer, or the written alternative. Traffic-light cards, a counselor partner and a de-role step are built in. Nothing asks for a personal story.

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 5: Carry out the First 10 Minutes response, state your own reporting duty and local route, protect evidence without viewing images, document facts, and follow a personal care plan.
  • CLO 9: Apply legal and policy frameworks (mandated reporting, state instruction mandates, CIPA, FERPA, COPPA, PPRA) to classroom decisions, mark what is unverified, and know when to ask counsel.

Before class (required reading)

  • Hackles Teacher Academy, Module 6, Parts 1 to 4 and Part 6 (read Part 5 scripts before class)
  • Disclosure and Incident Guide, Sections 1 to 4 (Why this guide exists; First 10 Minutes; Step-by-step; What to say and not to say)
  • Child Welfare Information Gateway, 'Mandatory Reporting of Child Abuse and Neglect' (statutes current through May 2023) [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in kit; confirm your own state's current statute]

Optional reading

  • State Alignment Atlas (23_State_Atlas) entry for your state, as a starting point only [DRAFT; VERIFY]

In class

MinutesSegmentWhat we do
10Opening and content noteThe counselor partner and the instructor open together. Content note: today's cards are fictional and non-graphic, but the topic can touch real life for anyone. You are in charge of your participation. Traffic-light cards out (green keep going; yellow slow down; red pause the scene). Instructor reporting obligations explained (see Instructor Notes).
25Mandated reporting basicsFive questions to answer for your state and district: Am I a mandated reporter? What is the standard (reasonable suspicion, not proof)? Who do I report to and how? How fast? Is my duty personal? Telling a principal may not satisfy the duty [VERIFY your state].
20The First 10 MinutesStep by step: receive it, listen and reassure, ask only what you need, be honest about what happens next, protect evidence safely (never view, copy, forward or screenshot suspected sexual images of a minor), report, document, follow up with care. The three sentences: 'Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble.'
10BreakStretch, water, step outside. Nobody has to explain a longer break.
20Activity 6.2 Say It Out LoudPractice each phrase in your calmest voice and softest face. Phrases to avoid and why (Why did you send it? That is disgusting. I won't tell anyone. Let me see.).
25Demonstration and warm-up roundFacilitator Kit method (Half-Day Guide, Segment 6): the instructor and counselor demonstrate the version we are trying not to do, then the calm version. Trios: Teller, Listener, Observer. Round 1 uses card PC1 'The Oops' (fictional). 4 minutes plus 1 minute observer feedback, then swap.
25Round 2 and Round 3Cards from the Practicum Card deck (Assignments and Rubrics, Section 3). No points, no audience, no timers shown. Observers use the 11-item checklist. If a card pulls too close, show red and step out; nobody asks why.
15De-role and debriefStand, shake out, say 'I'm [real name], that was pretend.' Skills only, no stories: a phrase to keep, the hardest thing to avoid saying, and who in the room needs to look at their Contact Card.
20Contact Card and Reporting MapActivity 6.1. Start filling in your Contact Card and look up your state's mandated reporting summary at childwelfare.gov. Finish for homework; verify with your administrator or counselor. Counselor partner closes with a one-minute care moment and says how to reach campus counseling [INSTITUTION].
10ClosingExit ticket and open door. Instructor and counselor stay for ten minutes after class.

Discussion prompt

Optional alternative to role-play for anyone who prefers: read the Script B debrief notes (Module 6) and post what the teacher did well, what is missing, and the next three steps after the conversation ends. Do not post personal experiences.

Journal prompt

Write about preparation and care in general terms: What do you notice in your body when you imagine a child telling you something hard? What is in your care plan (right after, same day, that evening, that week)?
