Week 9: Responding to Disclosures II: Practicum, STOP-SAVE-TELL-REPORT, Bullying and Self-Care (Assessment 4)

Academy module: M6. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Content note. This week practices the first minutes after a student discloses something hard, using fictional, non-graphic cards only. You choose how you take part: Teacher, Observer, or the written alternative. Traffic-light cards, a counselor partner and a de-role step are built in. Nothing asks for a personal story.

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 5: Carry out the First 10 Minutes response, state your own reporting duty and local route, protect evidence without viewing images, document facts, and follow a personal care plan.
  • CLO 9: Apply legal and policy frameworks (mandated reporting, state instruction mandates, CIPA, FERPA, COPPA, PPRA) to classroom decisions, mark what is unverified, and know when to ask counsel.

Quiz M6: Sensitive Topics and Disclosures (self-check, 10 items)

Mastery is 8 of 10. Answers and rationales are inside each item. Copy these into your LMS quiz tool or use the QTI file.

  1. What is the teacher's role in a disclosure?

    • A. Counselor who treats the child
    • B. Detective who confronts the other person
    • C. Recognition and response: listen, reassure, protect evidence safely, report, hand off, document, and look after the child and yourself
    • D. Investigator who finds out what happened
    Show answer

    Answer C. You are not an investigator, a therapist or a police officer.

  2. Why should you not promise a child 'I won't tell anyone'?

    • A. Because teachers are not allowed to speak to children privately
    • B. Because children do not care about secrecy
    • C. Because secrets are always safe to keep
    • D. You may be required to tell people whose job is to help, and breaking a promise hurts more; say 'I can't keep this a secret, because I want to get you help. I will tell you who I tell.'
    Show answer

    Answer D. Honesty about limits builds trust.

  3. A second grader says, 'Somebody on my tablet game said not to tell my mom. He gives me gems.' What is the best response?

    • A. 'Show me the chat.'
    • B. 'He is probably just a kind player.'
    • C. 'Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble. Can you tell me a little more?' Then report on reasonable suspicion without asking for names or details
    • D. Phone the child's mother before anyone else
    Show answer

    Answer C. Secrecy and gifts from an unknown person are a reasonable-suspicion pattern. Do not interrogate, and do not contact the family first.

  4. A student says a classmate has an intimate image on a phone. A colleague suggests you 'just look to check.' What do you do?

    • A. Delete the image to protect the classmate
    • B. Look briefly to confirm
    • C. Ask another student to forward it to you
    • D. Do not look; ask the student not to delete or forward anything; secure the device per policy; report personally and promptly
    Show answer

    Answer D. Good intentions are not a safe assumption. Authorities collect evidence lawfully.

  5. In STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT, what is saved?

    • A. The password to the account
    • B. Nothing; everything is deleted
    • C. Account names, profile links, dates and threatening text, never images
    • D. Copies of any image sent
    Show answer

    Answer C. SAVE means text evidence.

  6. Which entry belongs in your documentation of a disclosure?

    • A. Your guess about what really happened
    • B. An opinion about the family
    • C. A diagnosis
    • D. The child's exact words in quotation marks, date, time, place, who you told and what you did
    Show answer

    Answer D. Document facts only.

  7. After a lesson on pressure online, a student says, 'It's kind of me.' after speaking of 'a friend.' What now?

    • A. 'Why did you not say so?'
    • B. Promise to tell nobody
    • C. Thank them calmly, say the three sentences, be honest about telling people who can help, and do not ask to see messages or images
    • D. 'I knew it was you.'
    Show answer

    Answer C. Accept the shift calmly. Honest limits and no pressure.

  8. What is secondary traumatic stress, in plain language?

    • A. Hearing about a child's hard experience can affect you even though you were not there; it is common and does not mean you did something wrong [VERIFY with a mental health source if cited]
    • B. A form of blame
    • C. A sign that you should not teach any topic
    • D. A condition only counselors get
    Show answer

    Answer A. Care plan layers: right after, same day, that evening, that week, longer term.

  9. Which statement is a phrase to avoid in a disclosure, and why?

    • A. 'I believe you.' It is too short
    • B. 'Your parents will be so upset.' It adds fear
    • C. 'Thank you for telling me.' It is a form of praise
    • D. 'What would help you feel safest?' It is leading
    Show answer

    Answer B. The avoid list also includes why questions, shock, 'I won't tell anyone,' 'I'm sure it's fine,' 'Let me see,' and 'Are you sure?'

  10. Which of these needs immediate escalation to an administrator and counselor?

    • A. A game score argument
    • B. A late assignment
    • C. A mean nickname used once
    • D. Threats of violence, self-harm statements, sexual content, doxxing, or a child saying they feel unsafe at school or home
    Show answer

    Answer D. These are the red flags listed in the bullying section.
