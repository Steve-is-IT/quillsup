Week 2: The Cybersecurity You Need I: Accounts, Updates and Backups

Academy module: M2. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 2: Demonstrate and explain basic personal and classroom security habits (unique passphrases or passkeys, 2-step verification, phishing recognition, updates, backups, privacy settings) using fake data only, and say each one in words suited to a learner's age.

  • CISA, 'Require strong passwords' (Secure Our World), cisa.gov/secure-our-world/require-strong-passwords [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in kit]
  • Concepts Lab Index, Parts 1 and 2 (Do, Debrief, Teach)

Quiz M1: Why This Matters (self-check, 10 items)

Mastery is 8 of 10. Answers and rationales are inside each item. Copy these into your LMS quiz tool or use the QTI file.

  1. Which set of four features does Module 1 say shows up across the seven risk families?

    • A. Passwords, updates, backups and filters
    • B. Speed, volume, cost and location
    • C. Strangers, screens, games and money
    • D. Pressure, secrecy, a request, and a move somewhere more private
    Show answer

    Answer D. The kit teaches the pattern, not the app. Pressure, secrecy, a request and a move somewhere more private recur whether the setting is a game, a chat or a feed.

  2. What is the Hackles routine?

    • A. Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.
    • B. Look. Listen. Learn.
    • C. Block. Delete. Forget.
    • D. Stop. Drop. Roll.
    Show answer

    Answer A. Notice the feeling, do nothing yet, check whether it is real and who is asking, then tell a trusted adult.

  3. Why does Module 1 say the old 'stranger danger' slogan is not enough online?

    • A. Strangers are never a danger to children online
    • B. Only adults who are strangers can cause online harm
    • C. Much online harm starts with accounts that look like peers or people a child half-knows, so children need to notice pressure, secrecy and requests whoever is asking
    • D. Children cannot tell who is a stranger, so they should avoid all online contact
    Show answer

    Answer C. The skill taught is noticing the pattern and telling. The slogan misses accounts that seem friendly and familiar.

  4. A candidate wants to quote 'one-third of children told no one and only 3 percent went to police or a helpline.' Which handling is best?

    • A. Say the figures come from UNICEF's first 13 countries (Africa and Southeast Asia), not the US, and confirm them in the primary reports before quoting [VERIFY]
    • B. Present them as US figures because the topic is the same
    • C. Quote them as exact and universal
    • D. Add that 97 percent of US children tell a parent
    Show answer

    Answer A. Module 1 states the geographic limit. The Round 1 report could not confirm the figures on the project page, so they stay marked for checking.

  5. What does the Finkelhor and colleagues (2021) review, as summarized in Module 1, suggest about lesson types?

    • A. One large assembly is the best-supported approach
    • B. Lectures on rules outperform role-play
    • C. Active, skill-based, repeated programs are better supported than one-off assemblies or information-only lessons
    • D. Fear-based warnings are proven to work best
    Show answer

    Answer C. The summary table in Module 1 favors active, structured, repeated programs. The kit marks the citation [VERIFY] until read in full.

  6. Your principal says, 'One big assembly and we are done for the year.' Which reply fits Module 1?

    • A. Refuse and explain that assemblies are harmful
    • B. Affirm the goal, then offer both/and: a short kickoff plus ten short classroom lessons, and offer to pilot with one grade
    • C. Tell the principal the evidence proves assemblies never work
    • D. Agree, since any program is better than none
    Show answer

    Answer B. Both/and keeps the visible response the principal wants and adds repeated skill practice. Overstating the evidence would not be honest.

  7. Which of these is NOT part of a teacher's role in Hackles?

    • A. Reporting as required
    • B. Noticing and listening
    • C. Handing off to trained people
    • D. Investigating who did it and confronting a suspect
    Show answer

    Answer D. The role is recognition and response. Investigation, confrontation, viewing images, searching phones and counseling are not the teacher's job.

  8. Module 1 Calm Fact 1 says most children use technology safely most of the time. What follows for teaching?

    • A. Build skills calmly for the minority of moments that go wrong, rather than skipping lessons or using scare tactics
    • B. Assume every child is already being harmed
    • C. Skip online safety lessons
    • D. Teach with graphic stories so the minority takes it seriously
    Show answer

    Answer A. The fact supports calm skill-building. The existence of serious cases is the reason to teach at all.

  9. An article says Thorn found that about 1 in 25 teens have had deepfake nudes created of them. What does the kit's check say?

    • A. The article is right; Thorn's figure is 1 in 25
    • B. Thorn reported 1 in 17 teens; '1 in 25' is the UNICEF figure for some countries, so the article's attribution is wrong
    • C. Thorn's figure is 1 in 8, which is the same thing
    • D. Neither organization has reported such a figure
    Show answer

    Answer B. Round 1 corrected this exact error: 1 in 17 (Thorn, 1,200 US young people) and 1 in 8 who know someone targeted; 1 in 25 is UNICEF in some countries.

  10. Which sentence for a parent handout is the most defensible?

    • A. 'Every statistic in this handout is guaranteed accurate.'
    • B. 'Studies show Hackles prevents sextortion.'
    • C. 'Research proves fear-based warnings backfire in online safety.'
    • D. 'The evidence on fear appeals is mixed, and direct evidence for online safety is thin, so we teach calm skills with practice.'
    Show answer

    Answer D. The kit says not to claim fear appeals are proven to backfire in this field and that no trial shows the program prevents harm.
