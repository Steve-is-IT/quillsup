Week 3: The Cybersecurity You Need II: Phishing, Social Engineering and Privacy

Academy module: M2. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 2: Demonstrate and explain basic personal and classroom security habits (unique passphrases or passkeys, 2-step verification, phishing recognition, updates, backups, privacy settings) using fake data only, and say each one in words suited to a learner's age.
  • CLO 9: Apply legal and policy frameworks (mandated reporting, state instruction mandates, CIPA, FERPA, COPPA, PPRA) to classroom decisions, mark what is unverified, and know when to ask counsel.

Before class (required reading)

  • Hackles Teacher Academy, Module 2, Parts 2, 3 and 6 (phishing, privacy, the Five Habits poster)
  • Concepts Lab Demo 3 (Anatomy of a Phish) and Demo 8 (Social Engineering Defense), teacher guides
  • FBI IC3 public service announcement on family code words (PSA241203) and FTC consumer alert on voice-cloned family emergency calls [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in kit]

Optional reading

  • Concepts Lab Demo 9 (Data Footprints and Data Brokers) and Demo 11 (Malware Metaphors)
  • studentprivacy.ed.gov FAQ, 'Who is a school official under FERPA?' [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in kit]
  • FTC COPPA rule page, ftc.gov [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in kit]

Due this week

Journal entry 2. Demo 2 Part A done.

Assigned this week

Journal entry 3. Module 2 self-check (covers Weeks 2 and 3; due Week 4). Module 3 reading. Prepare one Curiosity Interview plan for Week 4 (adults only unless your placement approves).

Field tie-in

Field office confirms placement site and mentor teacher. In-service teachers: identify the counselor and the reporting contact at your school (you will need both in Week 8).

Discussion prompt

Post a fake message you wrote yourself (invented sender, invented link) and annotate it with the SPOT or FLAGS signals. What would the Own-Door check be?

Journal prompt

Describe a near-miss with a scam or phishing message (yours or a composite). What made your hackles go up, or what do you wish had? Keep it shame-free.

Reminder: no real student names, work, passwords or incident details appear in any assignment. Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.
