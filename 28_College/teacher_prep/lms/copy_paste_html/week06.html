Week 6: Running a 15-Minute Hackles Lesson

Academy module: M5. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 4: Design, teach and refine a skills-based 15-minute Hackles lesson (Hook 2, Activity 8, Discuss 3, Takeaway 2) and a five-lesson unit that spirals across a grade band and maps to standards.

Before class (required reading)

  • Hackles Teacher Academy, Module 5: Running a 15-Minute Hackles Lesson (all parts, including the Micro-Teach Rubric)
  • Peer Coaching and Observation Guide, Sections 3 (Fun, Safe, Skill-based rubric) and 8 (Two Glows and a Grow)
  • One Teacher Guide lesson of your choice from a non-sensitive list (see Assignments and Rubrics, Section 2)

Quiz M4: Teaching Safety That Works (self-check, 10 items)

Mastery is 8 of 10. Answers and rationales are inside each item. Copy these into your LMS quiz tool or use the QTI file.

  1. Which is a skill-based objective (the 'Teacher test')?

    • A. After this, students will appreciate security
    • B. After this, students will know that passwords are important
    • C. After this, students will be aware of hackers
    • D. After this, students can say 'No, I'll check with my grown-up' when someone asks for a password
    Show answer

    Answer D. A skill is a verb students can show. Know, be aware and appreciate cannot be observed.

  2. Why does role-play work for safety skills?

    • A. It tests children on facts
    • B. It replaces the need to tell an adult
    • C. It scares children into compliance
    • D. It makes the words available when the real moment comes; a child who has practiced 'I need help' can say it once in a hard moment
    Show answer

    Answer D. Practice builds a reflex.

  3. Which framing replaces a fear message with a tool?

    • A. 'If you talk to a stranger, something terrible could happen.'
    • B. 'If anyone asks for secrets or pictures, your hackles go up. Pause and tell.'
    • C. 'Your life will be ruined.'
    • D. 'You will be hacked!'
    Show answer

    Answer B. The Hackles framing gives a signal and an action.

  4. Which phrase best replaces 'Why did you click it?'

    • A. 'You should have known better.'
    • B. 'Good kids do not do that.'
    • C. 'What happened next?'
    • D. 'How could you?'
    Show answer

    Answer C. 'Why' questions can sound like blame.

  5. Why does the kit say 'never' language invites failure?

    • A. Adults should say 'always' instead
    • B. Children inevitably share some things, so 'never share your name' sets them up to feel they failed; 'think first' and 'check first' are more honest
    • C. Children are not capable of safe behavior
    • D. 'Never' is too gentle for safety lessons
    Show answer

    Answer B. Prefer 'think first,' 'ask a trusted adult,' 'check first.'

  6. Two grade 4 password lessons are compared. Which is the good lesson?

    • A. The teacher reads ten password rules from slides and gives a quiz
    • B. The teacher warns that bad passwords will cost students everything
    • C. The teacher assigns a chapter on password history
    • D. Groups build the funniest four-word passphrase, then use a crack-timer card; exit ticket asks for a new rule and when to tell a grown-up
    Show answer

    Answer D. The good lesson has students create, compare, discuss and decide in a calm tone.

  7. Two grade 8 lessons on pressure online: A shows graphic stories of what happens to children who share pictures; B uses calm case files about a fictional character and rehearses STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT. Which statement is correct?

    • A. A is better because it motivates through fear
    • B. B follows the evidence-informed approach: no graphic detail, a skill, and help-seeking, while A risks shame, fear and silence
    • C. A is better because it is more realistic
    • D. Both are equally good
    Show answer

    Answer B. The kit's Good versus Weak pair says exactly this.

  8. Which item is on the 'good lesson' checklist?

    • A. Gives students a role other than victim
    • B. Includes a graphic example for realism
    • C. Requires every student to share a personal story
    • D. Runs 45 minutes
    Show answer

    Answer A. Also: one clear skill, a rehearsal, no fear or shame, ends with who to tell, about 15 minutes, a check, a safe way to disclose.

  9. A colleague says, 'Can't we just show a video? Fear gets attention.' What is the best reply?

    • A. A video alone is information; add a short rehearsal and a calm tone, because fear without a skill is just fear and shame blocks telling
    • B. Say a video is the same as an active lesson
    • C. Agree, because attention is the main goal
    • D. Say videos are always banned
    Show answer

    Answer A. The kit's rules favor rehearsal and calm skill practice. A video can serve as a hook only.

  10. A student who 'already knows it all' keeps interrupting. What does the kit suggest?

    • A. Quiz them in front of the class
    • B. Send them out of the room
    • C. Ignore them for the rest of the term
    • D. Give them a role, such as a helper, detective or demonstrator
    Show answer

    Answer D. Module 5's management table says to give know-it-alls and jokers a role.
