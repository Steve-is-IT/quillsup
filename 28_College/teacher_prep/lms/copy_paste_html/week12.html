Week 12: Law, Policy and Standards: What a Teacher Needs to Know

Academy module: M1. Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).

Learning outcomes this week

  • CLO 9: Apply legal and policy frameworks (mandated reporting, state instruction mandates, CIPA, FERPA, COPPA, PPRA) to classroom decisions, mark what is unverified, and know when to ask counsel.
  • CLO 4: Design, teach and refine a skills-based 15-minute Hackles lesson (Hook 2, Activity 8, Discuss 3, Takeaway 2) and a five-lesson unit that spirals across a grade band and maps to standards.

Before class (required reading)

  • Florida HB 379 Crosswalk (20_Florida), Sections 1 and 2, as a worked example of a state instruction mandate [DRAFT; VERIFY; not legal advice]
  • State Alignment Atlas, summary table for your state (23_State_Atlas) [DRAFT; VERIFY]
  • Concepts Lab Index, Part 4 (CSTA-style standards map) and Part 9 (Safety box)

Optional reading

25Federal baseline in plain languageCIPA: E-rate schools certify that their internet safety policy includes educating minors about appropriate online behavior [VERIFIED in kit: USAC]. COPPA: operators of services directed to children under 13. FERPA: education records; school official exception for vendors. PPRA: surveys on protected areas (see below). Your job is to know when to ask counsel, not to be counsel.
30PPRA and the surveys you will want to giveSurveys of students on the protected areas can require parent review and written consent. As reported, the US Department of Education's Aug 26, 2026 letter says a survey on those areas given by a teacher, counselor or other school official is treated as required, so an opt-out is not enough [VERIFIED in kit: studentprivacy.ed.gov; re-verify and confirm with district counsel]. Keep student surveys to knowledge, skills and confidence.
