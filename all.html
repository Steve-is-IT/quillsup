I.Method

Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.

Hackles are the hairs that rise on your neck when something feels off: a message from someone you do not know, or a pop-up that is a little too friendly. This kit teaches one habit for that moment, in three steps.

Margin noteA false alarm is a good result. Checking and finding nothing is the habit working.

  1. Pause.

    Stop. Take one breath. Do not click, reply, pay or send. Nothing has to happen in the next minute.

  2. Check.

    Does this look right? Look again, or ask someone. If it turns out to be nothing, good. That is what checking is for.

  3. Tell.

    Tell a trusted adult. If something already went wrong, it was never your fault, and telling someone is still the next step.

Each lesson takes 15 minutes: a 2-minute hook, an 8-minute activity, a 3-minute talk and a 2-minute takeaway with an exit ticket. The scripts are written out word for word. Most lessons need no device and no login.

Six strands run through every grade

  • Lock ItAccounts, passphrases and keeping them safe. Latch.
  • Spot ItScams, fakes and things that look slightly wrong. Peeper.
  • Share SmartPrivacy, and choosing what to show. Pip.
  • Safe PeopleTrusted adults, and who to tell. Buddy.
  • Kind and WellBeing kind online and looking after yourself. Ember.
  • Fix ItWhat to do when something goes wrong. Fixit.

VI.For families

For families

You do not need to understand the technology to help. You need a calm voice and a few minutes.

At school, children practise one habit: Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell. Younger children learn to ask a grown-up before they click. Older students learn to spot pressure and to check what they see before they share it.

What helps at home

  1. Choose a family code word. If a call or message claims to be from someone you know and asks for money or secrets, ask for the word.
  2. Say this out loud once: “If something online scares or confuses you, tell me. You will not be in trouble.”
  3. Ask a real question now and then: “Has anything online felt off lately?” Then listen all the way to the end.

The Family Engagement Pack has the letters, the conversation starters by age and a page on what to say if your child tells you something scary. You can also download the Family Pack or read it en español (borrador).

Some lessons cover hard topics, always calmly. Your school should tell you before they are taught, and you can ask to opt out. See the note on sensitive content.

If a child is in danger nowCall your local emergency number. This site cannot respond to emergencies.

VII.A note for the adults who teach

Sensitive content

Some lessons touch real experiences. K-2 Lesson 9 covers body safety. Lessons in grades 6-12 cover grooming, sextortion, fake images and pressure online. Two Plus lessons cover trafficking and mental health. They are written to be calm, free of shame and not graphic. A child can still recognize their own life in them.

Teachers and counselors still report under their own policy and state law. Hackles does not change that.

Before you teach one of these lessons

  1. Give your school counselor a heads-up. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit has the message.
  2. Send the family letter and offer an opt-out, with an alternate activity and no penalty to the student.
  3. Read the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Children sometimes tell a trusted adult something serious.
  4. Read the whole lesson first. You know your students best.
  5. Hold the Plus lesson on trafficking until a trafficking-prevention specialist has reviewed it, and the Plus lesson on mental health until a counselor or clinician has.

IX.Privacy

No accounts, no tracking, no student data

This website sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, runs no scripts, has no forms and loads nothing from other sites. Even the fonts come from this server. We cannot see who visits or downloads.

This site never asks a visitor's age. The age doors on the home page are ordinary links.

The kit asks children for no accounts and no personal information, and the printable materials never will. The Reader keeps one optional thing in your browser: your theme choice, such as dark or white background. It never leaves your device.

Please never send us a child's name, photo or any identifying detail.
