Grades K-2Ages 5-7

Hackles

Ten short lessons for children aged 5 to 7. They learn who their helpers are, and that asking a grown-up before a click is always a good move.

Every lesson is 15 minutes with a script you can read aloud. Most need crayons, a poster and your voice.

This page is for the grown-up. Children do not need to visit this site.

II.A three-step start

Teach it this week

  1. Read two things first

    Read the Teacher Primer (about 10 minutes) and the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Check your school's mandatory-reporting policy.

  2. Get Lesson 1 ready

    Read the card for Lesson 1 and print the reproducibles you need. Hang the Hackles poster where every child can see it, and pick a calm spot for a child who needs a quiet moment. Send the family letter.

  3. Teach Lesson 1

    Teach Lesson 1, My Tell-Someone Team. It takes 15 minutes. Send the family card home afterwards.

After that, teach one lesson a week for 10 weeks. Each Teacher Guide has its own pacing guide, and that one wins.

IV.A note for the adult who teaches

Sensitive lessons and the counselor heads-up

Young children tell things at odd moments: during a story, in the lunch line, halfway through a drawing. This program teaches them to tell, and some will. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit marks two K-2 lessons as sensitive.

Sensitive lessons, grades K-2
LessonWhy it is sensitive
Lesson 9. My Body Is MineBody safety, telling about unsafe requests
Lesson 6. Online Friends vs. Real FriendsSecrets and unsafe contact

Still pending. The Lesson 9 scripts, the parent notice and the counselor co-teaching plan have not yet been reviewed by a prevention specialist.

Teachers and counselors still report under their own policy and state law. Hackles does not change that.

Before you teach one of these lessons

  1. Ask whether your district requires parent notification or opt-out for Lesson 9 or for body-safety instruction in general. Some do. [VERIFY] Send the notice at least a week ahead of Lesson 9.
  2. Give your school counselor a heads-up. Consider co-teaching Lesson 9 with the counselor, or ask the counselor to be available that day. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit has the message.
  3. Teach Lesson 9 exactly as written. Do not add details, graphic examples or stories.
  4. Read all ten "If a student discloses" boxes before you teach Lesson 1, and the Disclosure and Incident Guide.
  5. Plan 10 to 15 minutes after Lesson 9 when you or the counselor can talk privately with any child who needs it.

V.The family piece

For families of ages 5 to 7

A family card comes home with each lesson. At home you need a relaxed moment and one question at a time: a walk, a car ride, bedtime.

Three questions to try at home

  1. Who are three grown-ups you could tell if something felt yucky or scary?
  2. What is a secret you should always keep, and a secret you should tell?
  3. What is the Oops Button, and what do you do when you press it?

These come from the ten conversation starters for ages 5-7 in the Family Engagement Pack. The pack also has the letters, the family code word guide and a page on what to say if your child tells you something scary.

If your child shares a mistake, begin with “Thank you for telling me.”

If a child is in danger nowCall your local emergency number. This site cannot respond to emergencies.
