Scoring and comparing#

  • Part A: Add your 18 scores (range 18 to 90). Divide by 18 for an average (range 1 to 5).
  • Part B: Note each score separately; "dread" is information, not failure.
  • Part C: Answers: 1 c, 2 d, 3 a, 4 d, 5 c.
  • Compare pre and post: Which items moved up the most? Which did not move? What does that tell you about where to go next?
Scoring and comparing
Average (Part A) What it suggests
1.0 to 2.0 Starting out. The Academy is made for you. Take your time.
2.1 to 3.0 Some confidence. Choose the modules that matter most.
3.1 to 4.0 Solid. Aim for Hackles-Ready and consider mentoring.
4.1 to 5.0 Very confident. Check item 10 and 11 (response readiness) in particular; confidence is great, readiness is better.

A word on overconfidence: if your post-survey average is lower in some areas than your pre-survey, that may be because you now know what you did not know. This is a well-known pattern in learning. It is a good sign.

3. Quick Start Mini-Check (5 questions, 5 minutes)#

For Quick Start learners. Answer without looking; then check the key. A score of 4 or 5 earns the Quick Start Completion Note. A score of 3 or less: re-read the First 10 Minutes card and retry.

  1. What are the four steps of the Hackles routine?
    a) Look, Listen, Learn, Leave
    b) Stop, Block, Delete, Forget
    c) Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.
    d) Read, Write, Click, Share

  2. A child tells you something serious. Which response is best?
    a) "I promise I won't tell anyone."
    b) "Why didn't you tell me earlier?"
    c) "Let me see the pictures."
    d) "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble. I need to tell people whose job is to help."

  3. What are the four parts of the 15-minute lesson?
    a) Lecture, Quiz, Homework, Test
    b) Hook, Activity, Discuss, Takeaway
    c) Video, Video, Video, Video
    d) Warm-up, Break, Break, Cool-down

  4. Which is a skill rather than just information?
    a) "Scammers are everywhere."
    b) "The internet is large."
    c) "If someone asks for my code, I say 'No, I'll check with my grown-up.'"
    d) "Passwords are secret."

  5. Before teaching a lesson with sensitive content, what must you know?
    a) The latest slang
    b) Your counselor's name, your school's reporting contact, and the First 10 Minutes card
    c) Every app
    d) Nothing

Answer key: 1 c, 2 d, 3 b, 4 c, 5 b.

4. Module self-checks: summary#

Each module ends with a 10-question self-check and answer key. The rule is the same for all: 8 out of 10 or better moves you on; below that, re-read the section the question came from and try again. Retakes are allowed, free and encouraged. No one is keeping count.

5. Observed practice: two short checklists#

5.1 Micro-teach rubric (Module 5, Part 7)#

Use the six-row rubric in Module 5, Part 7: Skill focus; Structure and timing; Active practice; Tone and language; Questioning and facilitation; Safety and closure. Score each 1 to 4. A total of 18 or more indicates Hackles-Ready practice.

5.2 First 10 Minutes role-play checklist (Module 6, Activity 6.3)#

The Teacher should do all of these (the Observer ticks them off):

  • Checkbox: Stayed calm; soft voice; neutral face
  • Checkbox: Said "Thank you for telling me"
  • Checkbox: Said "This is not your fault" and "You are not in trouble"
  • Checkbox: Used only open, non-leading prompts
  • Checkbox: Did not promise secrecy; was honest about telling people who help
  • Checkbox: Did not ask to see messages or images; did not view, copy or forward
  • Checkbox: Kept the setting private but visible
  • Checkbox: Named next steps honestly
  • Checkbox: Described how and to whom they would report, and how fast [VERIFY local rules]
  • Checkbox: Said how they would document (exact words, date, time, who was told)
  • Checkbox: Mentioned follow-up and self-care

A complete role-play has at least 9 of 11 ticked. Playing the Student is always optional; the Teacher part is what counts.

6. Capstone rubric#

The capstone has four pieces (see Module 8, Part 7): 1. Lesson Plan and Micro-Teach Evidence; 2. Response Readiness Pack; 3. Inclusion and Family Pack; 4. Action Plan and Reflection. The rubric has six rows, one per competency area. Score yourself first, then ask a reviewer to score you independently, and compare notes.

Levels: 1 Beginning, 2 Developing, 3 Hackles-Ready, 4 Mentor.

6. Capstone rubric
Competency 1: Beginning 2: Developing 3: Hackles-Ready 4: Mentor
A. Know: the landscape Describes risks vaguely or with fear language; repeats statistics without checking. Names several risks accurately; sometimes cites sources. Describes the main risk families calmly and accurately; distinguishes well-supported claims from those marked [VERIFY]; can state the evidence on what works. Explains the evidence base clearly to colleagues or families; models primary-source habits; corrects common myths kindly.
B. Protect: basic security Few or no habits in place; cannot explain the basics. Some habits in place (for example, 2-step on email); explains some basics. Has set up unique passphrases or a manager, 2-step verification, updates and a backup; explains each in kid-friendly words; spots common phishing signs. Coaches others through setup; adapts explanations by age; designs safe practice activities with fake data.
C. Connect: kids' online lives Judgmental or dismissive language; assumes knowledge of apps. Shows curiosity but slips into lecture or fear. Uses the curiosity script; names the four patterns; responds well to "my friend"; does not claim to know every app. Builds a regular practice of listening to students; helps colleagues shift from judgment to curiosity.
D. Teach: methods that work Lecture or information-only; fear or shame language; no practice. Some practice; structure uneven; tone mostly calm. One clear skill; four-part 15-minute structure; every student practices at least twice; shame-free language; the open door; micro-teach rubric total of 18 or more. Adapts lessons by age and need; coaches colleagues; designs new lessons that pass the "good lesson" checklist.
E. Respond: disclosures and incidents Cannot describe the First 10 Minutes; may promise secrecy; unsure of reporting steps. Knows most of the steps; unsure of local rules or timelines; contact card incomplete. Completed Contact Card with local reporting steps verified; says the three sentences; does not view images; documents correctly; has a self-care plan; role-play checklist at least 9 of 11. Leads others in practicing the First 10 Minutes; helps align school policy with the guide; supports colleagues after hard days.
F. Include and grow Plans assume a typical student and home; no plan for measurement. Some adaptations; basic family communication; some measures. Inclusion audit with at least three real adaptations; device-free take-homes; a warm family message; L.E.A.R.N. for pushback; a 30-60-90 plan; honest use of simple measures. Shares adaptations with others; leads family engagement; runs an honest pre/post analysis for a class or grade; mentors teachers in measurement.

How the capstone is judged#

How the capstone is judged
Result Requirement
Hackles-Ready At least five of six rows at 3 or above, and no row at 1. Row E must be at 3 or above.
Developing Anything less. This is not failure: revisit the relevant module and resubmit just that piece.
Mentor-level evidence At least four rows at 4, and the Mentor requirements in Academy_Certificate_and_Badges.

Note: Row E (Respond) must be at least Hackles-Ready for the certificate, because response readiness is the most important competency for child safety. Everything else can be strengthened over time.

Reviewer's guide#

If you have been asked to review a colleague's capstone:

  1. Read first, score second. Skim the four pieces (about 20 minutes).
  2. Look for evidence, not perfection. A one-page lesson plan with a clear skill and practice is better than a ten-page plan with none.
  3. Check privacy: no real student names, no real passwords, no real disclosure details.
  4. Check Row E carefully. Ask them to say the three sentences aloud. Ask, "Who do you report to, and how fast?" and confirm the answer matches policy. [VERIFY with your administrator]
  5. Give two glows and one grow in writing. Kind and specific.
  6. Sign the certificate if the criteria are met, or agree on one small next step if not.
  7. You do not need to be a technology expert. You do need to be kind, honest and careful.

7. A note on fairness and privacy#

  • Assessments are for growth, not judgment. They must not be used in teacher evaluation, ranking or discipline unless your administrator and district policy approve it. [VERIFY]
  • Keep records minimal. If your school keeps a list of who completed the Academy, keep it to name, date and level only.
  • Do not store sensitive notes or disclosures in capstone materials.
  • Accessibility: any assessment may be done orally, by recording, or with assistive technology, and may be completed in a language other than English with the reviewer's agreement. Extra time is always fine.
