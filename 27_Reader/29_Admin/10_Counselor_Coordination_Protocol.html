How an administrator, a teacher and a school counselor (or librarian, youth worker or college advisor in other settings) coordinate around Hackles. It builds on the kit's Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit and the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Fit it to your policy, your state's rules and the roles in your building. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]

1. Purpose and principles#

  • A counselor knows which lessons are coming and is ready for students and teachers afterward.
  • Families hear about sensitive lessons first from the school, in plain words, with a real choice.
  • A disclosure leads to help for the child and a report as the law requires. It never leads to blame.
  • Nobody is penalized for opting out.
  • Everyone records counts only. Case notes live in the school's confidential files, not in the tracker.

2. Roles#

2. Roles
Role Responsibility
Administrator Approves schedule and letters. Confirms reporting routes. Designates who secures devices. Gives the counselor time. Reports counts to the board
Hackles lead (teacher, librarian or counselor) Keeps the calendar. Sends reminders. Collects counts. Fixes errors
Counselor Reviews sensitive lessons. Is on call on lesson days. Meets students who ask or need it. Leads the alternate activity. Debriefs teachers
Teacher Teaches. Gives the heads-up. Sends family notices. Follows the disclosure steps. Records counts
Library or youth leader (non-school settings) Same as teacher. Names the person who plays the counselor role

5. Heads-up message (teacher to counselor)#

Subject: Heads-up: sensitive lesson on [date, period]

Hello [name], I will teach "[lesson title]" to [class/group] on [date]. It covers [one plain sentence]. It is not graphic. Could you: be available (or arrange backup) from [time] to [time]; tell me the best way to reach you during the lesson; tell me, privately and without sharing details with me, whether any students should have a check-in before or after; and tell me if you would like to co-teach the last five minutes? Family notice goes out on [date]. Opt-out slips so far: [count]. If a student discloses, I will follow the First 10 Minutes steps and bring you in the same day. Thank you. [name]

6. Opt-outs and alternates#

  1. Receive the family's choice in writing. Acknowledge it warmly the same day.
  2. Offer a preview first. Many families choose to take part after seeing the materials.
  3. Offer a respectful alternate that does not label the child: a quiet library or reading task, a general kindness or screen-balance activity from the same strand, a counselor-led small group on trusted adults, or the family-led version with the take-home card.
  4. For Plus P1 and P2, use the listed alternates (6-8 "Job Scams" and "My Limits"; 9-12 "Spot the Scam" and the Two-Week Experiment) and make sure each alternate still hands over the reporting or help card.
  5. Make sure the child knows the core help message in the family's own words: "If anything ever worries you, tell a grown-up you trust. You are never in trouble."
  6. Record the opt-out in the confidential file. The tracker takes the count only.
  7. Review each year. Families may change their minds at any time.

Never say: "Your child will be left out," "You are keeping them unsafe," or "Everyone else is doing it."

7. When a student discloses#

  1. The teacher or any adult who hears it follows the First 10 Minutes steps (see the Crisis and Disclosure Flowchart) and the Disclosure and Incident Guide.
  2. The adult reports personally under law and policy, then tells the administrator and counselor. [Local fill]
  3. The counselor supports the student. The counselor does not investigate and does not promise secrecy.
  4. The administrator decides, with counsel and the reporting authority, how and when families are told, keeping the child's safety first.
  5. If a family member may be involved, or the student fears for their safety, do not contact the family first. Follow the advice of the child-protection or law-enforcement contact.
  6. If images are involved, nobody views, copies, forwards or screenshots them. The device is secured as policy directs.
  7. If a student is at risk of self-harm, stay with them, follow the crisis protocol, and use 988 as appropriate. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in the kit's guides: 988lifeline.org]
  8. The counselor checks on the teacher the same day.

8. Counselor checklist for a sensitive-lesson day#

  • Checkbox: I know which classes have the lesson and when.
  • Checkbox: I am free, or have coverage, during and after each lesson.
  • Checkbox: A quiet private space is ready.
  • Checkbox: I have the list of opt-outs and each alternate arrangement.
  • Checkbox: I have a confidential list of students who may need a check-in before the lesson.
  • Checkbox: I have blank Disclosure and Incident Records and know our reporting steps. [local fill]
  • Checkbox: I know who the administrator on call is.
  • Checkbox: I have resource information ready: 988, Crisis Text Line, the NCMEC CyberTipline, Take It Down, our state child-abuse line [local fill] and, where relevant, human trafficking hotlines. [VERIFY all numbers before printing]
  • Checkbox: For Plus P1 and P2 I have confirmed that the review is done.

9. Counts the counselor reports (no names)#

Number of sensitive lessons delivered, number of students who asked to talk, number of referrals, number of reports made as required (only if policy permits sharing), themes and needs. These go to the administrator and, as tallies, into the tracker's "Support and safety" columns. A rising number of disclosures after a lesson is expected and is not a sign of failure.

10. Limits#

This protocol is guidance, not legal advice. Reporting duties differ by state, district and school type. The kit's guides have not had a human counselor or child-safety review as of 2026-10-05. Ask your own counselor and counsel to review before use. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
