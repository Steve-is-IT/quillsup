1. What it tracks#

1. What it tracks
Sheet Who fills it What goes in
Setup Hackles lead School name and year (optional), suppression threshold (default 10), opt-out review level (default 10%), minimum family notice (default 14 days)
Classes Hackles lead One row per class or group: a room or period label such as Rm 12 or Per 4, grade band, students enrolled
Lessons Teacher Yes, Partial or Not yet for lessons L1 to L10 and, in grades 6 to 12, Plus lessons P1 to P4
HelpLadder Teacher Number of Help Ladder practice sessions, students who practiced the first sentence aloud, optional rubric counts
Surveys Teacher (optional) Class totals from the Assessment Pack's Class Tally Sheet: pre and post, three core items, confidence to tell, matched pairs
Sensitive Teacher and counselor One row per sensitive lesson taught: class, lesson, lesson date, notice date, opt-out count, alternate offered, counselor availability, counts of students who asked to talk, concerns raised, referrals, reports
Family Teacher Families notified, reply slips returned, family-event attendees, previews held
Summary Reads itself School summary by band, laid out like the Assessment Pack's administrator impact summary
Dashboard Reads itself Seven key numbers and five charts
Checks Reads itself Flags data problems, class by class
ChartData, Lists Reads itself Values behind the charts; drop-down lists and lesson titles

2. The rules that protect children#

  1. Counts only. There is no free-text box for children. The only free text is a class label (limited to 24 characters) and the school name.
  2. Never use a person's name as a class label. Use a room or period.
  3. Small numbers are hidden. If fewer students than the threshold answered a survey, the Summary shows "n<10" instead of a percentage, and the charts skip it. Check your district's rule on small groups. [VERIFY]
  4. Disclosure, referral and report counts are optional. Include them only if your policy permits. In a small class a count of 1 can point to a child. Real case notes belong in your confidential files, not here.
  5. Do not enter surveys unless counsel has cleared them. Surveys that touch certain protected areas can need advance written parental consent under federal law, and the kit's own checklist flags a 2026-08-26 Department of Education letter on this. The Assessment Pack surveys are limited to knowledge, skills and confidence, but only counsel can say how your district applies the rule. [VERIFY; COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
  6. Store the file where your policy says. If you save it to a cloud drive, that drive's terms apply, not Hackles'.

3. Using it each month#

3. Using it each month
Step Who Time
Set up classes at the start Hackles lead 15 minutes
After each lesson, tick the Lessons cell Teacher 10 seconds
After a Help Ladder practice, add one to the session count Teacher 10 seconds
After pre and post surveys, type the Class Tally Sheet totals Teacher 5 minutes per class
After a sensitive lesson, add a Sensitive row Teacher and counselor 3 minutes
After family letters or events, update Family Teacher 2 minutes
End of month, open Checks, fix flags, glance at Dashboard Hackles lead 10 minutes

4. What the main numbers mean#

4. What the main numbers mean
Number How it is calculated What it can and cannot tell you
Core lesson completion Lessons marked Yes divided by 10 per class, summed over classes. Partial does not count Reach and pacing. It does not measure quality
Tell-Readiness before and after For each band, the total of best answers on its three core items divided by three times the students who answered. Core items: K-2 items 1, 4, 5; 3-5 items 1, 4, 7; 6-8 items 2, 5, 7; 9-12 items 1, 4, 10 How students' answers moved. Not cause, not behavior, not real-world safety
Change (percentage points) After minus before, times 100 The size of the shift in self-report
Confident to tell Students rating 3 or higher (4 or higher on a 5-point scale) divided by students who answered Self-reported confidence
Opt-out rate Opt-out requests divided by students enrolled in the classes that had the lesson A conversation starter. The workbook flags a rate at or above your review level so a person looks, not as a target
Notice check Days between family notice and lesson, compared with your minimum Whether your own notice rule was kept
Reply rate Reply slips returned divided by families notified Engagement, not agreement

Read the limits paragraph on the Summary sheet whenever you share results. These data come from short self-report surveys and teacher counts, with no comparison group. They show how answers and confidence changed. They do not show that Hackles caused the change, and they do not show that children are safer. After lessons, disclosures often go up because students feel safer to tell; do not treat an increase as a failure. Hackles has not been evaluated in a controlled study. [VERIFY]

Good report wording: "Between September and December, the share of best answers on the three core items for our 6th-grade classes rose from 46 percent to 83 percent (50 students answered before and 48 after; no comparison group)." Poor wording: "Hackles reduced sextortion at our school."

5. Example, with made-up numbers#

The EXAMPLE workbook has seven invented classes and eight sensitive-lesson sessions. Here is what its Summary shows.

5. Example, with made-up numbers
Measure Gr K-2 Gr 3-5 Gr 6-8 Gr 9-12 All bands
Classes 2 2 2 1 7
Students enrolled 42 47 52 8 149
Core lesson completion 65% 65% 85% 60% 70%
Help Ladder sessions 7 8 11 3 29
Tell-Readiness before 43% 47% 46% n<10 45%
Tell-Readiness after 78% 77% 83% n<10 79%
Opt-out requests 1 1 6 1 9
Reply rate 38% 40% 21% 25% 32%

The 9-12 column shows "n<10" because only 8 students answered. The Checks sheet flags two Sensitive rows: one family notice sent only 8 days ahead, and one lesson with no counselor available. These are the kinds of things the checks exist to catch. The example numbers are invented and prove nothing about the kit.

6. How the workbook was tested, and what that does not cover#

  • Loaded with Python's openpyxl. The test script tools/test_tracker.py checks the sheet list, sheet protection, that only entry cells are unlocked, drop-down and number limits, conditional formats, five charts, that every formula points to a sheet that exists, and that no column header asks for a name or ID.
  • Formulas evaluated without Excel. The same script includes a small formula interpreter written for this test. It computed all 2,330 formulas in both workbooks with no error values, and 181 results in the example workbook matched an independent Python computation from the raw example data. It also checked that deliberately broken entries are caught by the Checks sheet.
  • Budget workbook (Hackles Day) was tested the same way.
  • Not tested: Excel itself. Excel (and LibreOffice and Google Sheets) was not available. Nobody has seen how the sheets, drop-downs, shading, protection and charts look in a real spreadsheet program. Chart appearance, data bars and color scales are the most likely things to need adjusting. The interpreter is a test aid, not a copy of Excel's calculation engine, so a rare difference in how Excel treats blanks or text is possible. If you find a difference, tell the maintainers. [CONTACT: to be added]
  • Browser-based spreadsheet apps may not honor every protection or validation setting.
  • Protection has no password. That is deliberate so you can adapt the workbook. Unprotect with Review, Unprotect Sheet.

7. Adapting the tracker#

  • Add classes: the workbook has room for 40 classes and 120 sensitive-lesson sessions. For more, copy the file for another school or year.
  • Change lesson titles: edit the Lists sheet after unprotecting it.
  • Different notice period: change it on Setup.
  • Different small-number rule: change the threshold on Setup.
  • Add the optional delayed post survey (4 to 8 weeks later): copy the Surveys sheet and rename its columns, or record it in a second copy of the workbook.
  • Do not add any column that asks for a name, ID, birth date or a note about a child.

8. What this tool cannot do#

It does not evaluate whether Hackles works. A real evaluation would use a comparison group, pre-registered measures, ethics review and independent analysis, and would be run by a university or district research office. The kit's Risks document names this as a possible year-2 option. [VERIFY]
