What this document is#

This is a self-assessment written in the shape of a Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT) and Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR), the documents many districts and colleges ask vendors for. [VERIFY current VPAT edition names; the ITI VPAT 2.x editions map to WCAG, Section 508 and EN 301 549] It is not a VPAT, it was not produced by an accessibility auditor, and it makes no claim of conformance. Its job is to tell your accessibility coordinator exactly what was looked at, how, and what nobody has looked at yet.

Why it matters to you. If your public entity posts Hackles files on its own website or LMS, you own the accessibility of what you post. In the United States, the Department of Justice's Title II web rule adopted WCAG 2.1 AA for state and local government web content, with compliance dates of April 26, 2027 and April 26, 2028 depending on population, and the dates were affected by an interim final rule in April 2026. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in the kit's compliance checklist: ada.gov/resources/web-rule-first-steps; COUNSEL TO CONFIRM how it applies to you] Free third-party material is not exempt just because it was free. Plan to remediate or offer an accessible alternative on request.

Design choices recorded in the stylesheets Visible focus outline (3 px), reduced-motion media query, skip links on the Reader, color scheme support Read in the source on 2026-10-05; not tested with real users

These are heuristic and automated checks. Automated tools typically find a minority of real accessibility problems. A pass here means "the things we tested did not fail."

What is NOT claimed#

  • No claim of WCAG 2.0, 2.1 or 2.2 conformance at any level for any product.
  • No claim of Section 508 or EN 301 549 conformance.
  • No claim that any PDF is accessible. They are untagged.
  • No claim that Word files, slides or the Excel tracker have been visually or assistively verified.
  • No claim that screen readers (JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack, Narrator) or switch, voice-control or magnification software have been used on any page. If a district test finds a problem, report it so the kit can be fixed.
  • No claim about the accessibility of linked third-party sites.
  • No claim that the lessons themselves are suitable for every disability. A disability and inclusion review with a special educator is a pending gate, and the Inclusion and Equity Guide in the kit is guidance, not a review.

What a district should do next#

  1. Run the Reader and Lab on your own copy with a screen reader and keyboard only: tab through, activate every control, check focus is visible, check at 200% zoom and 320 px width.
  2. For PDFs you post, use the Word versions, tag them in your PDF tool, or link to the Reader pages. Offer an accessible format on request and publish your contact route. [local fill]
  3. Run Word's Accessibility Checker on any Word file you post.
  4. Re-open the PPTX decks in PowerPoint, check slide titles, reading order and alt text, and play the HTML decks with the keyboard.
  5. Record what you find. If you send findings back to the publisher, include no student information. [CONTACT: to be added]

Reporting a barrier#

[CONTACT: to be added]. Until an address is published, report barriers to your own technology or accessibility office. Say which file or page, what device and assistive technology you used, and what went wrong.
