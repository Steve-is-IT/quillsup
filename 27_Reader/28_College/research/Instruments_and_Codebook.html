© 2026 Competence Collective. Lesson text and teaching materials CC BY-NC 4.0. The Hackles name, logos, characters and artwork are not covered by that license: print and share them unaltered for classroom and family use; any other use needs written permission. [VERIFY with counsel]

The survey instruments from the Hackles Assessment Pack and the Teacher Academy, formatted for research use with scoring rules and a codebook. Item wording is copied from the kit so that studies stay comparable. Scoring rules follow Assessment Pack Section 7. Nothing here claims that the instruments have been validated: the kit reports no reliability or validity evidence, so treat them as unvalidated classroom check-ins. Hackles has not been evaluated in a controlled trial.

No student accounts, no student data collected, ever. The default research design uses aggregate class tallies, so no student identifier ever reaches the researcher.

1. Rules for using these instruments in research#

  1. Keep the wording. Change nothing in an item without recording it as an adaptation. Adapted items are labeled "adapted from the Hackles Assessment Pack" and are not pooled with originals without a sensitivity check.
  2. Knowledge, skills and confidence only. Do not add items about a student's own experiences, sex behavior or attitudes, illegal or demeaning behavior, mental or psychological problems, family relationships, religion, politics or income. The Assessment Pack explains why: such surveys can fall under the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment (PPRA) and need parental review and written consent [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM; see the privacy checklist].
  3. Identical pre and post forms. The pre and post surveys are identical so results can be compared. The post survey adds two feedback items that are not compared.
  4. Student surveys are administered by the teacher as ordinary classroom practice, on paper, with a pseudonymous matching code held by the teacher (Assessment Pack, Section 2). Researchers receive class-level tallies (Section 4 of this file), never individual surveys.
  5. Read open answers the same day. An open answer can contain a disclosure. Follow the Disclosure and Incident Guide and school policy. The research protocol must say so (see the IRB template).
  6. Small groups. Do not report any subgroup under 10 students [VERIFY your district's suppression rule].
  7. K-2 picture surveys. The kit allows them as classroom check-ins. This toolkit does not recommend collecting K-2 data for research without a separate ethics review, because assent and understanding at ages 5 to 7 are hard to establish [IRB TO DETERMINE].
  8. No validation claim. There is no reliability or validity evidence in the kit. Compute Cronbach's alpha or an item analysis in your own data only as a description, not as evidence of validity.

3. Student instruments (items verbatim from the Assessment Pack)#

Variable names: <band>_q<item>_raw holds the raw response and <band>_q<item>_score the scored value (see Scoring). Missing codes: -9 skipped, -8 not shown or form damaged, -7 unusable. Band prefixes: k2, g35, g68, g912. Wave: pre or post. Matching code: teacher-held student_code (never a name).

4. Scoring examples (plain language, Python and R)#

Grade 6-8 core indicator for one student. Item 2 is best if A, D and at least one of B or F are circled and neither C nor E is. Item 5 is best if the student rated 3 or 4. Item 7 is best if the open response scored 2. Core score = number of those three that are best (0 to 3). Class Tell-Readiness = total best answers across all students divided by total core responses, times 100.

# Python
q2 = int({'A','D'} <= s and ({'B'} & s or {'F'} & s) and not ({'C','E'} & s))   # s = set of circled letters
q5 = int(rating >= 3)
q7 = int(open_score == 2)
core = q2 + q5 + q7

# R
q2 <- as.integer(all(c('A','D') %in% s) && any(c('B','F') %in% s) && !any(c('C','E') %in% s))
q5 <- as.integer(rating >= 3); q7 <- as.integer(open_score == 2); core <- q2 + q5 + q7

Worked check from the Assessment Pack. If pre is 26 of 72 (36 percent) and post is 51 of 72 (71 percent), the change is +35 percentage points. The test suite in code/test_all.py checks this example.

Open-response scoring. Two trained raters should score open items (6-8 item 7; 9-12 item 10) using the kit's rubric, blind to wave and condition when possible. Report agreement (percent agreement and Cohen's kappa) and resolve disagreements by discussion. Raters see only the response text, never a name. Teachers who score their own students' open answers may bias results; in a research design use a second rater who does not know the condition [IRB TO CONFIRM how this is possible without sharing student text; the privacy-by-design option is for the teacher to score and report counts, and to say so as a limit].

5. What the codebook file contains#

Hackles_Instruments_Codebook.csv lists every variable with its instrument, label, type, allowed values, scoring rule, best answer, core flag, direction, missing codes and notes. Load it into your statistics package as a data dictionary.

These instruments are educational tools, not diagnostic or clinical measures. Do not use them to label a child. Hackles has not been evaluated in a controlled trial.
