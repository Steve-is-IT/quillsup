How to use this bank#

  • 150 items: multiple choice (MC) and scenario-based multiple choice (SCN). One correct answer each; options are shuffled with a fixed seed so the letters are stable.
  • Tagged by Academy module (M1 to M8) and by cognitive level (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate).
  • The 8 module quizzes in the LMS shell draw 10 items each from this bank. Use the rest as retakes, practice sets or exam questions.
  • Known limit: the correct option tends to be the longest in many items. Before high-stakes use, have a colleague review and balance option lengths. No item has been field-tested or psychometrically analyzed.

M1: Why This Matters#

HCQ-M1-01 (Remember, MC)

Which set of four features does Module 1 say shows up across the seven risk families?

  • A. Passwords, updates, backups and filters
  • B. Speed, volume, cost and location
  • C. Strangers, screens, games and money
  • D. Pressure, secrecy, a request, and a move somewhere more private

Answer: D. The kit teaches the pattern, not the app. Pressure, secrecy, a request and a move somewhere more private recur whether the setting is a game, a chat or a feed. Source: Academy Module 1, Part 1 (The thread that connects them).

HCQ-M1-02 (Remember, MC)

What is the Hackles routine?

  • A. Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.
  • B. Look. Listen. Learn.
  • C. Block. Delete. Forget.
  • D. Stop. Drop. Roll.

Answer: A. Notice the feeling, do nothing yet, check whether it is real and who is asking, then tell a trusted adult. Source: Academy Overview; Module 1, Part 4.

HCQ-M1-03 (Understand, MC)

Why does Module 1 say the old 'stranger danger' slogan is not enough online?

  • A. Strangers are never a danger to children online
  • B. Only adults who are strangers can cause online harm
  • C. Much online harm starts with accounts that look like peers or people a child half-knows, so children need to notice pressure, secrecy and requests whoever is asking
  • D. Children cannot tell who is a stranger, so they should avoid all online contact

Answer: C. The skill taught is noticing the pattern and telling. The slogan misses accounts that seem friendly and familiar. Source: Academy Module 1, 'A word about stranger danger'.

HCQ-M1-04 (Remember, MC)

Which figure did Module 1 report for NCMEC's 2025 count of financially motivated sextortion reports?

  • A. More than 500,000, up from more than 360,000
  • B. More than 5,000, up from more than 3,600
  • C. About 80,000, a rise of 37 percent
  • D. More than 50,000, up from more than 36,000 the year before

Answer: D. These are the counts verified on NCMEC's own page. The 80,000 and 37 percent figures could not be found on the primary pages and were removed from the kit. Source: Academy Module 1, Calm Fact 2; Fact-Check Report Round 1, items 4.1 to 4.4.

HCQ-M1-05 (Apply, SCN)

A colleague drafting a newsletter wants to write 'sextortion reports rose 37 percent.' What is the best advice?

  • A. Give the counts from NCMEC's page (more than 50,000 in 2025, more than 36,000 in 2024) and avoid a percentage the primary pages do not state
  • B. Keep 37 percent because several news sites use it
  • C. Round the rise up to 40 percent so it has impact
  • D. Drop all numbers and say reports are 'exploding'

Answer: A. The kit's check found the 37 percent figure only in snippets, and the counts imply about 39 percent. Quote the counts from the primary source. Source: Evidence file, Section 2.1; Fact-Check Report Round 1, item 4.2.

HCQ-M1-06 (Understand, MC)

Why does Module 1 call children not telling 'the most important number for teachers'?

  • A. It shows that lessons are unnecessary
  • B. It proves that children cannot be trusted to report
  • C. It shows that most children are being harmed
  • D. It shows the most powerful thing to teach is that telling is safe, never gets you in trouble, and brings help

Answer: D. The point is help-seeking. Shame and fear of blame keep children silent, so the lesson design aims at telling. Source: Academy Module 1, Calm Fact 4.

HCQ-M1-07 (Analyze, SCN)

A candidate wants to quote 'one-third of children told no one and only 3 percent went to police or a helpline.' Which handling is best?

  • A. Say the figures come from UNICEF's first 13 countries (Africa and Southeast Asia), not the US, and confirm them in the primary reports before quoting [VERIFY]
  • B. Present them as US figures because the topic is the same
  • C. Quote them as exact and universal
  • D. Add that 97 percent of US children tell a parent

Answer: A. Module 1 states the geographic limit. The Round 1 report could not confirm the figures on the project page, so they stay marked for checking. Source: Academy Module 1, Calm Fact 4; Fact-Check Report Round 1, item 4.11.

HCQ-M1-08 (Understand, MC)

What does the Finkelhor and colleagues (2021) review, as summarized in Module 1, suggest about lesson types?

  • A. One large assembly is the best-supported approach
  • B. Lectures on rules outperform role-play
  • C. Active, skill-based, repeated programs are better supported than one-off assemblies or information-only lessons
  • D. Fear-based warnings are proven to work best

Answer: C. The summary table in Module 1 favors active, structured, repeated programs. The kit marks the citation [VERIFY] until read in full. Source: Academy Module 1, 'What the research says works'.

HCQ-M1-09 (Understand, MC)

Which statement is an honest caveat about the evidence for Hackles-style teaching?

  • A. A large randomized trial proves the program prevents sextortion
  • B. Every claim in the kit has been independently verified
  • C. Much of the best evidence comes from an adjacent field, and no rigorous trial of a modern K-12 sextortion or deepfake curriculum is known
  • D. Evidence for fear appeals in online safety is conclusive

Answer: C. Module 1 and the Assessment Pack both say plainly that no rigorous trial is known and that adjacent-field evidence is used. Source: Academy Module 1; Assessment Pack Section 13.

HCQ-M1-10 (Apply, SCN)

Your principal says, 'One big assembly and we are done for the year.' Which reply fits Module 1?

  • A. Refuse and explain that assemblies are harmful
  • B. Affirm the goal, then offer both/and: a short kickoff plus ten short classroom lessons, and offer to pilot with one grade
  • C. Tell the principal the evidence proves assemblies never work
  • D. Agree, since any program is better than none

Answer: B. Both/and keeps the visible response the principal wants and adds repeated skill practice. Overstating the evidence would not be honest. Source: Academy Module 1, Scenario 1.A.

HCQ-M1-11 (Apply, SCN)

A fourth grader says, 'I play every night with a guy named Max. He's 14. He gave me free coins.' What is the best first response?

  • A. Stay calm and curious ('Tell me more about the game'), do not interrogate, make a quiet note and let the counselor know per school process
  • B. Ask the child to show you the whole chat on their device
  • C. Ignore it, because it is just a game
  • D. 'That is dangerous! Never talk to him again!'

Answer: A. One comment alone is not a disclosure of harm. Gifts, an older 'friend' and nightly contact are a pattern worth noting. Alarm or interrogation ends the conversation. Source: Academy Module 1, Scenario 1.B.

HCQ-M1-12 (Analyze, SCN)

In the Max scenario, which added detail would change your response to treating it as a possible disclosure?

  • A. The child says other students also have game friends
  • B. The child says Max asked for secrecy, pictures, or a move to a private app, or made threats
  • C. The child says Max is good at the game
  • D. The child says the coins were colorful

Answer: B. Secrecy, pictures, threats or a move to a private app are the signals the kit says to treat as a potential disclosure and handle with the First 10 Minutes. Source: Academy Module 1, Scenario 1.B guidance.

HCQ-M1-13 (Remember, MC)

Which of these is NOT part of a teacher's role in Hackles?

  • A. Reporting as required
  • B. Noticing and listening
  • C. Handing off to trained people
  • D. Investigating who did it and confronting a suspect

Answer: D. The role is recognition and response. Investigation, confrontation, viewing images, searching phones and counseling are not the teacher's job. Source: Academy Module 1, Part 5.

HCQ-M1-14 (Understand, MC)

Why does Module 1 say 'fear without a skill is just fear'?

  • A. A child told only that dangers exist has nothing to do with that information, while a rehearsed skill gives them an action
  • B. Skills make fear unnecessary for adults
  • C. Children cannot feel fear about technology
  • D. Fear is never present in children

Answer: A. The first reason in 'Why not just scare them' is that fear needs a doable action attached to it. Source: Academy Module 1, Part 3.

HCQ-M1-15 (Understand, MC)

Module 1 Calm Fact 1 says most children use technology safely most of the time. What follows for teaching?

  • A. Build skills calmly for the minority of moments that go wrong, rather than skipping lessons or using scare tactics
  • B. Assume every child is already being harmed
  • C. Skip online safety lessons
  • D. Teach with graphic stories so the minority takes it seriously

Answer: A. The fact supports calm skill-building. The existence of serious cases is the reason to teach at all. Source: Academy Module 1, Calm Fact 1; self-check Q2.

HCQ-M1-16 (Apply, MC)

Which message is a well-formed skill message?

  • A. 'Hackers are everywhere.'
  • B. 'Everyone online is lying.'
  • C. 'Never share anything online.'
  • D. 'If an account asks for your password or code, hackles up: pause and check with a trusted adult.'

Answer: D. A good skill message names a signal, one concrete action and a person to tell, in a hopeful, shame-free tone. Source: Academy Module 1, Activity 1.3; self-check Q10.

HCQ-M1-17 (Evaluate, SCN)

An article says Thorn found that about 1 in 25 teens have had deepfake nudes created of them. What does the kit's check say?

  • A. The article is right; Thorn's figure is 1 in 25
  • B. Thorn reported 1 in 17 teens; '1 in 25' is the UNICEF figure for some countries, so the article's attribution is wrong
  • C. Thorn's figure is 1 in 8, which is the same thing
  • D. Neither organization has reported such a figure

Answer: B. Round 1 corrected this exact error: 1 in 17 (Thorn, 1,200 US young people) and 1 in 8 who know someone targeted; 1 in 25 is UNICEF in some countries. Source: Fact-Check Report Round 1, items 4.8 to 4.10.

HCQ-M1-18 (Evaluate, SCN)

Which sentence for a parent handout is the most defensible?

  • A. 'Every statistic in this handout is guaranteed accurate.'
  • B. 'Studies show Hackles prevents sextortion.'
  • C. 'Research proves fear-based warnings backfire in online safety.'
  • D. 'The evidence on fear appeals is mixed, and direct evidence for online safety is thin, so we teach calm skills with practice.'

Answer: D. The kit says not to claim fear appeals are proven to backfire in this field and that no trial shows the program prevents harm. Source: Academy Module 1; Assessment Pack Section 13.

M2: The Cybersecurity You Need#

HCQ-M2-01 (Remember, MC)

In the kit's rule of thumb for a passphrase, what is the minimum?

  • A. One clever word with symbols
