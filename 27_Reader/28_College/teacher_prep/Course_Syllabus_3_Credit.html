2. Catalog description#

Prepares future and current K-12 teachers to teach digital safety and cybersecurity with skills-first methods. Candidates learn the real risks and the evidence behind them, practice basic security habits with fake data, plan and teach 15-minute lessons, rehearse the first ten minutes after a student's disclosure, include every learner and family, and measure their teaching honestly. Includes a supervised field experience, background-check and mandated-reporter orientation. No technical background required.

3. Why this course exists#

Most teachers were never taught how to talk with a ten-year-old about a stranger in a game chat, or how to answer a student who says, "My friend got a weird message." Many have never been told what to do in the first ten minutes after a child tells them something hard. This course closes those gaps.

The routine is simple and the same one students learn: Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell. If you can listen, stay calm and run a good 15-minute activity, you can teach this. You do not need to be a cybersecurity expert. You do need to be honest about what the evidence shows, careful with sensitive moments, and kind.

4. Course learning outcomes#

By the end of the course, candidates will be able to do the following. Each outcome maps to one of the six Hackles Teacher Academy competencies (A Know, B Protect, C Connect, D Teach, E Respond, F Include and grow), to the InTASC Model Core Teaching Standards, and to the ISTE Standards for Educators. 5. How the course teaches#

Every week follows the same shape, so nobody has to wonder what is next: an opening ritual, short input, practice (the Teacher Academy "Do it yourself" activities, Concepts Lab demos you do first and then teach, and Facilitator Kit methods such as the Fake Phish Derby, Say-It-Right Relay and Disclosure Role-Play with Safety Rails), discussion, and a calm close.

  • Concepts Lab. Fourteen mostly unplugged demos. You do each one first (Do, Debrief, Teach), then learn to teach it in 15 or 45 minutes. Defense only; no real systems, accounts, malware or people.
  • Facilitator Kit methods. The same activities used to train staff, so you experience what you will later lead.
  • Fake data only. Every hands-on activity uses invented people and invented accounts. Nobody types, shares or screenshots a real password, a real student or a real private image.
  • Field experience. Observation, then two supervised lessons, then a family or community workshop (see the Field Placement Guide).

6. Weekly schedule#

Each week is 180 minutes. Readings are due before class. Full week-by-week plans, with minute-by-minute agendas, are in the Weekly Course Plans document and in each LMS module.

9. Accessibility statement#

This course is built so that every candidate can take part.

  • Many ways in. Most activities can be done by speaking, writing, drawing, pointing or acting. In role-plays you may choose Teacher, Observer, or the written alternative; reading the Student lines aloud is also fine. Nobody has to explain why.
  • Materials. Slides use large type, high contrast, real titles and alt text. Documents use plain language, headings and tables with header rows. Videos, if you add any, need captions [INSTITUTION: confirm your standard, such as WCAG 2.1 AA, with your accessibility office; VERIFY]. Request alternate formats at any time.
  • Time and setting. Extra time, breaks, a quiet space, a seated option and assistive technology are always fine for any activity or assessment.
  • Accommodations. Contact [DISABILITY SERVICES OFFICE, EMAIL, PHONE] early; your instructor will implement approved accommodations and will not ask you to disclose a diagnosis [VERIFY with your office].
  • Low-device and low-bandwidth. Every demo has a paper version. No assignment requires a device you do not have.

10. Academic integrity#

You are preparing to be a trusted adult for children, so the standard here is plain honesty.

  • Your own words and your own practice are the point of the journal, practicum and reflections.
  • Cite what you use. For statistics, cite the primary source (the organization's own page or report), not a news story about it. If you cannot confirm a figure, say so and mark it [VERIFY]. Invented citations and invented numbers are the most serious integrity violation in this course.
  • The Hackles kit is CC BY-NC 4.0 for lesson text: credit it when you adapt it. The Hackles name, logos, characters and artwork are not covered by that license; use them unaltered, for classroom and family use.
  • Collaboration: you may talk through ideas and give each other feedback. Submit work that is your own. Group work is labeled as such.
  • Privacy is part of integrity: never put real student names, student work, real passwords, or any real incident detail in an assignment, a chat, or an AI tool.

Violations follow [INSTITUTION'S ACADEMIC INTEGRITY POLICY].

This policy tries to be honest about how people actually work and workable for a whole term. It uses three tiers.

Why this shape. AI tools can be useful for planning and for removing barriers. They are weak at the things this course grades: your own honest reflection, your own spoken response to a hard moment, and statistics checked at the source. They also invent citations. Verifying what a tool says is itself a course skill (see Week 1, Number Detective).

How it will be checked. The instructor will not rely on AI-detection software as the sole basis for a finding, because such tools are not reliable enough for that [VERIFY with your institution's guidance]. Instead the course uses process evidence: the AI use note, drafts kept in your document history, short in-class writing and a brief conference about your unit plan. If something looks off, the instructor will ask you to talk it through before drawing any conclusion.

Your own future classroom. The course teaches what the kit says to teachers: do not paste student names, student work with personal details or disclosure notes into a chatbot, and follow your district's AI policy [VERIFY].

12. Learning together about hard topics: care for you#

Some of this material touches real life for some of us. The course is built with trauma-informed practice for the candidates as well as for the children you will teach.

  • Content notes. Each high-care session begins with a content note. Cards are fictional and non-graphic. Nothing asks you to describe a personal experience, and you will never be asked to play a child who is harmed.
  • Choice and opt-out. You may take any role, pass, step out, or choose the written alternative without penalty and without explanation. Traffic-light cards (green, yellow, red) are used in role-plays.
  • Predictable routine. Same opening and closing every week. We take a breath, we de-role ("I'm [name], that was pretend"), and we close calmly.
  • Counselor partner. [NAME] attends Weeks 8 and 9 and is available all term. Campus counseling: [CENTER, PHONE]. If you are in distress at any time, call or text 988 [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: 988lifeline.org], or text HOME to 741741 (Crisis Text Line) [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: crisistextline.org].
  • If you have lived this. If you are a survivor, it is okay to ask a colleague or counselor to lead sensitive lessons in your future classroom. That is good judgment.
  • A note on what you tell me. Keep personal experiences out of class discussion and written work unless you choose to share them. Your instructor may have reporting duties for some disclosures. For child abuse and neglect the duty depends on state law and role, and many campuses designate instructors as responsible employees for sexual misconduct [VERIFY your campus policy and state law; COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]. If you want complete confidentiality, campus counseling is the better place. [INSTITUTION: insert your Title IX and responsible-employee language here.]

16. Honesty statement about the kit#

Hackles has not been reviewed, approved or endorsed by any state education agency or accrediting body. It has not been evaluated in a controlled trial, and this course does not claim that it works to prevent harm. What the kit offers is a set of teaching methods consistent with what prevention science suggests about active, repeated, skill-based teaching, much of which comes from an adjacent field, plus simple ways to measure and report changes in students' survey answers. Part of your job in this course is to say that plainly to colleagues, parents and administrators.
