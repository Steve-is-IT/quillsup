1. What Hackles Day is (and is not)#

Hackles Day is a reinforcement and celebration event, not the program itself. Research on youth internet-safety education suggests that one-off assemblies and single-exposure events are weak, while skills-based, repeated teaching is more promising (Finkelhor and colleagues, 2021 [VERIFY]). Hackles Day works because it comes after a semester of weekly lessons. Students practice what they already know, teach it to others, and show families what they can do.

Goals

  1. Practice "Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell." and the Help Ladder in a fun, low-pressure setting.
  2. Celebrate students' skills and kindness.
  3. Connect school and home with the Family Code Word, the Family Tech Agreement, and conversation starters.
  4. Show older students as helpers and teachers (capstone teach-back).
  5. Collect a little evidence (a short feedback form and optional pre/post "Quick Check").

Principles

  • Unplugged-first. Every station works without devices or internet.
  • No scare tactics. No graphic stories, frightening videos, or "what could happen to you" talks.
  • No demonstrations of attacks. We teach recognition and response, not how to carry out harm.
  • No shame. No student is singled out. Participation is always optional.
  • Everyone can join. Stations are designed for different abilities, languages, and levels of access to devices.
  • Safe adults on every station. Volunteers follow school policy for vetting and supervision. [VERIFY]
  • A counselor is on site. Hackles Day may prompt a student to disclose.

Who it is for. All four bands: K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12. Schools can run all bands in one building, or run a smaller version for a single band or classroom.

The Hackles Pledge (read aloud; edit as you like)#

"We are Hackles kids. We Pause. We Check. We Tell. We are kind online. We help our friends. And we know that telling a trusted adult is never getting in trouble."

"Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell." chant (with motions)#

  • Pause: hold up a hand (stop).
  • Check: make a magnifying glass with the hand.
  • Tell: cup hands around mouth and point to a trusted adult.

4. Stations by grade band#

Each station lists the time, group size, materials, steps, key message, accessibility, and the "if a student discloses" note. Stations are designed to work with 6 to 10 children at a time, and with one adult and, where possible, one helper.

For every station

  • Disclosure note: If a student says something that worries you, stay calm, thank them, and follow the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Radio or send a runner for the counselor. Do not promise secrecy.
  • No student is required to share a personal experience.
  • Everyone gets a stamp. Stamps go in the Passport (see Section 12).

All bands: Hackles Hunt station (10 to 15 minutes; adapt by age)#

  • Materials: a "Hackles Up" sign and a "Hackles Down" sign; scenario cards written for the band (K-2: large pictures such as a pop-up prize, a stranger asking for a picture, a kind message from a grandparent; 3-5: short game-chat and message cards; 6-12: realistic message, DM and request cards, no real names or real accounts); a "Hackles Up" hand-signal poster (hand raised and brushed up the back of the neck).
  • Steps: (1) Show a card. (2) Children walk to the Hackles Up or Hackles Down sign, or show the hand signal, and say what made them feel "uh-oh" (too good, pushy, secret, scary) or why it felt fine. (3) For Hackles Up cards, the group does the rest of the habit together: Pause, Check (who is it from, what do they want), Tell. (4) Include at least one safe card so children learn that a calm feeling is fine too.
  • Key message: "Trust your hackles. A false alarm is fine. If you missed it or got tricked, it is never your fault. Tell someone."
  • Accessibility: Children may point or use the signal instead of speaking. Never ask a child to share a personal experience.
  • Stamp: a spiky "uh-oh" star in the Passport.

K-2 stations (ages 5-7): "Hackles: K-2 (Ages 5-7)"#

K-2 Station 1: Tell-Someone Team (10 to 12 minutes)

  • Materials: large cutouts or pictures of trusted adults (teacher, counselor, family helper, coach); sticky dots; a poster with a big speech bubble reading "I need help."
  • Steps: (1) Children place dots on the pictures of grown-ups they could tell. (2) The helper models the line "I need help." (3) Each child says it once in a loud voice and once in a whisper. (4) Children shake hands or high-five the volunteer.
  • Key message: "Telling is never trouble."
  • Accessibility: Children may point instead of speaking. Add a picture symbol of "help."

K-2 Station 2: Secret Password Silly Words (10 minutes)

  • Materials: three bags of picture cards (animals, foods, actions); a poster with a lock.
  • Steps: (1) Each child draws one card from each bag and makes a silly three-word password, such as "dancing purple pickle." (2) Children say it aloud once, then whisper it, then place it in their "lock" (pocket of a paper lock) and keep it secret. (3) Remind: only share with a grown-up who helps you.
  • Note: Children should not use their real passwords. This is a pretend practice.
  • Key message: "Passwords are private."

K-2 Station 3: Pause and Ask Pop-Ups (10 minutes)

  • Materials: large paper "pop-ups" showing prizes ("You won!"), pretend free offers, a "Stop sign" paddle for each child.
  • Steps: (1) The helper holds up a pop-up. (2) Children hold up the Stop paddle and call "Pause and ask." (3) The helper then holds up a grown-up cutout, and children say, "I'll ask."
  • Key message: "Pause and ask a grown-up."

K-2 Station 4: The Oops Button (10 minutes)

  • Materials: a large red paper "Oops button" and a floor ladder with three steps (Stop, Tell, It's okay).
  • Steps: (1) Children are told a pretend story about clicking something yucky by accident. (2) Each child presses the Oops button, steps on the ladder, and says "I'll tell." (3) The helper says "Thank you for telling. You are not in trouble."
  • Key message: "Oops happens. Tell, no trouble."

K-2 Station 5: Real or Pretend? (10 minutes)

  • Materials: pairs of pictures: a real photograph and a drawing or a picture made to look unusual (use safe, teacher-made images, never images of real children); "Real" and "Pretend" signs.
  • Steps: (1) Children vote by walking to a sign. (2) The helper explains that some pictures and voices can be made-up by computers. (3) "If you're not sure, ask a grown-up."

K-2 Station 6: Kind Clicks Wall (10 minutes)

  • Materials: paper hearts, crayons, tape.
  • Steps: Children draw or dictate a kind message, and hang it on the wall.

Grades 3-5 stations (ages 8-10): "Hands-On Hackers (the good kind)"#

3-5 Station 1: Passphrase Crack Timer (15 minutes)

  • Materials: index cards with fake passwords in several lengths; a "crack timer" chart (pre-made from the lesson: how long guessing takes for 4 characters, 8, 12, and a passphrase); stopwatch.
  • Steps: (1) Students draw a card. (2) They find its length on the chart and read the time. (3) Teams build the strongest passphrase from random-word cards. (4) Discuss why long and random beats clever.
  • Note: These are fake examples. Do not use real passwords.
  • Key message: "Length beats cleverness."

3-5 Station 2: Secret Code Lab (15 minutes)

  • Materials: cipher wheels (a paper wheel with the alphabet), message strips, pencils.
  • Steps: (1) Students set the wheel to a shift of 3. (2) They encode a short friendly message. (3) They swap with a partner and decode it. (4) Talk about how encryption hides messages so only the right people read them.
  • Key message: "Encryption keeps messages private."

3-5 Station 3: Scam Spotters Sort (15 minutes)

  • Materials: 12 message cards (6 real: school notice, family message, store receipt; 6 scam: fake prize, "your account is locked," "send a gift card," "don't tell anyone"); two bins labeled "Real" and "Scam or Pause."
  • Steps: (1) Teams sort cards. (2) For each scam, they circle the clue: urgency, secrecy, prize, asks for a password. (3) They state what they would do: notice their hackles, then Pause, Check, Tell.
  • Key message: "Urgent and secret? Pause."

3-5 Station 4: Data Detective (15 minutes)

  • Materials: a fake profile card for "Alex, age 10" with small clues (a school mascot, a bus number, a pet's name, a favorite park) written on separate slips.
  • Steps: (1) Teams get one clue at a time. (2) They combine clues to see how much they can piece together about "Alex" (not real). (3) They discuss which details to keep private.
  • Key message: "Small details add up."

3-5 Station 5: Help Ladder Role-Play (15 minutes)

  • Materials: scenario cards (a stranger asks for a secret; a friend is being excluded in a group chat; a pop-up shows something yucky); a floor ladder with three rungs: Friend, Trusted Adult, Report.
  • Steps: (1) Trios role-play one scenario, with a "teller," a "listener," and an "observer." (2) The listener says "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault." (3) Rotate roles.
  • Key message: "Climb the ladder until someone helps."

3-5 Station 6: Family Code Word Practice (10 minutes)

  • Materials: the Family Code Word guide (from the Family Engagement Pack); index cards.
  • Steps: Students practice asking, "What is our code word?" and the callback rule. They write ideas for a family code word (not the real one) to take home.

Grades 6-8 stations (ages 11-13): "Case Files"#

6-8 Station 1: Help Ladder Practice (15 minutes)

  • Materials: disclosure scenario cards (fictional), starter sentence strips ("Something happened online and I need help"), a trusted-adult map poster.
  • Steps: (1) Pairs practice saying a starter sentence to a "listener." (2) The listener practices responding with "Thank you for telling me. It's not your fault." (3) Switch roles.
  • Key message: "Saying the first sentence is the hardest part. Practice makes it easier."

6-8 Station 2: Account Defense Check (15 minutes)

  • Materials: a paper "account dashboard" for a fictional user; a checklist: unique password, two-step verification, recovery email current, unknown logins.
  • Steps: Teams audit the fictional account and mark what's missing. Then each student writes one action for their own accounts at home.

6-8 Station 3: Phish Lab (Defense Only) (15 minutes)

  • Materials: printed fictional DMs and emails, some real and some deceptive; highlighters.
  • Steps: Teams highlight clues (urgency, link mismatches, requests for codes or passwords). They decide: report, block, or ask an adult. Do not teach how to build such messages.

6-8 Station 4: Grooming Pattern Cards (15 minutes)

  • Materials: a short fictional chat (see Puzzle Hunt, Clue Set B) split into stages: trust, gifts, secrecy, isolation; sorting labels.
  • Steps: Teams place chat excerpts in order and identify the pattern. Then they discuss: what could a friend do? Key message: "The pattern is the clue, not the person's age."
  • Note: Station follows the sensitive-topic procedures. Send the pre-notice and keep the counselor close.

6-8 Station 5: STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT Drill (15 minutes)

  • Materials: four large floor cards (STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT), scenario cards.
  • Steps: Students stand on the card that matches the first action for each scenario and say what they would do, using no-blame language. Emphasize: do not view or forward images; evidence is the account name and messages. Name trusted adults and helplines. [VERIFY numbers]

6-8 Station 6: Fake or Real? Verification (15 minutes)

  • Materials: pairs of safe cards (a teacher-made headline, a screenshot of a fictional post, a labeled AI-created cartoon image); verification clue cards (source, date, other reports, reverse image search idea).
  • Steps: Teams decide what they can verify and what they cannot, and practice saying "I'm not sure. I won't share."

6-8 Station 7: Chatbots and AI Companions (10 minutes)

  • Materials: statement cards ("A chatbot always tells the truth," "A chatbot can keep a secret," "A chatbot can't replace a trusted person").
  • Steps: Students move to "Agree," "Disagree," or "It depends," then discuss. Key message: AI can help with some things, but serious worries go to a trusted human.

Grades 9-12 stations (ages 14-18): "Your Digital Defense Plan"#

9-12 Station 1: Threat-Model Yourself (20 minutes)

  • Materials: worksheet with three columns: Assets (accounts, photos, location), Risks, Protections; sticky notes.
  • Steps: Students list assets and rank them. They name one risk and one protection for each. They keep their sheet private.

9-12 Station 2: Passkeys and Password Managers (15 minutes)

  • Materials: printed explainer cards; a "match the protection to the risk" card game.
  • Steps: Students match actions with the risk they address (reuse, takeover, phishing, shared devices).

9-12 Station 3: Social Engineering Case Lab (Defense Only) (20 minutes)

  • Materials: fictional case files describing a manipulation pattern (urgency, authority, rapport, secrecy).
  • Steps: Teams name the manipulation tactic used and the defense. They propose a "pause script" to use in similar situations. No station may teach how to run a manipulation.

9-12 Station 4: Sextortion and Image Abuse Response (20 minutes)

  • Materials: a one-page response card (STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT with help resources [VERIFY]); fictional scenarios; a trusted-adult map.
  • Steps: Students practice how to support a friend and how to seek help, using no-blame language. Emphasize that images are never to be viewed, forwarded, or copied by students, or by adults other than those your school policy and law enforcement direct. Federal law treats knowing possession of such images as a serious crime, and state law varies. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: 18 U.S.C. 2252A, law.cornell.edu; confirm state law with district counsel]
  • Note: A counselor or trained staff member must lead this station. Pre-notice required.

9-12 Station 5: Synthetic Media and Truth (15 minutes)

  • Materials: cards with sources and claims; a provenance flowchart.
  • Steps: Teams walk a claim through the verification flowchart. They decide what to share, hold, or verify.

9-12 Station 6: Be the Helper (20 minutes)

  • Materials: friend-in-crisis scenario cards; a "what I can do and what an adult must do" chart.
  • Steps: Students role-play listening, not promising secrecy about danger, and connecting a friend to an adult. They practice boundaries and self-care.

9-12 Station 7: Ethics and Careers in Security (15 minutes)

  • Materials: career cards; links to free, legal skill-building resources (such as capture-the-flag sites and security club programs) [VERIFY current sites]; an "ethical or not?" dilemma set.
  • Steps: Students sort dilemmas (permission, disclosure, legality), and explore legal career pathways.

5. The Puzzle Hunt (with answer keys)#

Overview. Teams of 3 to 5 students visit clue stations around the room or building. Each solved clue yields a word (or a letter). When all clues are solved, the team assembles a final message. The hunt is cooperative. Every team that finishes gets a prize ribbon, and no team is ranked below another.

Hunt A: "ALWAYS TELL" (grades 3-8; works for 9-12 as a warm-up)#

Each clue's answer starts with a letter. The first letters, in clue order, spell ALWAYS TELL.

Hunt A: "ALWAYS TELL" (grades 3-8; works for 9-12 as a warm-up)
Clue Puzzle Answer Letter
1 Decode the shift cipher (move each letter back 3 places): DGXOW ADULT A
2 Riddle: "I keep nosy people out of your screen. I use a number or a pattern. I have four letters." LOCK L
3 Check-step question: "When you see something online, ask: ___ made this, and why?" (three letters) WHO W
4 Unscramble: PPA (a program on your phone) APP A
5 Fill in the blank: "If someone scares you online, it is never ____ fault." YOUR Y
6 Odd one out: Four messages: a school reminder, a note from a grandparent, a friend's birthday invitation, and "You WON a free phone! Send a gift card to claim it!" What is the last one? (four letters) SCAM S
7 Complete the habit: Hackles up? Pause, Check, ____ TELL T
8 Missing letters: "Turning a message into a secret code is called E N _ R Y P T" (the missing letter is C; the full word is the answer) ENCRYPT E
9 Fill in the blank: "Do not click an unknown ____." (four letters) LINK L
10 Complete: "The Tell-Someone ____ has rungs: friend, trusted adult, report." (six letters) LADDER L

Final message (teams read the first letters): ALWAYS TELL. Then the team stands and says: "Always tell a trusted adult. You are never in trouble."

Station set-up. Print each clue on a card and place the clues at ten spots. Give each team a clue sheet and a pencil. Number the clues so teams can go in any order. Post the shift-cipher wheel at Clue 1.

Hunt B: "Case File" (grades 6-12)#

Scenario packet. Each team receives a fictional case file with four short documents. The names and places are fictional. Teams answer questions about the patterns and the best responses. No sexual content or graphic detail is used.

Document 1: A fictional chat (shortened)

Mira (age 12) and "Jay" (says he is 13), in a game chat

Jay: nice build! u r really good
Mira: thanks!
Jay: want some free gems? i have extra
Mira: sure
Jay: sent. we should talk on another app tho, this chat is laggy
Mira: ok, which one?
Jay: the private one. dont tell your parents, they wont get it
Mira: ok
Jay: ur so mature for your age. most ppl ur age r boring. u can trust me
Jay: can u send a pic so i know ur real?

Questions for the team

  1. Circle the moments that are warning signs. (Key: free gifts; move to a private app; "don't tell your parents"; flattery that makes Mira feel special; request for a picture.)
  2. Sort the warning signs into stages: Trust and flattery, Gifts, Move to private, Secrecy, Request. (Key: Trust and flattery: "nice build," "so mature"; Gifts: "free gems"; Move to private: "another app"; Secrecy: "don't tell your parents"; Request: "send a pic.")
  3. What could Mira do next? (Key: Pause, don't send anything, save the chat (text), block, tell a trusted adult, report on the game.)
  4. What should Mira's friend say if Mira tells them? (Key: "Thank you for telling me. It's not your fault. Let's tell a trusted adult.")
  5. Complete the sentence: "The pattern is the clue, not the person's _____." (Key: age or name.)

Document 2: Account takeover timeline (fictional). A timeline of a fictional student's account: reused password, a link from a "friend," a login from an unfamiliar device, messages sent to contacts. Teams mark where the account could have been protected, and what the student should do now. (Key: unique passwords, two-step verification, check links with the sender in another way, change password, log out other devices, tell friends and an adult.)

Document 3: Fake or real? Three fictional social posts with provenance clues. Teams decide what they can verify and what they should not share. (Key: unknown source and no corroboration means do not share; it's fine to say "I'm not sure.")

Document 4: Friend in trouble. A fictional text from a friend: "Someone has a picture of me and says they'll send it to everyone unless I pay. Please don't tell." Teams write a reply and a plan. (Key: reply with empathy; "It's not your fault"; do not pay; save account name and messages; tell a trusted adult together; report; do not view or forward any image; encourage using a helpline.) The counselor reviews this document before use. [Pre-notice and counselor presence needed.]

Final team task: Each team writes a "Help Card" on a sticky note: "If this happens, I will ____." Place it on the Tell Someone Wall.

Hunt C: "Hackles Stops" (grades K-2)#

A simple picture trail of six stops. At each stop, children do an action and get a stamp.

  1. Tell team: point to a trusted grown-up. Stamp: a heart.
  2. Lock it: whisper a silly password. Stamp: a lock.
  3. Pause sign: hold up the stop paddle. Stamp: a hand.
  4. Oops button: press and say "I'll tell." Stamp: a button.
  5. Real or pretend: vote. Stamp: a star.
  6. Kind click: say something kind. Stamp: a smile.

When all six stamps are on the Passport, children get a Hackles Sticker.

Tips for running the hunt#

  • Give younger teams a helper.
  • Include an "ask a volunteer for a hint" option. Hints are not penalized.
  • Provide audio or read-aloud support for clues. Provide large print on request.
  • Offer a "quiet team" option for students who prefer less noise.
  • Keep the scenarios fictional and age-appropriate.

Volunteer rules (print and give to every volunteer)#

  1. Check in at the welcome table and wear your badge.
  2. Follow the school's volunteer and visitor policy. [VERIFY: background checks and sign-in requirements]
  3. Never be alone with a child. Stay in sight of other adults.
  4. Do not take photos on personal phones unless the policy permits it.
  5. Do not share scary stories, graphic details, or personal experiences about crime or harm.
  6. Do not ask children about their families, devices, or accounts.

10. Safety, disclosure, and guest-speaker rules#

Disclosure at Hackles Day#

  • Hackles Day can prompt students to share. A counselor is on site for the whole event and is visible. Post a "Counselor Corner" sign with a quiet space. Provide a card with the counselor's name and photo.
  • Any adult who hears a worrying statement follows the Volunteer Rules: calm, thank, do not promise secrecy, do not question, take the child to the counselor with another adult in sight, and write down the child's words.
  • Staff follow the Disclosure and Incident Guide, including personal reporting duties. [Confirm policy and state law with district counsel]

Guest speakers (including law enforcement, nonprofits, and industry)#

  • Vet speakers with your administrator before inviting them. Use only people and organizations you trust. [VERIFY your district's policy]
  • Share the Hackles Day principles in writing: no scare tactics, no graphic stories or images, no shaming, no demonstrations of how to attack systems or people, and no sharing of real student information.
  • Review talk outlines and slides in advance. Ask for a skills-based message that fits the grade band.
  • Ensure a school staff member is always present.
  • Avoid one-time assemblies that stand alone. If a speaker is invited, pair the talk with a classroom follow-up from the weekly lessons.
  • Make sure speakers know how to refer disclosures to the counselor.

Photos and media#

  • Use only photos of students whose families have given permission. [VERIFY policy]
  • Give students who do not have permission a wristband or sticker and tell photographers.
  • Do not post photos with names. Do not post children's work that includes personal information.
  • Never photograph students at the counselor corner.

Physical safety and accessibility#

  • Check allergies before any food.
  • Keep exits clear. Post a map.
  • Provide step-free routes and a quiet space for students who are overwhelmed.
  • Have a plan for weather, fire drills, and emergencies. Know your school's emergency procedures.
  • Keep a list of staff phone numbers.

Technology#

  • Stations are unplugged. If any station uses devices (for example, a slideshow), do not connect to student accounts and do not collect data.
  • Do not ask students to demonstrate on their real accounts or devices.

  • Tell Someone Wall: a large board for sticky-note "Help Cards" and a "Trusted Adults" photo gallery.
  • Directions and accessibility signs.
  • Quiet space.

Passport (one per student)#

A folded paper with a space for each station stamp and a "My Trusted Adults" page. Include the Hackles up, Pause, Check and Tell icons. Provide a family version with one conversation starter.

Supplies list (per 100 students)#

  • Paper, cardstock, markers, crayons, sticky notes, sticky dots
  • Index cards, cipher wheels (printed), scissors, tape
  • Stamps and ink pads, stickers
  • Name badges and lanyards for volunteers
  • Timers or bells
  • Clipboards and pencils for the hunt
  • Large poster boards and easels
  • First aid kit, hand sanitizer, tissues
  • Printed Family Engagement Pack pages
  • Certificates and ribbons

Low-cost options#

  • Use classroom and library supplies.
  • Ask community partners for donations. Do not accept sponsorship that includes advertising to children or collects student data. [VERIFY district sponsorship policy]
  • Use the printed kit materials and avoid devices.
  • Ask older students to make props.
