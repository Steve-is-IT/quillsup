Your Digital Defense Plan: Teacher-Only Answer Guide#

A free kit by Competence Collective · competencecollective.org · quillsup.org · © 2026 Competence Collective. Lesson text and teaching materials: CC BY-NC 4.0 (free to print, share and adapt for non-commercial use with credit). The Hackles name, logos, characters and artwork are not covered by that license: print and share them unaltered for classroom and family use; any other use needs written permission. [VERIFY with counsel]

Teacher-only: do not copy this file for students. Answers are guides, not scripts. Many items accept more than one defensible answer. Reward reasoning.

W1. Threat-Model Worksheet#

  • No single correct grid. Expect most groups to place account takeover and scam messages near "likely", and pressure or blackmail, AI fakes, and a data breach near "big harm". Accept any placement with a clear reason.
  • Strong "smallest fix" answers are specific and doable: "unique passphrase and a passkey for my email", "turn off precise location for two apps", "agree on a callback rule with family", "ask before tagging". Weak answers are vague ("be more careful") or unrealistic ("delete everything").
  • Persona note. A teen with a public following needs more attention to impersonation, harassment, and location; a teen sharing a family phone may prioritize who can see messages and reset accounts; a teen in a controlling situation may prioritize safe devices and Tell.

W2. Passphrase Builder and Recovery Map#

  • Rank the doors (weakest to strongest): (e) 1, (a) 2, (b) 3, (c) 4, (d) 5. The order of (e) and (a) is debatable; accept a reasoned alternative. Note: the gap between (b) and (c) is about interception and phishing of texted codes; CISA says SMS or voice codes should only be used as a last resort, and that FIDO/WebAuthn is the only widely available phishing-resistant option [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: CISA fact sheet].
  • 36-word table caveat. The classroom table has 36 words, so a 4-word phrase has about 1.7 million possibilities (weak). A 7,776-word list gives about 3.7 quadrillion [RECOMPUTED 2026-10-05: 7,776^4 = 3,656,158,440,062,976]. The table is for practice only.
  • "Fluffy2009!" versus dice passphrase. The first follows a pattern automated guessers try early (a word, a year, a symbol); the dice phrase has no pattern and is longer.
  • Recovery map. Most students will circle email or the phone number as the point of failure. Prompt: "If someone owned that, what else would they reach?" The strongest-protection star should be on email (or whichever account resets others).
  • Never accept a real password or code on the page.

W3. Case Lab Answer Guide (Case Cards C1-C8)#

W3. Case Lab Answer Guide (Case Cards C1-C8)
Case Hooks The ask The Check Fix It if already done
C1 Account locked text H, O, S Click a link and enter login and a code Open the app or site yourself; real companies do not need codes by text reply Lock (change password, sign out sessions, 2-step), Tell, Report to platform
C2 Friend needs a code H, O, S (K soft) Read a code sent to your phone to a "friend" Contact the friend another way; a code sent to you belongs to you only Lock the account whose code was sent, tell the friend by another channel their account is hacked, Report
C3 Easy money job O, K, S, H Move money through your own account and forward it Real jobs do not use your personal account to move money; ask a trusted adult; search the offer Stop, Tell, contact the bank, Report to FTC or law enforcement. Teaching point: moving money for someone else can be a crime even if you were told it was a job [VERIFY]
C4 Scholarship fee O, H, S Pay a fee by gift card Real scholarships do not charge a fee to receive money; check with a counselor or the real organization Contact the card issuer, Tell, Report to FTC
C5 Cousin in trouble H, O (familiar voice), K, S Send money fast and tell no one Hang up; call the cousin on a saved number; family code word; voices can be cloned from short clips [VERIFY] Contact bank or payment app, Tell, Report
C6 Ticket deal O, H, S Pay first by a payment method with no protection Use official resale or a protected payment method; walk away if pressured on payment type Contact payment provider, Tell, Report to platform and FTC
C7 Free currency giveaway O, H, O (familiar), S Log in via a link with your game account Open the official store yourself; giveaways never need your password Lock, Tell, Report to platform
C8 School IT notice O (authority), H, S Confirm current password via a link Go to the school portal directly or ask IT in person; IT will not ask for your current password by email Lock, Tell IT and an adult, Report as phishing

Common hook counts: Hurry and Strange method appear in all eight; Keep it quiet appears in C3, C5, and sometimes C2. If a group says "none" for Keep it quiet on C1, prompt with "what would the sender gain if you did not tell anyone?".

W4. Mosaic Map and Settings Checklist#

Jordan's Fragment Cards (R4):

W4. Mosaic Map and Settings Checklist
Fragment Inference
F1 Team photo with mascot jersey and partial school sign School or area
F2 Public fitness route that starts and ends at the same corner each morning around 6:30 Home area; routine
F3 Photo of a new bike in front of a blue door with part of a house number and street sign Home area, possibly the exact house
F4 "Sweet sixteen today" with the date Age and birthday
F5 Friend's tagged story "same spot after practice Tue and Thu, 5 pm" at a park Routine; a predictable place and time
F6 A public comment "see you at the bus stop on Elm" Route to school and a place and time
F7 Story "leaving for the beach tomorrow, gone all week" with live location When the house is empty
F8 Original photo file of the dog with location data embedded Exact home location
  • Which single removal breaks the most? There is no single answer; common strong picks are F2 (the route reveals home and routine), F3 (reveals the house), F8 (exact location), or F7 (reveals an empty house in real time). Reward the reasoning and a matching fix (privacy zone, remove house number and street sign from photos, turn off location tags, post after returning).
  • Settings check. Reasonable answers: Maps (While using; precise yes), Weather (While using or Never; approximate fine), Game (Never or While using; precise no), Social photos (Never or While using; precise no), Shopping (Never; precise no), Fitness (While using; precise often needed; use privacy zones). Flashlight app needs contacts? No. Game needs microphone? Only if it has voice chat and you choose to use it. Photo editor needs location? No.
  • Data brokers. Accept answers about adult help with opt-outs; check [VERIFY] any specific state-law claim before confirming.

W5. Response Drill Sheet (Rowan and Riley)#

  • Pattern markers: fast closeness and flattery; a push to move to a different, private app; "you first" or "I will send one if you do"; sudden change of tone; a deadline; a payment demand in gift cards or similar; a threat to share with followers and school; "do not tell anyone."
  • STOP: stop replying; do not pay (payment usually leads to more demands) [FBI and NCMEC; VERIFY]; do not send more; do not delete the account or the conversation. Should not: negotiate, try to "out-trick" the person, delete everything, or send "proof."
  • SAVE: the username and profile link; threatening and payment-demand messages; dates and times; the payment method named. Should not: copy, forward, or re-send any nude image of a minor, even as proof.
  • TELL: a trusted adult tonight, using the first sentence. If one adult reacts badly, try another.
  • REPORT: the platform; NCMEC CyberTipline; Take It Down for images made when under 18; with an adult, local law enforcement or the FBI. Should not: wait until the deadline.
  • Marking tip: Accept answers in the student's own words. Full marks require the "details, not images" distinction and a named Tell rung.

W6. Verify Lab Answer Guide (Claim Cards V1-V9)#

W6. Verify Lab Answer Guide (Claim Cards V1-V9)
Card First move Good evidence Confidence Action
V1 Audio of the principal announcing closure Source The school's official channels or a call to the office Do not know yet Do not share until confirmed
V2 Crypto giveaway video with a public figure Source, then Trace The public figure's verified channels; the "send one, get two" pattern is a scam pattern Probably fake or a scam Do not share; report to the platform
V3 Dramatic local flood photo Trace Reverse image search or local news confirming time and place Do not know yet Hold until traced
V4 8-second video of a political figure saying something shocking Pause, then Second source A full-length source and reliable coverage Do not know yet Do not share
V5 Anonymous screenshot of a "private chat" Source The original conversation, which is not available Do not know yet; screenshots are easy to edit Do not share; the harm is real either way; Tell if a person is targeted
V6 AI chatbot statistic and a "study" you cannot find Trace The actual study in a reliable source Do not know yet; likely invented or misquoted Do not cite until verified
V7 Voicemail from a relative asking for money, do not tell parents Check Call back on a saved number; family code word Treat as a scam until confirmed Do not send money; Tell
V8 Image of a classmate that looks like a sexual image Not "is it real?" It is harmful whether or not it is real Not the main question Do not forward; STOP, SAVE details (not the image), TELL, REPORT per Lesson 5
V9 Chart "Teen screen time is up 300%!" with an unlabeled axis and a source of "a survey" Source, then Second source (the data check) Who ran the survey, when, how many people, what the axis measures and where the scale starts; a second independent source Do not know yet Do not share until the source and the scale are clear; if it spreads as fact, report it in the app and tell an adult

Key teaching points. "Do not know yet" is a valid and strong answer. For V7 and V8, the correct behaviors are call-back and code word (V7) and no forwarding with the Response Drill (V8).

W7. Line-Up Answer Guide (Statements S1-S8)#

W7. Line-Up Answer Guide (Statements S1-S8)
Statement Teacher view Reason
S1 Location sharing by mutual choice, either can turn off any time Green Chosen and easy to exit
S2 They check my phone every night "because they love me" Red Monitoring is control regardless of the reason
S3 We swapped passwords to show trust Yellow Trust is not proven by access; can you say no or change it without a fight?
S4 Threat to leave and tell people if no photo Red Pressure and threats are not consent
S5 Asked to delete a photo and they did Green Respecting a request
S6 Posted a private chat in the group to embarrass me Red Sharing private messages to humiliate is abuse
S7 Friend angry if no reply within minutes; says other friends are "a problem" Red or Yellow Isolation and demanding availability
S8 Relative checks location; agreed on when Yellow Transparent and agreed; depends on whether it can be discussed and adjusted

Accept "it depends" answers when the student names what it depends on (choice, ability to say no, transparency, mutuality, safety).

W8. Helper Role-Play Answer Guide (Cards F1-F6)
Card Level HEAR HELP HAND OFF
F1 Photo and a threat from a game chat contact Amber "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault." Do not pay; help save details (not images); help report Go with them to a trusted adult tonight; use the Response Drill
F2 "I do not want to be here anymore" Red "I am really glad you told me. I care about you." Ask directly: "Are you thinking about hurting yourself?" Stay with them; do not discuss methods Bring in an adult now; call or text 988 or 911; do not keep it secret [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: official sites of the named services]
F3 Partner took phone, "do not tell anyone" Amber "That sounds scary and it is not okay." Offer to go with them; avoid confronting the partner Bring in a counselor or trusted adult; consider loveisrespect

F5 Group chat is piling on me Green or Amber "That sounds awful." Offer to save details and report; step away Tell an adult if it continues or threats appear
F6 Clicked a link and entered a password Green "Thanks for telling me, this is fixable." Help Lock: change password from a safe device, sign out sessions, 2-step Tell a trusted adult; they may be less upset than expected

Observer note. The Hand off sentence must say who and when, and offer to go along ("I will go with you").

Deep-Dive Case Labs: Answer Guide#

Case Lab 1: The Takeover
(1) First signal: the unexpected verification code. Second: a message from "Imani" to a friend with a link.
(2) Probably email or phone number; whoever controls it controls resets. Recovery email changed.
(3) Suggested order: change the password on a safe device and sign out other sessions (if still possible); contact the platform's recovery from the official app; tell a trusted adult; tell friends by another channel; report to the platform; change reused passwords elsewhere. Accept reasoned alternatives, but "tell friends so they do not click" should be early.
(4) Sample message: "My account was hacked, so please do not click any links from it. I am fixing it now." No blame.
(5) Passkey or authenticator app plus unique passwords would help most; a recovery plan helps afterward.

Case Lab 2: The Group Chat
(1) Names and faces, a recognizable store, a bus number, the group's makeup, the time.
(2) Taylor: "Please take that down; I am not okay with my location being shared." Upstander: "Let us take it down, it is not funny."
(3) Amber when someone shows they know where Taylor is; Red if there are threats, stalking, or a physical-safety concern.
(4) STOP engaging; SAVE details (usernames, screenshots of the threat, dates) but not private images; TELL a trusted adult; REPORT to the platform and, with an adult, to law enforcement if threats.
(5) Roles: a friend, a family adult, a school counselor or administrator, the platform, and law enforcement.

Case Lab 3: The Clip
(1) Pause; Source (an account made yesterday is a signal); Second source (any reliable outlet or the school); Trace (the full video).
(2) Known: it is circulating and the account is new. Unknown: whether it is real. Confidence: do not know yet.
(3) If fake: the school official and the community are harmed; if real: the situation needs a real response. Either way, harm from rapid sharing is real.
(4) "I am not sure it is real, so I am not sharing it. Let us wait for a source."
(5) Report if it is harassing or defaming a person, spreading widely, or being used to threaten.

Case Lab 4: The Found Flaw
(1) Yellow: you found it by accident; stop and tell. Permission test: you did not have permission to look at others' data, but you did not seek it; the right action is to stop and report.
(2) Do: stop, close the page, report privately. Do not: save, screenshot, post, test further, or tell only friends.
(3) Sample: "I used the booking page today and noticed the confirmation page showed other patrons' names and phone numbers. I did not save or share anything. Please contact me at ____ if you need details."
(4) A librarian, a teacher, or a trusted adult; the library's staff or tech contact.
(5) Staying within what was seen by accident keeps you inside the Permission Test and makes your report credible.
