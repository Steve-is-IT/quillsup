Read this first#

This workbook is yours. It is how you build your own Digital Defense Plan over ten short lessons. You will use pencil or pen. You do not need an account, a login, or a device.

Three rules for this workbook

  • Never write real passwords, codes, account names, home addresses, or other people's names or private details. Use roles ("a friend", "an aunt") or made-up examples.
  • Your plan is private. Your teacher will not read your personal pages unless you choose to share them. What gets looked at is your Teach Card and your short reflections.
  • It is never your fault. If something has happened to you or someone you know, there are people who can help, and you are not in trouble for telling. See the Help Page at the back.

The routine we use every lesson: Hackles up? · Pause · Check · Tell

  • Hackles up? Hackles are what rise on the back of your neck when something feels off: too good, too pushy, too secret, too scary. That feeling is information. Trust your instincts enough to notice it, then threat-model it: what is this feeling picking up on? A false alarm is fine. If you missed it or got tricked anyway, it is never your fault; tell someone.
  • Pause: Notice the pull (hurry, flattery, fear, secrecy). Take one beat before anything you cannot undo.
  • Check: Confirm using a second channel you chose yourself.
  • Tell: Bring in a person. Friend, then trusted adult, then a formal report. You can start at any rung.

The Response Drill (for threats and images): STOP · SAVE · TELL · REPORT

The Helper plan (for friends): HEAR · HELP · HAND OFF

Deep-Dive Case Labs (extended-block, homework, or a security club; defense only)#

All cases are made up. They teach recognition and response. Never write real names or details.

Case Lab 1: The Takeover#

Imani (16) gets a text at 9:10 p.m.: a verification code she did not ask for. She ignores it. At 9:40, a friend gets a message from Imani's account: "Can you vote for me in this contest? Here is the link." At 7:00 a.m., Imani cannot log in. The recovery email on the account has been changed.

(1) Which was the first signal? Which was the second?
(2) Which account was probably the master key? Why does that matter?
(3) Put these in the best order, and add any you would add: tell a trusted adult; contact the platform's recovery process from the official app or site; change the password on a safe device; sign out other sessions; tell friends by another channel that the account was compromised; report to the platform; check other accounts that used the same password.
(4) Write the message Imani sends to friends by another channel (two sentences, no blame).
(5) Which prevention would have helped most: unique passwords, a passkey, an authenticator app, or a recovery plan? Why?

Case Lab 2: The Group Chat#

A group chat shares photos from a birthday outing. One photo shows five friends, a recognizable corner store, and a bus number. A friend reposts it to a bigger group with names and the location. Taylor asks that it be taken down. People joke "it is funny" and keep it up. Later, someone in the bigger group says they know "where Taylor gets off the bus."

(1) What mosaic pieces does the photo and post reveal?
(2) What is one sentence Taylor can say to ask for removal? What is one sentence an upstander can say?
(3) When does this move from "annoying" to "Amber" on the Escalation Ladder? What would make it Red?
(4) Walk through the Response Drill for Taylor (STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT), using details, not images.
(5) Who are the trusted adults and the formal reports? (Use roles.)

Case Lab 3: The Clip#

An 8-second video appears on a short-video app. It appears to show a school official saying something offensive. It spreads fast. The account that posted it was made yesterday. A friend says, "It is obviously real."

(1) Apply the four moves (Pause, Source, Second source, Trace). What would you look for at each?
(2) What do you know? What do you not know? Give your confidence rating.
(3) If it is fake, who is harmed? If it is real, what changes?
(4) What would you say to the friend, and to the group chat?
(5) When does this become something to report or tell an adult, even if it is not clear whether it is real?

Case Lab 4: The Found Flaw#

You are using a library's online booking page for a study room. After you book, the confirmation page accidentally shows other patrons' names and phone numbers.

(1) Apply the Permission Test (P, S, H, T). What is the right category: Green, Yellow, or Red? Why?
(2) What should you do right now? What should you not do (copy, post, test further, tell only friends)?
(3) Draft a plain message to the library (what you saw, that you did not save or share anything, how they can reach you).
(4) Who is the right person to tell if you are not sure where to send it?
(5) Why does it matter that you stayed within what you saw by accident?

Glossary#

  • Asset: something that matters to you (an account, photos, money, privacy, reputation).
  • Threat: something that could harm an asset.
  • Threat model: your plain answer to "what am I protecting, from whom or what, how bad, and what will I do about it?"
  • Passkey: a login method that uses a private key stored on your device, tied to the real site, so there is no password to steal.
  • 2-step verification (MFA): a second proof of who you are in addition to a password.
  • Social engineering: persuading a person to give access, money, or information.
  • HOOKS: Hurry, Official, Offer or outrage, Keep it quiet, Strange method.
  • Mosaic effect: small details that combine to reveal something private.
  • Data broker: a company that gathers and sells personal data it did not collect directly from you.
  • Synthetic media (deepfake): images, audio, or video created or altered by AI.
  • Provenance: where a piece of media came from and what changed.
  • Sextortion: blackmail using real or fake intimate images.
  • Consent: freely given, specific, ongoing, and can be withdrawn.
  • Coordinated (responsible) disclosure: telling the owner privately about a flaw so it can be fixed.
  • Authorization: permission from the owner, given clearly and in scope.
