One complete puppet show, plus 20 short role-play scripts

Teacher Quick Start (one page, print and keep)#

Puppet show: "Gifty's Big Giveaway"#

What it is: a puppet show where a silly gold box (Gifty, played by an adult) tries tricks that never work, and the Crew shows the whole routine: Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell. The audience shouts, signals and groans along.

Puppet show: "Gifty's Big Giveaway"
Length Short version about 10 minutes (Scenes 1, 2, 5, 8). K-2 version about 13 minutes (Scenes 1, 2, 5, 7, 8). Full show about 18 to 20 minutes (all 8 scenes; the script is about 1,500 words, and the audience calls add time).
People 1 adult is enough (Gifty plus the Narrator, a few paper puppets on sticks). 2 adults are better (one Gifty, one Crew). Older students may work Crew puppets; never Gifty.
Props 8 puppets or paper puppets on sticks, up to 7 paper hats for Gifty (4 for the short show), a Pause Palm sign, a bell, a kazoo, a sparkle shaker, a quill puppet with a squeaker, a table-on-its-side stage. See the Printables checklist.
Teach first (1 min) The audience signals: Hackles up (neck brush), Pause (palm), Check (eyes), Tell (heart and reach), "No thanks, Gifty!", and "IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!" whenever Gifty puts on a hat.

Do: keep Gifty silly, never scary. Pop, bell and kazoo are optional (use a waving flag if any child is sound-sensitive).
If a child is frightened: stop, say "Gifty is a silly box with a hook," and make Gifty wave and make a funny noise.
Never: let a child play Gifty or any tricker; make Gifty sad or lonely (he is a silly trick, not a person who needs a friend); describe real harm.

Role-plays (R1-R20)#

What it is: two-minute practice scripts in groups of three: a Teller (practices the skill), a Listener (practices the kind reply), and an Observer (reads the Message Card and gives one star and one wish). Nobody plays the bad actor: the tricky message is read off a printed card.

Role-plays (R1-R20)
Step Do Time
1 Put children in triads (28 children = 9 triads and one pair; the teacher is the Observer for the pair). 1 min
2 Hand out one script and one Message Card per triad. Say the two promises. 1 min
3 Round 1: triads run the script (Teller says the lines; Listener says the Good Grown-up Reply). 2 min
4 Observer gives a star and a wish. Rotate roles. 1 min
5 Repeat so every child is the Teller once. Debrief with 2 questions. 5 min

While triads work, you circulate. Stand near any triad that goes quiet. A Teller may say "Pass" at any time. If a child starts a real story, pause kindly, thank them privately, and follow your disclosure box (no promise of secrecy).

Part 1: The Puppet Show#

"Gifty's Big Giveaway"#

A puppet show for K-5 (ages 5-10): about 10 minutes (short), 13 (K-2), or 18 to 20 (full)

"Gifty's Big Giveaway"
Learning goal Children see the whole Hackles routine acted out (Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell), laugh at a silly trickster, hear that a mistake is fixable, and practice Pause and Tell with the puppets.
Lessons K-2 L1, L2, L3, L4, L5; 3-5 L1, L2, L3, L10
Strands All six
Performers The teacher or an adult volunteer does Gifty's voice and puppet. Other puppets can be worked by the teacher, a co-teacher, older student volunteers (never as Gifty), or you may use flat paper puppets on sticks. One person can run the whole show with a few puppets and a hat for Gifty.
Time Full show about 18 to 20 minutes (about 1,500 words plus audience calls). 10-minute short version: Scenes 1, 2, 5, 8 (marked [SHORT]; skip the Crew roster in Scene 1). K-2 version about 13 minutes: Scenes 1, 2, 5, 7, 8. 30-minute extended: full show + audience practice (Part 1D) + Q&A. (Playtest note: the first draft said "12 minutes", but the script alone is about 12 minutes of speaking before any audience call, so the real full show runs 18 to 20.)
Audience Up to 60 children. Works in a classroom, library or hall. Sit sound-sensitive children toward the back and offer ear defenders or the flag cue.

Cast (the puppets)#

Cast (the puppets)
Puppet Look (Art Style Guide v2) Voice
Hackle (they/them) Baby hedgehog, pale-yellow quills tipped tangerine that stand up with a sparkle Curious, a little silly, slow and clear.
Peeper Sky-blue magnifying-glass/owl-ish critter, big eyes "Hmmmm!" in a detective voice.
Latch Mint-green padlock with a keyhole smile Whispery, proud, a bit bossy about secrets.
Pip Coral hermit-crab/snail Tiny, shy, hides in the shell when nervous.
Buddy Grape bear/dog-ish pal with a heart badge Warm and deep, a gentle hugger.
Ember Teal flame/sun sprite Gentle and glowy. Speaks softly.
Fixit Tangerine helper robot with a toolbelt Cheerful, tinny, "Beep! Click!"
Gifty (the Too-Good Box) A gold gift box with a sparkly bow and a tiny hook A game-show host with a cold, dramatic and very silly. Played by an adult only. Never scary.
Narrator The teacher (or a puppet with a hat) Friendly storyteller.

Puppet-making ideas (no sewing): paper plates and craft sticks; socks; paper-bag puppets; felt on a ruler; cardboard cut-outs from the coloring sheets (see Art Style Guide: thick outlines, flat colors, big shapes). Gifty is a gold-wrapped cardboard box with a ribbon and a small hook curl (a pipe-cleaner curl).

Stage and props#

  • A table turned on its side, or a cloth-draped chair back, as a stage.
  • A Pause Palm sign (a big cardboard hand).
  • A Quiet Meter star (optional).
  • Sound effects: a bell (Pop-up!), a kazoo (Gifty entrance), a sparkle shaker (Hackles Up).
  • A quill puppet that pops up with a squeaker for the Hackles Up moment (a bunch of orange pipe cleaners on a stick for the quills).

Audience participation signals (teach before starting, 1 minute)#

Audience participation signals (teach before starting, 1 minute)
When you hear... Audience does...
"Hackles up?" Hand brushes up the back of the neck.
"Pause!" Pause Palm (flat hand).
"Check!" Fingers to eyes (K-2) or magnifier circle (3-5).
"Tell!" Hand on heart, reach out.
"No thanks, Gifty!" Wave and shout "No thanks, Gifty!"
The kazoo Everybody giggles.
Gifty puts on a hat Everybody shouts "IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!"

Part 1A: The Script#

(S = scene. Scenes marked [SHORT] are used in the 10-minute short version. [K-2 ADD] is added for the 13-minute K-2 version. Running gags: Gifty's hat of the scene (everyone shouts "IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!"), Gifty's hook snags on things, the "forty-seven problems" counter that grows, and "Aw, shucks!" at the end of each scene.)

SCENE 1: Hello, Hackles! [SHORT]

(Narrator enters. Kazoo is quiet. Hackle puppet pops up behind the stage.)

NARRATOR: Good morning, everybody! I'd like you to meet a friend. This is Hackle!

HACKLE: (quills down, waving) Hi, everybody! See my quills? When everything's calm... it's Hackles Down. (quills lie down) Ahhh. But when something feels a little bit off... (quills pop up with a squeak and a sparkle shaker) POP! That's Hackles Up!

NARRATOR: Can you do Hackles Up, everybody? Brush up the back of your neck! (audience does it) Wow, look at all those spikes!

HACKLE: My quills pop when something feels odd, too good to be true, pushy, secret, or a little bit scary. And then we do four things. Help me out!

NARRATOR: (to audience) Pause! (Pause Palm) Check! (eyes) Tell! (heart and reach)

HACKLE: Together: Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell! (audience joins) Perfect! Now meet the Crew.

(SHORT version: skip the roster, bring up only Peeper. Full version: each Crew puppet pops up, one at a time, with a one-line hello and a gag.)

PEEPER: (holds the magnifying glass in front of one eye, so one enormous eye fills the stage) Hmmmmm! I'm Peeper. I spot clues. (to Hackle) What lovely quills. Very... many. (pulls the glass away) Sorry. Occupational hazard.
LATCH: (whispering, which is the loudest voice in the room) I'm Latch. I keep secrets. SHHHH. (audience: "SHHH!") Not so loud!
PIP: (tiny voice, half in the shell) I'm Pip. I decide what to share. (audience cheers; Pip hides, then peeks out) ...Is it over? (audience: "Not yet!")
BUDDY: (warm) I'm Buddy. I'm here to help you find a trusted grown-up. Any time.
EMBER: (glowy) I'm Ember. I keep it kind. (audience: "Awww")
FIXIT: (beep) I'm Fixit! If something breaks, I help fix it! (a bolt drops off; Fixit looks at it, then tucks it away) It's fine. That one was a spare.

HACKLE: And we have one more visitor today...

(KAZOO plays. The audience giggles. Gifty pops up with a "Ta-da!" He is wearing a tiny paper hat that says "TOTALLY NORMAL GROWN-UP". The Gifty puppet is a gold box with a bow and a tiny hook.)

GIFTY: (game-show voice, a little stuffy) Hello, hello, hello, friends! I am a perfectly normal grown-up! With a hat! I have free things! Lots of free things! (Sniff.) Pardon my sniffles, I'm a little stuffy today.

AUDIENCE: (taught earlier) IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!

GIFTY: (gasps) Who told you?! The hat was a disguise!

HACKLE: (quills pop up) Hmm! My hackles are up!

GIFTY: How rude! (his hook snags the curtain; he tugs, stuck; the Narrator unhooks him) Thank you. I'm fine. Everything's fine.

SCENE 2: The Pony Pop-Up [SHORT]

GIFTY: (switches to a hat that says "OFFICIAL") Behold! A pop-up! (BELL rings) YOU WON A PONY! Tap now! Only five seconds left! Five... four... (long, slow pause) ...three... (snoring noise) ...two-and-a-half...

PEEPER: (zooming in with the magnifying glass) Hmmmm! A pony in the tablet? With a countdown? Let's look for clues. Is it in a hurry?

AUDIENCE: Yes!

PEEPER: Is it too good to be true?

AUDIENCE: Yes!

GIFTY: (offended) A pony is not too good! A pony is the right amount of good!

HACKLE: (quills pop up) Hackles up! So what do I do?

AUDIENCE: Pause!

HACKLE: (Pause Palm) Pause! No tapping! (takes a breath) In... and out...

GIFTY: (clearing throat, taps the hat) Ahem. I mean... this is a very official message. Your tablet has forty-seven problems.

PEEPER: Forty-seven? Hmmmm. Last time it was forty-six.

GIFTY: I'm... growing!

AUDIENCE: IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!

HACKLE: Let's check! Who can I tell? (looks to audience) Who do you tell, friends?

AUDIENCE: A grown-up!

BUDDY: (appears, warm) That's right! A trusted grown-up. Every time. And you won't be in trouble.

GIFTY: (sighs, then his hook snags Peeper's magnifying glass, and Peeper's huge eye swings around at him) Ugh. Fine. No pony today.

HACKLE: Say it with me: "No thanks, Gifty!"

AUDIENCE: "NO THANKS, GIFTY!"

GIFTY: (pop-pop, disappears, pops back up to say "Aw, shucks!" and ducks.)

SCENE 3: Latch's Secret

(Latch pops up. Gifty peeks from the back, now wearing a hat that says "PASSWORD INSPECTOR".)

GIFTY: (sneaky-silly voice) Pssst, Latch! What's your secret password? I'll give you a hundred hats!

AUDIENCE: IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!

LATCH: (whispering) Shhhh. It's a secret. It's three silly words. (whispers, but so loudly that everyone hears) Purple... banana... trumpet.

GIFTY: (gasp) Oh! Oh, thank you! Purple banana trumpet! I'll write it down... (pulls out a pencil as big as he is)

LATCH: (gasp) Oh no! I just said it out loud! Passwords are secret! I forgot my own rule!

HACKLE: (quills pop up) Hackles up! That was an oops. But oops is okay! What do I do?

FIXIT: (beep) Oops Button! Oh, I'm okay. Off and out. People (tell). Secure it. Latch, let's make a new password together, with a grown-up. Silly. Long. Secret.

LATCH: (calming) Thank you, Fixit. A new password. I'll tell it only to my trusted grown-up. And this time I will whisper it... (mouths "mm-mm-mm-mm" and nothing comes out) ...like that.

GIFTY: (squints at the pencil, which now has no word to write) Aw, shucks.

(Fixit hands Latch a "new key.")

FIXIT: (to audience) Everybody makes oopses. Even locks. It is never your fault. Telling fixes it! (Click, click, fixed!)

SCENE 4: Pip's Shell

(Pip pops up. Gifty appears from behind, now wearing a hat that says "FRIEND".)

GIFTY: (sweet voice) Hello, little Pip! I'm your friend! You can tell because of the hat. Where do you live? What school do you go to? I'll send you a very special present... with a hook.

AUDIENCE: IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!

PIP: (retreating into its shell) Shell!

GIFTY: (confused) Shell?

PIP: (from inside the shell) Some things are for sharing. Some things are for my shell. My address is for my shell.

PEEPER: Hmmm! Let's see, friends. Which one's a "share" and which one's a "shell"? My favorite color?

AUDIENCE: Share!

PEEPER: My home address?

AUDIENCE: Shell! (curl up)

PEEPER: My favorite song?

AUDIENCE: Share!

PEEPER: My phone number?

AUDIENCE: Shell!

PIP: (peeking out) You're great at this! That's called being Share Smart. (tiny cheer)

GIFTY: (grumbling) Nobody EVER tells me their address. It's almost like they know something.

HACKLE: We do, Gifty. We do.

SCENE 5: The Secret Prize [SHORT]

(Gifty comes forward, wearing a crooked hat that says "TRUSTED GROWN-UP". Narrator whispers to the audience: "Uh-oh, Gifty has a new trick.")

GIFTY: (in a hush) Children... come close. I am a very trustworthy grown-up. I have a secret prize for you. (whispering) But it's a secret. You can't tell any other grown-ups. They'd want a share.

AUDIENCE: IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!

HACKLE: (quills pop with a loud squeak) Wait. A grown-up who says "don't tell the other grown-ups"? HACKLES UP! (to audience) What did Gifty say? Was it a... what?

AUDIENCE: A secret!

HACKLE: A secret! When someone says "don't tell a grown-up," my hackles go UP! What do I do?

AUDIENCE: Pause! Check! Tell!

BUDDY: (large, warm) I'm here. Come tell me, or any trusted grown-up. A real prize doesn't need a secret. And a real trusted grown-up never asks you to keep secrets from other grown-ups.

GIFTY: (shrinking, the hat falls over his eyes) Oh no. My secret trick. Foiled by a puppet with a heart badge.

BUDDY: (holding up five fingers) Do you have trusted grown-ups? As many as you can count on one hand? (audience holds up a hand, any number of fingers)

SCENE 6: Ember's Warm Words

(Ember glows. Gifty has put a joke "pop-up" on screen.)

GIFTY: Look! A picture of someone's goofy hat! Let's send it to everyone and laugh at them!

EMBER: (softly) Hmm. Would that make them smile or make them sad?

GIFTY: ...Sad?

EMBER: If it makes someone sad, we don't send it. We say a kind word. We tell a grown-up. (warmly) And look, here's a kind thing: (to audience) Give your neighbor a thumbs-up! (audience does) See? Warm!

GIFTY: (pats his own box) Hm. That is... warm. Is it supposed to do that? I'm a box.

SCENE 7: Fixit's Oops [SHORT for K-2: [K-2 ADD]]

(Fixit rolls out with a clatter. Gifty sneaks behind with the bell, wearing a hat that says "BUTTON SALESMAN".)

GIFTY: (rings BELL, voice sparkly) Fixit! Look! A shiny button! Press it! Press it!

AUDIENCE: IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT!

FIXIT: (beep) A shiny button? I love buttons! (taps) Click!

(SOUND: whoosh. Gifty flaps with a "Ta-da!" and a puff of confetti. Fixit freezes.)

FIXIT: Oh. Oh no. I tapped a thing. And now there's confetti. (small voice) Oops. I think I made an oops. (sighs) I'm... embarrassed.

HACKLE: (gently) Fixit, it's okay! What are the Oops steps? Everybody!

AUDIENCE: Oh, I'm okay! Off and out! People! Secure it!

FIXIT: (slowly) Oh, I'm okay... (takes a breath) Off and out... (closes the confetti) People... (waves to Buddy) Buddy, I made an oops and I need help.

BUDDY: (kneeling) Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's fix it together.

FIXIT: (sparks) Click, click, fixed!

NARRATOR: (to audience) Did Fixit get in trouble?

AUDIENCE: No!

NARRATOR: Is telling a good idea?

AUDIENCE: Yes!

SCENE 8: Gifty Gives Up (the finale) [SHORT]

(All puppets gather. Gifty is small and droopy, wearing a tiny paper party hat that says "SPOTTED!")

GIFTY: And I'd like it noted that I now have forty-nine problems.

HACKLE: Gifty, what is the best gift?

GIFTY: (thinks) ...A pause?

HACKLE: Close!

BUDDY: A trusted grown-up.

GIFTY: (wipes the hat off his eyes) That's actually a very good one. May I be the Silly Warning puppet?

HACKLE: (to the audience, warm and clear) One more thing, friends. Gifty is a silly box. Real tricky messages are not silly, they don't wear hats, and they are never sorry. So what do we always do?

AUDIENCE: Pause! Check! Tell!

(Everyone does the routine. The audience joins.)

NARRATOR: And that's the show! Remember: A false alarm is fine. It is never your fault. Everybody do the Tell Wave!

(Audience does the Tell Wave. All puppets bow. KAZOO.)

Part 1B: Discussion questions (5 minutes)#

  1. What did Gifty say that made Hackle's quills pop up? (A pony, a countdown, a secret, a request for private info.)
  2. What does "too good to be true" mean?
  3. What did Latch do wrong? What did Fixit say? (An oops. Telling fixes it.)
  4. What did Fixit do after the oops? (Told Buddy.) Did Fixit get in trouble? (No.)
  5. Who are your trusted grown-ups? Hold up a finger for each.
  6. What did Gifty want in the end? (To be a helper. Everyone can learn.)

Part 1C: Variations#

  • For K-2: Do Scenes 1, 2, 5, 7, 8 (about 13 minutes). Use Pause and Tell only. Keep Gifty's voice gentle. For very young children (K), cut the "forty-seven problems" joke.
  • For 3-5: Add Scene 3 and 4. Add the SPOT letters: after Scene 2, Peeper says "That was a P for Pressure and an O for Offer too good." After Scene 5, "That was an S for Secret."
  • For 6-8 (as a parody): Older students may write and perform a "Gifty Goes Corporate" parody for younger classes (see below), with Gifty voiced by the teacher.
  • Older students as puppeteers: 6-8 students can perform the show for K-2 as a service project. Rule: an adult does Gifty. Older students learn the content deeply by teaching it (the "protégé effect"). Preview the script for tone before sharing with younger children.
  • Sign-language and visual access: use big motions; hold up the Tell Wave poster; have a student sign "help" (see Lily's sign in K-2 materials [VERIFY with a Deaf educator]).

Part 1D: Audience practice (extended 30-minute version)#

After the show, invite children to practice with the puppets:

  1. Peeper's Pause: One child holds Peeper and says "Hmmmm" when the teacher reads a Message Card (K-2: "WIN A PONY!"). The class does the signals.
  2. Hackle Says: Teacher says "Hackles up if..." statements (e.g., "...if a pop-up says tap NOW!" Yes. "...if grandma sends a cat photo." No. Hackles stay down!). Kids brush their necks only for tricky ones.
  3. Fixit's Fix: Students practice the Tell sentence with a puppet: "I made an oops and I need help." Puppet: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault."
  4. Draw Gifty: Children draw Gifty with a party hat (no scary Gifty).

Part 1E: Teacher notes#

  • Gifty is never scary. If a child seems frightened, stop and say: "Gifty is just a silly box with a hook. He's a puppet." Have Gifty wave and make a funny noise.
  • Gifty is a silly trick, not a sad stranger. Do not give him a sympathetic backstory. In the first draft Gifty was "lonely" and "just wanted to give real gifts"; that could teach children that people who ask for secrets or addresses just need a friend. The finale now ends with a clear line: real tricky messages are never silly, never sorry, and never wear hats, so we always Pause.
  • Sound and sensory: the bell, kazoo, squeaker and sparkle shaker are optional. For sound-sensitive children offer a waving flag cue, seat them toward the back, and keep Gifty's voice playful rather than loud. No flashing lights.
  • Access: hold up a printed card for each audience call ("HACKLES UP", "PAUSE", "CHECK", "TELL", "NO THANKS, GIFTY", "IT'S GIFTY IN A HAT"); a sign-language interpreter or a student who signs "help" can stand beside the stage [VERIFY with a Deaf educator].
  • Nobody (child) plays Gifty or any tricker. Children voice the Crew, who notice, pause, check and tell.
  • Do not frighten. The show never describes real harm. "Secret" and "private info" are the strongest ideas.
  • Reuse the signals (Hackles Up, Pause Palm, Check Eyes, Tell Wave) so children generalize.
  • Optional prop: put a "Gifty's Offers" suggestion box in the classroom; children draw silly offers (a hat made of cheese). Teachers pick the best for the next puppet show.

Part 2: Role-Play Scripts Library (20 short scripts)#

How role-play works in Hackles#

Role-play is rehearsal, not performance. The goal: say the Tell sentence, practice the Pause, and feel the skill in your mouth. These scripts are short (1 to 2 minutes) and safe:

  1. Nobody plays the bad actor. The tricky message or pushy voice is printed on a Message Card, read aloud in a normal voice by the Reader (an Observer or the teacher). The people practicing never play the person causing harm.
  2. Three roles in every script: Teller (the person with the problem; practices the skill), Listener (a trusted adult or friend; practices the kind reply), Observer (reads the Message Card and holds the checklist; gives one star and one wish).
  3. Rotate roles so everyone is the Teller at least once. A Teller may say "Pass" at any time. Room plan: 28 children = 9 triads and 1 pair (the teacher is the Observer for the pair); run 2-minute rounds; the teacher circulates and stands near quiet triads.
  4. No real names or events. Characters are invented.
  5. Good Grown-up Reply (the Listener's key line): "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."
  6. Star and wish: The Observer says one thing that went well (star) and one thing to try next time (wish).
  7. Heavy scripts (H): R17 and R18 touch on pressure and threats. Remove them for opted-out classes. The Teller never role-plays the person being threatened with real details; they practice the response steps with a fictional card.
  8. If a student discloses during a role-play: pause the activity, follow your guide's disclosure box, and do not promise secrecy.

Format of each script: Band, lesson, skill. Cast. Message Card (what the Reader reads). Script (the lines). Notice (what the Observer looks for). Variation. Time: 1 to 2 minutes each.

The 20 scripts#

R1: Tell a Grown-Up About the Pop-Up (K-2)#

Skill: Say a short Tell sentence. Cast: Teller (Maya), Listener (a trusted grown-up: Dad, Mum, Gran, a carer, whoever your class chooses), Observer (reads the card).
Message Card: "A pop-up says: YOU WON A PONY! TAP NOW!"
Script:

  • Teller: (Pause Palm, then walks to the grown-up) "I need help. A pop-up said I won a pony."
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's look at it together."
  • Teller: "I got a tingly feeling."
  • Listener: "That's your hackles. Great noticing. A false alarm is fine."

Notice: Did the Teller pause first? Did the Teller say "I need help"?
Variation: Swap the grown-up for a teacher, grandparent, or the school counselor. Repeat with a different Message Card ("A game says: Tell me your password").

R2: The Oops at School (K-2)#

Skill: Tell a trusted adult at school after an oops. Cast: Teller (Dev), Listener (teacher), Observer.
Message Card: "Dev tapped something on the class tablet and now the screen looks weird."
Script:

  • Teller: (Breathes, open hands) "I made an oops, and I need help."
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. Let's fix it together. You are not in trouble."
  • Teller: "I tapped a thing."
  • Listener: "That's okay. Everyone taps things. You did the right thing by telling."

Notice: Did the Teller breathe? Did the Listener say "not in trouble"?
Variation: Add Fixit puppet as an Observer who says "Click, click, fixed!"

R3: Rung Two Says "Ignore It" (3-5)#

Skill: The Next-Rung Rule. Cast: Teller (Kenji), Friend (Sam), Trusted Adult (Aunt Lin), Observer. (Friend and Adult are Listeners.)
Message Card: "A player asked for Kenji's password and said 'don't tell.' Kenji tells his friend Sam. Sam says, 'It's probably nothing.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "Sam, a player asked for my password. I'm not sure what to do. Will you come with me?"
  • Friend (Sam): "I don't know... It's probably nothing. Maybe ignore it."
  • Teller: (Takes a breath.) "Thanks, Sam. I think I should tell a grown-up anyway. Will you come with me?"
  • Friend: "Okay."
  • Teller (to Aunt Lin): "I need help with something online. A player asked for my password and said not to tell. I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something."
  • Aunt Lin: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."

Notice: Did the Teller climb to the next rung without being embarrassed?
Variation: Add a busy Adult ("I'm cooking, later") and practice keeping going: "It's important. Can I tell you now?"

R4: The Tell-3 Script (6-8)#

Skill: A three-sentence Tell, naming barriers. Cast: Teller (Jordan), Listener (Ms. Alvarez), Observer.
Message Card: "A person Jordan doesn't know asked to chat somewhere more private and said not to tell anyone."
Script:

  • Teller: "Something is going on and I need help figuring out what to do." (Sentence 1)
  • Teller: "Someone I don't know asked me to move to a private chat and said not to tell anyone." (Sentence 2: facts)
  • Teller: "I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something." (Sentence 3: ask)
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. You did exactly the right thing. It is not your fault. Let's figure out the next steps together."

Notice: Three sentences: need, facts, ask.
Variation: The Teller starts with a barrier: "I'm worried you'll take my device." Listener: "Adults who care about you mostly want to fix the problem, not punish you."

R5: No Thanks, Gifty (K-2)#

Skill: Pause and say a polite no. Cast: Teller (any child), Gifty (a puppet or card voiced by the teacher), Observer.
Message Card (teacher/Gifty puppet): "I have a SHINY prize! Tap me! Don't tell anyone!"
Script:

  • Teller: (Pause Palm) "Hackles up! No thanks, Gifty!"
  • Teller: (To a grown-up.) "Gifty said it's a secret."
  • Listener: "Great pausing. Thank you for telling me."

Notice: Pause Palm? Said "no thanks"? Told?
Variation: The teacher changes Gifty's offers: a hat made of cheese; 47 problems; a pony. No child plays Gifty.

R6: Check a Different Way (3-5)#

Skill: Check without using the link. Cast: Teller (Priya), Friend/Listener (Amara), Observer.
Message Card: "A text from a number you don't know says: 'Hi! It's me, your friend! Click this link to see a funny video!'"
Script:

  • Teller: (Pause Palm) "Hackles up. I don't know this number."
  • Listener (Amara): "What will you do?"
  • Teller: "Check a different way. I'll ask my friend in person, not use the link."
  • Listener: "Good plan. And then?"
  • Teller: "Tell my dad."

Notice: Did the Teller name "a different way"?
Variation: Add a second card: "Your friend says it was NOT them." Teller: "Then I tell a trusted adult and we delete it."

R7: The New Number Text (6-8)#

Skill: Verify with a second channel. Cast: Teller, Friend/Listener, Observer.
Message Card: "Text from an unknown number: 'Hi, it's Coach. New phone. Can you buy 4 gift cards for the team? I'll pay you back.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "Hackles up. Fast, strange sender, gift cards. That's F and S on the FLAGS."
  • Listener: "How will you check?"
  • Teller: "I'll call Coach on the number I already have. Or ask in person."
  • Listener: "And if it's not Coach?"
  • Teller: "Don't reply. Tell a trusted adult. Report it."

Notice: Did the Teller name the flags and a second channel?
Variation: The Teller practices saying it to a parent: "I got a text like this."

R8: The Voice That Sounds Like Cousin (9-12)#

Skill: Family Code Word; call back on a known number. Cast: Teller, Family Member (Listener), Observer.
Message Card: "A voice message sounds like your cousin: 'I'm in trouble. I need money right now. Don't tell my parents.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "It sounds like him, but it's urgent and secret. That's F and G."
  • Teller: "I'll call him back on the number I already have. I'll ask for our code word."
  • Family Member (Listener): "And if he does not answer?"
  • Teller: "I'll tell you and we'll call his parents together."

Notice: Did the Teller use a second channel, not the message?
Variation: Add "AI voices can sound real [VERIFY]." The Teller says, "Voices can be copied. So we confirm."

R9: I Clicked by Mistake (K-2)#

Skill: Oops; no blame. Cast: Teller (Lily), Listener (Mom), Observer.
Message Card: "Lily tapped a link by mistake, and the screen changed."
Script:

  • Teller: "Oops! I tapped a link by mistake. I need help."
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's fix it together."
  • Teller: "I feel funny."
  • Listener: "That's okay. Let's take a breath."

Notice: Did the Listener say "not your fault"?
Variation: Add Fixit puppet.

R10: The Password Slip (3-5)#

Skill: The OOPS card. Cast: Teller (Mateo), Listener (Abuela), Observer.
Message Card: "Mateo told a friend his game password and feels uneasy."
Script (Observer holds the OOPS card):

  • Teller (O): "Oh, I'm okay." (breath)
  • Teller (O): "Off and out." (closes the app)
  • Teller (P): "Abuela, I made an oops and I need help. I told a friend my password."
  • Listener: "Gracias for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's fix it together."
  • Teller (S): "Let's change it and add a second lock."

Notice: All four OOPS steps?
Variation: The Listener is the school counselor.

R11: The Link and the Password (6-8)#

Skill: Oops path. Cast: Teller (Remy), Listener (Theo or Mom), Observer.
Message Card: "Remy clicked a link in a message and typed a password into a page that looked like the app."
Script:

  • Teller: (Pause) "I clicked a link and typed my password. I think it was a trick. I need help."
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. What do we do first?"
  • Teller: "Change the password. Turn on 2-step. Change other accounts that share it. Watch for odd messages."
  • Listener: "Good plan. Let's start."

Notice: Did the Teller go from "Tell" to "Fix"?
Variation: Add "Remy tells the friend whose message was sent from the hacked account."

R12: Game Chat Red Flags (3-5)#

Skill: Pause, block, tell. Cast: Teller (Kenji), Listener (Aunt Lin), Observer.
Message Card: "A new player asks: 'What school do you go to? Let's go to a private chat. Don't tell anyone.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "Hackles up. Three red flags: private info, private chat, don't tell. I'm pausing."
  • Teller (to Aunt Lin): "I need help with something online. A player asked where I go to school and said not to tell. I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something."
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. Let's block and report together."

Notice: Did the Teller name the flags and tell?
Variation: The Listener is busy ("later"); the Teller uses the Next-Rung Rule.

R13: Say It (6-8)#

Skill: Upstander: Say It. Cast: Teller (Anika), Friend (Listener), Observer.
Message Card: "In a group chat, someone posts a mean joke about a classmate."
Script:

  • Teller: "Hey. That's not cool. Please stop."
  • Friend: "Relax, it's a joke."
  • Teller: "It might hurt them. I'm not going to join in."
  • Friend: "...Okay."

Notice: Calm tone, short, kind. Did the Teller avoid insults?
Variation: Practice three tones (soft, firm, funny) and discuss which feels safest.

R14: Stand With (6-8)#

Skill: Upstander: Stand With. Cast: Teller, Target (a card-voiced friend, read by the Observer), Observer.
Message Card: "The classmate who was joked about says: 'I didn't know how to say anything.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "Hey. I saw the chat. That's not okay. Are you okay? I'm here."
  • Observer (as Message Card): "Thanks. I didn't know how to say anything."
  • Teller: "Do you want to tell someone together? I can come with you."

Notice: Did the Teller offer to come with the person?
Variation: Add "Want to sit with us at lunch?"

R15: Be the Helper (9-12)#

Skill: Listen; do not promise secrecy; connect to an adult. Cast: Helper (Teller), Friend (Listener, reads the card), Observer.
Message Card: "A friend says: 'I got a weird message and I'm freaking out. Please don't tell anyone.'"
Script:

  • Helper: "Thank you for telling me. I'm glad you did. I can't promise to keep it secret if it could hurt you, but I promise I'll help, and I won't blame you."
  • Friend: "I'm scared I'll get in trouble."
  • Helper: "You're not in trouble for telling. Let's go to a counselor together. I'll come with you."

Notice: Did the Helper avoid promising secrecy and avoid blame?
Variation: The friend says no. The Helper: "I'll stay with you while you decide, and I will tell someone if I'm worried about your safety. I care about you."

R16: Keep It: Don't Pass It On (3-5)#

Skill: Upstander: Keep It. Cast: Teller (Amara), Friend (Listener), Observer.
Message Card: "A friend forwards you a mean meme about a classmate and says, 'Send it on!'"
Script:

  • Teller: "I'm not going to send it. It could hurt them."
  • Friend: "It's just a meme."
  • Teller: "I'll keep it. I'm not passing it on. I'll tell a trusted adult if it keeps going."

Notice: Did the Teller stay calm and say "I'm not passing it on"?
Variation: Add the Four Moves signs: Say It, Stand With, Keep It, Tell It.

R17 (H): Stop, Save, Tell, Report (6-12)#

Skill: The Response Drill with a non-sexual threat. Cast: Teller, Listener (Counselor), Reader (reads the card), Observer.
Note: Remove for opted-out classes. The Message Card is fictional and not about images.
Message Card (read aloud by the Reader): "Someone says: 'I'll post your private chats to everyone unless you send me $30 in gift cards. You have one hour.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "STOP. I will not reply or pay."
  • Teller: "SAVE. I'll screenshot the words, link and username."
  • Teller: "TELL. I need help. Can I show you?"
  • Listener: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. We will report it."
  • Teller: "REPORT. In the app, and with an adult to the right helpers."

Notice: Order and calm. "Do not pay" said clearly.
Variation: Practice with Pause Palm as STOP; Tell Wave as TELL.

R18 (H): Help a Friend Who Got Threatened (9-12)#

Skill: Be the Helper plus the Response Drill. Cast: Helper (Teller), Friend (reads the card), Counselor (Listener), Observer.
Note: Remove for opted-out classes.
Message Card: "A friend shows you a message that says: 'Pay me or I'll tell everyone your secret.' They are shaking."
Script:

  • Helper: "Thank you for showing me. This is not your fault. Don't reply and don't pay."
  • Helper: "Let's save the messages and go to someone together. Who do you trust?"
  • Friend: "Ms. Alvarez, I guess."
  • Helper: "I'll come with you. I'll sit with you."
  • Counselor: "Thank you for telling me. We will report it together."

Notice: No promise of secrecy; no blame; evidence saved.
Variation: Teller notices they need support too and uses a rung.

R19: The Family Code Word (3-12)#

Skill: Callback rule and code word. Cast: Teller, Family Member (Listener), Observer. Teacher: the word on the card is only a pretend example. Real families pick their own word, never say it in public, and never type it in a chat.
Message Card: "A call says: 'It's your uncle. I'm in trouble. I need gift cards right now.'"
Script:

  • Teller: "Hackles up. That's urgent and secret. I will hang up."
  • Teller: "I'll call my uncle back on the number I already know. What's our code word?"
  • Family Member: (reads the pretend word from the card) "It's pickle."
  • Teller: "If he can't say it, it's not him. I'll tell you."

Notice: Callback, code word, tell.
Variation (3-5): Use a toy phone and a different pretend code word ("noodle"). (9-12): Add a voice-clone caution [VERIFY].

R20: Saying No Kindly (K-12)#

Skill: Refuse a request for a password without losing the friend. Cast: Teller, Friend (Listener, reads the card), Observer.
Message Card: "A friend asks: 'Can I borrow your login for a minute?'"
Script (pick a level):

  • K-2: "No thanks. Passwords are only for me and my grown-up. Let's play together!"
  • 3-5: "Nope. My passphrase is just for me. If you want to team up, let's play together."
  • 6-8: "I don't share logins. If you need something, I'll help you do it."
  • 9-12: "I'm not comfortable with that, but I'd love to help another way."

Notice: Kind, firm, no lecture.
Variation: Practice three tones: soft, firm, funny. Which feels easiest?

Teacher notes for role-play#

Debrief questions (use after any script)#

  1. What was the Teller's first move? Did it feel natural?
  2. What did the Listener say that helped? Which words would you use?
  3. What was hard? What would make it easier?
  4. What is one sentence you will remember?

Common pitfalls#

  • Over-acting. Keep it plain and calm. Over-acting makes it a show, not practice.
  • The bad voice. If a student wants to play the person causing harm, redirect: "We read that part from the card."
  • Real stories. If a Teller starts telling a real story, pause and thank them privately; follow your guide's disclosure box.
  • Perfectionism. There is no wrong way to say "I need help."

Accessibility variants (puppet show and role-plays)#

Accessibility variants (puppet show and role-plays)
Need What to do
Color-vision difference Puppets are identified by shape and name tag (padlock, magnifier, shell, bear, flame, robot, hedgehog, gift box), not by color. Message Cards and checklists use bold black text.
Low vision / blind Describe each puppet's entrance in one sentence ("Peeper holds a magnifying glass over one eye"); use sound cues (bell, kazoo) and keep voices distinct; large-print scripts (at least 20 pt).
Deaf or hard of hearing Hold up a printed card for every audience call; a signer or student who signs beside the stage; face the audience; a vibrating drum for the kazoo cue. Role-play: written Message Cards and script cards; Tellers may sign or write.
Dyslexia / reading difficulty The teacher reads the script; role-play Tellers use a sentence-starter card ("I need help with something online"). Nobody reads aloud unless they choose.
English language learners Use the repeated audience calls (they teach the vocabulary); allow a Teller to rehearse the sentence in the home language first, then English; pair with a bilingual Listener by choice.
Nonverbal or AAC users A Teller can use AAC, a picture card ("HELP", "TELL"), a Tell Wave, or point to the sentence on a card. The Listener says the Good Grown-up Reply.
Motor / mobility Puppets on sticks can be worked with one hand or by a partner; role-plays work seated; a "puppeteer's helper" role.
Sensory / noise Waving flag instead of kazoo/bell; seat at the back; quiet corner; no flashing lights; role-plays in whispers.
Anxiety about being the center Everyone works in triads, not in front of the class; a Teller may Pass; the "star and wish" are always kind; nobody performs alone.
