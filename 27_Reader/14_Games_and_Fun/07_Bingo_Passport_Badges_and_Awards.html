BINGO CARDS, THE HACKLES PASSPORT, BADGES AND AWARDS
Celebrating the skills, not the scores

Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

A free kit by Competence Collective · competencecollective.org · quillsup.org

No student accounts, no student data collected, ever. The Passport and badges are paper. They belong to the child. Nothing is uploaded, logged or tracked.

Teacher Quick Start (one page, print and keep)#

Hackles Bingo#

What it is: a cooperative bingo where the Caller (you) reads a clue and teams cover the matching skill word on their card (Pause, Secret, Second Channel...). When a team has a line they whisper-shout "HACKLES!" and the whole class cheers. Nobody is out.

Hackles Bingo
Step Do Time
1 Hand a card to each pair or team (3 x 3 for K-2, 5 x 5 for 3-12). Cover the FREE: HACKLES UP! square. Say the two promises. 1 min
2 Read one clue from the Caller's list. Read it twice, slowly. Teams whisper, then cover if they have it. Check off the clue on your list. about 40 sec each
3 A team with a line shows a Pause Palm and whisper-shouts "HACKLES!" You read its answers aloud to check. Class cheer: Hackles Pop. 20 sec
4 Keep going: line, then four corners, then blackout. Do not restart the call list between rounds. see below

How long (script-tested, 2,000 simulated games per card set):

Hackles Bingo, table 2
Card set First line Four corners Blackout
K-2 (3 x 3, 16 clues) about the 4th clue about the 10th clue about the 14th clue
3-5 and 6-12 (5 x 5, 30 clues) about the 12th clue about the 18th clue about the 29th clue (every clue)

So K-2 Quick Bingo = play to blackout (about 10 minutes), because a K-2 line arrives after 3 or 4 clues. Quick Bingo for 3-12 = one line (about 8 minutes). Full Bingo = all three rounds with the same list (about 22 to 25 minutes).

If...

  • Two teams win a line at once (about 15 in 100 games): cheer for both; that is the point.
  • A team covered a wrong square: "Oh, a false alarm! That is fine. Let's check." No penalty.
  • A child cannot read the words: K-2 cards have a picture beside every word; point to the picture.
  • You have more than 6 teams: print duplicate cards or use Build-Your-Own Card (see variants).

Never: make a team "lose", display a score list, or ask for real passwords or addresses.

Passport, badges and awards in one minute#

Give every child a Passport in week 1 (a folded paper booklet). Stamp for doing, not for being right: 5 seconds per stamp, during or right after a game. Give a badge for any observed moment (say the reason aloud). Give every child an end-of-unit certificate. No leaderboards, no counts by child, no record kept. Details and the exact lists are below.

1. Principles (read first)#

  1. Celebrate doing, not being right. Stamps and badges are for noticing, pausing, checking, telling, helping, recovering. A child who says "false alarm?" and was wrong earns the same stamp as a child who was right.
  2. Everyone earns something. No one is left with an empty passport. No leaderboards, no rankings, no public score lists.
  3. Telling is the star. The Tell Stamp can be earned many ways, including by telling about anything (a worry, a game, an oops) without giving details.
  4. No private information. Never ask children to write passwords, addresses or real accounts in the Passport or on a bingo card. Use pretend examples.
  5. Teams and classes can earn too. Class awards celebrate the group.
  6. Privacy by design. Paper only. If a child loses their Passport, give a new one. Do not keep a record of who has which stamp. If you want one for your own use, keep it on paper in a locked drawer, never in a system, and follow your school's policy.
  7. No competition for prizes. Rewards are stickers and certificates, not toys or ranks.
  8. Accessible. Stamps can be stickers, hand-drawn marks, or a punch; the Passport has large print and symbols.

Part 1: Hackles Bingo#

1.1 At a glance#

1.1 At a glance
Players A whole class, in pairs or teams of up to 4 sharing a card.
Time 10-minute "Quick Bingo" (3-12: one line, about 8 minutes; K-2: play to blackout, about 10 minutes) or 30-minute "Full Bingo" (three rounds: line, four corners, blackout; about 22 to 25 minutes)
Bands K-2 (3 x 3 picture cards), 3-5 (5 x 5), 6-12 (5 x 5)
Style Cooperative: when any team gets a line, the whole class celebrates, and the class keeps going. Nobody is out.
Learning goal Connect the clue (a scenario or skill description) to the skill word (Pause, Secret, Second Channel...). Words become tools.
Needs One printed card per team (or pair); markers (paper scraps, buttons, dry beans, or crayon X's); the Caller's clue list

1.2 How to play (all bands)#

  1. Hand out a card to each pair or team. Mark the FREE: HACKLES UP! space with a marker. (It is always free!)
  2. The Caller (the teacher or a volunteer) reads one clue at a time from the Clue List below. Read slowly.
  3. Teams whisper (Pause!) and look for the answer square that matches. If it is on their card, they cover it.
  4. When a team has a line (row, column or diagonal), they quietly show a Pause Palm and then whisper-shout: "HACKLES!"
  5. The Caller checks by reading the answers aloud. If correct, the class cheers (Hackles Pop: hands pop up on the head). The class keeps playing; later lines get a different cheer.
  6. Do not eliminate a team that gets it wrong. If a square was covered by mistake, say "Oh, a false alarm! That is fine. Let's check." No penalty.
  7. Rounds: Round 1 is a line. Round 2 is the four corners. Round 3 is a blackout (every square), a class-wide celebration. Keep the same call list for all three rounds (do not reshuffle): with the 30-clue lists a blackout needs almost every clue, so the whole list is used once, and the Caller always has enough clues. On 5 x 5 cards, six distinct cards share most of their squares (any two cards share 19 to 22 of 24), so ties are common and fine.

Tip: Mix the clue order. Keep a "called" list so you can verify.

Making more than 6 cards: the six ready-made cards per band are enough for one class if teams share. For a bigger group, print copies (cards 1 to 6 repeated) or use Build-Your-Own Card: give each team a blank grid and the word bank and let them place the words themselves.

Cooperative option ("One Big Card"): Draw the 5 x 5 card on the board. When any team covers a square, the class covers it on the big card. The class wins when they cover a line. Then another line, then the blackout.

1.3 Variants#

  • Clue Cards: Print clues on cards; teams draw one, read it, and the class marks the answer.
  • Reverse Bingo: The Caller says the skill word; teams find the clue on a card of clues.
  • Build-your-own card (3-5, 6-8): Teams get a blank 5 x 5 grid and a word bank; they fill it in themselves. Great for review.
  • Scenario Bingo (6-12): The Caller reads a full scenario and teams cover two squares: the flag and the second channel.
  • Silent Bingo: All calls are on cards held up; no voices (good for noisy days or Deaf/hard-of-hearing students).
  • Movement Bingo: When a team covers a square they do the matching motion (Hackles Up, Pause Palm, etc.).

1.4 K-2 Picture Bingo (3 x 3)#

Learning goal: Link the words and pictures of the Hackles routine and the Crew's skills. Lessons: K-2 L1-L5.
How to use: Put a small picture next to each word (a lock for PASSWORD, a hand for PAUSE, a heart for TELL, a shell for SHELL, etc.). Children point to the picture. The Caller reads the clue aloud.
Time: 10 minutes for one line; 20 minutes for three rounds.

1.5 3-5 Skills Bingo (5 x 5)#

Part 2: The Hackles Passport#

2.1 What it is#

A small paper booklet (or a single folded sheet) where children collect stamps (or stickers) for trying skills. It is a keepsake and a conversation starter at home. It is not a grade. No one ever "fails" a Passport.

2.1 What it is
Format One sheet of paper folded into an 8-panel mini booklet: 1 cover, 2 my routine, 3 to 5 stamp pages (up to 4 stamps per page), 6 my trusted helpers, 7 my one thing, 8 back cover. Or 4 pages in a bigger format. (The first draft said "cover, 6 stamp pages and a back page" but then described 9 panels; it now adds up to 8.)
Who gives the stamps Teachers, counselors, librarians, grown-ups at home (using the family page), older student helpers (for the Helper stamp).
How many stamps K-2: 8 stamps. 3-5: 12 stamps. 6-8: 12 stamps. 9-12: 12 stamps. (Each band is its own booklet.)
Stamp style Stickers (Crew characters), rubber stamps, hole punch, or hand-drawn star. For 6-12 use simple geometric icons (see the Art Style Guide: strand icons, not cartoon characters).
Levels Explorer (3 stamps), Trailblazer (6 stamps), Hackles Hero (all stamps). Each has a certificate (Part 4).

2.2 The Passport pages (layout described)#

Panel 1, cover: "[Logo: Hackles] My Hackles Passport. Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell." A line for "My name" (first name only or a nickname is fine). Hackle waves (small icon in 6-12).

Panel 2, "My Hackles routine" (the poster): the four moves with pictures and hand signals. K-2: pictures only. 6-12: plain text.

Panels 3, 4 and 5, the stamp pages. Each stamp is a circle (large enough to be a sticker) with the skill name, a sentence of how to earn it, and a place for a grown-up to initial or draw. K-2: 8 stamps (3 + 3 + 2). 3-12: 12 stamps (4 + 4 + 4).

Panel 6, "My trusted helpers": the Trusted Hand (K-5): a hand outline with five fingers and a line for each (some children will fill fewer; that is fine); 6-12: a Help Ladder with four rungs and a line for "who I would tell". Do not require them to list real names; pictures, initials or roles ("teacher", "Aunt") are fine.

Panel 7, "My one thing": "One thing I will do this month: ____" (a Tell sentence, a changed password, a code word).

Panel 8, back cover: "Never your fault. Always tell. Trust your hackles. A free kit by Competence Collective · competencecollective.org · quillsup.org"

Ways to earn a stamp without reading, writing or speaking English: any stamp can be earned by drawing, pointing, signing, using a picture card, or saying it in your home language. The adult may stamp for a clear attempt. Children who are absent or have limited access still receive stamps later in the year.

2.7 How to run the Passport#

  • Introduce it in week 1: give a Passport to each child and a sticker for "Hackles Up."
  • Stamp during or right after a game or lesson. A stamp takes 5 seconds.
  • Do not ask children to share their Passport publicly. Let them show family.
  • Give a bonus "Surprise Stamp" when a child does something kind (Ember) or tells a grown-up (Fixit); a Tell stamp can be given any time a child tells, for anything.
  • Make-up and absences: Stamps can be earned any time in the year.
  • Family page: send the Passport home with a note: "Ask your child to show you the Hackles routine and tell you about one stamp."
  • Parent signature is optional. No child is penalized for a missing signature.

Part 3: Badges#

3.1 What badges are#

Paper (or sticker) badges for skills and moments. They are not graded and not ranked. A teacher may give a badge for any observed moment of noticing, pausing, checking, telling, helping or recovering. Every child should receive at least three badges in a term.

Art notes (Art Style Guide v2): each badge is a circle or soft shield, thick outline, flat fill in the Crew color, one simple icon, and a large simple word. For 6-12, geometric icon only. Clean black-line versions for coloring.

3.3 Badge rules#

  1. Give for moments, not rankings. "I saw you notice that. Here's a badge." Say the reason out loud.
  2. No scarcity. Make plenty. Enough for everyone.
  3. Class badges (Rescue Ribbon, Peak Climber) go on a class poster.

  5. No public leaderboards. Never display counts by child.
  6. Teen version: offer badges as small cards or "challenge coins" made of paper; many teens will pass, which is fine.
