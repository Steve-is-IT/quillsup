MISSION: QUIET DOOR
Twelve classroom escape-room-style missions, all defense, all paper, no devices

Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

Hackles · a free kit · quillsup.org

No student accounts, no student data collected, ever.

Teacher Quick Start (one page, print and keep)#

What it is: a paper escape room. Teams solve four short puzzle stages. Each stage's answer matches one labeled envelope; the right envelope holds the next clue, a wrong one holds a calm "Pause" card. At the end the class whispers the Door Phrase and the Quiet Door opens. Everybody escapes.

You are the Door Keeper. You read clues aloud (K-2), hand out hints, give stamps, and do any "trickster voice" yourself. Children never play the bad actor.

Teacher Quick Start (one page, print and keep)
When What you do Time
Night before Print the mission pages from the Printables checklist, cut the cards, write the answers on envelopes (the correct label plus 2 to 5 decoy labels per stage), put the next clue inside the correct envelope. A mission takes about 25 to 30 minutes to prepare. 25-30 min
Start (3 min) Make 4 to 6 teams of 3 to 5. Hand out 3 "Ask Buddy" cards per team. Read the story. Do the Hackles routine. 3 min
Puzzle (22 min Full Escape / 8 min Quick Door) Teams solve stages at their own pace, one stage at a time, and open the envelope that matches their answer. Walk around. Praise hint requests out loud. 22 min
Door (5 min) Everyone gathers at the Quiet Door. Teams whisper the Door Phrase. Open the door: "Thank you for being calm." Debrief with the 3 or 4 questions. 5 min

Hints (free and cheerful): Hint 1 = a nudge, Hint 2 = names the skill, Hint 3 = walks through the method (not the answer). After 3 hints, hand over the right envelope ("Teamwork. That is what Fixit is here for.").

Quick Door (10 minutes): run the two stages named in the mission's Time line (about 4 minutes each) plus the Door ritual. If a stage you skip feeds a later one (M1 Stage 3 uses Stage 1's answer; S1 Stage 4 uses Stage 3's key; S2 Stage 4 uses its own count), hand the needed answer over on a "gift card."

If...

  • The room gets loud: raise a Pause Palm; take a star off the Quiet Meter for the room, never a child. Add it back when calm.
  • A team rushes or guesses envelopes: hand them a "Pause" card and three slow breaths, kindly. Then back to the clue.
  • A child cannot read, spell or write the answer: pair by choice, point and say the answer, or use the picture-symbol envelopes.
  • A child shares something real: pause the game, thank them, follow your disclosure box. No promise of secrecy.

Never: shame a team for hints, use a real student's name for a character, or let anyone play the stranger.

Part A: How Mission: Quiet Door works#

The idea#

At Hackles HQ there is a very special door: the Quiet Door. It only opens for a team that is calm. If anybody panics, shouts or rushes, the door stays shut and a little sign flashes: "Pause." Each mission is a locked-room adventure where the class (in teams) solves four puzzle stages in order, then whispers the Door Phrase to the Door Keeper (the teacher). The door opens. Everyone does the Hackles routine and walks out together.

The twist: speed does not win. Calm wins. A team that rushes gets a free "Pause" from the Door Keeper and has to take three slow breaths before continuing. A team that asks for a hint is praised, not penalized (asking for help is the whole point of the kit). Everybody escapes. No one is out.

Defense only: the rules for every mission#

  1. Nobody plays the bad actor. Clues are printed documents. The "trickster voice" belongs to the Door Keeper or to a printed script card.
  2. No real brands, apps, or people. Everything uses invented names. Links end in .example.
  3. No scary content. No graphic detail, no frightening statistics, no sexual content. The 6-8 and 9-12 missions use non-sexual synthetic-media scenarios (a fake school announcement, a fake bridge closure). The sensitive lessons (6-8 L7/L8; 9-12 L5/L6/L7) handle other material with their own safeguards.
  4. Everyone escapes. No team is "stuck" forever: the Door Keeper has a free hint ladder.
  5. Pass cards are always available.
  6. We teach recognizing, pausing, checking, telling, saving, reporting, recovering. We never teach how to build a scam, break into an account, or make a fake.

Setup (used for every mission)#

Room. Four "stations" (tables or desks with a colored cloth) numbered 1 to 4, plus the Door Keeper's Desk and the Quiet Door (a classroom door with a paper sign, a decorated cardboard door, or a ribbon across a doorway).

The Answer-Envelope system. Each station holds a stack of sealed envelopes. Each envelope is labeled with a possible answer and contains the next stage's clue. A team solves a puzzle, finds the envelope that matches their answer, and opens it. There is only one correct envelope per stage; all other labeled envelopes contain a "Pause" card that says "Hmm, that is not it. Take three slow breaths, check your work, and try again. Ask Buddy for a hint if you like." This makes every mission self-checking (the teacher does not have to check each answer) and kind (a wrong answer gives a calm reminder, not a buzzer).

Setup (used for every mission)
Equipment What it is
Envelopes One correct envelope for each stage that has an answer (3 or 4 per mission), plus 2 to 5 "Pause" decoys with different labels for each stage (for example, for a correct "26": decoys "12", "16", "24"). Label with the answer on the front and a symbol (star, moon, sun) so no child has to rely on reading or color. Every correct label is a real answer; there is exactly one correct envelope per stage.
Door Keeper's Desk Holds the Hint Cards, the stamps or stickers, and the Door Phrase.
"Ask Buddy" cards Each team gets 3. A team may turn one in at the Door Keeper's Desk for a hint. Hints are cheerfully given.
The Quiet Meter A poster with 5 stars. If the room gets too loud, the Door Keeper removes one star. If the room is calm, add one star. At 5 stars the door is "extra open" (a fun reward). Never use as a punishment; use as a calm cue. It measures the room, never a child or a team, and it is switched off for any child who is Deaf or hard of hearing, has Tourette syndrome, or is a loud-by-nature speaker: their voice never costs a star.
Teams 4 to 6 teams of 3 to 5. Every team solves every stage (so every child practices). In 10-minute modes, assign each team a different mission stage.
Pass cards Green.
Timer Optional; a quiet "sand timer" or 10-second breath cue works better than a countdown.

10-minute and 30-minute modes (for every mission)#

10-minute and 30-minute modes (for every mission)
Mode What to do
10-minute "Quick Door" Every team solves the two stages named in the mission's Time line (about 4 minutes each), then does the Door ritual (2 minutes). If a stage you skip feeds a later one, the Door Keeper hands the needed answer over on a "gift card." Rotation variant for 6 teams: each team solves a different stage and the class puts the answers on the board together; the Door Keeper then gives out the Door Phrase. 30-minute "Full Escape" Every team solves all four stages. 3 minutes: story and Hackles routine; 22 minutes: puzzle; 5 minutes: Door Phrase and debrief.

Roles in a team (rotate each stage)#

  • Reader: reads the clue aloud (or the teacher reads for K-2).
  • Finder: finds the envelope and brings it back.
  • Recorder: writes or draws the answer (no writing in K-2; use stickers).
  • Calm Captain: keeps the team quiet and calls a Pause (Pause Palm) when voices rise.
  • Hint Holder: keeps the Ask Buddy cards.

In teams of 3 combine roles. All roles are equally important. In K-2 the teacher is everyone's Reader.

The Door ritual (the same for all missions)#

  1. The whole class gathers at the Quiet Door and stands in a circle.
  2. Hackles up? (hand brushes the neck, quiet). Pause. (one slow breath, palm up). Check. (eyes). Tell. (hand on heart, reach out.)
  3. The teams each whisper the Door Phrase to the Door Keeper (K-2: the class whispers it together). Where a mission says so, each solved stage gave the team one Door Phrase word card; otherwise the Door Keeper simply tells the team the phrase when the last stage is done. Teams that cannot whisper may sign small, or hold up the word cards in order.
  4. The Door Keeper opens the door and says: "The Quiet Door is open. Thank you for being calm."
  5. Debrief (see each mission).

Accessibility notes#

Mission M3: The Snow Day That Wasn't#

Mission M3: The Snow Day That Wasn't
Theme The deepfake detective (non-sexual)
Lesson link 6-8 L8 Fakes That Hurt (the non-sexual thread); L3 Phish Lab
Learning goal Students understand that glitch-spotting is weak evidence, that source checks are stronger, how to verify using a second channel, and the response STOP · SAVE · TELL · REPORT.
Time 10 min: Stage 1 + Stage 3. 30 min: all
Materials 6 evidence cards, source table, A1Z26 cipher card, 8 response cards, 5 envelopes

Story.

It's Sunday night. A voice message that sounds just like the principal says: "School is closed tomorrow because of snow." A photo shows the school buried in snow. The messages are all over the class chats. But the weather forecast says clear skies. Which is real? (The people and the school are pretend.) The Quiet Door only opens when you can prove it.

Stage 1: Weak or Strong Evidence? Six statements. Which are strong evidence? Count them.

  1. The voice sounds exactly like the principal. (WEAK: voices can be copied [VERIFY])
  2. The photo has no weird hands or text. (WEAK: fakes keep improving)
  3. It has been shared 5,000 times. (WEAK)
  4. The school district's website and official app say school is open. (STRONG)
  5. The account that posted it was made yesterday and has no other posts. (STRONG ENOUGH TO COUNT: a brand-new empty account is a serious warning sign, though not proof by itself)
  6. No news station or weather service mentions a storm. (STRONG)

Answer: 3. Envelope: "3".

Stage 2: Trace the Source. A table of posts:

Mission M3: The Snow Day That Wasn't, table 2
Poster Time Account age Says
m_ortiz 6:19 a.m. 4 years "Sharing from @snowwatch_news"
snowwatch_news 6:02 a.m. 1 day "SCHOOL CLOSED! Voice memo attached."
riley.k 6:31 a.m. 3 years "Is this true?"
jo_the_goat 6:40 a.m. 2 years "Someone confirm?"
school_district_official 6:52 a.m. 8 years "School is OPEN today. Please ignore false reports."

Which post came first? Write its time without the colon.
Answer: 602 (snowwatch_news, a one-day-old account). Envelope: "602".

Stage 3: A1Z26 Cipher. A = 1, B = 2 ... Z = 26. Decode:

3 8 5 3 11 / 20 8 5 / 19 15 21 18 3 5

Answer: CHECK THE SOURCE. Envelope: "CHECK THE SOURCE".

Stage 4: What Do You Do? A different fake (a voice clip of a real classmate saying something mean) starts going around. Eight response cards. Pick the four best moves:

DO: Do not forward it. · SAVE the words and links (not the clip itself). · TELL a trusted adult. · REPORT it in the app.
DON'T: Forward it "so people know it's fake." · Confront the poster in the chat. · Delete everything. · Post a mean reply.

Put the four DO cards in order: STOP (don't forward), SAVE, TELL, REPORT.
Answer: S-S-T-R. Envelope: "SSTR".

Door Phrase: "Check the source."

Hints: Stage 1: "Which clues are about how real it looks, and which are about where it came from?" Stage 2: "Compare the times and the account ages." Stage 3: "A=1, so 3 is C." Stage 4: "What is the first thing you do? Do nothing yet. That's the Pause."

Debrief: (1) Why is "it sounds real" weak evidence? (2) What is a second channel you could use? (3) What do you do when a fake targets a classmate? (4) Where do you report?

Teacher note: Do not show or create any real synthetic media in class. If a student brings up fake sexual images, say "we handle that in a different lesson, with supports" and refer to the counselor per your guide.

Mission S1: The Case of the Fake Giveaway#

Mission S1: The Case of the Fake Giveaway
Theme Fake giveaway (social engineering)
Lesson link 9-12 L3 Social Engineering Case Lab
Learning goal Students apply FLAGS to a DM, recognize lookalike domains, evaluate an over-broad permissions screen, order the Oops path, and decode a Vigenère cipher.
Time 10 min: Stage 1 + Stage 2. 30 min: all
Materials DM card, URL cards, consent-screen card, Oops cards, cipher card with tabula recta (or a key strip), 5 envelopes

Story.

A "streamer" account DMs hundreds of students: "You're one of our supporters. Claim your prize." You've been asked to analyze it for the student council's safety newsletter. The Quiet Door opens when your analysis is complete. (Names are pretend.)

Stage 1: FLAGS. Read the DM:

From: StreamSquad_Official: Hey! You were picked for our $500 supporter giveaway! Verify your account at supporter-verify.example/claim to get your prize. 30 minutes left!

FLAGS = Fast, Login ask, Amazing offer, Gotta keep it secret, Strange sender or link. Which FLAGS letter is missing?
Answer: G (there is no secrecy ask). Envelope: "G".

Stage 2: Lookalike Domains. The real site is streamsquad.example. Six links:

  1. supporter-verify.example/claim
  2. streamsquad.example/help
  3. streamsquad.example.verify-now.example/login
  4. streamsqaud.example/claim
  5. streamsquad.example/news
  6. streamsquad-support.example/login

For this puzzle, only links on exactly streamsquad.example are official. How many links are lookalikes?
Answer: 4 (1, 3, 4, 6). Envelope: "4".
Debrief point: You cannot always tell by looking at a link, so go to the official site yourself instead of using the message's link.

Stage 3: The Consent Screen. The link opens a "Sign in with" screen asking for:

[ ] See your public profile
[ ] See your email address
[ ] Read your private messages
[ ] Send messages as you
[ ] See and manage your friends list
[ ] Post on your behalf
[ ] Stay signed in forever (offline access)

A giveaway needs, at most, your public profile. How many of the permissions are clearly excessive (not needed for a giveaway, and risky)?
Answer: 5 (private messages, send as you, manage friends, post on your behalf, offline access). The email address is borderline. Envelope: "5", which contains the cipher key: PAUSE and the cipher text.

Stage 4: Oops, Then Decode. (a) The team puts six Oops cards in order (each card has a letter on the back; shuffle the cards first): Tell a trusted adult and report the message (S); Revoke the app's permissions and sign out other sessions (E); Change the password on the real site (C); Turn on 2-step or passkeys (U); Warn friends if messages were sent as you (R); Watch for new sign-in alerts (E). The backs read SECURE when the order is right, so the team can check itself. (b) The team then decodes a Vigenère cipher with key PAUSE. (Each letter of the key tells how many steps to shift each plaintext letter back: P = 15, A = 0, U = 20, S = 18, E = 4. Repeat the key across letters and skip spaces and the key continues.)

KELAJN OH S WTCIFH RHUFRTL

Answer: VERIFY ON A SECOND CHANNEL. Door Phrase.
Oops order (the answer): tell and report; revoke permissions and sign out; change the password on the real site; turn on 2-step or passkeys; warn friends; watch alerts. The envelope labeled "SECURE" holds the final Door Phrase card. [VERIFY with the L2/L3 Oops path wording: this order puts Tell first, as the kit's Oops path does. Other orders are defensible in real life, so the Door Keeper explains that this puzzle uses the kit's order. The first draft left the order open, so the answer was not unique.]

Door Phrase: "Verify on a second channel."

Hints: Stage 1: "Write out F, L, A, G, S and check each against the message." Stage 2: "Compare every character." Stage 3: "What data does a giveaway need?" Stage 4: "Key letter P: shift back 15, so K becomes V."

Debrief: (1) Which FLAGS were the loudest? (2) Why does "who gains if I act now" matter? (3) What is the second channel for this DM? (4) What is your Oops path? (5) Which permission would you refuse?

Mission S2: The Credential Vault#

Mission S2: The Credential Vault
Theme Password vault
Lesson link 9-12 L2 Credentials and Passkeys
Learning goal Students develop intuition about password strength, understand reuse blast radius, rank sign-in methods, and assemble a recovery kit.
Time 10 min: Stage 1 + Stage 2. 30 min: all
Materials Combination cards, account grid, 4 sign-in cards, 8 recovery-kit cards, Vigenère cipher card, 5 envelopes

Story.

Your cyber-club is locked out of the club's shared vault. The previous treasurer left four puzzles. Solving them reveals how the vault was protected and how to set it up better. The Quiet Door opens at the end. (Pretend scenario.)

Stage 1: Combinations. Rank these from most possible combinations to fewest (assuming truly random choices):

A: 8 random characters from 94 symbols: about 6.1 x 10 to the 15th power
B: 4 random words from a 2,000-word list: 1.6 x 10 to the 13th power
C: 5 random words from a 2,000-word list: 3.2 x 10 to the 16th power
D: 6 random words from a 7,776-word list: about 2.2 x 10 to the 23rd power
E: "P@ssw0rd!" (a common password with swaps): effectively a guess in the first few attempts

Answer: D, C, A, B, E (write as DCABE). [VERIFY the math; also note that people rarely choose truly randomly, so the numbers are best-case; length and randomness are what matter.] Envelope: "DCABE".

Stage 2: Reuse Blast Radius. Five accounts and their passwords (A, B, C are three different passwords):

Mission S2: The Credential Vault, table 2
Account Password
Email A
Shop A
Social A
Game B
School portal C

The Shop site is breached and password A is stolen. Name the one account whose takeover would let someone reset the other accounts, and is also exposed here.
Answer: EMAIL. Envelope: "EMAIL".

Stage 3: Sign-In Ladder. Rank from most to least resistant to phishing. The backs of the cards give a four-digit code:

Passkey or hardware security key (back: 3)
Authenticator app code (back: 1)
Text message code (back: 5)
Password only (back: 7)

Answer: 3157. [VERIFY: passkeys and security keys are generally the most phishing-resistant; app and text codes can still be phished in real time; a text code is better than nothing.] Envelope: "3157".

Stage 4: The Recovery Kit. Eight cards: which belong in a good Recovery Kit? Pick them, count them, then decode the cipher with the key given in the envelope.

IN: Backup codes saved offline in a safe place · A second trusted device or recovery contact · A written list of which accounts use which email (no passwords) · Password-manager emergency access · An up-to-date recovery phone number or email
OUT: Passwords on a sticky note on the monitor · A screenshot of backup codes in the photo gallery · Giving a friend your codes "for safekeeping"

Answer: 5 items IN. The envelope "5" contains key: TELL and the cipher text:

IEDDDIJ MXEED IEDDPSCO

Decode with Vigenère key TELL (T = 19, E = 4, L = 11, L = 11; shift back; the key repeats and skips spaces): PASSKEY BEATS PASSWORD [VERIFY: "usually, against phishing"].

Door Phrase: "Passkey beats password." (Teacher adds: usually, and only if you set up recovery.)

Hints: Stage 1: "More words beat more symbols if the list is large. Compare exponents." Stage 2: "Which account sends reset links?" Stage 3: "Which method is tied to the real site?" Stage 4: "Would a thief benefit from this item?"

Debrief: (1) What is the cheapest step that makes you safer? (2) Which account is your "keys to the kingdom"? (3) What would your recovery kit hold? (4) Who is your person to tell?

Mission S3: The Bridge That Wasn't Closing#

Mission S3: The Bridge That Wasn't Closing
Theme The deepfake detective
Lesson link 9-12 L6 Synthetic Media and Truth
Learning goal Students separate weak evidence (looks real) from stronger evidence (source, corroboration, provenance), trace a post to its origin, choose responsible sharing behavior, and decode a cipher.
Time 10 min: Stage 1 + Stage 2. 30 min: all
Materials 8 evidence cards, source table, 8 action cards, Atbash cipher card, 5 envelopes

Story.

A video circulates of the (pretend) mayor of Maplewood announcing, "The Maple Bridge closes tomorrow for 3 months." Commuters are panicking. The mayor's office says nothing. You are the school media club, deciding what to do before you share anything. The Quiet Door opens when the club has a plan. (All names are pretend.)

Stage 1: Evidence Strength. Eight statements. Which are strong evidence? Count them.

  1. The audio sounds perfectly like the mayor. (weak)
  2. His mouth movements match the words. (weak)
  3. It has been shared thousands of times. (weak)
  4. The video is high resolution. (weak)
  5. The town's official website and channel say nothing about a closure. (STRONG)
  6. No local news outlet reports it. (STRONG)
  7. The first uploader is an account created two days ago with no history. (STRONG)
  8. Provenance data (such as content credentials) is missing or shows the video was generated or edited [VERIFY the state of provenance standards such as C2PA in your region]. (STRONG, with caveat)

Answer: 4. Envelope: "4".

Stage 2: Lateral Reading. A table of sources:

maplewood_gazette 22:15 Local paper, 20 years: "We are investigating"

commuter_carl 21:55 Personal: "Is this real?"
news_now_xyz 21:30 New site: "Mayor announces..." (copy)

Who posted first? Write the time without the colon.

Stage 3: What Do You Do? Eight cards. Choose the responsible ones and count them.

DON'T: Forward "just in case" · Quote-share it with outrage · Dunk on the person who shared it

Door Phrase: "Trust the source, not the pixels." (Teacher: today's pixels are easy to fake; sources and corroboration are what we can check [VERIFY].)

Hints: Stage 1: "Which clues are about how real it looks?" Stage 2: "Find the earliest time." Stage 3: "Which actions slow the spread and help truth?" Stage 4: "A is Z, so G is T."

Debrief: (1) Why is "looks real" no longer enough? (2) What would you check first? (3) What is the harm in forwarding "just in case"? (4) What is the first thing a newsroom does with a viral clip?

Teacher note: No synthetic sexual content appears in this mission. If students raise it, refer to Lesson 5 and 6 supports and the counselor.

Part G: Make your own mission (defense-only template)#

  1. Pick one skill from a lesson (for example, "check a link").
  2. Write 4 puzzles that each practice one piece of the skill: Notice (find the flag), Check (verify a second way), Tell (practice the sentence), Fix (recover).
  3. Give each puzzle a checkable answer (a number, word or order).
  4. Tie the answers to the Door Phrase (cipher, assembled phrase, or the Hackles routine).
  5. Remove anything that teaches how to cause harm. Ask: "Would a child learn how to trick someone?" If yes, rewrite it.
  6. Test with two students before the class.
