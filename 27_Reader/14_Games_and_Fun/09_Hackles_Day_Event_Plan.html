HACKLES DAY: THE GAMES AND FUN ADD-ON
Twelve stations that turn the whole Games & Fun Pack into a school-wide celebration

Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

1. Naming note: it is "Hackles Day"#

NAMING NOTE. The kit's event is called Hackles Day everywhere. The master event kit is 07_Assessment/Hackles_Day_Kit.md and it remains the master for planning timeline, volunteer roles, parent night, certificates, safety rules and logistics. This document is an add-on that supplies 12 game-based stations and an event flow built on the Games & Fun Pack.

Keep the word "cyber" only where it describes a field or an outside resource (for example CYBER.ORG), and do not rename external organizations. The base kit already uses the Hackles Pledge and chant; keep them. [VERIFY with a final search for the old event name before publication.]

Teacher Quick Start (one page for the event coordinator)#

What it is: a school-wide (or class-sized) celebration with 12 game stations. Groups of 10 to 12 students rotate through the stations in 15-minute slots (12 minutes at the station, 3 minutes walking). Older students are Station Buddies. Every station ends with a Passport stamp.

Pick your version:

Group rule (new): build rotation groups from one grade band (or two neighboring bands) so each station can use the right band's cards. Mix ages only at the opening and closing, and at Stations 4, 9 and 12.

Day-of checklist: counselor visible near Stations 7 and 12; quiet space marked; Pass cards at every station; all (H) cards removed where families opted out; photo list checked; every adult knows: never promise secrecy, never play Gifty if you are a student, thank them and call the counselor.

If...

  • A station runs over: the whistle or bell still ends the slot; groups finish with a Hackles Up and move on. A stamp is still earned.
  • A child is overwhelmed: Calm Corner, Pass card, a Station Buddy walks with them.
  • A child discloses: a runner fetches the counselor; do not promise secrecy; the group continues with the other adult.
  • You are short of adults: use Option B (6 stations) or C (no volunteers).

Principles specific to the games (read with the base kit's Section 1)#

(Reminder from the playtest: group composition is by grade band, Gifty is always an adult, and every station has a Pass card and a quiet exit.)

  1. Everyone plays; nobody is out; nobody loses.
  2. Nobody plays the bad actor. Gifty and any other trickster are played by a trained adult only (puppet or prop) or appear only on cards. Older students may be Station Buddies, but they never play Gifty or any person who causes harm.
  3. Volunteers practice the games first (see the volunteer training mini-session below).
  4. Opt-outs and sensitive material. The stations use the non-sensitive parts of the pack. Remove all (H) cards and any station that touches a sensitive lesson for families who opted out.
  5. A counselor is on site and visible. Quiet space is available.
  6. Participation is always optional. A green Pass card is available at every station.

Floor plan (grid; adapt to your building)#

Think of a gym or cafeteria as a 4 x 3 grid. Put loud stations at one end, quiet stations at the other, and a path wide enough for wheelchairs (about 36 inches or 90 cm, [VERIFY local guidance]).

Floor plan (grid; adapt to your building)

Row 2 8 Bingo Lounge 1 Peeper's Pond 7 Tell-Someone Booth 4 Storytime Tent
Row 3 10 Riddle Café 9 Passport and Badge Studio 11 Passphrase Studio 3 Quiet Door Escape Rooms (side classrooms)

Notes: Put the Hackles Stage and Puppet Theater far from the Calm Corner and Storytime Tent. Put the Tell-Someone Booth near the counselor (visible, not hidden). Put Escape Rooms in separate classrooms so noise does not bother others.

4. Station cards#

Each station card lists: Crew guide, bands, rotation time, group size, adults and Station Buddies needed, materials, set-up, steps, key message, learning goal, accessibility, disclosure note, and stamp. Use the base kit's station-lead briefing for the first 10 minutes of the day.

Disclosure note for every station: If a child says something that worries you, stay calm, thank them, and follow the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Send a runner for the counselor. Do not promise secrecy. No child is required to share a personal experience.

Station 1: Peeper's Pond (Spot the Fishy)#

Station 1: Peeper's Pond (Spot the Fishy)
Crew guide Peeper (sky blue, magnifying glass)
Bands K-12 (use K-2 star cards, K-5 deck or 6-12 deck by group)
Rotation / group 10 to 12 minutes; 6 to 12 children
Adults 1 adult + 1 Station Buddy (older student)
Materials Printed deck, 3 answer cards per team, Pond Mat, 30 pearls, Pass cards, a "Peeper" cutout
Set-up Table or floor with the Pond Mat. Put the deck face down. Post the three answer cards poster.
Steps Play Quick Catch (8 cards). The Station Buddy reads; the adult guides. Name the flags (SPOT or FLAGS). Do the Hackles move after each card.
Key message "Pause and ask is always a safe move. A false alarm is fine."
Learning goal Sort fishy, real, and "can't tell" messages; name clues.
Accessibility Cards read aloud; answers by pointing; large type.
Stamp Peeper's Peek (Passport #6 K-2; SPOT Spotter 3-5; FLAGS Reader 6-12)

5. Rotation plans#

Option A: Half-day, two sessions (the whole school)#

Each group has 10 to 12 students (12 groups means 120 to 144 students per session; larger schools run duplicate copies of popular stations or two parallel rotations) (same-band groups led by a Station Buddy, or classroom-based groups; see Group composition below). Use 12 groups per session. Each slot is 15 minutes: 12 minutes at a station + 3 minutes to move. Run Session 1 (stations 1 to 12, odd or even), a break, then Session 2 (the other half).

Option B: Single-band, 90 minutes#

Choose 6 stations (for example, 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 11). Each group does 4 stations of 15 minutes; 10 minutes opening and 10 minutes closing.

Option C: Classroom mini Hackles Day, 45 minutes (no volunteers)#

  1. Opening (5 min): the Hackles Up Chant and the Pledge.
  2. Three stations (10 min each), all in the classroom: (a) Spot the Fishy Quick Catch (table 1); (b) a Quick Door mission (table 2); (c) Tell-Someone Booth role-plays or Bingo (table 3).
  3. Closing (10 min): Passport stamps, the Big Pause, and a certificate.

Option D: Evening family version (75 minutes)#

Combine with the base kit's Parent Night. Offer stations 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 12 to families together: children teach parents the Hackles routine, parents try Spot the Fishy, and families create a Family Code Word. End with the Pledge.

30-minute volunteer training (use during the base kit's training session)#

  1. Play Spot the Fishy and Hackles Trail for five minutes each (so volunteers feel the games).
  2. The three rules: everyone plays; nobody plays the bad actor; if a child says something that worries you, thank them and call the counselor.
  3. Words that work: "Great noticing!" "A false alarm is fine." "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault."
  4. Practice the Tell sentence with a partner.
  5. Walk through your station card.

Volunteer rules (print and give to each volunteer)#

  • You do not have to be a technology expert.
  • Keep it light. Laugh with, never at.
  • Never ask a child to share a personal story.
  • Never promise secrecy. Promise to help.
  • Never take photos of children unless the school says so.
  • Send a runner for the counselor if you are worried.

8. Accessibility and inclusion#

  • Visit the space with a wheelchair user or a mobility consultant before the day.
  • Provide quiet space and sensory-friendly times (fewer groups).
  • Provide large print, picture cards and visual schedules.
  • Offer sign language for the Opening and Puppet Theater if possible.
  • Translate station signs into the languages of your school community.
  • Provide Pass cards and a no-photo marker for any child who needs one.
  • Do not use flashing lights.
  • Food allergies: check lists before offering snacks.

9. Safety, disclosure, photos and media#

  • Follow the base kit's Section 10 (Safety, disclosure and incident rules, guest speakers, photos and media, physical safety and accessibility, technology).
  • Disclosure plan: a counselor in sight at Stations 7 and 12; a runner to call them from any station; a private space; no promises of secrecy.
  • Photos: only with permission lists; never post a child's name with an image; avoid images of children at the Tell-Someone Booth.
  • Technology: unplugged by design. If a speaker is used, play only instrumental beats.
  • No real accounts, no real passwords, no real names, no real incidents at any station.

10. Closing ritual and awards#

The Big Pause (1 minute). Everyone stands in a circle. Hackles up (neck brush). Pause (palm, breathe). Check (eyes). Tell (hand on heart and reach out). A false alarm is fine. It is never your fault.

Class awards. Hand out the class awards from 07: The Class Pond, The Gold Peak Flag, The Rescue Ribbon, The Quiet Door Key, The Bingo Blackout Banner. Display them in the hall.

Passport certificates. Explorer (3 stamps), Trailblazer (6), Hero (all). Every child receives one.

Tell-Someone Pledge Wall. Every child adds a sticky note with a trusted-adult icon or the word "TELL." The wall stays up for a month.

11. Follow-up and feedback#

  • A one-week check-in: classroom teachers repeat the Hackles Up Chant and reread one read-aloud.
  • Feedback: a short form for volunteers and teachers: Which station did children love? What was confusing? What would you change? [CONTACT: to be added] Do not collect any child data.
  • Optional pre/post "Quick Check" from the Assessment Pack, anonymous and on paper.
  • Next year: teams of students may help design a new station.
