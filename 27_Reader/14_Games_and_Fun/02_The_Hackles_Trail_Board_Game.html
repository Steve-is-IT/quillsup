THE HACKLES TRAIL
A cooperative whole-class board game: the Crew versus the Too-Good Box

Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

A free kit by Competence Collective · competencecollective.org · quillsup.org

No student accounts, no student data collected, ever.

Teacher Quick Start (one page, print and keep)#

What it is: a whole-class board game. The class moves one Crew Token from Home Hollow to Hackles Peak by answering "what would you do?" cards. A silly gold box named Gifty tries to tempt you every few turns. The class always reaches the Peak.

You need: the board (poster paper or floor tape), 1 Crew Token, a clear cup (the Glitter Jar) with 2 pom-poms beside it, a Gifty Coin (any coin, one side marked with a hook), a Class Ladder strip (3 rungs), 10 Rung Tokens, 3 answer cards per team (circle A, triangle B, square C), the card piles, a timer.

Teacher Quick Start (one page, print and keep)
Moment What you do
Setup (3 min) Make 4 to 6 teams. Put the token on START, the jar empty. Shuffle each pile face down. Read the story. Say: "A false alarm is fine. It is never your fault."
A team turn (about 1.4 min) 1) Read the top Event card for the zone the token is in (twice, slowly, with the A/B/C choices). 2) Every team whispers for 20 seconds. 3) Say "Peek!" and all teams show A, B or C at once. 4) The active team's card decides the move: Best = 5 spaces, OK = 4, Oops-path = 3. Nobody ever moves backward for an answer. 5) Read the reasons for all three choices as a story. Everyone says "Nice noticing!" 6) Do the space you land on (see the legend). 7) Next team.
Gifty's turn After every third team turn, and whenever the token lands on a G space: read a Lure card in Gifty's voice. The whole class holds up the Pause Palm and names the flag ("P for Pressure!"). Then flip the Gifty Coin: hook side = a Glitter Slip (a trick sometimes gets through, even when everyone does everything right): add 1 pom-pom to the jar, Crew slips back 2 spaces (the first slip is free), draw a Fixit card. Plain side = Gifty is beaten: cheer.
Sparkle Storm When the jar holds 2 pom-poms it overflows. Everyone stands, jazz hands, then each team says one Help Ladder sentence (the Ladder Rescue). Empty the jar. The class earns a Rescue Ribbon.
Ending The token reaches or passes space 40: Hackles Peak. Do the Big Pause. Gold = 0 Storms, Silver = 1, Bronze = 2 or more. Every tier gets the same cheer and certificate.

Time check: Full Trail is about 30 minutes (9 team turns, 3 Gifty turns, 2 Ladder scenes). Sunrise Wind rule: if the token is not past space 32 when 24 minutes have gone, say "A friendly wind lifts the Crew up the Peak Path!", move the token to space 36, play the last event, and finish. Mini Trail is about 10 minutes (15 spaces).

Say, not "wrong": "Fixit's Oops Path." Never say "wrong" or "you lost".

If...

  • A team wants to pass: "Pass to the Guides": the other teams' majority answer decides and counts as OK.
  • A child looks scared of Gifty: stop, say "Gifty is a silly box with a hook," show the Gifty prop, make a funny noise.
  • A child shares something real: thank them, follow your disclosure box, do not promise secrecy.
  • A card does not fit your class: remove it (H cards for opted-out classes) and draw another.

Never: let a child play Gifty, name real students on cards, or let a team be blamed for the class's score.

1. At a glance#

1. At a glance
Players A whole class (up to 30) in 4 to 6 Crew Teams. Also plays with a small group of 3 to 6 at a table.
Time 10-minute "Mini Trail" (about 10 minutes) or 30-minute "Full Trail" (about 28 to 30 minutes; the Sunrise Wind rule guarantees you finish). An optional 45-minute "Epic Trail" can be split over two days.
Ages K-12. One board, four card sets: K-2 (read-aloud), 3-5, 6-8, 9-12.
Style 100 percent cooperative. One shared Crew Token climbs from Home Hollow to Hackles Peak. The class plays against a silly trickster, not against each other. The class cannot lose; it earns a Gold, Silver or Bronze Peak (all three are celebrated).
Needs A board (poster paper or floor tape), 1 Crew Token, a Glitter Jar (clear cup + 2 pom-poms), a Gifty Coin, a Class Ladder strip, Rung Tokens, 3 answer cards per team, printed cards, timer
Strands All six: Lock It, Spot It, Share Smart, Safe People, Kind & Well, Fix It

The learning goal#

Players will practice the Hackles routine (Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.) in many different situations, practice saying the Tell sentence out loud and climbing the Help Ladder (including the Next-Rung Rule: if the first person does not help, climb to the next rung), and learn that recovering from a mistake is a normal, shame-free part of the journey. In grades 6-12 they also practice STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT and "second channel" checking.

The story (read this aloud first)#

Teacher (K-5): "The Hackles Crew is going on a hike to the top of Hackles Peak! Latch has the locks. Peeper has the magnifying glass. Pip has the privacy shell. Buddy has the trusted hand. Ember has the warm glow. Fixit has the toolbelt. And Hackle has the quills that stand up when something feels off.
But wait. Do you see that shiny gold box on the path, with the sparkly bow and a teeny hook peeking out? That is the Too-Good Box. Everyone calls him Gifty. Gifty LOVES to offer you things that are too good to be true. 'Free coins! Free ponies! Free everything! Just tap and keep it a secret!' He is not scary. He is just very, very silly, and he wants to be your friend. But we know the trick. Everything Gifty offers has a hook!
Your job: get the whole Crew to the top of Hackles Peak. Every time something feels off, Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell. Ready to hike?"

Teacher (6-12): "This is a cooperative game. You are a team of hikers working through situations you might meet. Gifty, the Too-Good-To-Be-True offer, shows up now and then. He is a symbol, not a person. He is not scary. He is just persistent. We will use Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell, plus the Help Ladder, and you will decide as a class what to do. You cannot lose. You can only earn a Bronze, Silver or Gold Peak depending on how many times the glitter storm hits us."

2. Components#

2.1 The Board (build it in 15 minutes)#

Build: Use 4 sheets of poster paper taped together, or tape a path on the floor. Draw 40 circles in a winding path divided into 8 zones. Color each zone with its Crew color. Put a big gold star at the end (Hackles Peak). Add decorations: Latch's pine trees, Peeper's pond with a hook-fish, Pip's meadow with shells, Buddy's forest with a heart-shaped trail sign, Ember's glowing cove, Fixit's bridge with a toolbelt, Hackles Peak with a big quill flag. (See the Art Style Guide: thick outlines, flat bright colors, big shapes, no scary imagery.)

Code Name What happens

3. Rules#

Setup#

  1. Divide the class into 4 to 6 Crew Teams. Teams pick a Crew name (Latch Locks, Peeper Pals, Pip Pods, Buddy Bunch, Ember Krew, Fixit Fixers, or a name the team invents) and write it on a tent card. Hand each team its A, B and C answer cards.
  2. Place the Crew Token on START. Put the Glitter Jar (empty) and the Gifty Coin by the board. Put the Class Ladder where all can see it. Put the Rung Tokens in a "Rung Basket."
  3. Shuffle each card set into its own pile: Events (sort by zone: 3 cards per zone), Lures, Ladder Scenarios, Helpers, Fixit. Place face down.
  4. Read the story. Say the two promises: "A false alarm is fine. It is never your fault."
  5. The teacher is the Reader and does Gifty's voice. Later, a student volunteer may read (never forced).

A turn (about 1.4 minutes)#

  1. Draw and read. The Reader draws the top Event card for the zone the token is in (any card if that zone's three cards are used up) and reads it aloud twice, slowly, saying "A, circle... B, triangle... C, square...".
  2. Everyone whispers. Every team huddles for 20 seconds and chooses A, B or C, and the active team also says which Hackles step it used (Hackles up? Pause? Check? Tell?). No shouting out. (This is what stops 4 teams from waiting while 1 plays.)
  3. Peek! On "Peek!" all teams hold up A, B or C at the same moment.
  4. The active team's card decides the move (the team whose turn it is, rotating clockwise). The other teams are the Trail Guides: their cards are practice, and the Reader says "Guides, who spotted it?" with a cheer for every correct Guide.
  5. Reveal. The Reader reads the Best, OK and Oops-path results as a short story about all three choices, never "you were wrong". Then the Crew Token moves:
    • Best choice: move 5 spaces. Also take one Rung Token if the best choice was a Tell.
    • OK choice: move 4 spaces.
    • Oops-path choice: move 3 spaces and draw a Fixit card ("Oops Is Okay"). Everyone is fine. The Fixit card is a gift.
    • Pass to the Guides: a team may say "Pass to the Guides." The Guides' most common card decides, and it counts as an OK move (4). No questions asked.
  6. Land. If the token lands on a special space (L, H, B, G), do its action. E spaces: one quick Hackles Up signal and nothing else.
  7. Rotate to the next team.

The rule that makes this safe: Nobody moves backward for an answer. An Oops-path choice still moves forward. The only way the Crew slips back is a Glitter Slip (below), and that happens to the whole class by the flip of a coin, never because of one team.

Gifty's turn (after every third team turn, and when the token lands on a G space)#

  1. The Reader draws a Gifty Lure card and does Gifty's voice (see Section 8). (6-12: reads the "Today's Too-Good Offer" card in a plain voice.)
  2. The class always resists. Everyone shows the Pause Palm and says "Hackles up!" and names the flag ("S for Secret!" / FLAGS). 6-12: name two flags and a second channel.
  3. Flip the Gifty Coin.
    • Plain star side: Gifty is beaten. Cheer. Nothing else happens.
    • Hook side: a Glitter Slip. Even when everyone does everything right, a clever trick can sometimes get through. That is true in real life, and it is never anyone's fault. Put 1 pom-pom in the Glitter Jar. The Crew Token takes a Detour: back 2 spaces ("slipped on glitter"), except that the first Glitter Slip of the game is free (no Detour). Draw a Fixit card.
  4. Undo a Detour. The class may spend one Rung Token and say a Tell sentence aloud to cancel a Detour ("Fixit says: we told someone, and the glitter was swept away!").
    (Why a coin and not a class vote? In the first draft the class "voted" to be fooled or not, and every class simply resisted every time: in 1,000 simulated games Gold Peak happened 999 times, so the Glitter Meter, Detours and Fixit cards never mattered. A tempted child could also choose "Tap" on purpose for fun. The coin gives the Storm a real chance and teaches a true lesson: tricks sometimes get through, so we have a Ladder.)

The Glitter Jar and the Sparkle Storm#

The Glitter Jar holds 2 pom-poms. When the second one goes in, the jar overflows and Gifty throws a Sparkle Storm: a silly glitter blizzard. The whole class stands, does jazz hands, and then does the Ladder Rescue: each Crew Team says one Help Ladder sentence aloud (a friend, a trusted adult, a report). Empty the jar. The token does not move back. The class earns a Rescue Ribbon. Rescue Ribbons count for scoring. They are not shameful. They show the class used the Ladder. B spaces, the Hackle's Quills Helper and the Wild Pause Helper each take 1 pom-pom out of the jar.

The Class Ladder#

Each L space asks the class to climb a rung (friend, trusted adult, reporting channel) and say the sentence. When all 3 rungs are filled, the class earns a Ladder Wish: choose one: (1) empty the Glitter Jar, (2) skip ahead 3 spaces, (3) draw 2 Helper cards. Then clear the strip for another go.

Winning: the Peak Tiers#

When the Crew Token lands on or passes space 40, the class reaches Hackles Peak. All tiers are wins.

Sunrise Wind (keeps you on time)#

If the token is not past space 32 when 24 minutes of play have gone, the Reader says "A friendly wind lifts the Crew up the Peak Path!" and moves the token to space 36. Play one last Event, then finish.

The Big Pause (end-of-game ritual, 1 minute)#

  1. Everyone does the Hackles Up signal (spikes!).
  2. Everyone does the Pause Palm and takes a slow breath.
  3. Everyone does Check Eyes.
  4. Everyone does the Tell Wave.
  5. The Reader (as Gifty) gives a grumpy-then-giggly speech. Examples: "Fine, fine, you win. But I'd like it noted that my gift was very shiny." "I just wanted to give you something... with a hook. Okay, that's not a good plan." "Honestly? The best present is 'Ask a trusted adult.' I'm going to go put on a party hat."
  6. Team announcements: each team says one thing they will remember.

4. The Help Ladder on the Trail (the heart of the game)#

The Help Ladder in this kit: Rung 1: You (Pause). Rung 2: A friend who can come with you. Rung 3: A trusted adult (parent or caregiver, relative, teacher, counselor, coach, nurse). Rung 4: A reporting channel (the platform's report tool, school staff, a helpline, NCMEC CyberTipline for serious cases [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: report.cybertip.org]). The Next-Rung Rule: if the first person you tell does not help, you are not done. Keep climbing until someone helps.

  • K-2: teach "Trusted Hand" (five fingers = up to five trusted grown-ups; some children will have fewer, and that is fine) and only use Rung 3. Ladder cards for K-2 say: "Tell a trusted grown-up" and the sentence starter.
  • 3-5: teach the three climbing rungs (friend, trusted adult, report). The Trail adds "Rung 1: Pause" as the ground. Use the 3-5 sentence starters: "I need help with something online." "Something happened and I'm not sure what to do." "I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something."
  • 6-8: teach all four rungs and the Tell-3 script.
  • 9-12: the 9-12 Teacher Guide teaches three rungs (friend, trusted adult, formal report) with Pause as the ground; the Trail treats that as the four-rung strip. Teens can start at any rung and may skip up a rung.
  • Numbering note (rung numbers differ by guide). The Trail numbers the rungs 1 You (Pause), 2 Friend, 3 Trusted adult, 4 Report. The 3-5 and 9-12 guides count only the climbing rungs (1 Friend, 2 Trusted adult, 3 Report); the 6-8 guide matches the Trail. When you play with a 3-5 or 9-12 class, say the rung by name, not by number ("tell a trusted adult"), so nobody is confused. See the Help Ladder entry in 11_Teacher_Academy/Academy_Glossary.md.

Good-grown-up reply (the Listener's line when practicing): "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."

5. Gifty: how he works (never scary)#

  • Gifty is a cardboard (or drawn) gold box with a sparkly bow and a tiny hook. He is not alive in the scary sense. He is a symbol of every offer that is too good to be true. In the games he is always silly, a little bit vain, and defeated by a calm pause. He is a box with a hook, not a sad stranger who just needs a friend: real tricky messages are never sorry, so we always Pause.
  • The teacher plays Gifty in a goofy voice (a game-show host with a cold, a dramatic opera singer, a very polite pirate). Students do not play Gifty; nobody practices tricking.
  • Gifty never takes anything away from the class. The worst he can do is make the Crew slip on glitter (a Glitter Slip), and the Crew always gets up again.
  • Gifty is always beaten by a Pause. Make sure the class knows Gifty's catchphrase: "Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell. Aw, shucks!"
  • For 6-12: Gifty appears as a small icon on cards labeled "Today's Too-Good Offer." No voices. The class names flags with the FLAGS list.

Some Gifty lines the teacher can use (mix and match)#

  • "Hello, friend! I have a free thing! It's free! Totally free! (It has a hook. A very small hook.)"
  • "I would never trick you. I'm a gift box. I'm wrapped!"
  • "Only 3 seconds left! 3... 2... 2.5... Okay, take your time."
  • "I'm not saying you're special. I'm saying you're EXTRA special. Give me your password."
  • "Everybody wins! Everybody who gives me their address. Well, 'wins' is a strong word."
  • "Don't tell your grown-up. They will want a share of my prize. (They won't. There isn't one. Hmm.)"
  • "Aw, shucks. You Paused. Again."

6. 10-minute "Mini Trail" and 30-minute "Full Trail"#

10-minute Mini Trail (a warm-up, station or recess game)#

  1. Use three zones only: Fishy Pond (11-15), Share Meadow (16-20), Fix-It Bridge (31-35). Lay them end to end as a 15-space trail. START is before 11 and FINISH is after 35. Treat the H space (14) as a B (Breathe) space, because Helper cards are skipped.
  2. Pull 6 Events (any three zones), 3 Lures, 2 Ladder Scenarios. Skip Helper cards.
  3. The Glitter Jar holds 2 pom-poms. Gifty's turn after every second team turn. Moves are the same as the Full Trail (Best 5, OK 4, Oops-path 3).
  4. Tell the story in 3 sentences. About 4 team turns, 2 Gifty turns and 1 Ladder scene (1,000 simulated games: about 4 turns; about 9 minutes of play plus a 1-minute Big Pause).
  5. Peak Tiers: Gold 0 Storms, Silver 1. (In simulation about 94 games in 100 are Gold.)
  6. End with the Big Pause.

30-minute Full Trail#

Use the full board, 40 spaces, all card types. Expect about 9 team turns, 3 Gifty turns, 2 Ladder scenes (1,000 simulated games: mean 9 team turns, finishing in 26 to 33 minutes with an 8-minute story and ritual; the Sunrise Wind rule trims the long tail). Budget:

Minutes What
0 to 4 Story, promises, teams, Hackles routine practice
4 to 26 Play (about 1.4 minutes per team turn, 1 minute per Gifty turn, 2 minutes per Ladder scene)

45-minute Epic Trail (optional)#

Play the full trail, then continue to the Peak Challenge: each team gets one "Bonus Peak Question" from Hackles Peak (any E card unused) and the class creates a 3-line Crew Promise ("We promise to Pause, to Check, to Tell, and to be kind about mistakes").

7. Trail Event cards#

Each card reads: Situation. A, B, C. Best, OK, Oops-path (and why). (The Oops-path is the choice that leads to Fixit's help; the Reader never calls it "wrong".) A teacher may print them as cards or read directly from this list. Option letters are spread out so the best answer is not always B (Best answers are about one third each A, B and C in each deck; the first draft had B as the best answer in 16 of 24 K-5 cards and 23 of 24 6-12 cards, which a skeptical player spots in about two cards). Also watch the length: the Best answer is often the longest, so read all three options in the same voice and speed.

Best: B. OK: A. Oops-path: C.

Zone 8: Hackles Peak (all)

F22. A video shows your teacher announcing a snow day. It sounds right.
A) Share it. B) Check the school's official channel. Do not share until confirmed. C) Ignore it.
Best: B. OK: C. Oops-path: A.

F23 (H). A message says: "I will post your private chats unless you send me $30 in gift cards. You have 1 hour."
A) STOP (do not pay, do not reply), SAVE (words, links), TELL a trusted adult, REPORT. B) Delete the chat. C) Pay.
Best: A. OK: none. Oops-path: C and B (deleting evidence makes it harder to help). It is never your fault.

F24. The Peak: pick one of these for your own Defense Plan.
A) Turn on 2-step verification on one account. B) Know one person you will tell and one sentence to say. C) Both.
Best: C. OK: A or B. Oops-path: none. Say them out loud to a partner.

12. Variants for each grade band#

K-2 "Trail Walk" (ages 5-7)#

  • Floor board: tape 20 spaces on the floor in a winding line (use 5 zones: Home Hollow, Password Pines, Fishy Pond, Share Meadow, Fix-It Bridge) with colored paper plates that also carry a picture (a lock, a magnifier, a shell, a bridge). One child per turn is Hackle's Walker and carries the Crew Token plush. Walkers always get a turn; rotate gently, with passing allowed. Children who cannot walk the path can send the plush with a "Walker's Helper" or move it on a tabletop copy.
  • Cards: use the K-2 subset (12 Events). The teacher reads the situation and the three choices with hand signals (A = Hackles Up, B = Pause Palm, C = Tell Wave) or pictures on paper. Everyone answers with the signal at the same moment.
  • No counting: the Walker always takes 3 steps, whatever the answer. The teacher never says "wrong". If the answer was the Oops-path, the teacher says "Let's ask Fixit," and the class does the Tell Wave.
  • Gifty: a puppet or a cardboard box with a ribbon. Gifty only asks silly things. The class answers "Pause Palm!" in unison. The teacher says "Gifty never wins!" K-2 has no Detours and no Glitter Slips: every Gifty turn is a cheer.
  • Glitter Jar: skip it for K-2 (no Storm). Add a pom-pom to a "Hackles Jar" for every cheer, and shake it at the Peak.
  • Ladder: use the Trusted Hand: up to five fingers, five trusted grown-ups.
  • Time: 20 spaces at 3 steps is about 7 turns, about 10 minutes. Two sessions of Mini Trail make a 30-minute lesson.
  • Lessons: L1, L2, L3, L4.

3-5 "Hands-On Hackers Trail" (ages 8-10)#

  • Full rules. Teams pick Crew names from the Hands-On Hackers characters (Mateo's Codebreakers, Amara's Gamers, Priya's Detectives, Kenji's Tellers, Sam's Artists).
  • Bonus clue: if the active team names the SPOT letter on a Fishy Pond event, the Crew takes +1 step.
  • Role cards: Reader, Navigator (moves token), Reporter (announces), Timekeeper. Rotate every few turns so every child gets a job.
  • Lessons: L1, L2, L3, L6, L10.

6-8 "Case File Trail" (ages 11-13)#

  • Use the 6-12 Event cards; remove (H) cards if needed.
  • Name the rung and the signal: every answer must include "Hackles up? because ..." and "I would tell ..." to earn a Best.
  • Double Check rule: for every Best answer, the team must name a second channel.
  • Evidence tokens: a class "Evidence Folder" collects SAVE details (what to save: words, links, usernames, dates, but not private images). Teams add a note on a sticky.
  • Lessons: L1, L2, L3, L7 (with H cards), L9.

9-12 "Trail Briefing" (ages 14-18)#

  • Use the 6-12 Events. Before choosing A, B or C, teams must debate for 45 seconds: "What is the risk? Who benefits? Which answer is easy to do at 1 a.m.?"
  • Defense Budget: each team has 3 "Effort Chips" to spend on any Event for a bonus move. Learn that defense has costs and benefits; the best protection is often cheap (2-step, a code word, a person to tell).
  • Peak Capstone: at Hackles Peak each team writes one line of their personal Defense Plan (Lesson 10): "I will turn on 2-step on _. My person to tell is _. My sentence is ____." (They keep it private.)
  • Lessons: L1, L2, L3, L8, L10.

13. Teacher notes#

Why a trickster?#

Kids love a villain who loses. A silly trickster means that threats feel manageable and funny, and the protagonist is always the class. But never make Gifty a person: do not dress as a stranger, do not role-play "the person who tricks you". Gifty is a box with a hook. A funny box is a safe way to rehearse a scary-adjacent skill.

Common student reactions#

  • "I want to be Gifty!" Offer a "Gifty Voice Contest" at recess: students invent silly offers a box might make (e.g., "Free socks! They are lonely!"), but they never write or practice real tricks or ask real questions. Keep it absurd.
  • "This is too easy." Move to the next band's cards or add the debate rule.
  • "Is it okay to say the wrong thing?" Yes. An Oops-path answer still moves you forward and brings a Fixit gift.

Cautions#

  • Do not use real students' accounts, names, or incidents as event cards.
  • Remove (H) cards and L7/L8 events if families opted out of sensitive lessons.
  • If a student seems triggered (shaky voice, wants to leave), allow a Pass, use the quiet corner, and follow the disclosure box in your guide.
  • Always end on the Big Pause so the group returns to calm.

Debrief questions (3 minutes)#

  1. Which event was the trickiest? What made you hesitate?
  2. What was your favorite Pause? What was your favorite Tell?
  3. Name one flag that Gifty uses a lot.
  4. Who is one trusted adult you could tell? What is one sentence you would say? (Say it to your neighbor.)

14. Accessibility variants
Need What to do
Color-vision difference Zones are named and carry a picture and a symbol, not just a color. Answer cards are circle / triangle / square with big letters and different notched corners. Pass cards say PASS and have a check mark. The Glitter Jar and Gifty Coin are told apart by shape and word.
Low vision / blind Floor-tape board with textured tape for zones; a plush or heavy Crew Token you can feel; large print cards (at least 24 pt); read every card twice. The coin has a raised hook side (a dab of glue) so it can be felt.

English language learners A helper repeats the card in the home language; answers by card or signal; allow a team to discuss in any language. Gifty's catchphrases are chants, so repeat them together.
Motor / mobility 36-inch (about 90 cm) paths if the board is on the floor; a tabletop copy; a helper moves the token for any child. Answer cards on a stand or velcro.
Sensory / noise Whisper huddles; waving hands instead of cheers; Gifty's voice kept gentle; a quiet corner and Pass cards. The Sparkle Storm can be done silently (jazz hands only).
Anxiety about being wrong or being the center Every team answers every card; the active team decides but can say "Pass to the Guides"; the Oops-path is read as a story, not a grade; the coin flip (not a person) causes any slip.
K-2 / developmental differences Use the Trail Walk variant: no counting, no Detours, signals instead of letters.
