Locked template used in every draft#

Header table, standards, materials, Hook (2 min), Activity (8 min), Discuss (3 min), Takeaway plus exit ticket (2 min) = 15 minutes. Then the disclosure box, counselor heads-up and opt-out note, Differentiate, family card with reply slip, and teacher notes. The routine is Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell. with the Help Ladder (yourself, a friend, a trusted adult, a report). No images, no explicit content, no graphic detail, no real cases, no personal stories requested. "Pass" is always allowed; Pass cards and the Quiet Corner stay in place. [Kit rules, 03_6-8 and 04_9-12 Teacher Guides]

Guide cross-reference: FLDOE's "A Student's Guide to Internet Safety" (May 2025)#

FLDOE's own student guide is organised around the same six components and lists what students "learn how to" do and "skills to practice" for grades 6 through 12 (pp.5-16). It is a supplementary home-use resource that "does not constitute complete instruction" (p.4). Statements about the guide in this folder were read in the PDF [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: FLDOE Student's Guide to Internet Safety, May 2025 PDF]; page numbers are the guide's. The table says which guide statements each draft satisfies, fully or partly, and what vocabulary was added to the draft after reading the guide (see the "Guide vocabulary add-on" at the end of each lesson). Nothing here weakens a safeguard, and nothing here claims FLDOE approval of Hackles.

NEEDS EXPERT REVIEW BY A TRAFFICKING-PREVENTION SPECIALIST BEFORE USE. Also requires counselor heads-up, family pre-notice and an alternate activity (below).

Header#

Header
Field Detail
Grade band 6-8 (ages 11-13)
Strand D, Safe People (with B, Spot It)
Statute component C5d: identify human trafficking on the Internet (with C6 reporting)
Big idea Some people use tricks, pressure and control to make others work or be used for their benefit. Online, it can start with an offer that is too good or a "friend" who is too helpful. You can spot the flags, say no, and tell. It is never your fault.

Other standards (approximate) ISTE 2b Digital Citizen. CSTA 2-IC-20. CASEL Relationship Skills; Responsible Decision-Making. [VERIFY]

READ THIS BEFORE YOU TEACH FL-1

This lesson names human trafficking. It contains no explicit content, no images, no stories of real people and no description of what happens to victims. Students practice noticing warning signs in made-up offers and saying who they would tell. Do not teach it until every item below is done.

  • Specialist review complete (see banner). Counselor review complete.
  • Family Pre-Notice letter and opt-out slip sent at least 5 school days ahead (template in District_Adoption_Guide.md).
  • Counselor Heads-Up checklist done (same checklist as 6-8 Appendix B), including a check on known incidents in this class or school. If any exist, decide with the counselor and administrator whether to teach, delay or use the Alternate Activity for the class.
  • Know your reporting duty and route: Florida Abuse Hotline 1-800-962-2873; Florida Human Trafficking Hotline 1-855-FLA-SAFE; 911 for danger. Have the Reporting Quick Card in hand.
  • Teach mid-week, not last period, not before a break, test or major event. Not on the same day as 6-8 Lessons 6, 7 or 8.
  • Never show a real image, a real ad or a real profile. Never ask for a personal story.

Materials#

Unplugged default: Offer Cards (six, in sets for groups of 4-5), Flag Strips, Control Signs card, Reporting Quick Card (RFL-1), Pass cards, Quiet Corner, pencils. Optional tech version: none.

Hook (2 min)#

SAY (noticing step): "Hackles up? When an offer online feels too good, or someone says 'keep this between us,' notice that feeling. Trust it, even if you cannot say why. Nothing here has to be decided in the next five minutes."

SAY: "Today's file is about a made-up kid, Mika. Every person and message in today's lesson is invented. Nobody here has to share anything personal, and you can use your Pass card any time."

SAY: "Here is a plain fact. Human trafficking is when someone uses tricks, pressure or threats to control another person and make them work or be used for someone else's gain, when that person has not freely agreed. It can happen to kids, teens and adults. It is a crime in Florida. Most of what we learn today is about the early warning signs, because early is when you can still walk away, and when telling helps the most."

(Teacher note: the definition is a plain-language paraphrase of the Legislature's finding that victims are "subjected to force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of sexual exploitation or forced labor." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 787.06(1)(a), leg.state.fl.us] Do not add detail about sexual exploitation at this grade. Specialist to confirm wording.)

Activity (8 min)#

Grouping: whole class, then groups of 4-5.

Step 1 (1 min). Read the case file. Read CF-FL1 aloud:

CF-FL1: The Offer
Mika is thirteen. After posting a short video, Mika gets a message from an account that says it is a "talent scout": "You could make real money. No experience needed. Don't tell anyone yet, in case someone steals the spot. I'll cover everything. We just need to chat on a different app." Mika feels flattered and a little uneasy. The scout's profile has few posts. Mika closes the app and wonders what to do.

SAY: "Notice Mika's two feelings: flattered and uneasy. Both are normal. Our job is to listen to the uneasy one."

Step 2 (3 min). Offer Check (groups). Give each group the six Offer Cards and the Flag Strip. Each card is a short, fictional offer (for example: an online job that pays cash up front and says to keep it secret; a new online "friend" who sends gifts and then says "you owe me"; a stranger offering a free ride or a place to stay; an invitation to move to a private chat "so nobody sees"; a club job posted by the school; a babysitting request from a neighbor your family knows). Groups sort each card GREEN (looks fine, check with an adult), YELLOW (check first) or RED (stop and tell), and circle the flags they see:

  • SECRET: "Don't tell anyone."
  • TOO GOOD: big rewards for little effort.
  • MOVE: push to another app or to meet in person, alone.
  • OWE: gifts or favors that turn into a debt.
  • CUT OFF: someone pulls you away from family or friends.
  • TAKES: someone wants to hold your money, phone, documents or accounts.

SAY: "Notice one card is fine. Not every offer is a trick. The skill is asking: who is this, what do they want, and who else knows?"

Step 3 (2 min). Control Signs. Read the Control Signs card aloud. "These are things you might notice about someone who is being controlled, not things anyone is doing wrong. Someone else holds their money, phone or ID. They are told not to talk to family or friends. They owe a debt that keeps growing. They seem scared to say no or to talk about where they go. If you notice these about yourself or a friend, you do not have to figure it out alone." (Teacher note: these reflect techniques the Legislature describes, such as isolating victims from the public and family members, confiscating identification documents, and controlling the victims' funds. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 787.06(1)(c), leg.state.fl.us] Specialist to confirm the age-appropriate list.)

Step 4 (2 min). Say it and report it. Triads rehearse the Tell-3 from 6-8 Lesson 1: (1) "I need your help with something online." (2) One sentence: "Someone I do not know offered me money and said not to tell anyone." (3) "Can you help me figure out what to do?" The Listener answers: "Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble. Let's work it out together."

Then read the Reporting Quick Card (RFL-1): "Most of the time you tell a trusted adult first: a parent or caregiver, a teacher, the school counselor. A trusted adult can call the Florida Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-855-FLA-SAFE, or the Florida Abuse Hotline, 1-800-962-2873. Anyone can make a report. If someone is in danger right now, call 911."

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. Why might someone ignore the uneasy feeling? What made Mika's offer sound good?
  2. Which flag would be easiest to miss? Why?
  3. Why is "keep it a secret" a flag, and what is a safe secret? (Connect to 6-8 Lesson 6: surprise party versus a secret that gets someone hurt.)
  4. If a friend tells you about an offer like this, what could you say and who could you tell?
  5. What if someone already said yes or already did something? (Draw out: still tell. It is not your fault. People who use tricks are the ones responsible.)

Takeaway (2 min)#

SAY: "Pause before you answer an offer that is secret, too good, or pushing you to move. Check: who is this, what do they want, and who else knows? Tell a trusted adult. It is never your fault if someone tried to trick you, and it is never too late to tell."

Exit ticket (about 1 min)#

"Write one flag from today and one adult or helper you could tell." Optional line: "[ ] I would like to talk to the teacher or counselor privately."

If a student discloses

If a student discloses (FL-1: Offers That Don't Add Up)

A student may say that they, a friend or someone in their family is being pressured, controlled, kept from leaving or made to work, or that someone is holding their money, phone or papers.

SAY:

  • "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault, and you are not in trouble."
  • "I am glad you told me. I want to help."
  • "I cannot keep this secret, because I need to bring in people who can help. I will tell you who and why."

DON'T SAY:

  • "Why did you go along with it?" or "Are you sure?"
  • "I promise I will not tell anyone."
  • Do not investigate. Do not ask for details, names or images. "Tell me more" and "What happened next?" are enough. Do not contact the person or the family yourself.

ESCALATE:

  • Move to a private place, within sight of other adults per school rules. If a child is in immediate danger, call 911.
  • Report immediately to the Florida Abuse Hotline, 1-800-962-2873, or to the Florida Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-855-FLA-SAFE (1-855-352-7233), as district procedure directs [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1006.481(1)(d) and FLDOE human-trafficking page; hotline numbers read on DCF and FDLE pages]. School personnel who report to the abuse hotline must give their name [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 39.201(1)(b), leg.state.fl.us].
  • Tell the counselor and administrator per protocol. Document the student's own words, date and time, and who you told.
  • If there is any hint of self-harm, follow your district protocol and use 988 [VERIFY].
  • Do not tell the student's family until the agency or administrator advises; a family member may be involved [VERIFY with specialist and district counsel].

Counselor heads-up and opt-out note#

  • Give the counselor this lesson, the box above and the Offer Cards five school days ahead. Ask about students who may need a private word beforehand (past or current exploitation, an open case, a family involved in the topic) and about any school or community incident.
  • Opt-out: a family may ask that their child do the Alternate Activity ("Offers That Don't Add Up: Job Scams"), which teaches the same flags and the Tell-3 using a non-exploitation scenario (a fake job offer that asks for a fee). The alternate must still teach the C5d and C6 skills so that no student misses the safety message. No reason is asked or stated. In Florida, the statute I read gives parents a legal exemption only for reproductive health and disease instruction (s. 1003.42(5)); an opt-out from this lesson is a district or school policy choice. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us 1003.42(5); VERIFY the district's policy]
  • A student may use the Pass card, Quiet Corner or Step-Out card at any time. Check in privately afterward.

Differentiate#

  • Support: Use only three Offer Cards (one green, one yellow, one red). Provide the Tell-3 as a sentence frame. Pair a student with a buddy.
  • Extension: Students write a three-line "Offer Check" card they could give a younger cousin using the flags.
  • Low or no internet at home: All paper. Offers can also arrive by text, calls or in person; the flags work everywhere.
  • Language access: Provide the Flag Strip and Reporting Quick Card in home languages [VERIFY translation]. The National Human Trafficking Hotline lists service in 200+ languages [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: humantraffickinghotline.org/en/contact]. Do not ask students to interpret for family.

They practiced saying "I need help with something online." At home you might ask: "If someone online offered you something and said not to tell anyone, who would you tell?" If you ever suspect trafficking or abuse, call the Florida Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-855-FLA-SAFE or the Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-962-2873. In danger now: 911. The school counselor is also available.

Reply slip (optional):

  • Checkbox: We talked about who our child can tell.
  • Checkbox: We would like more information or a conversation with the counselor.
  • Preferred language for family messages: ____
  • Student name (optional): ____ Date: __

READ THIS BEFORE YOU TEACH FL-2

Same preparation as FL-1: specialist and counselor review done; family pre-notice and opt-out slip sent 5+ school days ahead; counselor heads-up checklist done; reporting routes known; Alternate Activity ready; mid-week, not last period; not on the same day as 9-12 Lessons 5, 7 or 8. Materials#

Unplugged default: RFL-2 Message Excerpt Cards (one set per group of 3-4), Pattern strip, Helper Card, Reporting Quick Card, Question Box, Pass cards.

Hook (2 min)#

SAY (noticing step): "Hackles up? When an online offer or a new relationship moves fast, asks for secrecy, or makes you feel you owe something, your gut is picking up on something. Name it and ask what it is picking up."

SAY: "Florida law describes trafficking as people being subjected to force, fraud or coercion for sexual exploitation or forced labor, and notes it affects 'young children, teenagers, and adults.' It also says traffickers often use techniques that are less obvious than locks: isolating victims from family, taking identification documents, controlling money, and telling victims they will be arrested or deported if they contact authorities [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 787.06(1)(a) and (c), leg.state.fl.us]. Today is not about shock. It is about the pattern, so that you can spot it early, help someone else, and know where to report."

(Teacher note: Specialist to confirm that this framing is appropriate for 9-12 and add any local context.)

Activity (8 min): Pattern Match#

Groups of 3-4. Each group gets eight fictional Message Excerpt Cards (RFL-2) and the Pattern strip: HOOK (flattery, an opportunity, a "special" relationship), MOVE (shift to a private app, a call, an in-person meeting alone, travel), BIND (secrecy, a favor that becomes a debt, shame, a fee, a threat to tell your family), CONTROL (someone holds your money, phone, documents or accounts; you are cut off from people).

  • Step 1 (1 min): Explain the pattern. Say: "Most real offers are fine. These patterns show up in scams and in exploitation, including both labor and sexual exploitation, and the first steps often look like opportunity or romance. We will practice on made-up messages."
  • Step 2 (4 min): Groups label each excerpt with the stage it shows, circle the exact words that gave it away, and mark GREEN (check it), YELLOW (slow down) or RED (stop; tell). Include two GREEN excerpts (a real internship posted through the school; a family friend's job offer that the family already knows) so students practice discernment, not fear. Examples of RED language (fictional, non-explicit): "You can pay it back by working for me." "Give me your phone and ID, I'll hold them so you don't lose them." "Do not tell your mom; she won't understand." "If you leave, I'll tell everyone."
  • Step 3 (2 min): Say it, help a friend, report it. Pairs use the Helper Card from 9-12 Lesson 8: Hear ("Thank you for telling me."), Help ("It is not your fault. What do you need right now?"), Hand off ("Let's tell [counselor/trusted adult] together."). Then the Reporting Quick Card: "A trusted adult, or you, can call the Florida Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-855-FLA-SAFE (1-855-352-7233), or the National Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-888-373-7888 (24 hours). For a child who may be abused or neglected: Florida Abuse Hotline, 1-800-962-2873. If someone is in danger now: 911."
  • Step 4 (1 min): Name one place in your Defense Plan to add: "If something online asks me to keep a secret, owe a favor or hand over a document, I will ___ and tell ___."

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. Which stage is easiest to miss: HOOK, MOVE, BIND or CONTROL? Why?
  2. Why do secrecy and debt work so well for someone trying to control another person?
  3. How is this different from a job scam? How is it the same? (Draw out: the same early flags; the difference is the control.)
  4. What could a friend say that would help someone who is worried they "agreed" to something?
  5. What makes reporting hard? What would make it easier? (Draw out: it is not your fault; reporting is not tattling.)

Takeaway (2 min)#

SAY: "Pause when an opportunity or a relationship asks for secrecy, speed or something you cannot undo. Check who they are, what they want and who else knows, using a channel you choose. Tell: a trusted adult first, and the hotlines when it is possible trafficking. It is never your fault, and it is not too late to tell."

Exit ticket (about 1 min)#

"Write the stage (HOOK, MOVE, BIND or CONTROL) that would be hardest for you to notice, and one person or number you would use to get help." Optional: "[ ] I would like to talk to the counselor privately."

If a student discloses

If a student discloses (FL-2)

  • Why it might come up: a student may say they, a friend or someone in their family has been pressured to work, owes a debt, has had documents or a phone taken, is being kept from leaving, or has been pushed into sexual activity for someone else's gain.
  • Say: "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble. I want to get you help, and I will tell you exactly who I need to talk to."
  • Don't say: "Why didn't you leave?" "Are you sure?" "You agreed, right?" Do not promise secrecy. Do not ask for images or screenshots. Do not contact the person or the family. Do not investigate.
  • Escalate when: any hint of trafficking, abuse or immediate danger. Call 911 for immediate danger. Teacher preparation for FL-3

  • Teach after 6-8 Lesson 10 (Built to Keep You Scrolling); it builds on the design-features language. Teach mid-week, not last period.
  • Give the counselor a heads-up (5 school days) and know your self-harm protocol. A family note is recommended; formal opt-out is not required but is honored if requested.
  • Use "a character" and "some people." Never ask students to share their own screen time, mood or experiences. Never name specific apps or companies.
  • No self-harm methods or details, no statistics about suicide. Follow your district's safe-messaging guidance [VERIFY district policy].

Materials#

Unplugged default: RFL-3 Signal Cards (one set per group of 4-5), Signal Sort mat, Reset Menu, My Help Ladder card from Lesson 1, pencils. Optional tech version: none.

Hook (2 min)#

SAY (noticing step): "Hackles up? Notice how you feel after you have been on a screen: tired, restless, left out, or fine. That feeling is information. It is never a failure."

SAY: "Today's file is about Pia, who is made up. Here is what many people notice. Social media can be great: you stay in touch, laugh, learn things. It can also affect people in hard ways: late-night scrolling can cut into sleep, comparing yourself with other people's posts can make you feel worse, being left out of a group chat hurts, and sometimes it is hard to stop even when you want to. Florida wants every student to hear about these effects, including the harder ones, so that you can notice them early and get support. Today is about noticing and choosing, not about blame."

Activity (8 min)#

Grouping: whole class, then groups of 4-5.

Step 1 (1 min). Read the case file. Read CF-FL3 aloud:

CF-FL3: Pia's Night
Pia is twelve. Most nights Pia scrolls in bed "for a few minutes." Last night it was two hours. Pia is tired in the morning and snapped at a friend. Looking at other kids' posts makes Pia feel like everyone else has a better life. CF-FL6: The Closing Rumor
Tavi is twelve. A post says, "SCHOOL CLOSED TOMORROW because of a danger. Tell everyone!" It has a scary photo. Friends are forwarding it. Tavi's heart is pounding and Tavi's thumb hovers over "share."

Step 2 (1 min). The Check Strip. Write on the board: WHO made it? PROOF: what shows it is true? WHO ELSE says so (a school, a trusted news source, an official)? FEELING: is it trying to scare, anger or rush me?

Step 3 (4 min). Claim Check (groups). Groups work through four Claim Cards (fictional): a school-closing rumor; a health "miracle cure" from an influencer; a screenshot of a "private message" about a classmate; a chatbot answer stated very confidently. For each: which question to ask first, what would count as good evidence, and the decision: SHARE, CHECK FIRST, DON'T SHARE, or TELL AN ADULT.

Step 4 (2 min). Share Pause and when to tell. Students write their own one-line Share Pause rule: "If I cannot say who made it and who else says so, I do not pass it on." SAY: "If a post is spreading rumors about a person, is scaring people, or is asking for money or personal info, it is not just a wrong fact; tell an adult and report it in the app. If a rumor targets a classmate, treat it like the pile-on in Lesson 9." For AI-made fakes see Lesson 8.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. Why do false posts spread faster when they make us angry or scared?
  2. What is the difference between a mistake and an attempt to trick people? Does it matter if you share either one?
  3. Why might someone with good intentions share something false?
  4. What can you do if a friend shares something false? (Draw out: a private message, not a pile-on.)
  5. Who would you check with? Who could you tell?

Takeaway (2 min)#

SAY: "Pause before you share. Check who made it, the proof and who else says so. Tell an adult if it is hurting someone or scaring people. It is never your fault if you shared something false by mistake; you fix it by telling people and not passing it on."

Exit ticket (about 1 min)#

"Write one question from the Check Strip you will ask before sharing and one person you could check with."

If a student discloses (FL-6)

A student may say a rumor, fake or false post about them or a friend is spreading, or that they shared something that hurt someone.

SAY: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's get help to stop it from spreading."
DON'T SAY: "Why did you share it?" Do not ask to see a harmful image. If it includes an intimate or sexual image of a minor, do not view, copy or forward it.
ESCALATE: counselor and administrator; if the post targets a student, treat as bullying per district policy; if it is a sexual fake of a minor or a threat, follow the kit's Response Drill (STOP, SAVE, TELL, REPORT) and report per policy and Florida law [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 39.201(1); myflfamilies.com]. Self-harm: district protocol and 988 [VERIFY]. Document the student's own words.

Teacher preparation for FL-7

Counselor heads-up recommended (students may disclose being targeted). Use fictional situations only; never ask who has been bullied. Do not use real group chats or screenshots. Teach mid-week.

Hook (2 min)#

SAY (noticing step): "Hackles up? When something online starts as teasing and keeps going, or makes someone go quiet, notice it. You do not need to be sure to pause."

SAY: "Florida law defines bullying and cyberbullying and says that cyberbullying includes bullying through technology or electronic communication [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1006.147, leg.state.fl.us; read in part]. Today we ask four questions that help tell mean from harmful, then practice what to do and where to report."

Activity (8 min): Four Questions and the Response Menu#

  • Step 1 (1 min): Write the Four-Question Strip on the board: REPEATED (has it happened more than once?), HURTFUL (does it humiliate, threaten, exclude or spread private information?), PUBLIC OR HIDDEN (are many people seeing it, or is it anonymous or in a group chat?), POWER (is the target outnumbered, less popular, or targeted for who they are?). Say: "If two or more are yes, treat it as harmful, not as a joke."
  • Step 2 (3 min): Pairs sort eight fictional Situation Cards (for example: a repeated nickname in a group chat; a "joke" account about a classmate; a quote screenshot passed to a new group; a fake profile; an exclusion; one rude comment with no pattern; a threat; a sexual picture being passed around). They mark: ANNOYING, HURTING, TELL NOW and the Four Questions that apply. The last two should land at TELL NOW.
  • Step 3 (3 min): Response Menu. Groups assign each card a first move from the menu: Don't retaliate. SAVE words, usernames and times (never the image). Block or mute. Report in the app. Support the target privately. Tell a trusted adult. Report in school. Read the rule: "If an intimate image of a minor is involved, do not save or forward it. Use the Response Drill from Lesson 5."
  • Step 4 (1 min): Where to report. SAY: "Your school has a process for reporting bullying and harassment under Florida law; know who takes the report [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1006.147 requires a district policy; VERIFY how your school receives reports]. For threats or crimes, local law enforcement. For abuse, the Florida Abuse Hotline, 1-800-962-2873 [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: myflfamilies.com]. In danger: 911."

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. Which question helps most in telling mean from harmful? Why?
  2. Why do bystanders matter? What can one private message do?
  3. Why is "do not retaliate" hard, and why is it a good first rule?
  4. How can a screenshot be used for help and for harm?
  5. What would make you more likely to report? What gets in the way?

Takeaway (2 min)#

SAY: "Pause before you reply or add to the pile. Check the four questions. Tell a trusted adult and report in the app and at school. It is never the target's fault, and saving evidence is for adults who can help, not for spreading."

Exit ticket (about 1 min)#

"Write the first move you would make for a card that was TELL NOW and one person you would tell." Optional: "[ ] I would like to talk to the counselor privately."

If a student discloses

If a student discloses (FL-7)

  • Why it might come up: a student may be, or may have been, a target, a bystander or a participant.
  • Say: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. We will work it out together." For a student who took part: "Thank you for being honest. We can fix this; you can stop and make it right."
  • Don't say: "Just ignore it." "Why didn't you tell sooner?" Do not ask for images.
  • Escalate: threats, sexual images of a minor, self-harm, hate or stalking: same-day counselor and administrator; law enforcement per policy; Florida Abuse Hotline for abuse; district bullying procedure under s. 1006.147 [VERIFY]; self-harm: district protocol and 988 [VERIFY]. Document the student's own words, date and time, and who you told.

Counselor heads-up and opt-out note#

Counselor heads-up recommended; ask about known incidents in the class or school. No formal opt-out is expected; the alternate is the Response Menu applied to two cards individually. Opt-out is district policy; no statutory opt-out for this instruction in the text I read. [VERIFY with the district]

Differentiate#

  • Support: Use four cards and a labeled Response Menu.
  • Extension: Students design a one-page "upstander guide" for younger students.
  • Low or no internet at home: The strip and menu work for texts, calls and in-person bullying too.
  • Language access: Translate the strip and menu [VERIFY].

Family card:

Power?) and practiced what to do: do not retaliate, save words and usernames (not images), block, report, support, tell. If your teen is being targeted, please contact the school counselor or an administrator; schools have a bullying and harassment reporting process. In danger: 911.
