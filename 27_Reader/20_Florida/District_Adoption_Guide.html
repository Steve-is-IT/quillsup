1. What Florida requires, in plain terms#

1. What Florida requires, in plain terms
Topic What I confirmed from primary sources
The instruction For students in grades 6 through 12, instruction on "the social, emotional, and physical effects of social media," which "must include, but need not be limited to" six items: negative effects on mental health including addiction; misinformation; how social media manipulates behavior; permanency of sharing materials online; how to maintain personal security and identify cyberbullying, predatory behavior and human trafficking; how to report suspicious behavior. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(2)(o)5.a., leg.state.fl.us, 2026 Florida Statutes]
Who teaches it "Members of the instructional staff of the public schools, subject to the rules of the State Board of Education and the district school board." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(2)]
Parent notice "The Department of Education shall make available online the instructional material being used pursuant to this subparagraph, and each district school board shall notify parents of its availability." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(2)(o)5.b.] FLDOE's page adds that districts must "ensure that notification is accessible on the district website homepage." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: fldoe.org effects-sm.stml] Note: the statute text I read requires a homepage notice and a link for the reproductive-health exemption in subsection (5); I did not find the word "homepage" in 5.b. itself. The FLDOE page is the stricter reading, so follow it. FLDOE's page cites "Section 1003.42(6)" for this duty; subsection (6) in the statute is the implementation plan, so the citation on that page appears off by a subsection. [VERIFY with FLDOE]
State materials FLDOE publishes an Effects of Social Media Instructional Guide (Word) and "A Student's Guide to Internet Safety" (PDF) at fldoe.org/academics/standards/instructional-materials/effects-sm.stml. The guide is a benchmark-based planning resource, "flexible" and "updated as needed." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: FLDOE page; guide (Word) read in part; the student guide PDF was later read in full. It describes itself as supplementary, home-use material that "does not constitute complete instruction" (p.4), so handing it to students does not deliver the required instruction; see Crosswalk Sections 9 to 11]
Annual plan Each district submits a Required Instruction Implementation Plan by July 1 each year through the Instructional Materials and Library Media District Certifications and Plans Portal (districts.flimadoption.org), on FLDOE's template. The plan must describe (a) how instruction will be delivered at each grade level, (b) the professional qualifications of the person delivering it and (c) the materials and resources used. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: Rule 6A-1.094124(1)-(2), F.A.C., effective 8/25/2026, flrules.org; fldoe.org required-instr.stml]. The statute also requires the plan to be posted on the district website (s. 1003.42(6)) and gives FLDOE 45 days' notice rights if a plan or reported instruction does not meet requirements (s. 1003.42(7)). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us]
Enforcement The State Board may take actions under s. 1008.32(4) if a district fails to comply. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(8)]
Effective date CS/HB 379 (Chapter 2023-36) took effect July 1, 2023. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: enrolled bill, flsenate.gov; laws.flrules.org/2023/36]
Parent opt-out The only parental exemption I found in s. 1003.42 is for "reproductive health or any disease, including HIV/AIDS" instruction (subsection (5)). I found no statutory opt-out for the social media instruction. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(5)] Any opt-out from Hackles lessons is therefore a district or school policy choice. [VERIFY with district counsel]
State approval of materials s. 1003.42(1)(b) requires department approval for materials used to teach reproductive health and disease. I found no equivalent state approval requirement for social media materials. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(1)(b)] Districts may run their own instructional-materials review under s. 1006.283 (reviewers, public access at least 20 calendar days before a board hearing, public meeting, parent notice of local access). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1006.283(2)] Whether a free supplemental kit is "instructional materials" (defined in s. 1006.29(2) as "items having intellectual content that by design serve as a major tool for assisting in the instruction of a subject or course") and must go through that process is for district counsel. [VERIFY]
Content limit "Health education and life skills instruction and materials may not contradict the principles enumerated in subsection (3)." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(2)(o)] Have counsel and reviewers check Hackles against subsection (3). [VERIFY]
Staff training Under s. 1006.481 (2025), schools must require instructional, administrative and educational support personnel who have contact with students to complete human trafficking awareness training and to submit an acknowledgment that the school retains; the training protocol requires reporting suspected trafficking of a child to DCF or the Florida Human Trafficking Hotline. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1006.481, leg.state.fl.us; fldoe.org human-trafficking page]

2. Related Florida laws a district might cite#

Each row says what I read. I did not read case law or litigation status.

2. Related Florida laws a district might cite
Law What I confirmed Relevance to Hackles
s. 1003.02(1)(g)3.-5. (added by HB 379): district Internet safety policy; prohibit student access to social media platforms on district Internet "except when expressly directed by a teacher solely for educational purposes"; prohibit TikTok on district devices and district Internet. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us 1003.02; enrolled bill] District network blocks social media; Hackles lessons are unplugged and need no student accounts or logins, so they fit the policy.
s. 1006.07(2)(f) as amended by HB 379: code of student conduct must prohibit student use of wireless communications devices during instructional time except as directed by a teacher. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: enrolled bill, Section 4] Hackles is paper-based; no device needed.
s. 501.1736 (HB 3, 2024), "Social media use for minors." Social media platforms must prohibit minors younger than 14 from holding accounts (with termination duties) and must not allow 14- and 15-year-olds to hold accounts without parental consent; defines "social media platform" with "addictive features" (infinite scrolling, push notifications, personal interactive metrics, auto-play, live-streaming). Enforced by the Department of Legal Affairs; history: ch. 2024-42 and ch. 2024-54. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 501.1736, leg.state.fl.us] A district may cite it as context; it regulates platforms, not schools. I did not confirm its effective date, enforcement status or any court challenge. [VERIFY]
s. 1006.481 (2025): staff human trafficking training and acknowledgment. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05] Staff teaching the trafficking Plus lessons (6-8 P1, 9-12 P1) should have completed it.
Rule 6A-1.094124, F.A.C. (effective 8/25/2026): annual implementation plan; child trafficking prevention education for K-12 covering signs, resources, prevalence and strategies to reduce risk, healthy boundaries, how to seek assistance safely, and how social media and mobile device applications are used for human trafficking; a minimum of five hours of resiliency education for grades 6-12. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: flrules.org rule text read from the rule's Word file] Strongest Florida hook for the trafficking gap in Hackles, now addressed by Plus Lesson P1 in each band (pending specialist review).
s. 39.201(1) mandatory reporting: "A person is required to report immediately" to the central abuse hotline if he or she "knows, or has reasonable cause to suspect" child abuse; teachers and other school personnel who report must give their name. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us] Every adult in the room, not only teachers, is covered; the kit's disclosure boxes should cite the Florida hotline.
s. 787.06 human trafficking (including legislative findings). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05, read in part] Source of the framing in the trafficking Plus lessons (6-8 P1, 9-12 P1).
s. 1006.147 bullying and harassment prohibited; definition of cyberbullying. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05, read in part] Source for 9-12 Plus Lesson P4.

3. How a district or school can adopt Hackles#

Before anything else, Hackles as written does not cover every statutory component (see 20_Florida/Florida_HB379_Crosswalk.md): human trafficking is a Gap in both bands, mental health and addiction is partial or a Gap, and platform manipulation is a Gap in 9-12. A district that wants to teach the statutory components using Hackles should plan to use the seven draft gap lessons (Gap_Lesson_Stubs.md) after they pass review, or other materials for those components.

Options#

Options
Option How Notes
A. Supplement to FLDOE's guide (suggested) Teachers plan from FLDOE's Instructional Guide benchmarks and use Hackles lessons as ready-made 15-minute activities. List Hackles in the implementation plan as a supplemental resource. Lowest friction. The FLDOE guide is the posted "instructional material" for the parent notice; Hackles supplements it.
B. Adopt through the district materials process Submit to the district's instructional-materials review under s. 1006.283 if the district uses it for supplemental materials. Needed if district counsel says the kit is "instructional materials." Allow time for reviewer selection, 20-day public access and a noticed board hearing. [VERIFY]
C. Pilot One school, one grade, one band for a semester with counselor review and a teacher debrief. Collect no student data. Fits the kit's release gates.

Steps (suggested)#

  1. District counsel reads s. 1003.42, Rule 6A-1.094124, FLDOE's effects-sm page, and this guide.
  2. Instructional-materials office decides whether the kit needs formal review (Option B) or can be a supplement (A).
  3. Counselors review the sensitive lessons (6-8 L6, L7, L8, P1, P2; 9-12 L5, L7, L8, P1, P2) and the opt-out toolkit.
  4. A trafficking-prevention specialist reviews the trafficking Plus lessons (6-8 P1, 9-12 P1); a clinician or counselor reviews the mental-health Plus lessons (6-8 P2, 9-12 P2) before use. The drafts formerly called FL-1 to FL-7 are now the Plus lessons; the map is in Gap_Lesson_Stubs.md.
  5. Superintendent or designee updates the Required Instruction Implementation Plan for the next July 1 submission (Section 5).
  6. District updates its website notice (Section 4.2) and sends the family pre-notice for sensitive lessons.
  7. Staff complete s. 1006.481 training and confirm their reporting routes.
  8. Teach; keep the class-level compliance log (Section 8).

4. Sample language (edit with district counsel)#

4.1 Board memo language (sample)#

Subject: Supplemental resource for required instruction on the effects of social media (grades 6-12)

Background. Section 1003.42(2)(o)5., Florida Statutes, requires instruction for students in grades 6 through 12 on the social, emotional, and physical effects of social media, including the negative effects on mental health (including addiction), misinformation, how social media manipulates behavior, the permanency of sharing materials online, personal security and identifying cyberbullying, predatory behavior and human trafficking, and how to report suspicious behavior. The Department of Education makes instructional materials available online, and the board must notify parents of their availability.

Proposal. Staff recommends using Hackles, a free, unplugged online-safety kit published by the Hackles team (quillsup.org) under a CC BY-NC 4.0 license, as a supplemental classroom resource alongside the Department's Effects of Social Media Instructional Guide. 7. Handling opt-outs#

  • Legal frame. The only parental exemption I found in s. 1003.42 is for reproductive health and disease instruction (subsection (5)). The social media instruction has no statutory exemption in the text I read. Whether and how to allow opt-outs is a district policy decision. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 1003.42(5); VERIFY with counsel]
  • Alternate must teach the component. Because the component is required, an opted-out student should still receive instruction on it. The kit's alternates (for example, 6-8 R7-ALT "Pressure Without Pictures"; 6-8 P1 "Job Scams"; 9-12 P1 "Spot the Scam: Job Offers That Ask for Your ID") teach the same skills using non-sexual, non-exploitation scenarios. Make the alternate available before the lesson.
  • No questions, no penalty. Do not ask for a reason; do not grade it; do not single out a student. Supervise in a library, counselor's office or another room. A student may also step out mid-lesson with a Pass card or Step-Out card.
  • Records. Keep the district's usual signed form in the student's file per district rules; keep Hackles-side counts as numbers only (for example, "3 opted out of Lesson 7") on the class-level log. Do not put names or reasons on the Hackles log. [VERIFY the district's own records rules with counsel]
  • Send the family card to all families, opt-outs included.

9. What NOT to claim
Do not say Why Say instead

"This satisfies the state's requirement" That is a determination for the district and FLDOE. "The district will use Hackles together with ___ to deliver the required instruction."
