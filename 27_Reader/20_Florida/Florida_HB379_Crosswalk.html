1. Bottom line#

1. Bottom line
Question Answer
What does Florida require? Instruction for students in grades 6 through 12 on the social, emotional and physical effects of social media, with six named content components (Section 2). Section 1003.42(2)(o)5.a., Florida Statutes. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us 1003.42, 2026 Florida Statutes]
Does Hackles cover all six components? The core ten lessons alone: no. First pass, nine rows (C5 split into four): grades 6-8 = 5 Full, 3 Partial, 1 Gap; grades 9-12 = 4 Full, 2 Partial, 3 Gap (Section 5). With the Plus lessons and the applied edits: yes on paper. All nine rows are Full for the statute's wording in both bands, but 3 rows in 6-8 and 5 rows in 9-12 rest on Plus lessons that no outside reviewer has seen, including both trafficking lessons, which must not be taught until a specialist signs off. Against FLDOE's own guide, C5a stays Partial in both bands. See Section 5.1.
Biggest gaps in the first pass (each now has a Plus lesson, pending review) (1) Human trafficking recognition and reporting: not taught in either band (one passing mention in a 9-12 teacher box). (2) Negative mental-health effects of social media, including addiction: only a partial treatment in 6-8 Lesson 10, none in 9-12. (3) How platforms manipulate behavior: Full in 6-8 (Lesson 10), not taught in 9-12.
What changed after reading FLDOE's Student's Guide (May 2025)? Two verdicts weaken on the guide's own expectations (6-8 C3 Full to Partial; C5a Full to Partial in both bands) and 14 expectations the first pass missed are listed (Section 9.3). Divergences to handle: evidence screenshots versus never saving intimate images [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM], block-and-report order, school resource officer, the labor-only trafficking definition (Section 10).
Fix (done 2026-10-05) Seven 15-minute Plus lessons built into the Teacher Guides, Workbooks, Reproducibles, Answer Keys, Assessment Pack and Family Pack, plus edits P-1 to P-10 applied to existing lessons (Section 7). Every Plus lesson still needs the review gates before classroom use; the trafficking lessons need review by a trafficking-prevention specialist first, and the mental-health lessons review by a school counselor or clinician.
Is there a Florida approval step? I found none in the statute or rule text for this component. The statute requires FLDOE to post materials and districts to notify parents; districts must also list their materials in an annual implementation plan. See District_Adoption_Guide.md.

2. What the statute says (the six components)#

Source text, quoted: "For students in grades 6 through 12, the social, emotional, and physical effects of social media. This component must include, but need not be limited to, the negative effects of social media on mental health, including addiction; the distribution of misinformation on social media; how social media manipulates behavior; the permanency of sharing materials online; how to maintain personal security and identify cyberbullying, predatory behavior, and human trafficking on the Internet; and how to report suspicious behavior encountered on the Internet." [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us 1003.42(2)(o)5.a.; same words in the enrolled CS/HB 379, flsenate.gov, VERIFIED 2026-10-05]

2. What the statute says (the six components)
Code Component (statutory wording)
C1 The negative effects of social media on mental health, including addiction
C2 The distribution of misinformation on social media
C3 How social media manipulates behavior
C4 The permanency of sharing materials online
C5a How to maintain personal security on the Internet
C5b How to identify cyberbullying on the Internet
C5c How to identify predatory behavior on the Internet
C5d How to identify human trafficking on the Internet
C6 How to report suspicious behavior encountered on the Internet

The list is a floor ("must include, but need not be limited to"). The Hackles crosswalk below treats the six semicolon-separated items as C1 to C6 and splits C5 into four because the kit covers its parts very differently.

Who and when, in one place:

  • Who provides it: members of the instructional staff of public schools, "subject to the rules of the State Board of Education and the district school board," under s. 1003.42(2). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us 1003.42(2)]
  • Effective date: CS/HB 379 (Chapter 2023-36, Laws of Florida) "shall take effect July 1, 2023"; approved by the Governor May 9, 2023. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: flsenate.gov enrolled text; laws.flrules.org/2023/36]
  • Parent notice: the Department of Education "shall make available online the instructional material being used pursuant to this subparagraph, and each district school board shall notify parents of its availability" (s. 1003.42(2)(o)5.b.). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: leg.state.fl.us 1003.42]
  • Materials: FLDOE posts an Effects of Social Media Instructional Guide and a student guide at fldoe.org/academics/standards/instructional-materials/effects-sm.stml. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: FLDOE page, read in the built-in browser; the guide's Word file was read in part; the student guide PDF was later read in full, see Section 9]

3. How the ratings work#

3. How the ratings work
Rating Meaning
Full A lesson step explicitly teaches the component to students, with an activity, at an age-appropriate level. Wording may differ from the statute.
Partial The idea appears (in a hook, a discussion question, a teacher note or part of an activity) but is not taught as a main objective, or an important sub-part is missing. A teacher could miss it.
Gap Not taught, or only mentioned in passing.

Ratings are the author's reading of the 6-8 and 9-12 Teacher Guides (and a keyword check of the Reproducibles and Student Workbooks, which add no further coverage). They are not an FLDOE or district determination. Lesson numbers are from 03_6-8/6-8_Teacher_Guide.md and 04_9-12/9-12_Teacher_Guide.md. The gap lessons formerly called "FL-1" to "FL-7" are now the Plus lessons (see the Legacy IDs table in Section 1); the original stubs remain in Gap_Lesson_Stubs.md for audit.

4.1 Grades 6-8 ("Case Files")#

4.1 Grades 6-8 ("Case Files")
Component Lesson (number and title) and the activity that covers it Rating Notes
C1 Mental-health effects, incl. addiction L10 Built to Keep You Scrolling: Hook (feeling stuck on an app), Step 3 My Limits plan, Discuss Q5 (sleep, mood, friendships), Level-Up "Seven-Day Experiment" (sleep and mood). L5 Chatbots & AI Companions: Step 4 "Hand off to a human" habit and the 988 line (companion bot discouraging real help). L9 Group Chat Dynamics: Step 2 Escalation Meter (exclusion, humiliation). Partial The kit avoids the word "addiction" on purpose ("Do not frame it as addiction; avoid diagnosis", L10 teacher notes). That is a sound clinical stance, but the statute names addiction and "negative effects ... on mental health," so students should hear the plain language (compulsive or hard-to-stop use; effects on mood, sleep, self-image, loneliness) with honest, balanced framing. No lesson names comparison, anxiety, loneliness or low mood as effects. Stub: FL-3.
C2 Misinformation L5: Step 4 "Check it" habit (compare facts with two other sources); chatbot gives a wrong answer (CF-5). L8 Fakes That Hurt: Step 2 real or fake, same harm (about fake images, not about false claims spreading). L3 Phish Lab: FLAGS on fake messages (scams, not misinformation). Partial No lesson teaches how false or misleading claims spread through sharing, or how to check a claim before passing it on. The pieces exist but are about scams, chatbots and image abuse. Stub: FL-6.
C3 Platform manipulation of behavior L10: Step 2 Design Detective (endless scroll, autoplay, streaks, notifications and badges, like counts, variable rewards), the persuasive-design framing in the Hook. L5: companion bots built to keep you talking. Full Strongest match in the kit. Uses a fictional app (ScrollSpot) and no brand names.
C4 Permanency L4 Footprint & Permissions: the four questions, incl. "Can I undo it?" (Step 2), Discuss Q2, teacher note "Can I delete everything? (Often partly; others may have copied it)". L9: Step 3 "Save, do not spread" (screenshots travel). 9.1 What the guide is, and what it is not#

10. Divergences and cautions#

Where the guide and the kit disagree, the kit does not tell students the state guide is wrong. It keeps its safeguards and adds a plain reconciliation for teachers. Each item states what the guide says [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: FLDOE Student's Guide to Internet Safety, May 2025 PDF], what the kit says, and how to handle it.

D1. Saving evidence with screenshots versus never saving intimate images of minors [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]#

  • Guide: Under suspicious activity, step 4 tells students to keep a record of inappropriate messages, comments or posts by taking screenshots or saving copies of conversations as evidence (p.14). Under reporting, step 1 says to gather all evidence "in any form" such as screenshots, emails or messages and to give it to law enforcement (p.15).
  • Kit: STOP-SAVE-TELL-REPORT keeps text, usernames, links and dates only; students must never save, copy, forward or screenshot a nude or sexual image of anyone under 18, including a fake, and not even "as proof" (9-12 Part A, Response Drill; 6-8 L7 and L8). The Disclosure and Incident Guide tells staff not to view, copy, download, forward, print or screenshot such images and not to ask a student to show them.
  • Reconciliation (teacher guidance). The guide's advice is right for most evidence and the kit agrees with it. There is one exception, and it is the one that protects students from a crime.
    1. Words and records (harassment, threats, rude or cruel comments, scam messages, usernames, profile links, dates and times): screenshots and saved copies are fine and match the state guide. Keep them private, give them to a trusted adult, and never post or forward them to classmates ("Save, do not spread," 6-8 L9).
    2. Sexual or intimate images of anyone under 18, including the student themselves, a classmate, or an AI or edited fake: do not screenshot, save, forward or show anyone, including a teacher or friend. Instead write down or screenshot only the surrounding text (the username, the words of the message or threat, the link, the date). Tell a trusted adult. The adult uses the platform's report tool and, where right, NCMEC or law enforcement, who can collect the material lawfully. Take It Down can help remove it.
    3. Not sure what kind it is? Do not capture it. Tell a trusted adult first; telling matters more than saving.
      - Say to students who have read the guide: "The state guide says to keep a record, and that is right for words and usernames. There is one exception: if a picture is a nude or sexual picture of someone under 18, do not keep it or send it, even to prove something. Keep the words and the username. A grown-up can get the rest the safe way."
      - If a student says they already saved or screenshotted an intimate image: do not view it and do not ask them to show you; ask them not to forward it and not to delete things on their own initiative; do not shame them; secure the device per policy, bring in an administrator, and follow the district's mandated-reporting steps (Disclosure and Incident Guide).
      - Why this needs counsel: the kit's statement of federal and state law on possessing or sharing such images is itself marked [VERIFY], and how staff and school resource officers handle a device is a district policy question. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM: wording of the exception, handling by staff and school resource officers, and whether the district's family letter should repeat the exception where it mentions the FLDOE guide.] Ask the district counsel or school policy before this guidance goes to families.
      - Add to family cards wherever the FLDOE guide is distributed: one sentence: "The state guide suggests saving evidence. If it is a nude or sexual picture of a young person, do not save or send it; tell a trusted adult." [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]

D2. "Block and report" versus the safe-reporting order#

  • Guide: use the platform's tools to block the account holder and report to the platform administrators (p.14).
  • Kit: for sextortion and exploitation, the order matters. The Disclosure Guide asks the child not to delete the conversation and not to block the account until a trusted adult has recorded the username, profile link and dates, if a reporting adult advises it; the 6-8 L7 background notes NCMEC's advice to block but not delete messages [VERIFIED 2026-10-05 in the kit's own fact-check, not re-read here].
  • Handling: for ordinary harassment, spam or rude behavior, blocking and reporting right away (after capturing the words) is fine and matches the guide. For a threat, a request for images or money, or anything an adult might need to act on, say "Tell first, then block with the adult's help, and do not delete anything." One line in the Reporting Quick Card (P-9).

D3. "Do Not Respond" versus upstander phrases#

  • Guide: it is best not to engage with suspicious, unwanted or unsafe situations, because responding may have unintended consequences (p.14).
  • Kit: STOP means stop replying, and do not pay. 6-8 L9 and FL-7 also teach upstander phrases and private support to a target in a peer group chat.
  • Handling: teach two cases. With a stranger, scammer, suspected predator or someone threatening you: do not reply. In a peer group chat where someone is being targeted: do not retaliate or pile on; leaving the chat or messaging the target privately is allowed, and a short calm phrase is optional. Neither contradicts the guide, which is about not engaging with the person causing the harm.

D4. "Report to a school resource officer" versus the kit's counselor-first routing#

  • Guide: it names the school resource officer as a trusted adult in three places (Trust Your Instincts, p.14; Talk to Someone, p.14; Seeking Help, p.6).
  • Kit: the Help Ladder lists a parent or caregiver, relative, teacher, counselor, coach and school nurse; the Next-Rung Rule says keep telling until someone helps; "local police or school resource officer" is a blank to fill in locally (Part 6). Staff routing after a student discloses goes to an administrator, the counselor and, where required, law enforcement. The counselor-first step is staff protocol and lesson prep, not a rule about whom a student may tell.
  • Handling: do not tell students that the officer is the wrong person. In the Florida edition, add the school resource officer (or school safety officer, where the school has one) as a local fill-in on rung 3 or 4 (P-9). Remind teachers that any adult who hears a disclosure follows mandated-reporting law and district policy no matter who the student chose. Some students and families have hard experiences with law enforcement on campus; the Inclusion Guide applies, and the student chooses from the list. How an officer handles a device with an intimate image remains [VERIFY with counsel] (Disclosure and Incident Guide).

D5. The guide's definition of human trafficking#

  • Guide: it says trafficking happens when a person is sold or used for labor, often harmed, deceived, forced or manipulated into working (p.13), and defines it in the glossary box as unlawfully subjecting people to exploitation, fraud or coercion for the purpose of forced labor (p.13).
  • Kit and law: FL-1 and FL-2 use the Legislature's wording, force, fraud or coercion for the purpose of sexual exploitation or forced labor, and note that victims are children, teens and adults [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 787.06(1), leg.state.fl.us].
  • Handling: teachers should expect students to arrive with the labor-only framing from the guide. Say: "The state guide talks about forced labor. Florida law also covers sexual exploitation. Both involve force, tricks or pressure, and both are crimes." Keep it general (no detail about acts). The trafficking-prevention specialist review (Gap_Lesson_Stubs.md banner) should decide the final wording. Do not teach the "sold" or "stranger snatches a child" picture; FL-1 teacher notes already say so.

D6. Password wording#

  • Guide: use a long string of random, unrelated words along with numbers and symbols (p.13).
  • Kit: length and uniqueness matter more than complexity rules, consistent with current NIST guidance as recorded in the kit [kit marked VERIFIED 2026-10-05].
  • Handling: the two agree on length and uniqueness. Students may add numbers or symbols if a site asks. If a test or family handout uses the guide's wording, accept it.

D7. Home activities and the kit's privacy rules#

  • The guide's home activities include searching the names of yourself, family or friends and tracking screen time and notifications (pp.6, 12). The kit forbids using real names in class, never asks students to report their own screen time or feelings, and keeps experiments private. Keep these as home activities with a parent or guardian, as the guide itself frames them (p.4); do not run them as class tasks or collect results.

D8. Platform minimum ages (HB 3)#

  • The guide says people under 14 may not hold a social media account and 14- and 15-year-olds need explicit parent or guardian permission (p.4). Hackles does not teach platform minimum ages. The statute text was read earlier (s. 501.1736), but its enforcement and litigation status are unconfirmed [VERIFY]. Do not ask students whether they have accounts. Teach "it is never your fault" and keep reporting open to students whose accounts may break the rule; add one sentence in teacher notes only: "Some students may already have accounts; do not shame or ask."

D9. Cache clearing and tracking#

  • The guide says clearing the browser cache can help you avoid being tracked (p.11). That is a simplification: tracking can also use account logins and network identifiers. In P-8, teach it as one small step among several, and [VERIFY] the wording with district IT.

Short paragraph for a district memo.

Hackles is a free, independent classroom resource (quillsup.org). Its core routine, Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell., teaches the same habit that the Florida Department of Education's own A Student's Guide to Internet Safety (May 2025) lists as "Trust Your Instincts": when something online feels wrong, take a protective step right away and tell a trusted adult. In Hackles, "Hackles up" names the gut feeling, "Pause" is the immediate protective step, "Check" is a quick look at what is really happening, and "Tell" sends students to a trusted adult through the Help Ladder. Hackles has not been reviewed, approved or endorsed by the Florida Department of Education or by any district, and this paragraph does not claim alignment with, or approval by, any state agency; it says only that the two resources give students the same habit. Districts decide whether and how to use Hackles in their own required-instruction plans.

Easy version for teachers (say it in one breath). "The state guide says to trust your instincts, protect yourself and tell a trusted adult. Our routine is the classroom version: Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell."

Do and do not.

  • Do cite the guide by title and date, and say what it says (p.14, tip 8).
  • Do not say Hackles is "based on," "aligned with," "approved by" or "endorsed by" FLDOE, and do not use FLDOE's name or logo as a badge on kit materials.
  • Do not suggest that giving students the FLDOE guide delivers the instruction; the guide says it is supplementary, home-use material (p.4).
  • Keep the evidence exception (D1) in any family letter that mentions the guide. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
