1. At a glance

Group 6 to 25 kids, two adults, teams of 3 to 5 grouped by a mix, not by friends
Session length 45 to 60 minutes (club). 30 minutes (story time)

3. Session 1: Hackles Up!#

Goal: Kids can do the Hackles routine with their hands and name two grown-ups they would tell.
Crew: Hackle (a stuffed yellow toy or a drawing on a stick), Buddy.
Supplies: Paper, crayons, a toy or picture of Hackle, a timer, the Hackles Chant page.

3. Session 1: Hackles Up!
Min What
5 Gather and promises
5 Warm-up: Hackles Chant, three ways (whisper, robot, slow-motion)
15 Main game: Pause Palm Freeze
10 Trusted Hand craft
4 Calm-down: Ember's Warm Breath
5 Close

Leader script#

Gather. SAY: "Welcome to Hackles Club! This is Hackle. Hackle has quills that stand up when something feels odd. Watch: pop! That is called hackles up. When everything is fine, the quills lie down. Show me hackles up!" (Kids brush the back of the neck.)
SAY: "Two promises. First: a false alarm is fine. Second: it is never your fault. Say it with me."

Main game: Pause Palm Freeze. SAY: "We will dance. When the music stops, I turn into Gifty, a silly gift box who tries to trick you. When you hear Gifty, freeze, show your Pause Palm and shout 'Hackles up! No thanks, Gifty!'"
Play music, or clap. Stop. In a silly voice: "FREE PONIES! TAP THE BOX! DON'T TELL ANYONE!" Kids freeze. Gifty groans: "Aw, shucks!" Play 6 rounds with offers like a hat made of cheese or a spaceship. A child who wobbles gets "That is called a false alarm! Keep dancing!" Nobody is out. Gifty is always the adult. Children can suggest silly offers.

Trusted Hand. SAY: "Trace your hand. Put one grown-up you trust on each finger. It can be a grown-up at home, a teacher, a librarian, a coach, a grandparent or a carer. Draw them or write the first letter. Do not say names aloud if you do not want to." Leaders help with words. Kids who cannot name five can name two. Say: "This is your Tell team. When something feels odd, you start at the thumb."

Tell practice (with a puppet). The second leader is the "grown-up". Each child says, "I need help with something online." The leader answers with the Good Grown-up Reply: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."

Calm-down. Ember's Warm Breath: cup your hands like holding a tiny flame, breathe in for 4, out for 6, "Warm in, soft out."

Close. Hand out the take-home card. Say: "Hackles up! Pause! Check! Tell!" and the Tell Wave.

Take-home family card#

Hackles Club: Hackles Up! Today your child learned four moves for when something online feels odd: Hackles up, Pause, Check, Tell. Ask them to show you the hand signals.
Try at home (5 minutes): Look at your child's Trusted Hand drawing together. Add a name if you want to. Say: "If something ever feels odd, you can tell me, and you will never be in trouble for telling."
A false alarm is fine. It is never the child's fault.

If it goes wrong#

  • Kids are too wild at the freeze. Switch to slow motion with no music. Freeze when you raise your own Pause Palm.
  • A child cannot name any trusted grown-up. Do not press. Say: "Let's put your Club leader on the first finger for today." Tell your safeguarding lead gently the same day. Use the Start Here card if the child says more.
  • A child says something about a person at home or a thing that happened to them. Thank them, use the "If a child tells you something" card, and tell your safeguarding lead the same day.
  • A child is scared of Gifty. Show them the box is cardboard, let them hold it, and leave Gifty out.

4. Session 2: Latch and the Silly Words#

Goal: Kids can say that a good password is long, silly and secret, and who can know it.
Crew: Latch (a mint padlock drawing or puppet), Hackle.
Supplies: A decorated box (the Silly Vault), a paper key with a tangerine quill on top, 8 picture cards, a poster of the Trusted Hand, 5 envelopes.

4. Session 2: Latch and the Silly Words
Min What
5 Gather, promises, chant
5 Warm-up: Silly Words Hop
20 Main game: Latch's Silly Vault (adapted from Mission K1, Games Pack file 03)
8 Talk: who can know
4 Calm-down
5 Close

Leader script#

Warm-up: Silly Words Hop. SAY three silly words ("purple... banana... trumpet!"). Kids hop three times, whisper the words back and say "Shhh, it's secret!"

Main game. SAY: "Oh no! Latch the padlock has forgotten the Silly Words that open the Silly Vault. Inside is the Hackles Key. Latch says: 'Please help me remember what makes a good password! But we must whisper. Passwords are secret.'"

  • Stage 1, Secret or Share? Eight picture cards go into two hoops (or two sheets of paper): SECRET and OK TO SHARE. Cards: my password, tablet passcode, home address, phone number (secret); favorite color, favorite animal, favorite song, a big hello smile (share). Read each card. Count the secret cards on your fingers: 4.
  • Stage 2, Latch's Silly Choice. Three pictures: a sad name tag "DEV 123"; a purple banana, a trumpet and a dinosaur in a row; a birthday cake with a number. SAY: "Which is long, silly and hard to guess?" Answer: the three silly words.
  • Stage 3, Who Can Know? Five people cards: a grown-up at home, a teacher, a librarian or counselor (trusted), a pop-up that talks and a stranger in a game (not trusted, goes in the HACKLES UP bin). Three go on the Trusted Hand poster, two in the bin. Keep it silly. Do not role-play a stranger.
  • Stage 4, Whisper Your Silly Password. SAY first: "These are practice words, not your real password. Never whisper a real one to anybody but your trusted grown-up." Each team makes up three silly words and whispers them to the Latch puppet, who says, "Perfect! Now only tell your trusted grown-up." The Silly Vault opens. Inside is the Hackles Key.

Door Phrase (whisper together): "Hackles up. Pause. Check. Tell."

Hints: Stage 1: "Which would be a bad thing to give a stranger?" Stage 2: "Which is the silliest?" Stage 3: "Raise your hand if you trust this person to help."

Talk (3 questions). What makes a password strong? (long, silly, secret.) Who can know it? (me and my trusted grown-up.) What if someone asks? (Hackles up, Pause, Tell.)

Calm-down. Ember's Warm Breath, then a Latch stretch: reach arms up like a tall key, then lower them slowly.

Take-home family card#

Hackles Club: Latch and the Silly Words. Today we learned: long, silly, secret. Ask your child to whisper three silly practice words to you. (They are never real passwords.)
Try at home: Make up a silly three-word password for a stuffed animal. Say who is allowed to know it.

If it goes wrong#

  • A child whispers a real password. Do not repeat it. Say, "That one sounds like a real one. Let's keep real ones just for you and your trusted grown-up. Try a silly one." Tell the family at pickup in one calm sentence.
  • A child does not know what a password is. Skip Stage 2 and use only Stages 1 and 3. The goal is still met.
  • The team argues. Say, "Let's all vote with our hands." Count, move on.

5. Session 3: Peeper's Pop-Up Peek#

Goal: Kids can sort pop-ups and messages into FISHY, REAL and PAUSE AND ASK.
Crew: Peeper (sky-blue magnifier critter), Gifty (an adult voice).
Supplies: Set A cards (01_Club_Card_Sets.md), a Pearl Jar and 20 pom-poms, a Peeper picture on a stick, a decorated box for Gifty.

5. Session 3: Peeper's Pop-Up Peek
Min What
5 Gather, promises, chant
5 Warm-up: Pause Palm Freeze, 2 rounds
20 Main game: Fishy Fish Hunt (Quick Catch, 8 cards)
8 Puppet scene: "No Thanks, Gifty"
4 Calm-down
5 Close

Leader script#

Teach the three answers. SAY: "Peeper looks closely at everything. Peeper has three answers. FISHY is a hackles-up hand: brush the back of your neck. REAL is star hands. PAUSE AND ASK is the Pause Palm. Show me FISHY! REAL! PAUSE AND ASK!"

Play. Peeper says "Hmmmm." Read the card twice, slowly. Count to ten quietly. Say "Peek!" Everyone answers at once. Read the back. Say "Nice noticing!" to every group. After every card the whole circle does the right action for 5 seconds. Every card adds one pom-pom to the Pearl Jar, always. The jar is full after 8 cards. Use Quick Catch cards A1, A3, A5, A7, A8, A10, A14, A15 (3 FISHY, 3 REAL, 2 PAUSE AND ASK).

Puppet scene: "No Thanks, Gifty" (adapted from Role-play R5, Games Pack file 06). Leader 1 is Gifty (silly voice). Leader 2 is Peeper.

  • Gifty: "Hello! I am a very official pop-up! You have won 47 problems! Tap me!"
  • Peeper: "Hmmmm. Hackles up! Pause! Kids, what do we say?"
  • Everyone: "No thanks, Gifty!"
  • Gifty: "Aw, shucks!" (Gifty sinks behind the table.)

Calm-down. Ember's Warm Breath, with eyes closed or soft on the floor.

Take-home family card#

Hackles Club: Peeper's Pop-Up Peek. Today we played FISHY, REAL and PAUSE AND ASK. Ask your child to show you the three signals.
Try at home: Next time something pops up on a screen, ask your child, "FISHY, REAL or PAUSE AND ASK?" Then check together. Say "Nice noticing!" every time.

If it goes wrong#

  • Every child shows PAUSE AND ASK. Great. Ask, "What would you check?" One answer is enough ("I would ask a grown-up").
  • A child says "this happened to me" or "my uncle fell for that". Thank them. "It is never anyone's fault. The tricker did it." Move on warmly, and use the Start Here card if more comes out.
  • A child freezes or looks upset. "Pass, please" is always fine. Offer the Pass card.

6. Session 4: Pip's Share or Shell#

Goal: Kids can sort what is fun to share from what stays in the shell, and know to ask before sharing a photo.
Crew: Pip (a coral hermit-crab; a paper shell on a stick).
Supplies: Paper plates (two per team: one labeled SHARE, one SHELL), item picture cards, Pip picture, crayons.

6. Session 4: Pip's Share or Shell
Min What
5 Gather, promises, chant
5 Warm-up: Share or Shell motions
20 Main game: Share or Shell (song and sort)
8 Photo role-play: "Do all my friends say OK?"
4 Calm-down
5 Close

Leader script#

Warm-up. Share: open both hands wide. Shell: curl up with arms round your knees (or arms in a circle if seated). Sing to the tune of "Frère Jacques" [PD TUNE] (original lyrics):

Pip is asking, Pip is asking,
Share or shell? Share or shell?
Color, song and smile, yay! Color, song and smile, yay!
Share, share, share! Share, share, share!

Pip is asking, Pip is asking,
Your address? Your address?
Password, house and phone, no! Password, house and phone, no!
Shell, shell, shell! Shell, shell, shell!

Main game. SAY: "Pip is a hermit crab. Pip decides what to show and what to keep inside the shell. I will call something out. You do the motion. If it is 'hmm', do Check Eyes and say 'Ask a grown-up!'" Call: favorite animal (share), my phone number (shell), my smile (share), my home address (shell), my name (hmm), my school (shell), a drawing I made (share), my password (shell).
Then each team sorts picture cards onto SHARE and SHELL plates. Ask each team to tell Pip one thing they sorted. Do not ask any child to share their real address or personal details as an example.

Photo role-play. SAY: "A grown-up wants to post a photo of you and your friends. Pip says: ask, 'Do all my friends say OK?'" Two leaders model it. Then pairs practice: "Is it okay if we share this photo?" "Yes" or "No thanks."

Calm-down. Shell breaths: curl small, breathe in 4, stretch out slowly for 6.

Take-home family card#

Hackles Club: Pip's Share or Shell. Today we sorted what is fun to share (a favorite animal, a smile, a drawing) from what stays in the shell (a password, an address, a phone number).
Try at home: Before sharing a family photo, ask the people in it, "Do all my friends say OK?" Practice once this week.

If it goes wrong#

  • A child starts giving real details (a street or school name). Say, "Pip says that one goes in the shell!" Laugh kindly. Do not repeat it.
  • A child says "my mom posts me all the time". Say, "Grown-ups decide a lot. You can ask, 'Do I say OK?'" Do not criticise the family.
  • The group will not stop giggling. A shell stretch and a Warm Breath reset the room.

7. Session 5: Fixit and the Oops Button#

Goal: Kids know that an oops is okay, and can say "I made an oops, I need help."
Crew: Fixit (tangerine helper robot), Rinse (raccoon who runs the calm "what next?" chat).
Supplies: A toy or picture of Fixit, a cardboard "Oops Button" (a paper circle), paper, crayons.

7. Session 5: Fixit and the Oops Button
Min What
5 Gather, promises, chant
5 Warm-up: "Oops, oops, we all make 'em!" rhyme
20 Main game: Oops role-play (R2 and R9, Games Pack file 06)
8 Fixit's Two Left Shoes
4 Calm-down
5 Close

Leader script#

Rhyme (call and echo).

Oops, oops, we all make 'em!
O: Oh, I'm okay. (breathe) O: Off and out. (clap)
P: People help. (Tell Wave) S: Secure it! (turn a pretend key)
Telling makes it right for you!

Main game. SAY: "Fixit says: Everybody clicks the wrong thing sometimes, even grown-ups. When it happens, there is a button in your heart called the Oops Button. Press it and say: 'I made an oops, and I need help.'" Everyone presses their pretend button.
Role-play R2 (adult and child puppet). Leader 1 is Dev (a puppet or a toy). Leader 2 is the teacher.

  • Dev: (breathes, open hands) "I made an oops, and I need help."
  • Teacher: "Thank you for telling me. Let's fix it together. You are not in trouble."
  • Dev: "I tapped a thing."
  • Teacher: "That is okay. Everyone taps things. You did the right thing by telling."

Then each child practices in pairs, one the Teller and one the Listener, using the Good Grown-up Reply.

Fixit's Two Left Shoes. Invent a silly oops (two left shoes, pudding in the backpack, a fish in the bathtub). Say, in silly voices, "I made an oops, I need help!" It takes the shame out of mistakes. Keep it away from anything dangerous (no toasters, no heat, no sharp things).

Rinse's check-in. SAY: "After an oops, Rinse asks three calm questions: What happened? Are we safe? What do we do next?" Say them together once.

Calm-down. Warm Breath, then "Click, click, fixed!" Mime tightening a bolt.

Take-home family card#

Hackles Club: Fixit and the Oops Button. Today we practiced saying, "I made an oops, I need help." The answer we want kids to hear is: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."
Try at home: The next time anyone in the family has an oops, say those words. Children copy what grown-ups do.

If it goes wrong#

  • A child describes a real oops (clicked something, got scared). Thank them. "You did the right thing by telling." Offer to let them tell their grown-up at pickup, with you beside them.
  • A child says they were punished for an oops. Do not comment on the family. Say, "I am glad you told me. Thank you." Tell your safeguarding lead the same day.
  • A child will not practice. Let them be Fixit and say, "Click, click, fixed!" instead.

8. Session 6: Kind Clicks and the Trail Walk (the Club finale)#

Goal: Kids know that kind words matter online and off, that screens need breaks, and that the class can walk the whole Hackles Trail together.
Crew: Ember (teal flame sprite), Hackle, Gifty (adult voice).
Supplies: Masking tape for a 10-space floor trail, the Trail Walk scenes (Set A), a stuffed toy or beanbag as the Crew Token, a jar and 10 pom-poms, certificates (file 13).

8. Session 6: Kind Clicks and the Trail Walk (the Club finale)
Min What
5 Gather, promises, chant
10 Kind Clicks (Ember)
20 Trail Walk (10 spaces)
5 Calm-down: Ember's Warm Breath
5 Certificates and close

Leader script#

Kind Clicks. SAY: "Ember says: kind words are warm, like a little flame. Unkind words burn. Let's practice kind clicks." Read three short statements. For each, kids give a thumbs-up if it is kind and the Pause Palm if it is unkind. Examples: "Great drawing!" (kind), "Nobody likes you" (unkind), "Want to play with us?" (kind). Then everyone says a kind sentence to a partner. If a child tells you someone was unkind to them, say, "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing."

Screen balance. SAY: "Ember also says: screens need breaks. Stretch up high, shake your hands, touch your toes. That is a screen break." Do three stretches.

Trail Walk (10 spaces). Tape ten spaces in a winding line on the floor. One child at a time is Hackle's Walker and carries the Crew Token. Read a scene from the Set A Trail Walk list (TA1 to TA10). Everyone answers with a hand signal at the same moment. The walker takes 3 steps whatever the answer. If the answer was the oops-path, say, "Let's ask Fixit," and everyone does the Tell Wave. Gifty (an adult voice) pops out of the box once every three scenes with a silly offer. The whole group says, "No thanks, Gifty!" There are no detours, no losing, and no wrong answers. Every Gifty turn ends in a cheer. Walkers always get a turn. A child who cannot walk can send the token with a helper, or move it on a tabletop copy. Passing is always allowed.
At the finish: add one pom-pom to the Hackles Jar for every scene, shake it and cheer.

Calm-down. Warm Breath, three times. Then the Tell Wave.

Certificates. Call each child by the first name they chose (a made-up Club name is fine). Say, "You are a Little Hackle." Hand out the Club certificate and a Passport if you use one.

Take-home family card#

Hackles Club: Kind Clicks and the Trail Walk. Today your child walked the Hackles Trail and practiced kind words and screen breaks.
Try at home: Pick one screen-free time each day this week. Do a stretch together. Say one kind thing to each other.
A false alarm is fine. It is never your child's fault. If your child ever says "my hackles are up", thank them every time.

If it goes wrong#

  • The trail takes longer than the clock. End after 6 scenes. The jar fills anyway.
  • A child is upset by an unkind-words example. Switch to kind-only examples.
  • A child says something unkind to another child. Say, "Ember says kind clicks, please. Try again." Then thank the child who spoke up.

9. Camp Session 7: The Big Trail Walk (camp only)#

Goal: The whole camp walks a 20-space floor trail as one team.
Supplies: Masking tape or colored paper plates (20), Crew Token, Set A scenes, Gifty box, a Hackles Jar, 20 pom-poms.

9. Camp Session 7: The Big Trail Walk (camp only)
Min What
5 Gather, promises, chant
40 Trail Walk, 20 spaces, five zones
5 Calm-down
5 Close

Zones (use pictures as well as words): Home Hollow (the Hackles routine), Password Pines (Latch), Fishy Pond (Peeper), Share Meadow (Pip), Fix-It Bridge (Fixit). Four spaces per zone. Read two scenes per zone, taken from Set A (TA1 to TA10) and the cards in Set A. Rotate the Walker so that everyone has a turn. After every third scene Gifty pops out with a silly offer.

Finish. The group crosses the Fix-It Bridge together, shakes the Hackles Jar and says, "Hackles up! Pause! Check! Tell!" Hand out stickers or stamps.

Take-home card: "Today our camp walked the whole Hackles Trail. Ask your child to tell you the five zones."

If it goes wrong. Shorten to 10 spaces and use scenes you have already read.

Goal: Kids show their families what they learned, in a calm, low-pressure way.
Format: 45 minutes, families invited, no child is made to perform. Follow the Family Night Kit (file 05) for stations.

Min What
10 Welcome: kids teach the Hackles Chant to families
20 Three stations: Fishy Fish Hunt, Share or Shell, Latch's Silly Vault
10 Short puppet show: "Gifty's Big Giveaway" (Games Pack, file 06, Scenes 1, 2, 5 and 8) with leaders as puppeteers
5 Certificates, Hackles Day pledge, goodbye

Rules. No photos unless your host organisation's permission list covers them (Start Here, Section 5). Kids may be "Hackles Helpers" who wave the signs. No child is required to speak.

Pledge (say together): "I will pause when my hackles are up. I will tell a grown-up I trust. I know it is never my fault."

If it goes wrong. If a family brings a real worry, thank them, use the family-facing page in the Family Engagement Pack ("What to do if your child tells you something scary") and tell your safeguarding lead.

11. Quick reference for leaders#

  • Session shape: Gather 5, Warm-up 5, Main game 15 to 20, Talk 8, Calm-down 4, Close 5.
  • Two promises: A false alarm is fine. It is never your fault.
  • Tell sentence: "I need help with something online."
  • Good Grown-up Reply: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."
  • Gifty: always an adult voice. Silly, never scary, never wins.
  • No elimination. No losing. Pass is always fine.
  • If a child tells you something: use the card in Start Here, Section 6.
