Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

A free kit by Competence Collective · competencecollective.org · quillsup.org

Status: DRAFT. [VERIFY] and [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM] flags must be cleared before publication.

No patron accounts, no child data, no devices. Hackles in the library runs on paper, cards, puppets and people.

1. The librarian's one page#

What it is. Hackles Club is a games-first set of short programs that teach children to notice when something online feels off, and to tell a trusted adult: Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell. A library can run it as a story time, a weekly club, a summer program or a one-day Hackles Day. It needs no screens.

Why a library. Libraries are trusted places where children already come to ask questions. A librarian is one of the trusted adults on a child's Trusted Hand.

What you need

1. The librarian's one page
Need Detail
Two adults Two screened adults at every session, per your library's child-safety policy [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
Space A story-time corner or a meeting room with a visible window or open door
Supplies Under $10 for a program (index cards, tape, pom-poms)
Materials Print from this folder. Read-alouds come from the Games and Fun Pack, file 04.
Time 30 minutes for a story time, 45 to 60 for a club, 60 to 90 for Hackles Day

What you do not do

  • You do not collect any information about a child. Use your library's normal registration only if you already need it, and keep it with the library.
  • You do not use library accounts, library computers or any device in the sessions.
  • You do not teach sextortion, grooming, trafficking, sexual content, threats or self-harm. If a family asks, thank them and refer them to the school counselor, the resources in the Disclosure and Incident Guide (kit/05_Teacher_Support/), or your library's safeguarding lead.
  • You never promise a child secrecy. If a child tells you something about their safety, use the card in Start Here, Section 6, and follow your library's policy and the law [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM reporting duty where you live].

3. Programming ideas#

A. Story time (30 minutes, ages 5 to 9)#

A. Story time (30 minutes, ages 5 to 9)
Min What
3 Welcome, two promises, Hackles Chant
12 Read-aloud from the Games and Fun Pack (file 04): Read-Aloud 1 "Hackle's Quills Pop Up", Read-Aloud 2 "Latch and the Silly Words", Read-Aloud 3 "Pip Picks a Shell", Read-Aloud 4 "Fixit and the Oops Button". Ages 8 to 9: Read-Aloud 5 "The Treasure That Wasn't", Read-Aloud 6 "Mateo's Passphrase Puzzle", Read-Aloud 8 "Sam's Whoops Day".

5 A quick game: Pause Palm Freeze, Share or Shell, or a Quick Catch of 6 cards
3 Ember's Warm Breath
2 Take-home card and a library stamp

Each session page in Track A and Track B has its own story-time version.

Six-week story-time series: Hackles Up, Latch, Peeper, Pip, Fixit, Kind Clicks.

B. After-school or weekly club (45 to 60 minutes)#

Run a track from file 02, 03 or 04.

C. Summer reading tie-in#

Add a Hackles Passport (Section 4) to your summer reading program as a second page. Children earn a stamp for each Hackles session they attend, or for each activity done at home with a family adult. No child's name goes on any list.

D. Drop-in games table#

Keep a table with a Quick Catch (8 cards) and a Pause Palm sign. Any child or family can stop and play for 10 minutes. A librarian runs it. Rotate through Sets A, B and C.

E. Escape-the-room afternoon#

Run the Quiet Door mission (Track B Session 6 or the Track C Account Vault) in a meeting room, with teams of 3 to 5 and a Door Keeper. Tape the Door on the floor. Allow 45 minutes.

F. Teens as helpers#

Teens aged 14 and up may help at younger sessions if your policy allows: hand out cards, read card backs, cheer. A teen never counts as one of the two adults and is never alone with a younger child [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM].

G. Family night#

Follow file 05. A library meeting room is ideal.

H. A "Ask a librarian" moment#

End each program with: "If you ever have a question about something online, a librarian is a person you can ask." Make sure your staff know how to respond: calm, kind, no judgment, and no investigation. Use the Start Here card.

I. Community partners#

If you invite a guest (a local police officer, a school counselor), they follow your library's guest-speaker policy and the Hackles rules: no scary stories, no real cases, no sensitive topics in the Club. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]

4. The take-home Hackles Passport#

What it is. A small folded card that a child keeps. A librarian or leader stamps or initials a box when the child does the activity, in the library or at home with a family adult. The library keeps no record.

How to make it. Print the passport page on card stock, cut on the line, fold in half. Stamp with any stamp, a sticker or a pen mark.

Passport rules#

  • A child can start any box.
  • Anyone, including a family adult, can stamp.
  • A completed passport earns a library certificate (file 13) or a sticker.
  • Passports are theirs to keep. Nobody checks them against a list.
  • If a child cannot read, a helper reads each line.

Passport at home#

Add a line on the back: "Do the activity at home with a family adult. Initial the box. Bring it back."

5. Hackles Day at the library#

Goal: A one-day, drop-in or timed event where children and families try four to six Hackles stations in the library.
Time: 60 to 90 minutes. Ages: 5 to 15, with adults.
Staff: at least 2 screened adults in the room at all times, plus one volunteer per station, plus a "door host".
Budget: under $10 on top of paper.

Stations (choose four to six)#

Stations (choose four to six)
# Station Based on Leader
1 Peeper's Pond (Spot the Fishy) Set A, B and C Quick Catch Volunteer
2 The Hackles Trail (a table-top Mini Trail) Trail scenes TA, TB, TC Volunteer
3 Storytime Tent Read-Alouds 1 to 4 A librarian
4 Gifty's Puppet Corner "No Thanks, Gifty" and R5, and a short puppet scene Two adults (Gifty is always an adult voice)
5 Latch's Passphrase Studio Word Bank cards and the dice demo Volunteer
6 Ember's Calm Corner and Code Word Table Warm Breath, family code word idea cards Volunteer

Station cards are in the Hackles Day Event Plan (Games Pack, file 09) and in the Family Night Kit (file 05).

Floor plan#

Put stations along the walls so every station is visible to the door host. Put the Calm Corner in the quietest corner. Keep a clear path. Hang the Hackles routine poster and the Help Ladder card by the door.

Run of show (75 minutes)#

Run of show (75 minutes)
Min What
0 to 10 Welcome. Two promises, Hackles Chant, how stations work, "no photos" sign.
10 to 60 Stations. Children and families wander. Each visit is 8 to 10 minutes. Passport stamp at each station.
60 to 70 Closing circle. Chant, pledge, thank you.
70 to 75 Take-home cards and goodbye. Certificates for passport holders.

Pledge#

"I will pause when my hackles are up. I will tell someone I trust. I know it is never my fault."

Rules for the day#

  • Two screened adults in the room at all times.
  • No photos unless your library's media policy and a family's written permission both cover it [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM].
  • No devices. No library computers used.
  • No real accounts, passwords, names or incidents at any station.
  • A counselor-like person. Name the staff member who is the "if something comes up" person. Put the name on the leader card. If your library has a social worker or a safeguarding lead, ask them to be on call.
  • Pass is always fine.
  • No child is required to perform.

After the event#

Fill in the impact tally (file 12). Write down what to change for next time. Do not note any child.

6. For library staff: if a child tells you something#

Print this card and keep it behind the desk. It is a short version of Start Here, Section 6.

  • Stay calm. Keep your voice soft.
  • Say: "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble."
  • Move to a place that is private for the child and visible to a colleague.
  • Listen. "Tell me more." Do not ask "Why did you...?"
  • Be honest: "I can't keep this a secret, because I need to get you help. I will tell you who I tell."
  • Write down their exact words afterward.
  • Tell your library's safeguarding lead the same day, or at once if the child is in danger. Report personally if the law requires it. [FILL IN local rule] [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
  • If a device holds pictures, do not look at, copy or forward them.
  • If a child may hurt themselves, stay with them and call your emergency or crisis number.
