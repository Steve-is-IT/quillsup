How to use this packet. Print on white or colored card stock (one color per card set helps you sort and store: see the color guide below). Cut along the table borders; each table cell is one card. Laminate if you can. Store each set in its own zip bag or envelope labeled with the set number. Every card is picture-first; words are 1 to 3 words only. Text in Illustration note ... brackets describes art for the illustrator and does not print. All scenarios are pretend. Cards use no brand names and show no real children. Nothing here asks a child to share real information.

SET 5: THE OOPS BUTTON CARD (Lesson 4)#

One per child. Print on card stock. Children color the button red. They keep it in a folder or take it home.

THE OOPS BUTTON Illustration note A huge round red button in the center with "OOPS!" in white letters and a small heart on top. Beneath it, three steps in a row with icons: (1) a hand tapping the button, "OOPS", (2) a hand on heart reaching out, "TELL", (3) a happy face with a heart, "FIXED." When something feels yucky, confusing, or scary: Press Oops. Tell a grown-up. Get it fixed. I am never in trouble for telling. It is never my fault. Illustration note Hackle holding the button and smiling.

Grown-up response card (for teacher, helpers, and family; cut and keep):

WHEN A CHILD PRESSES THE OOPS BUTTON, SAY: "Thank you for telling me." "It is not your fault." "Let's fix it together." Then: stay calm, turn the screen off together, give a hug or high five, and ask only simple, open questions such as "Is there anything else you want to tell me?" If the child is describing anything harmful, follow your school's disclosure procedure.

SET 6: GAME-CHAT SCENARIO CARDS (Lesson 6)#

Each card is a cartoon chat bubble from "Player123" (a simple stick-figure avatar with a question mark). The teacher reads each card aloud in a calm, matter-of-fact voice. Do not role-play the other person. After each card, children do Pause Palm and say what they would do.

SET 6: GAME-CHAT SCENARIO CARDS (Lesson 6)
CARD 1 Illustration note avatar with question mark, speech bubble. "Hi! What's your name? What school do you go to?" Answer: Pause. Private. Tell a grown-up. CARD 2 Illustration note avatar, gift icon, coin icon. "I'll give you free game coins if you tell me where you live." Answer: Pause. "Free" is tricky. Tell a grown-up.
CARD 3 Illustration note avatar, a door icon. "Let's talk somewhere else, just us." Answer: Pause. Tell a grown-up. CARD 4 Illustration note avatar, finger on lips icon. "Don't tell your mom we talk." Answer: Pause. A yucky secret. Tell a grown-up.

Teacher key: Cards 1 to 5 are Pause and Tell. Card 6 is a kind message; a grown-up still knows who we play with.

CARD A Illustration note a pretend game-chat bubble with a camera icon and a finger on lips. Below it, a child holding a Pause Palm; a heart-and-hand pointing to a smiling grown-up. CARD C Illustration note a person with a finger on lips and a heart-and-hand icon. Read aloud: "Someone says: 'This is our secret. Don't tell your grown-ups.'" Child's answer: That is a Tell secret. Thank you for telling me.
CARD E Illustration note a child with a hand outstretched toward one busy helper, then another helper kneeling to listen. Read aloud: "I told a grown-up, but they were too busy to hear me." Child's answer: Keep telling until someone helps. Go to the next helper on my hand. CARD F Illustration note a big heart with "NEVER YOUR FAULT" and Hackle hugging it. Read aloud: "Whose fault is it if someone asks for something yucky?" Child's answer: Never mine.

The Body Rules chant (post on the wall):

  1. My body is mine.
  2. No one needs to see my private parts or take pictures of them.
  3. If anyone asks, I say No, I Go, and I Tell.
  4. I keep telling a trusted adult until someone helps.
  5. It is never my fault.

SET 12: FAMILY CODE WORD CARD (take-home, Lesson 8)#

Send home with the Lesson 8 family card. Families fill it out and keep it. The card is not returned to school.

OUR FAMILY CODE WORD Illustration note A friendly padlock with a star, a ribbon, and a family (grown-up and child) whispering. Pretend voices and pictures can be made by computers. A code word helps us know it is really our family. How to choose one (together): 1. Pick a silly word or phrase only your family knows. (Not a pet's name or something people could guess.) 2. Do not write it on this card, post it online, or text it. 3. Practice saying it at home. 4. If a voice, message, or video asks your child to do something strange or scary, even if it sounds like a family member, your child can ask: "What's our code word?" 5. If a grown-up cannot say it, do not trust it. Stop and tell a trusted adult. 6. Change it if anyone else learns it. Remember: Our child is never in trouble for asking. Our code word is kept in our heads and hearts. [VERIFY: family code word guidance with current FBI/FTC consumer advice before printing.]

FAMILY CARD 4: The Oops Button
"Today we learned the Oops Button: if something yucky or confusing happens on a screen, we say 'Oops! I need help,' and a grown-up says 'Thank you for telling me.' Please tell your child you will never be upset with them for telling you."

Reply slip: [ ] We talked about the Oops Button. [ ] We said "Thank you for telling me" out loud. Our family's Oops sentence: ______

FAMILY CARD 5: Private vs. Public
"Today we sorted what is okay to share and what is private (like our name, address, school, and password). Please ask your child: 'What would you ask me before sharing something online?' and answer: 'Ask me first.'"

FAMILY CARD 6: Online Friends vs. Real Friends
"Today we learned that online we can't always tell who is who, and that a secret someone asks us to keep from our grown-ups is something to tell. Please ask: 'Is there anything in your games you want to show me?' and thank your child for telling you."

Reply slip: [ ] We talked about surprises vs. secrets. [ ] We checked our child's game or video chat settings. One rule we have for playing with others online: ______

FAMILY CARD 7: Kind Clicks
"Today we practiced kind clicks and what to do if mean words show up: Pause, don't write back, show a grown-up. Please tell your child: 'If anyone is ever unkind to you online, it is not your fault, and I want to know.'"

Reply slip: [ ] We talked about kind and unkind messages. [ ] We have a plan if someone is mean to us. A kind message our child wrote or said: ______

FAMILY CARD 8: Real or Pretend?
"Today we learned pictures and voices made by computers can look and sound real, even when they're pretend. Please choose a family code word together (card enclosed), and tell your child: 'If a voice or message asks you to do something strange, even if it sounds like me, check with the code word.'"
