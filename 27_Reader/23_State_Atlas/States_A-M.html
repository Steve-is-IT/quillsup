1. How to read this atlas#

  • Mandate column. YES = a statute or rule I read requires schools or districts to provide instruction (or, where stated, a policy) on the topic. CONDITIONAL = required only when something else is taught. PERMISSIVE = "may" or "encouraged". NO = none found. UNCLEAR = I could not settle it.
  • Two kinds of topic are shown. (1) Online-safety instruction: internet safety, social media, cyberbullying, digital citizenship, media literacy, sexting or sextortion. (2) Abuse-prevention instruction: Erin's Law-type child sexual abuse prevention, dating violence, trafficking. Both are shown because Hackles lessons 6 (grooming patterns) and 7 (pressure online) support both.
  • Confidence. High = I read the operative statutory text. Medium = I read an official summary, digest or agency page, not the operative text. Low = secondary or search-result pointer only.
  • Federal baseline (all states). Schools and libraries that take E-rate discounts must have an internet safety policy under the Children's Internet Protection Act, which includes educating minors about appropriate online behavior. [VERIFY: 47 U.S.C. 254(h) and 47 CFR 54.520, not read this session.] Several state pages below point back to it.

3. Jurisdiction profiles#

Format for each: Mandate (a), Standards (b), Reporting pointer (c), Student data (d), Adoption (e). Hackles fit is given where a mandate exists. Hackles collects no student data and has no accounts, so (d) is always a one-line relevance note.

Alabama#

  • Mandate. Child sexual abuse prevention: "All K-12 public schools shall establish a child sexual abuse prevention instructional program" with content set by the local board but at least (1) recognize abuse, reduce vulnerability and report; (2) at least four sessions; (3) at least annual sessions building year to year; (4) developmentally appropriate content by grade. Ala. Code 16-40-9(d). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: ALSDE School Safety and Discipline Manual 2026, alabamaachieves.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/SS_20260817_ManStLawSchSafetyDisp2026_v1.pdf] Online safety: boards had to adopt an internet safety policy for school-owned devices by July 1, 2025 (limits age-appropriate content, protects email and chat, bars social media except teacher-directed), Ala. Code 16-1B-4 (Act 2025-386). A written policy on discipline and education related to distribution of private images and child sexual abuse material (including AI-generated) was required before 2024-25, Ala. Code 16-3-41 (Act 2024-98). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: same manual] No state mandate found in primary sources searched for classroom internet-safety instruction. SB 74 (a cyber safety program on internet hazards, cyberbullying and sexting, presented by school resource officers in middle and high school) was introduced in 2025 and again in 2026; I did not find that it passed. [VERIFY: alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/SB74-int.pdf]
  • Standards. Alabama Course of Study: Digital Literacy and Computer Science; Health Education. [VERIFY: documents and codes not read]
  • Reporting. Mandatory reporting is Ala. Code 26-14-3 (listed in the ALSDE manual). [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: manual table of contents] Deadlines not read; point teachers to the Alabama DHR child abuse reporting page. [VERIFY]
  • Student data. Alabama student-privacy law not researched. [VERIFY] Hackles collects none.
  • Adoption. The Governor's Task Force sets Erin's Law guidelines (16-40-9(b)); ALSDE posts them. No approved-materials list found for Hackles-type content. [VERIFY]
  • Hackles fit. 6-8 L6 and L7 (grooming, pressure), L8 (fakes that hurt, private images) support 16-3-41 education; Erin's Law needs a body-safety program that Hackles K-5 touches only partly [VERIFY].

Alaska#

  • Mandate. Each district board must adopt a policy, a training program for employees and students, and parent notices on sexual abuse and sexual assault awareness and prevention for grades K-12, with warning signs, referral and resource information, and actions a child may take to prevent and report; parents may excuse a student. AS 14.30.355. Teen dating violence and abuse program, grades 7-12. AS 14.30.356. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp?media=print&secStart=14.30.355&secEnd=14.30.356] No state mandate found in primary sources searched for internet safety, social media or trafficking instruction; the Alaska Safe Children's Act page does not mention them. [VERIFY: education.alaska.gov/SchoolHealth/safechildact, read only through a summary tool]
  • Standards. Alaska content standards for health and technology/computer science. [VERIFY: documents not read]
  • Reporting. Alaska Office of Children's Services (Department of Family and Community Services) child abuse reporting page. [VERIFY]
  • Student data. Not researched. [VERIFY]
  • Adoption. DEED publishes a Safe Children's Act implementation guide (August 2024). [VERIFY: not read]

Arizona#

  • Mandate. HB 2029 (2025), "internet safety instruction; public schools" (grades 5-12, content on predators, scams, passwords, opioids; parent opt-out) was held in the Senate and did not become law. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: apps.azleg.gov/api/Bill/?billNumber=HB2029&sessionId=129, FinalDisposition "Held in Senate"; summary at azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/summary/H.HB2029_010825_ED.DOCX.htm] What is law: districts and charters must adopt and enforce policies that restrict student social media access on school internet (teacher-directed use allowed) and limit wireless devices during the school day, with annual notice. A.R.S. 15-120.05. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: azleg.gov/ars/15/00120-05.htm] Not checked: any 2026-session instruction bill. No state mandate found in primary sources searched for classroom instruction.
  • Standards. Arizona Educational Technology Standards, approved January 2022, seven pillars including "Digital Citizen". [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: HB 2029 House summary, stated as history] Arizona Computer Science standards; Health standards. [VERIFY]
  • Reporting. Arizona Department of Child Safety reporting page. [VERIFY]
  • Student data. A.R.S. 15-1046 (student data privacy). [VERIFY: title seen in search result]
  • Adoption. None found.
