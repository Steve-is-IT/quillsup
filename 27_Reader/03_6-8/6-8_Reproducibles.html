Unit subtitle: "Case Files"
Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

Card sets, fake phishing DMs, mock settings screens, response-drill cards, and posters. Print, cut, and sort. Everything here is fictional or generic. No real brands, real accounts, working links, or images appear anywhere in this kit.

Status: DRAFT for counselor, child-safety, and legal review. Items marked [VERIFY] must be checked before printing.

How to use this file#

  • Print one copy of each set per group of 4-5 students (6 groups for a class of 25-30) unless noted.
  • Cards are shown in shaded boxes. Cut along the box edges.
  • Answers are in the separate 6-8_Teacher_Answer_Key file. Do not hand out the answer key.
  • Handles and "links" in the sample DMs use made-up names and ".example" addresses that cannot lead anywhere. They are teaching examples, not copies of any real message.
  • Nothing in this kit shows or describes any image of a minor. Never add one.

R0. Posters and Everyday Cards#

R0-1: Hackles up? Pause · Check · Tell poster#

HACKLES UP? Something feels off, too good, pushy, secret, or scary? Trust that feeling. False alarms are fine. Then:
PAUSE. Stop. Do not reply, click, pay, forward, or share. Take one slow breath.
CHECK. Who is this, really? What do they want, and how fast? Is anyone asking me to keep it secret, send something private, or pay? How do I feel?
TELL. Tell a person you trust. Telling is not tattling. It is the strongest move.
It is never your fault.
[Small corner icon: Hackle]

R0-2: Help Ladder poster#

THE HELP LADDER
Rung 4: A reporting channel. The platform's report tool, school staff, a helpline, the NCMEC CyberTipline.
Rung 3: A trusted adult. Parent or caregiver, relative, teacher, counselor, coach, school nurse, or the school resource officer if your school has one (write in who).
Rung 2: A friend who can come with you. "Will you come with me while I tell someone?"
Rung 1: Yourself. Pause. Stop replying. Take a breath.
The Next-Rung Rule: If the first person does not help, tell the next one. Keep going until someone helps.

R0-3: FLAGS bookmark (print one per student)#

FLAGS: Check before you click
F: Fast. Does it rush you or threaten you?
L: Login ask. Does it ask for a password, code, or account info?
A: Amazing offer. Is it too good to be true?
G: Gotta keep it secret. Does it say not to tell anyone?
S: Strange sender or link. Does it look off?
Not sure? Ask an adult. Open the app or site yourself. Never use the link in the message.

R0-4: Pass cards (print one per student on green paper)#

PASS. Put this card on your desk any time. No questions asked.

R0-5: Step-Out card#

STEP OUT. Hold this up or hand it to your teacher. You can go to the Quiet Corner or the alternate activity. No explanation needed.

R0-6: Quiet Check-in card (leave a stack by the door)#

I would like to talk to someone privately. Name (optional): __ Best time: __
I would like to talk with: [ ] my teacher [ ] the counselor [ ] another adult: __
Fold and place in the box. Your teacher or counselor will follow up today or the next school day.

R1-B: Tell-3 Script Card (one per triad)#

THE TELL-3
1. Start: "I need your help with something online."
2. Say it in one sentence: "Someone I do not know asked me to chat privately and said not to tell anyone."
3. Ask: "Can you help me figure out what to do?"
You can use these words for any problem. Change the middle sentence to fit.

RP1-ALT: Alternate Activity packet: "Job Scams"#

For students who opt out or step out. A supervising adult is present. No reason is asked. The student teaches no one and shares nothing personal. It teaches the same six flags and the same telling skill with a scenario about a fake job that asks for a fee.

Case: Remy's Job Offer (same as Workbook alternate).

Remy is thirteen and wants to earn money. A message says: "Paid data-entry job, $40 an hour, no experience. Pay a $60 starter fee by gift card today to hold your spot. Do not tell anyone, the spot will go to someone else." Remy feels excited and a little uneasy.

Tasks (about 8 minutes):

  • Circle the flags (SECRET, TOO GOOD, and a fee that asks for money first). Mark the offer GREEN, YELLOW or RED, and say why.
  • Write one question Remy could ask to check the offer: who is this, what do they want, who else knows?
  • With a partner, say the Tell-3 for a friend: "A friend of mine was offered a job that asks for a fee and was told not to tell anyone."
  • Read the Reporting Quick Card together. A trusted adult can report a scam to the platform and to the bank if money was sent.

Debrief for the supervising adult: The scenario teaches the same flags with no exploitation content. Be warm and low-key. If the student says anything personal, use the disclosure protocol (Appendix C). Hand the student the Reporting Quick Card.

RP2. Plus Lesson P2 Cards: Scroll Science#

REQUIRES REVIEW BY A SCHOOL COUNSELOR OR CLINICIAN BEFORE CLASSROOM USE. All content is fictional. No real apps or companies. Nobody shares their own habits.

Evidence Cards (six; one set per group; cut apart; answers in the Teacher Answer Key)#

EVIDENCE 1. Apps use features like alerts, streaks and endless scrolling on purpose to bring you back.

EVIDENCE 2. Staying up late on a screen can cut into sleep, and being tired makes the next day harder.

EVIDENCE 3. Looking at polished posts can make some people feel worse about themselves.

EVIDENCE 4. "Social media causes depression."

EVIDENCE 5. "Every like gives you a dopamine hit, just like a drug."

EVIDENCE 6. Only a doctor or counselor can tell whether someone's use has turned into a health problem. A quiz or a friend cannot.

Three spots for the sort#

SOLID. Well supported.
STILL STUDIED. Some evidence. Scientists are still working out how much, and for whom.
HYPE. A popular claim that goes further than the evidence.

Signal Cards (four) and the three-lane mat#

SIGNAL (a). Pia stays up late scrolling and is tired at school.
SIGNAL (b). Pia wants to cut down on checking and cannot.
SIGNAL (c). A character has stopped doing things they used to love and mostly stays on the phone.
SIGNAL (d). A character says they feel hopeless or want to hurt themselves.

Mat lanes: TRY A RESET (notice it, try a change) | TALK TO SOMEONE (a trusted adult or the counselor) | GET HELP TODAY (tell an adult now).

Extra Signal Cards (swap in for (a) or (b) for a longer lesson; plain-word versions of the state guide's signs of dependency, never a self-test): "Pia checks the phone in class even though it gets in the way." "Pia feels cranky or worried when the phone is away." "Pia skips things Pia used to enjoy to keep scrolling." "Pia feels restless when trying to take a break."

Reset Menu (one per student)#

MY RESET MENU
Device sleeps outside the bedroom at night.
Turn off one kind of notification.
One hour with the app off.
Call or message a friend instead.
A walk, or something offline I enjoy.
Unfollow an account that leaves me feeling worse.
Set a timer.
A screen-free time or place (dinner table, bedroom).
A private note of how long I spend (never collected).
I can change my mind and try something else any time.
If screen time or an app makes me feel worse, I can tell a trusted adult. Crisis support: call or text 988 [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: 988lifeline.org]. In danger: 911.

RP3. Plus Lesson P3 Cards: Fact, Fiction or Fake?#

All claims are made up. No real news, people, schools, elections or products.

Forward Chain slips (one chain of six per row; whisper, do not show)#

SLIP A. "The cafeteria is giving out free pizza coupons if you tap the link."
SLIP B. "Everyone who taps the link by Friday gets two coupons for the cafeteria."
SLIP C. "There is a prize for the first fifty students who tap the cafeteria link."
(Use one slip per chain. Compare the last version with the first.)

Check Strip card (one per student)#

BEFORE YOU SHARE
WHO made it? Is the account real?
PROOF: what shows it is true? Fact or opinion?
WHO ELSE says so? Where did the numbers come from, and when?
FEELING: is it trying to scare, anger or rush me?
When it fits: LOOK CLOSER at pictures and videos. DATA: check who a chart is about and where it starts.

Claim Cards (six; one set per class; each group works three; answers in the Teacher Answer Key)#

CLAIM 1. A post says, "SCHOOL CLOSED TOMORROW because of a danger. Tell everyone!" It has a scary photo and no name.

CLAIM 2. An influencer says a juice mix "cures" a long illness in three days and links to a shop. Comments say "it worked for me!"

CLAIM 3. A friend sends a screenshot of a "private message" in which a classmate supposedly says something cruel about a teacher.

CLAIM 4. A chatbot answers a question about the school's lunch schedule very confidently, with exact times and no source.

CLAIM 5. A chart is titled "Our school's reading scores DOUBLED!" The two bars show 91 and 94. The side scale starts at 90, not at 0.

CLAIM 6. A photo of a flooded street is captioned "Downtown right now!" A comment says, "I think this is from years ago."

Share Pause card (one per student)#

SHARE PAUSE
"If I cannot say who made it and who else says so, I do not pass it on."
If it is hurting someone, scaring people, or asking for money or personal information: use the report button in the app, and tell an adult.
It is never your fault if you shared something false by mistake. Fix it by telling people and not passing it on.
