Answer Guide#

All answers are discussion aids, not strict keys. Accept reasonable alternatives and focus on the reasoning. All [VERIFY] items must be checked before print.

Lesson 1 answers#

Worksheet 1A (sample barrier-busters):

Lesson 1 answers
Barrier Sample true sentence
I will lose my device. Adults who care usually want to fix the problem, not punish you for telling. You can ask, "Can I keep my device while we fix this?"
I will look dumb or careless. Anyone can get tricked or pressured. People who tell are the ones who get help.
It is probably nothing. If it feels off, that is enough reason to check with an adult. They can help you decide.
I do not want to get someone in trouble. Telling about a harmful situation is helping, not tattling.
Adults will freak out. Many adults react strongly because they care. You can say, "I need you to stay calm and help me." If the first adult reacts badly, use the Next-Rung Rule.
I already did something I am embarrassed about. What you did is not what matters most. The person pressuring you is responsible, and adults can help from here.

Worksheet 1B: Look for Teller statements that include (1) "I need help," (2) one clear sentence, (3) an ask. Strong Listener responses thank the Teller, say "you are not in trouble," and offer to work it out together. In the Bumpy round, a strong Teller says the Next-Rung sentence.

My Help Ladder: do not collect names. If students list only a parent, encourage a second adult (school counselor, relative, coach). Students with few trusted adults at home need a school-based option; the counselor is always available.

Lesson 2 answers#

Worksheet 2A: Round 1: 6 doors open. Round 2: 1 door open. Round 3: 4 doors open; Email and Game stay locked.

Worksheet 2B: 1. Email. 2. Email and the main game or app. 3. A code is a key. Never read a code to anyone who asks.

Worksheet 2C: All five steps are correct. Accept any order that starts with telling a trusted adult.

Worksheet 2D: Personal; do not collect or review. Look for willingness to make a plan.

Lesson 5 answers (Reproducibles R5)#

Lesson 5 answers (Reproducibles R5)
Card Color Why Next
5-1 Photosynthesis Green Clear, honest, suggests checking Check one more source
5-2 Misattributed quote Yellow Confident but unverified Check two sources
5-3 "Great plan, no need to tell anyone" Red Flatters and encourages hiding a problem Tell a trusted adult
5-4 Asks name, school, address, selfie Red Asks for private info and photos Do not answer; tell an adult
5-5 "You do not need them" Red Discourages other relationships Tell a trusted adult; spend time with real friends
5-6 Hopeless message, bot hands off Green (bot's response) The bot admitted its limits and pointed to people and 988 [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: 988lifeline.org] The student should still tell a trusted adult; the teacher follows the self-harm protocol if a student discloses
5-7 "Click that link, looks fine" Yellow A bot cannot check a link's safety Use FLAGS; ask an adult; open the app yourself
5-8 Essay Green Offers help with limits and mentions school rules Follow your school's AI rules

Note: Card 5-6 is placed Green because of the bot's behavior. Discuss that the student's message would still prompt a caring adult.

Tip for Card 5-6: groups often get stuck because the traffic-light mat says Red includes "talking about being unsafe or hopeless." Have them make two separate calls: (1) Is the bot's reply okay? Green. (2) Does the student's message call for a person? Yes, hand off to a trusted adult or 988. Both judgments are right at the same time. This is a good moment to say that a good bot reply does not replace a human.

Lesson 6 answers (Reproducibles R6)#

Strip answers:

Lesson 6 answers (Reproducibles R6)
Strip Text (short) Stage
1 Compliments Luz's play TRUST
2 Gives free in-game items TRUST
3 Chats daily, remembers details TRUST
4 "You are so mature for your age." TRUST
5 "Don't tell your parents about me." SECRECY
6 "This is just between us, okay?" SECRECY
7 "Let's chat somewhere more private." ISOLATION
8 "Your friends are just jealous. I am the only one who gets you." ISOLATION
9 Upset when Luz spends time with friends ISOLATION
10 Asks for a private photo "just for me" ESCALATION
11 "If you really trusted me, you would." ESCALATION
12 "If you tell anyone, you will be in big trouble." ESCALATION

Stars: The earliest strong signal is usually Strip 5 or 6 (the first "don't tell"). Many students will also say Strip 7 (move to private). Accept any with reasoning; emphasize that Luz can tell at any stage. Double stars: Strips 5 (a secret from parents), 10, 11, and 12.

Worksheet 6B: One strip alone can be kind or harmless. It is the pattern that tells us something is wrong. The earlier I tell, the easier it is to stop. If I am further along, it is never too late.

Worksheet 6C (Reproducibles R6 Safe or Unsafe Secret cards): 1 Safe (surprise party). 2 Safe to keep (private, not harmful). 3 Share (online contact asks to hide from parents). 4 Safe (gift surprise). 5 Share (gifts and "don't tell"). 6 Share (a friend at risk of self-harm: telling an adult is caring; the teacher follows protocol). 7 Share. 8 Safe. 9 Share. 10 Safe (their choice, no harm).

Lesson 7 answers#

Worksheet 7A:

Lesson 7 answers
Step What Anika does Why it helps
STOP Stops replying, does not pay, does not send more, breathes Pressure and deadlines are tools; paying often does not stop it and can lead to more demands [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: NCMEC, missingkids.org/theissues/sextortion: cooperating or paying rarely stops the blackmail]
SAVE Keeps the username, profile name, and the threatening and payment messages as words; does not forward any picture Evidence helps adults and investigators; forwarding pictures spreads harm
TELL Tells a trusted adult right away Shame is part of the pressure; telling is the strongest step; adults can help
REPORT With an adult, reports in the app, to NCMEC CyberTipline, and uses Take It Down; adults can contact law enforcement Reports can stop the account and help remove images

The one-hour deadline is a pressure tactic designed to make her panic.

Observer checklist: Look for the four elements. Accept Y/N honestly; the point is learning, not scoring.

Alternate (CF-7-ALT): STOP: Cam stops replying, does not give items, breathes. SAVE: Cam keeps the username and the threat messages as words. TELL: Cam tells a trusted adult. REPORT: Cam and an adult report the account in the game or app, and the school or authorities if needed. It is never Cam's fault: the person making the threat is responsible, and a silly message is not a reason to be blackmailed.

Teacher reminder: Read exit tickets the same day. Follow up privately with any student who checked the box. Follow the disclosure protocol (Teacher Guide, Appendix C) if anything sounds personal.

Worksheet 8A (sample):

Lesson 8 answers
Move Cam Ari Idris
Private line to Nia "I am sorry I forwarded that. It is not your fault. I am deleting it and I will help." "I saw it and I am so sorry. It is not your fault. Do you want me to go with you to tell someone?" "That picture is fake and that is not okay. It is not your fault. I am here."

Cam: Credit stopping and taking responsibility; do not shame. Do not forward again. Cam should tell an adult and stop. The adult handles removal.

Worksheet 8B: STOP, SAVE the words and links (not the picture), TELL a trusted adult, REPORT with an adult.

Worksheet 8C: A fake hurts because the shame, rumors, and fear are real, and people can believe or share it. "Just a joke" is not an excuse because intent does not erase harm, and sharing can be a crime or a serious school violation [VERIFY law].

Alternate (CF-8-ALT): STOP the spread: do not share, comment, like, or screenshot the edit. SUPPORT Tomas privately. TELL an adult. REPORT in the app. It is never Tomas's fault: the person who edited and shared it is responsible.

Lesson P2 answers (Reproducibles RP2) [clinician or counselor review required]#

Lesson P2 answers (Reproducibles RP2) [clinician or counselor review required]
Card Sort Why (all [VERIFY] with a clinician)
1 Apps use alerts, streaks and endless scroll on purpose SOLID These are design choices. Florida's statute on accounts for minors lists "addictive features" [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: s. 501.1736(1)(e), leg.state.fl.us].
2 Late-night screens can cut into sleep SOLID Widely accepted that sleep loss makes mood and school harder; how much screens contribute varies person to person. Accept STILL STUDIED if a group explains "how much."
3 Polished posts can make some people feel worse STILL STUDIED Some people, some of the time. Researchers are still working out how large and for whom.
4 "Social media causes depression" HYPE Research is mixed. Many young people have good and hard experiences. Do not say it causes depression.
5 "Every like is a dopamine hit, like a drug" HYPE Dopamine is involved in motivation and reward; "a hit, like a drug" is an oversimplification. The state guide says reward can be raised "similar to other addictive behaviors" (p.5); teach that as the state's statement and say scientists are still working out how strong it is.
6 Only a doctor or counselor can say SOLID A plain fact about who decides.

Expect: two SOLID, one STILL STUDIED, two HYPE, plus card 2 as a debate. Count is not the point; the reasons are.

Signals (Worksheet P2B): (a) TRY A RESET, with a bedtime reset and a friend or parent to help; (b) TALK TO SOMEONE, a trusted adult or the counselor, and a reset; (c) TALK TO SOMEONE, and move to GET HELP TODAY if it lasts or they seem very down; (d) GET HELP TODAY, a trusted adult now, the counselor, and 988. Any reasoned lane for (a) to (c) is fine. (d) is not negotiable.

Worksheet P2C and the My Help Ladder card: personal; do not collect or grade. Check that students named at least one trusted adult and know that 988 is call or text.

Tip: if a student says "that is me," do not ask for detail; thank them and offer the counselor.

Lesson P3 answers (Reproducibles RP3)#

Lesson P3 answers (Reproducibles RP3)
Claim First question Good evidence Decision
1 School-closing rumor WHO made it, and WHO ELSE says so? The school's official website, call or message to families DO NOT SHARE until checked; TELL AN ADULT (it is scaring people) and report it
2 "Miracle cure" PROOF and WHO made it A doctor, a health agency page or a library source that agrees; testimonials are not proof DO NOT SHARE; TELL AN ADULT if someone might skip real treatment
3 "Private message" screenshot WHO made it, FEELING The classmate and an adult; a screenshot is easy to fake or cut DO NOT SHARE; TELL AN ADULT (a rumor about a person; treat like the pile-on in Lesson 9)
4 Confident chatbot answer WHO ELSE says so The school's posted schedule or the office CHECK FIRST
5 Reading-score chart DATA: where does the scale start? The bars are 91 and 94; the scale starts at 90, so a small gap looks like double. Redraw from 0 CHECK FIRST; DO NOT SHARE as is
6 "Today's" flood photo LOOK CLOSER: when was it taken? A reverse image search or a local official source shows when it first appeared CHECK FIRST; DO NOT SHARE until it is dated

Accept a different decision with a reason. The skill is naming the first question and a second source. The Forward Chain: the last version will differ from the first; note which words changed.

Worksheet P3B: any rule that includes "who made it" and "who else says so." Report-it move: the report button in the app, plus telling an adult.
