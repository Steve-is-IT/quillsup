How to build this deck#

Short on time? Build these seven slides first: 1, 2, 10, 12, 13, 33 and 41. The routine, the Most Important Sentence and the pledge carry the day; every other slide is optional support. Try this now: open the file and make slide 10 (one sentence, huge type) before you read any further.

  • Slide size: 16:9. One idea per slide. Minimum font size 28 pt for body text, 44 pt for titles.
  • Colors: use the Hackles palette from the Art Style Guide: warm cream backgrounds, dark ink (#2B2D42) text, sunshine-yellow and tangerine accents. Keep high contrast. Check legibility on a projector from the back of the room.
  • Hackle the mascot: use as a small icon in a corner on adult slides. In the workshop Hackle can be a bit sillier (a sticker, a sticky note on the scoreboard) because adults are the audience; in any slide that shows student-facing materials, follow the band rules (K-5 mascot, 6-12 corner icon only).
  • Animation: none, except a "boing" on the Hackles Up reveal slides and the countdown timers.
  • Accessibility: add alt text to every image. Do not rely on color alone. Use a font of at least 28 pt, a clean sans serif, and left-aligned text. Provide the notes below in a printed version for any presenter who is not comfortable with slides.
  • Where the notes go: paste each "Speaker notes" block into the slide's notes pane. The scripts match the Facilitator Guide word for word where they appear.
  • Tags: "90" means the slide is also used in the 90-Minute Version. "H" numbers refer to handouts.

Section 1: Welcome (0:00-0:12 including Line-Up)#

Slide 1: Hackles: Teaching Online Safety, Fun and Safe (90)#

On the slide

  • Title: Hackles: Teaching Online Safety, Fun and Safe
  • Subtitle: Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell.
  • Presenter name and role; date
  • Footer: A free kit by Competence Collective · competencecollective.org · quillsup.org

Speaker notes
Music on while people arrive. Say: "Good morning, everyone! Welcome to Hackles. I'm [name], and I am not a cybersecurity expert. I'm the person who got volunteered." Pause for laughter. "If you can listen, stay steady, and know who to call, you can teach this curriculum well." Click to Slide 2.

Visual: the Hackles logo (a baby hedgehog with pale-yellow, tangerine-tipped quills standing up) large on the right; a small row of five hand-signal icons across the bottom.

Slide 2: Three Promises (90)#

On the slide

  1. Nobody will scare you.
  2. Nobody has to tell a personal story.
  3. You will laugh at least once.

Speaker notes
"I promise three things. One: nobody will scare you. Two: nobody has to tell a personal story, and everything today is fictional. Three: you will laugh at least once. If we keep all three promises, we are doing it right. At the end I will ask whether we did." Say that the scare-free approach is the point: skills beat fear.

Visual: three large numbered circles. A tiny Hackle with eyebrows raised on the third.

Slide 3: Today's Agenda (90)#

On the slide (use the 3-hour table; for the 90-minute version use the short table)

  • Gut-Check Line-Up
  • Why this, why us
  • Fake Phish Derby
  • Pit Crew Stations
  • Break
  • Say-It-Right Relay
  • Disclosure Role-Play (with safety rails)
  • Reset
  • Micro-Teach Round
  • Monday Plan and Pledge

Speaker notes
Read the agenda aloud with the clock times from the Facilitator Guide, Section 6. Highlight which parts are active: "Nine of these are on your feet or hands-on." Mention the two protected moments: the break and the disclosure role-play, which is the one segment with no points and no audience.

Visual: a horizontal timeline with small icons (a finish flag for the Derby, a ladder, a speech bubble, a pickle trophy).

Slide 4: Housekeeping and the Reset Corner#

On the slide

  • Restrooms · water · snacks
  • Phones in pockets until the break
  • Reset Corner: use any time. No explanation needed.
  • Counselor: [name] is here. Look at H7 for how to reach them.

Speaker notes
"Some of this subject can touch real life. If you need a minute, the Reset Corner is yours, and nobody will ask why. [Counselor name] is here for the whole day." Point to the corner. Remind: "We'll take a break at 1:17."

Visual: a photo or sketch of a comfy chair with a small plant. Counselor's name in large text.

Slide 5: The Gut-Check Line-Up: How It Works#

On the slide

  • This wall is a line.
  • HACKLES DOWN = calm, I have got this.
  • HACKLES UP = something feels off, I am unsure.
  • Walk to the spot that fits you. No wrong places.

Speaker notes
"Everyone stand. I'll read a statement. Walk to the spot on the line that fits you. There are no wrong places. If standing is hard, point to where you would be." Give two examples with a neighbor first if the room is quiet.

Visual: an arrow line from green (down) to orange (up) with a cartoon neck with spikes at each end.

Slide 6: Five Statements#

On the slide (reveal one at a time)

  1. I feel confident explaining to a child what to do if they click on something they should not have.
  2. I know who I would call, by name, if a student told me something frightening.
  3. I know my state's deadline for reporting.
  4. I feel nervous about teaching this topic.
  5. I think kids learn this best when it is fun.

Speaker notes
After each, give 30 seconds, then take two volunteers: "Why did you pick that spot?" ("pass" allowed). After 3: "If many of us sit at the Hackles Up end, good. That is the reason we are here. By 3:00 you will have the number in your hand." After 5: "Remember that last answer. I'm going to hold you to it." Pull back the curtain: "Five questions, everyone answered, no worksheet."

Visual: big numerals; reveal with a single click each.

Section 2: Why this, why us (0:12-0:22)#

Slide 7: Why This? Most Kids Are Fine (90)#

On the slide

  • Children are online earlier and more often than we were.
  • Most children use technology safely, most of the time.
  • Some will be tricked, treated unkindly, or targeted.
  • Our job: build skills, not fear.

Speaker notes
Say it calmly and slowly. Do not add statistics here. If someone asks for numbers, say: "Statistics change fast. If we quote numbers to families, we quote the current figure from the organization that publishes it, such as NCMEC or the FBI, and we check it first." Then bring them back: "What protects children is not a fear speech."

Visual: a calm illustration of children with devices, smiling. No scary imagery, no hooded figures, no locks with skulls.

Slide 8: Skills, Not Scares (90)#

On the slide

  • Skills-based, repeated teaching is more promising than one-off scare assemblies.
  • Skills = practiced words, practiced moves, practiced telling.
  • Source: Finkelhor and colleagues, 2021 [VERIFY]

Speaker notes
"Research on prevention suggests that skills-based teaching, repeated over time, works better than a single scary assembly. [VERIFY the exact citation and finding before you quote it.] Skills mean practice. Practice means that children have rehearsed what to say, what to do and who to tell before they need it." Invite: "That's what we will be doing today."

Visual: two icons side by side: a megaphone with a red X (one-off assembly) and a repeated circular arrow with a check (practice).

Slide 9: The Biggest Obstacle Is Shame (90)#

On the slide

  • Kids stay silent because they fear blame, punishment, or losing their phone.
  • Your reaction to a small "oops" teaches whether it is safe to tell you a big thing.
  • Anyone can be targeted. Never "you should have known better."

Speaker notes
"The single biggest obstacle is shame. Children stay silent because they believe they will be blamed, punished, or have their device taken away. How we react to a small oops teaches them whether it is safe to tell us about a big one."

Visual: a speech bubble that says "Oops" with a warm hand on a shoulder.

Slide 10: The Most Important Sentence (90)#

On the slide

  • "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble."

Speaker notes
"If you take only one thing out of today, take this. Say it with me, three times, softer each time: Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble." The room says it together. "Everything today aims at making that sentence automatic."

Visual: the sentence in large type, dark ink on cream, with Hackle's quills lying flat (a calm Hackle).

Slide 11: Our Agreements (90)#

On the slide

  1. Fictional cases, not real children
  2. Pass is always allowed
  3. Step out any time, no explanation
  4. No shame, no gotchas
  5. What we share about ourselves stays here (except if a child may be in danger)
  6. Fun on purpose
  7. Phones in pockets

Speaker notes
Read aloud, with a thumbs-up check on each. Slow down at agreement 5. Say: "The honest limit: if a child may be in danger or being hurt, we get help, and the law and our policy decide how." This is a model of what we say to students.

Visual: a poster-like list with check boxes. Same text as H2 poster.

Slide 12: Hackles Up? Pause. Check. Tell. (90)#

On the slide

  • HACKLES UP? Something feels off: too good, pushy, secret, or scary.
  • PAUSE: stop. Breathe.
  • CHECK: who is it? What do they want? How fast?
  • TELL: a trusted person. Every time.
  • A false alarm is fine. If you missed it or got tricked, it is never your fault.

Speaker notes
Say it slowly, then fast, then in a whisper. Teach: "'Hackles' are what rise on the back of your neck when something feels off. That feeling is data. Praise the noticing, including false alarms." Mention that the routine opens and closes every lesson in every band.

Visual: four large tiles in a row (Hackles Up, Pause, Check, Tell), each with its hand-signal icon.

Slide 13: The Hand Signals (90)#

On the slide

  • Hackles Up: hand raised, brushed up the back of the neck
  • Pause: flat palm like a stop sign
  • Check: circle fingers like a magnifying glass at your eye
  • Tell: hand on chest, then open palm outward

Speaker notes
Everyone practices all four signals together. Run it as a quick game: "Say the word, do the move." Mention: "Teachers use these as a class cue. When you say 'Pause,' everyone freezes. It turns into a habit that transfers online." Pair-share: "When might a student need this? When might you?"

Visual: four large photographs or line drawings of the hand gestures. Sequence left to right.

Slide 14: Your Role: Recognize and Respond (90)#

On the slide

  • You are not an investigator, a detective, a therapist or a police officer.
  • You: notice, listen, reassure, report, hand off.
  • And you do not have to be a tech expert.

Speaker notes
"You are not expected to investigate, confront anyone, be a therapist, or solve it alone. Your job is recognition and response. And you do not need to know every app, because apps change every year. What helps children does not change: a calm adult, a clear routine, and the message that telling someone is always okay." Pull back the curtain: "Four minutes of talk, one chant, three gestures, one partner chat. Teach short, then move."

Visual: five icons in a row: eye (notice), ear (listen), heart (reassure), phone or form (report), handshake (hand off).

Slide 15: Fake Phish Derby: The Rules (90)#

On the slide

  • 12 cards (8 in the 90-minute version). Team decision: Hackles Up or Hackles Down
  • 1 point: right pile
  • 1 point: name the clue
  • 1 point: name the next move
  • Some cards are real and boring. Do not panic at everything!
  • 9 minutes (6 in the 90-minute version). Go!

Speaker notes
Read the rules script from Segment 3 in the Facilitator Guide. Emphasize: all cards are fictional, no real brand or person. Point to the scoreboard and the pickle trophy. Start the countdown timer. (Half-Day: 12 cards, 36 points. 90-minute version: 8 cards, 24 points.)

Slide 16: SPOT and FLAGS (90)#

On the slide

Slide 16: SPOT and FLAGS (90)
Letter Sign
S Secrets asked
P Pressure
O Offer too good
T Trick to move you
  • Older students use FLAGS: Fast · Login ask · Amazing offer · Gotta keep it secret · Strange sender

Speaker notes
"SPOT is what grades 3 to 5 learn. FLAGS is the 6 to 8 version. Same signals. Teens and adults use the Pause-Check-Tell habit. One signal means pause and check; two or more means probably a trick." Point to H4.

Visual: a magnifying glass over the letters S-P-O-T, with the word "SPOT" in orange.

Slide 17: Sample Card: D1#

On the slide

  • From: IT-Helpdesk helpdesk@district-support.example
  • Subject: URGENT: Your mailbox is full
  • "Your teacher email will be DISABLED in 2 hours. Click here. Reply with your password."

Speaker notes
Use as a worked example before they start. "Hackles Up or Down? What is the clue? What is the next move?" Take 20 seconds, then reveal: Up. S (password), P (2 hours), T (click). Next move: do not click or reply; open the portal yourself or call the helpdesk at a number you already have; tell IT. A real helpdesk never asks for your password.

Visual: a mock email in a clean frame with the clue words highlighted in orange after the reveal.

Slide 19: The Reveal (90)#

On the slide

  • 1 · 2 · 3
  • Cards we will discuss: D1 · D2 · D6 · D9 · D12 · D15

Speaker notes
"On three, hold up your Hackles Up pile. One, two, three!" Debrief with Appendix A in the Facilitator Guide. Remember: ask teams who were right for the clue and move; ask teams who missed it, "What made it look fine?"

Visual: a big "UP" arrow and a big "DOWN" arrow. A boing sound on click.

Slide 20: Same Message, Two Answers (D12) (90)#

On the slide

  • "Your Zorbly code is 482913. Do not share it with anyone."
  • Did you just ask to log in?
  • YES: fine and boring. Enter it where you asked.
  • NO: someone may be trying to get into your account. Do not share the code. Change your password. Tell.

Speaker notes
"This is the one I love. Same message, two answers. That is why we check, and why we do not teach kids 'always scary' or 'never scary.' We teach them to notice and check." Add: "If every message makes us panic, we learn to ignore the signal. Calm is allowed."

Visual: one text bubble splitting into two arrows: a green check and an orange hand.

Slide 21: The Own-Door Rule (90)#

On the slide

  • Do not use the link, number or contact in the message.
  • Open the app or site yourself.
  • Call a number you already had.
  • Ask in person.

Speaker notes
"Own door. Not the door the message points to. This one rule helps with many tricks." Mention that the rule applies to children too: "I will check by asking my grown-up, not by tapping the link." Link to the Say This, Not That card.

Visual: an illustration of two doors, one labeled "Their door" with a tangle of wires, one labeled "My door" with a friendly welcome mat.

Slide 27: Say This, Not That (90)#

On the slide

Slide 27: Say This, Not That (90)
Say this Not that
Thank you for telling me. Why did you do that?
This is not your fault. You should have known better.
You are not in trouble. I will have to take your phone.
I can't keep this secret... I will tell you who I tell. I won't tell anyone.
Tell me more. Detailed who-what-why questions

Speaker notes
Use after Round 1 as the debrief. Walk through four points: "Why did you click" sounds like blame; "I won't tell anyone" can never be promised; "Let me see the pictures" is never right for intimate images (do not view, copy, forward or screenshot); "That's nothing" teaches kids not to tell next time. Point to H5.

Slide 30: Traffic-Light Cards (90)#

Speaker notes

Slide 32: Trio Roles and Rounds (90)#

On the slide

Speaker notes
Hand out the decks, checklists and cards. "Tellers: if the listener asks for more than your card says, say 'I don't want to say more right now.' Listeners: open questions only. Observers: one glow and one try." Start the timer; announce halfway and one minute.

Slide 33: The First 10 Minutes (90)#

On the slide

  • Stay calm. Private but visible.
  • "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble."
  • Open questions only. Never promise secrecy.
  • Never look at, copy, forward or screenshot an image.
  • Report personally and promptly as law and policy require. [VERIFY]
  • Write down the child's exact words.

Speaker notes
Use during the debrief, not during the role-play. Point to H6 and H7. Emphasize: "Telling the principal may not satisfy your duty. [VERIFY with your administrator and policy.]" Mention that the full guide is the Disclosure and Incident Guide.

Slide 35: Hackle Says: Reset#

On the slide

Speaker notes

Slide 37: Is the Lesson Fun, Safe, Skill-Based? (90)#

On the slide

Slide 37: Is the Lesson Fun, Safe, Skill-Based? (90)
Look-for
Fun Most students moving, talking or hands-on within 2 minutes; kind laughter; every student has a role
Safe No scare, no shame, pass allowed, no personal sharing, "never your fault," a route to tell, a calm close
Skill-based One skill practiced aloud or by hand; ends with Pause-Check-Tell and one takeaway

Speaker notes
This is the rubric for peer feedback today and for peer observation later (see the Peer Coaching and Observation Guide, and H13). Say: "Fun gets them in. Safe keeps them in. Skill gets them out better equipped."

Visual: three large boxes in a row: a smiley, a shield, a toolbox.

Slide 38: Micro-Teach Protocol (90)#

On the slide

  • Prep 5 min (one person at a time)
  • Round: 5 min teach + 2 min feedback (90-minute version: 3 min + 1 min)
  • Three rounds in your trio
  • Audience = "students" (silly is fine; no real stories)
  • Cards: MT1-MT12 (no sensitive topics!)

Speaker notes
Model first: teach MT2 in about 3 minutes, then name the three rubric items. Then say: "Now it's your turn." Start the timer. Circulate and listen for fun, safe and skill. Remind trios to use the same chime for transitions.

Slide 39: Two Glows and a Grow (90)#

On the slide

  • Grow: "What if you tried ___?"

Speaker notes
