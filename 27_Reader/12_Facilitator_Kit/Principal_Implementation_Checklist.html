Hackles Facilitator Kit, Document 7 of 10. A practical, tick-the-box guide for a principal (or assistant principal, or any school leader) who wants to put Hackles in place well: policy, counselor, families, schedules, opt-outs and evaluation that collects no student data.

No student accounts, no student data collected, ever.

Consult counsel and policy. Reporting duties, parent notice, opt-out rights, curriculum review, photography and recording, and staff evaluation rules differ by state, district and school type. Every item marked [VERIFY] must be confirmed with your district office and counsel before you act on it. This checklist is educational guidance, not legal advice.

How to use this checklist#

Try this now (10 minutes): tick, or write a date next to, the first three boxes in "Before the first lesson" below, and put a 30-minute meeting with your counselor on your calendar. If you do nothing else today, do that.

  • Print it. Tick it. Date it. Make one copy for yourself and one for your counselor.
  • It is organized by when to act (Section 1), then by topic (Sections 2 to 10).
  • Every item is small enough to finish in a sitting. Most items have an owner column; write names in.
  • You do not need to do everything before the first lesson. The "Before the first lesson" list at the top of Section 1 is the minimum.

1. The 12-week implementation timeline#

Before the first lesson (the minimum list)#

  • Checkbox: The staff reporting route is written down, on a card, in every teacher's binder (H7 My School's Help Map). [VERIFY the route and the deadline with district counsel.]
  • Checkbox: The school counselor knows the plan and has agreed to be available for sensitive lessons.
  • Checkbox: Staff have had at least one training (the 90-Minute or Half-Day Workshop), including the disclosure practice.
  • Checkbox: Families have been told what Hackles is, and have a way to ask questions and preview materials.
  • Checkbox: The first lessons are scheduled (not squeezed in).
  • Checkbox: There is a written plan for opt-outs, with a respectful alternative activity.
  • Checkbox: The plan for evaluating the program uses no student data.

2. Policy alignment#

Hackles is a classroom curriculum. It must fit inside your school's and district's existing rules. Walk through this list with your administrator or district office.

Policy alignment: what to write down#

Keep a one-page "Hackles Alignment Note" in your files, with the date and who confirmed each item. If a policy and the kit differ, the policy wins. Tell the Hackles lead so that teachers are never given conflicting instructions.

3. Counselor involvement#

The school counselor (or psychologist or social worker) is the most important partner you have. The counselor is not a bottleneck, and teachers must never wait for them before they report. But the counselor makes the program safer.

The counselor's roles (agree on these)#

  • Checkbox: Advisor on sensitive lessons. Reviews lesson plans and the calendar. Agrees which lessons are sensitive in your building (see the Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit, Section 1).
  • Checkbox: On call during sensitive lessons. Available during and right after each one, with a backup.
  • Checkbox: Safety partner at staff training. In the room or next door during the disclosure role-play.
  • Checkbox: First stop for staff support. Debriefs a teacher after a disclosure (same day).
  • Checkbox: Small group host. Offers small groups for students with shared concerns (being targeted online, supporting a friend).
  • Checkbox: Anonymous summary. Gives the principal a short monthly tally with no names: lessons delivered, referrals, themes, needs.
  • Checkbox: Co-teacher option. Can co-teach the last five minutes of a sensitive lesson.

Meeting agenda (30 minutes with the counselor)#

Meeting agenda (30 minutes with the counselor)
Min Topic
0-5 Why this program; what the kit contains (Teacher Primer)
5-12 The sensitive lesson list and timeline (Counselor Toolkit)
12-18 The reporting route and any gaps (Disclosure and Incident Guide, H7)
18-24 Counselor capacity: how many lessons per week can you be on call for? What do you need from me?
24-28 Teacher support after a disclosure (self-care)
28-30 Next steps

Protect the counselor#

  • Do not add this to the counselor's workload without removing something. Offer coverage for lunch duty or paperwork.
  • Plan a backup for when the counselor is absent (assistant principal, psychologist, social worker, district crisis team). [VERIFY]
  • Schedule sensitive lessons to spread out across the week and the term. See Section 5.

5. Schedules#

Where does a 15-minute lesson fit?#

Where does a 15-minute lesson fit?
Slot Works well for Notes
Advisory or homeroom 6-12 A natural, protected block
Morning meeting or circle K-5 Fits the hook-activity-takeaway shape
Library or media center K-12 The librarian can co-teach
Technology or computer class 3-12 Good for passphrase and phish lessons
Health, counseling or SEL block 6-12 Good for sensitive lessons with counselor support
Specials rotation K-5 Library or counselor can lead
Hackles Day (once a year) All See the Hackles Day Kit. It reinforces, it does not replace, weekly lessons.

Pacing#

  • Weekly, 10 weeks (one 15-minute lesson per week) is the recommended pace. A longer or shorter pace is fine.
  • Start with non-sensitive lessons (for example K-2 Lessons 2, 3, 4; 3-5 Lessons 2, 3; 6-8 Lessons 2, 3; 9-12 Lessons 1, 2, 3) to build teacher confidence.
  • Spread sensitive lessons out. For grades 9-12, do not teach Lessons 5 and 7 on the same day, on a day with a major exam, on a Friday afternoon before a long weekend, or after a recent known incident in that class, without counselor input (as the 9-12 Teacher Guide advises).
  • Counselor availability first. Put the sensitive lesson on the calendar only when you know the counselor is free.
  • Mid-morning is best. Avoid the last period on a Friday.

Tone rules for family communication#

  • Warm, hopeful, practical.
  • No scary statistics, no stories about real children, no blame.
  • "We are partners. You know your child best."
  • "If you would like to opt out of a lesson, we will respect that."

7. Supporting sensitive-lesson opt-outs#

Respecting a family's wishes protects trust and the child. Handle every request with warmth and confidentiality. The full process is in the Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit, Section 6. This is what the principal owns.

Principles#

  • Checkbox: Never penalize the student, academically or socially.
  • Checkbox: Never ask a child to explain a family's decision.
  • Checkbox: Offer the review first. Many opt-outs become participation after a family sees the materials.
  • Checkbox: The core help message still reaches the child, in family-approved language: "If anything ever worries you, tell a grown-up you trust. You are never in trouble."
  • Checkbox: Confirm local rules. In some places opt-out is required, optional or not allowed. [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: Do not use the words "left out." Do not suggest a family is putting a child at risk.
  • Checkbox: Keep records confidential, per policy.

What to say to a concerned family (use a calm tone)#

  • "I can hear you care a lot about your child. Thank you for contacting me."
  • "Would you like to see the lesson? I can walk you through it."
  • "It uses no graphic content. It focuses on telling a trusted adult."
  • "If you prefer to opt out, we will respect that and offer an alternative. We will make sure your child knows who to tell if something ever worries them."

What not to say: "Your child will be left out." "You are keeping them unsafe." "Everyone else is doing it."

8. Data-free evaluation#

Hackles collects no student accounts and no student data, ever. You can still tell whether the program is working, by looking at adult implementation, adult confidence, classroom-level themes and counselor tallies without names.

A note about disclosures as a measure#

More children telling is a sign of success, not of failure. Do not use a rise in referrals as a mark against a teacher, a grade or the school. Explain this to your board and your community. [VERIFY any reporting your district requires.]

What not to do#

  • Do not collect student names, scores, accounts or device information.
  • Do not track individual students' progress, test results or responses.
  • Do not record students on video or audio. [VERIFY policy]
  • Do not ask students to share personal experiences for "evidence."
  • Do not add a software tool or a platform that collects student data.
  • Do not compare teachers by their disclosure counts.
  • Do not use student quotes in reports that could identify a child.

9. Disclosure readiness (principal's checklist)#

When Hackles works, children tell. Your school has to be ready.

  • Checkbox: Everyone knows the route. Every teacher has H7. Ask a few teachers, kindly and not as a test: "Who do you call first?" If anyone hesitates, fix the card, not the person.
  • Checkbox: The First 10 Minutes card (H6) is in every classroom binder.
  • Checkbox: Staff know their personal duty. Telling the principal does not by itself satisfy a teacher's duty to report in many places. Never say "tell me and I'll handle it" in a way that stops staff from reporting. [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: You, as principal, know your own duties. [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: Coverage plan: if a teacher needs to meet a child, a colleague covers the class.
  • Checkbox: Private, visible meeting spaces are identified (door open or windows).
  • Checkbox: Device handling policy: staff know not to look at, copy, forward or screenshot images, and who secures a device. [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: Documentation: the Disclosure and Incident Record template is available, with secure storage.
  • Checkbox: Crisis plan for a child at risk of self-harm: who, where, how, and 988. [VERIFIED 2026-10-05: 988lifeline.org]
  • Checkbox: Staff support: teachers know the debrief route and the EAP. [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: No retaliation: a teacher who reports in good faith will be supported.
  • Checkbox: No blame in the building: staff understand "never your fault" applies to adults too.

Colleagues, we are launching Hackles, a free 15-minute-lesson program that teaches students to notice when something online feels off and to Pause, Check and Tell. It uses paper and pencil, collects no student data, and comes with word-for-word scripts. We will begin with a [90-minute or 3-hour] workshop on [date]. You will not have to be a tech expert, you will not be asked to share any personal stories, and you will have fun. [Counselor name] is partnering with us for every sensitive lesson. Thank you. Family newsletter blurb#

Hackles: helping our kids trust their gut online. This term, students will learn a simple routine: Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell. The lessons are short, practical and not scary. We do not collect any student accounts or data. You are welcome to preview any lesson, and we will tell you in advance about any lesson on a sensitive topic. Please contact [CONTACT: to be added] with questions. Learn more at quillsup.org.

When someone asks "Is this age-appropriate?"#

"Great question. The lessons are age-banded. For the youngest, the focus is: pause, ask a grown-up, and tell. For older students, we use fictional cases and practice what to say and who to tell. Nothing is graphic, and nobody is asked to share a personal story. We are glad to walk you through the materials."

When someone says "Won't this give kids ideas?"#

"The lessons teach recognition and response, not how to commit harm. They give children words to say no and to tell. Research on prevention suggests skills-based teaching, not fear, is more effective [VERIFY Finkelhor et al., 2021]."
