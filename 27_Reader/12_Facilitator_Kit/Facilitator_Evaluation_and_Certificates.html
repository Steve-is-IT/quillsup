1. Principles for evaluating adult training#

Try this now (3 minutes): write the one question you most want answered about your next session (for example, "Did people feel safe to pass?") and put it on a sticky note on your clipboard. Everything below is optional machinery in service of that question.

  • Anonymous. Participants are never asked to put a name on a survey. If you need names for PD credit, use the separate sign-in sheet (H1).
  • Adults only. Every question is about the participant's own learning, confidence and plans. No student data of any kind is collected, requested or reported.
  • Short. A survey that takes more than five minutes gets skipped.
  • Used. Read the results within a week and tell participants what you will change. "You said, we did."
  • Safe. Do not ask participants to describe personal experiences or incidents. If a survey comment reveals a child may be in danger, follow your reporting route. [VERIFY policy] Remind participants at the top of the form: "Please do not write any details about a real child or any personal incident here. If you need support, ask [counselor name]."
  • Celebrate and improve. Share one thing that worked and one thing you will change.
  • Paper or a district-approved form. If you use an online form, use a district-approved tool and turn off name and email collection. [VERIFY district policy]
  • Peer coaching and surveys are separate from personnel evaluation. [VERIFY with your contract.]

3. Quick knowledge check (anonymous, 3 minutes, with answers)#

Optional. Use at the beginning and at the end (print on both sides or use two copies) to see learning at the group level, not the individual level. No names. The facilitator reads the answers aloud afterward for fun.

  1. What are the four steps of the Hackles routine?
    (Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.)

  2. Name the SPOT signs.
    (Secrets asked, Pressure, Offer too good, Trick to move you.)

  3. Which of these should you say to a child who tells you something hard? Circle all that apply.
    (a) "Thank you for telling me." (b) "Why did you do that?" (c) "This is not your fault." (d) "I won't tell anyone." (e) "I can't keep this secret, because I want to get you help. I will tell you who I tell."
    (Answers: a, c, e.)

  4. True or false: If a child shows you an intimate image on their phone, you should look at it to check how serious it is.
    (False. Do not view, copy, forward or screenshot. Secure the device per policy and report. [VERIFY policy])

  5. What is the "own door" rule?
    (Do not use the link or number in the message. Open the app or site yourself, call a number you already have, or ask in person.)

  6. True or false: If a child's hackles went up but they were tricked anyway, it is their fault.
    (False. It is never their fault. They tell someone.)

  7. A lesson is fun, safe and ______.
    (Skill-based.)

  8. Who is on your school's Help Map? Write one name and how to reach them.
    (Individual answer. This checks that H7 is filled in.)

Scoring tip: do not score individuals. Count how many people missed each question, and spend 60 seconds revisiting the most-missed one.

6. Facilitator self-reflection and co-facilitator debrief#

Self-reflection (5 minutes, same day)#

Self-reflection (5 minutes, same day)
Question My answer
What went well? (two specifics)
Which moments had the most energy?
Which moments dipped? Why?
Did I keep my promises (no scare, no personal stories, laughter)?
Did I model the methods (movement, competition, role-play, feedback)?
Did I say "pull back the curtain" after each segment?
Did I protect the disclosure block (no points, no audience, rails, de-role)?
Did anyone step out or pass? Did I handle it gracefully?
Did anyone share a real incident? Did I follow the script?
What did I say that I would change?
What did I not know, and what will I find out?
What is one thing I will do differently next time?
How am I doing (1 to 5)? Who will I talk to?

Co-facilitator debrief (10 minutes)#

  1. Each person shares one glow about the other.
  2. Each shares one grow about the day.
  3. Review the timing at the checkpoints (0:47, 1:40, 2:15 for the half-day; 0:30, 0:44, 1:04 for the 90-minute).
  4. Review the exit ticket themes.
  5. Decide: one thing to change before next time.
  6. Check in with each other: "How are you?"

Facilitator observation (when a Lead Facilitator observes)#

7. How to read the results (no student data)#

Step 1: Count and summarize (15 minutes)#

Step 1: Count and summarize (15 minutes)
Question How to summarize
Part A statements Compute the average for each statement (1 to 5). Note any average below 4.
Part B confidence before and after Compute the average before and after for each item. Look at the size of the change (for example, from 2.4 to 4.1).
Part C open comments Group into themes (fun, safe, skills, pace, materials, needs). Count how many comments in each. Pick two quotes (anonymous, with no identifying details) to share.
Part D recommendation Compute the percentage who answered 9 or 10 (promoters) minus the percentage who answered 0 to 6 (detractors).
Knowledge check Count how many people missed each question.

Step 2: Interpret (15 minutes)#

  • High fun but low confidence: more practice and coaching needed.
  • High confidence but low safe: revisit the rails and the disclosure block.
  • Low pace: trim or reorder.
  • "I need more time": use micro-PD sessions as follow-up.
  • "I need the counselor": schedule time and the reporting route.
  • "I do not know my reporting route": fix this first. Ask your administrator. [VERIFY]

Step 3: Share and act (10 minutes)#

Share a one-page "You said, we did" with staff within a week:

You said: [two quotes or themes].
We did: [one change].
Next: [the next session or step].

What not to do#

  • Do not link a survey to a person.
  • Do not use survey data to evaluate a teacher.
  • Do not report individuals to administrators.
  • Do not collect or report student data of any kind.
  • Do not use quotes that identify someone.
  • Do not post raw comments publicly.

Storing results#

Keep paper forms in a locked drawer for the school year, then shred them (or follow your district's records rules). [VERIFY] Keep the summary (not the forms) in your files.
