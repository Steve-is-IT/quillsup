Hackles Facilitator Kit, Document 3 of 10. A tight, energetic version of the half-day workshop for a single PD block, early-release afternoon or PLC. Read the Half-Day Facilitator Guide first for the full scripts and safety reasoning; this file is a complete, runnable agenda with the key lines included.

1. What you get in 90 minutes#

By the end of 90 minutes, each teacher can:

  1. Say and do the Hackles routine: Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell.
  2. Sort fake messages using SPOT or FLAGS and name the next move.
  3. Say "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble." in their own voice, and avoid the phrases that stop children from telling.
  4. Practice a disclosure response in a safe, structured role-play.
  5. Teach a 3-minute speed lesson and give or receive feedback using "Is the lesson fun, safe, skill-based?"
  6. Know their school's help map and make one commitment.

Is the 90-minute version enough?#

It is a good start, not a complete program. The research the kit leans on says repeated, skills-based teaching beats one-off events (Finkelhor and colleagues, 2021 [VERIFY]). Treat this block as the kickoff and follow it with the ten 15-minute Micro-PD sessions at your regular staff meetings. Do not cut the disclosure block to make time for anything else.

2. Before you run it#

Team#

  • Lead facilitator (principal, coach, librarian or tech coordinator).
  • Safety partner (counselor, school psychologist or social worker): in the room or on call next door for 0:44-1:04 at the latest. If nobody is available, do not run the disclosure block. Use the First 10 Minutes review only (see Troubleshooting in the Half-Day Guide).
  • Helper (optional): runs the timer, music and the Phisher cameo.

Preparation (do these a week before)#

  • Checkbox: Fill in H7 (My School's Help Map), confirming every number. [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: Ask your administrator: what is our state's staff reporting rule and deadline, and does telling the principal satisfy it? [VERIFY]
  • Checkbox: Print H1 to H8, H10 (relay cards R1 to R16), H11, H12, H14, H15 (see the Print Plan in the Handouts file). For this version use only 8 derby cards: D1, D2, D3, D6, D9, D12, D13, D15.
  • Checkbox: Send the participant email (Half-Day Guide, Appendix D), edited for 90 minutes.
  • Checkbox: Rehearse the Segment 6 role-play demo with your co-facilitator.
  • Checkbox: Recruit a Phisher (a colleague with a silly hat) for 60 seconds on stage.
  • Checkbox: Set up: team tables of 4 to 6 (4 to 5 teams), a Reset Corner, a scoreboard, the Parking Lot wall, the Group Agreements poster (H2).
  • Checkbox: Download the slides marked "90" from the Slide Deck Outline (slides 1, 2, 3, 7 to 16, 19, 20, 21, 27, 29 to 34, 36 to 42).

4. Segment run sheet#

Segment 1. Welcome and Gut-Check Line-Up (0:00-0:07)#

Segment 1. Welcome and Gut-Check Line-Up (0:00-0:07)
Min What happens
0:00-0:02 Welcome and the three promises
0:02-0:07 Line-Up with three statements (use 2, 3 and 5 from the Half-Day Guide)

SAY:

"Good afternoon! Welcome to Hackles. I'm [name], and I'm not a cybersecurity expert. I got volunteered. If you can listen, stay steady, and know who to call, you can teach this curriculum well. We have ninety minutes. I promise three things: nobody will scare you, nobody has to tell a personal story, and you will laugh at least once."

"Everyone stand. This wall is a line. This end is HACKLES DOWN: calm, I've got this. That end is HACKLES UP: something feels off, I'm unsure. I'll read a statement. Walk to the spot that fits you. There are no wrong places."

Statements: (2) "I know who I would call, by name, if a student told me something frightening." (3) "I know my state's deadline for reporting." (5) "I think kids learn this best when it is fun."

After statement 3: "If many of us sit at the Hackles Up end, good. That is the reason we're here. By 1:27 you'll have the number in your hand."

Pull back the curtain: "Three questions, everyone answered, no worksheet. You can do this with a class in two minutes."

Segment 2. Why this, why us, and the Hackles routine (0:07-0:14)#

Segment 2. Why this, why us, and the Hackles routine (0:07-0:14)
Min What happens
0:07-0:10 Why this, why us (condensed)
0:10-0:11 Group Agreements (thumbs-up)
0:11-0:14 Teach the routine with hand signals; pair-share

SAY (condensed):

"Most children use technology safely, most of the time. But some will be tricked, some treated unkindly, and a few targeted by people who are good at it. What protects them isn't a fear speech. Skills-based teaching, repeated over time, is more promising than a one-off scary assembly (Finkelhor and colleagues, 2021 [VERIFY]). And the biggest obstacle is shame: children stay silent because they fear blame, punishment or losing their phone. So the most powerful sentence in the kit is: 'Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble.' Everything today aims at making that sentence automatic."

Agreements (read H2 aloud, thumbs-up check): fictional cases, pass is allowed, step out any time, no shame, what we share stays here except when a child may be in danger, fun on purpose, phones in pockets.

Routine (say it slow, fast, whisper):

"Hackles up? Something feels off: too good, pushy, secret, or scary. (Hand brushed up the back of the neck.) Pause. (Flat palm.) Stop. Breathe. Check. (Circle fingers at your eye.) Who is it? What do they want? How fast? Tell. (Hand on chest, open palm out.) A trusted person. Every time. A false alarm is fine. If you missed it or got tricked, it is never your fault."

Pair-share (45 seconds): "When might a student need this? When might you?"

Segment 3. Fake Phish Derby and Live Phish Drop (0:14-0:30)#

Segment 3. Fake Phish Derby and Live Phish Drop (0:14-0:30)
Min What happens
0:14-0:15 Rules
0:15-0:21 Teams sort the 8 cards (D1, D2, D3, D6, D9, D12, D13, D15) into Hackles Up and Hackles Down, name the clue (SPOT) and the next move
0:21-0:22 Live Phish Drop (60 seconds)
0:22-0:30 Reveal and debrief using the Half-Day Guide Appendix A. Score and award a pickle trophy.

SAY (rules):

"Welcome to the Fake Phish Derby! Each team has eight messages. Fictional, silly brand names, no real accounts. For each: Hackles Up or Hackles Down. Then name the clue (SPOT: Secrets asked, Pressure, Offer too good, Trick to move you), then the next move. One point for the right pile, one for the clue, one for the move. Some of these are real and boring, so don't panic at everything. Six minutes. Go!"

Live Phish Drop (60 seconds). The Phisher enters at 0:21 wearing a silly hat.

PHISHER: "CONGRATULATIONS, TABLE THREE! You have WON a mystery prize! Hurry, only ten seconds, I need your team's secret password RIGHT NOW! Shhh, don't tell the facilitator!"

FACILITATOR: "Hackles up? Show me the signal! Pause! Check: what do we see? (Pressure, prize, secret, password.) Tell: who do we tell? (Everyone points at the facilitator.) Thank you, Mr. Phisher, you've been Paused, Checked and Told."

Scoring in this version: 8 cards at 3 points each is 24 points, plus the Relay (20). Update the scoreboard totals accordingly.

Safe use: a colleague who knows it is a game; no real passwords, accounts or brands; no fake emails to real inboxes. [VERIFY your district policy on phishing simulations.]

Reveal tips: On D12 say: "Same message, two answers. If you just asked for the code, it's fine and boring. If you didn't, someone may be trying to get into your account. That's why we check, not just react." On D15: "Hackles Down is a real answer. If every card makes us panic, kids learn to ignore the signal."

Pull back the curtain: "Sorting, a deadline, a surprise, a debrief where I asked questions instead of lecturing."

Segment 4. Say-It-Right Relay (short) (0:30-0:40)#

Segment 4. Say-It-Right Relay (short) (0:30-0:40)
Min What happens
0:30-0:31 Rules
0:31-0:34 Round 1: Sort the 16 relay cards (R1 to R16) into HELPFUL and NOT HELPFUL
0:34-0:37 Round 2: Rewrite relay. Pass the marker; rewrite 4 NOT HELPFUL phrases
0:37-0:40 Round 3: Thank-You Showdown. One volunteer per team delivers the sentence in a voice (Wizard, Gentle Grandparent, Calm Pilot); the room claps (about 2 minutes), then the debrief points below (about 1 minute)

SAY (rules):

"This is a relay race for words. Sixteen cards, each something an adult might say to a child who tells them something hard. Sort them into helpful and not helpful. Then rewrite four of the not-helpfuls, passing the marker. Then one volunteer per team delivers the most important sentence in the whole kit, and the room votes by applause."

Debrief points (30 seconds): "Why did you click" sounds like blame. "I won't tell anyone" can never be promised: "I can't keep this a secret, because I want to get you help. I will tell you who I tell." Never ask to see an image. Point to H5.

Scoring: Round 1 up to 8 points, Round 2 up to 8, Round 3 up to 4. Total 20.

Segment 5. Reset (0:40-0:44)#

SAY:

"Everybody up! This is Simon Says, but I'm 'Hackle,' and you only move when I say 'Hackle says.' Hackle says: Hackles up! Pause palm! Magnifying glass! Tell wave! Hackle says: shake out your hands. Hackle says: one slow breath in... and out."

90 seconds of Simon Says, then a slow breath (in four, hold four, out six), then 2 minutes for water and for the safety partner to arrive and be introduced.

Note: this is also the time to do the room reset for the role-play. Trios will spread out.

Segment 6. Disclosure Role-Play with Safety Rails (0:44-1:04)#

No points. No timers shown. No audience.

Before you begin: counselor in the room or just outside; traffic-light cards out; content note ready; H6 and H7 visible.

Segment 6. Disclosure Role-Play with Safety Rails (0:44-1:04)
Min What happens
0:44-0:48 Framing and demonstration (see below)
0:48-0:49 Form trios (decks and checklists already on the tables)
0:49-0:53 Round 1: warm-up (DR1 "The Oops"). 3 min scene + 1 min observer feedback. Swap roles.
0:53-0:57 Round 2: a card for your grade band (DR2 to DR6). 3 plus 1. Swap roles.
0:57-1:01 Round 3: a different card (any of DR2 to DR7). 3 plus 1, so everyone plays Teller, Listener and Observer once.
1:01-1:04 De-role and debrief (de-role 1 min, debrief questions 1.5 min, counselor close 30 seconds)

SAY (framing):

"We're about to practice the most important moment in this workshop: the first minutes after a child tells you something hard. Three things first. One: content. Everything is fictional and non-graphic, but this subject can touch real life for any of us. Two: you're in charge of your own participation. You have three traffic-light cards. Green: keep going. Yellow: slow down, I need a minute. Red: pause the scene right now, no explanation. You can also sit out the whole segment. [Counselor name] is here. Three: no real stories. If you slip into one, show a red card. This is practice, not therapy."

SAY (demo, with co-facilitator as a child who lost their game account):

(Child, small voice: "I think I messed up. I clicked a link and my account is doing weird stuff. I'm going to be in so much trouble.")
(Facilitator, over the top: "WHAT?! Why on EARTH did you click that?! Give me that tablet!") "The child went quiet. Let's try again."
(Child repeats. Facilitator, calm, sitting at an angle: "Thank you for telling me. You're not in trouble. This happens to smart people all the time. Tell me what happened.") "That's the skill. That was the only funny example. The rest are serious, so we're gentle with each other."

Roles and rules (say, 30 seconds): "Teller reads the card and stays in character; if the listener asks for more than the card says, say 'I don't want to say more right now.' Listener uses open questions only. Observer uses the checklist and shares one glow and one try."

De-role (read slowly):

"Stand up. Shake out your hands. Say to your trio: 'I'm [real name]. That was pretend.' One slow breath in... and out. Debrief, skills only, no stories. What's one phrase you're keeping? What was the hardest thing to avoid saying? Who needs to look at their Help Map right now and fix a blank line?"

Key debrief points: thank first, no blame; open questions only; never promise secrecy; do not investigate or ask to see images; report personally and promptly as law and policy require (telling the principal may not be enough) [VERIFY]; write down the child's own words; look after yourself.

Counselor close (60 seconds): "If anything in this block brought something up for you, I'm available today or later. You don't have to say what."

If someone shares a real incident: follow Section 8 of the Half-Day Guide: thank, pause, move privately with the safety partner, state the honest limit on secrecy, do not probe, offer support, follow up within 24 hours.

Segment 7. Speed-Teach Round (1:04-1:20)#

Segment 7. Speed-Teach Round (1:04-1:20)
Min What happens
1:04-1:06 Model: the facilitator teaches MT2 "Pause and Ask" in 90 seconds and names fun, safe, skill-based
1:06-1:08 Trios choose a card from MT1 to MT12 (non-sensitive only) and read it
1:08-1:20 Three rounds in each trio: 3 minutes teach + 1 minute feedback (Two Glows and a Grow, H12). The chime marks each 4 minutes; switch at once.

SAY (model line):

"I'll teach 90 seconds of K-2 Lesson 3, 'Pause and Ask.' You're kindergartners. (Teach with the dramatic pop-up, Pause Palm, 'I'll ask!') What did I do? Fun: a silly voice and a prop, everybody moving in 30 seconds. Safe: no scares, no personal stories, I said 'telling is never trouble.' Skill: you practiced a Pause Palm and the words 'I'll ask.' Fun, safe, skill-based."

Teach structure for 3 minutes: Hook 30 seconds, Activity 1:45, Takeaway 45 seconds, ending with "Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell." The real lessons are 15 minutes (Hook 2, Activity 8, Discuss 3, Takeaway 2); this is a speed version.

Feedback line: "I noticed ___ at . That made me . What if you tried ___?"

Pull back the curtain: "You just did peer observation in four minutes per teacher. That is the engine of the Peer Coaching Guide."

Segment 8. Monday Plan and Commitment Walk (1:20-1:27)#

Segment 8. Monday Plan and Commitment Walk (1:20-1:27)
Min What happens
1:20-1:23 Fill any blank on H7; write "I will find out ___ from ___ by ___"
1:23-1:25 H15 Commitment Card: one action, one date, one buddy
1:25-1:27 Commitment Walk: find someone new, say it in a sentence, high-five, twice

SAY:

"Look at your Help Map. A blank line is a question we'll answer this week, not a failure. Write one commitment on your card: not ten. 'I will teach K-2 Lesson 3 on Thursday.' 'I will ask the counselor to come to our PLC.' 'I will put the Say This, Not That card on my desk.' Name a buddy who will ask you about it next week. Stand, find someone new, say it, high-five, twice."

Segment 9. Closing, awards and pledge (1:27-1:30)#

Awards (60 seconds; your helper totals scores during Segment 8): Phish-Proof Champions (highest total) and Best Phisher. Everyone gets a sticker; certificates go out at the door.

Pledge (read together):

"I will notice. I will pause. I will check. I will tell. I will thank children who tell me. I will never blame them. I will keep telling until someone helps. And I will have fun, on purpose. Hackles up? Pause. Check. Tell."

Exit ticket (H15) at the door. Certificates (see Facilitator Evaluation and Certificates). Say: "Remember the three promises? Hold up your hand if we kept them."

5. Splitting the 90 minutes into two 45-minute sessions (PLC format)#

If your calendar gives you two 45-minute blocks (for example, two PLC days a week apart), use this split.

5. Splitting the 90 minutes into two 45-minute sessions (PLC format)
Session Content Minutes
Part A (45) Welcome and Line-Up (7), Why this and the routine (7), Derby (16), Relay (10), Reset (4), 1 minute for transitions 45
Part B (45) Recap and re-grounding: group agreements, routine, signal (3), Disclosure Role-Play with Safety Rails (20), Speed-Teach Round (14: model 2, pick a card 1, three rounds of 2 minutes teach + 1 minute feedback = 9, 2 spare for transitions), Monday Plan and close (8: Help Map and commitment card 5, one pass of the Commitment Walk 1, exit ticket and pledge 2) 45

Use the first minute of Part B for the recap: "Last time we practiced the routine and the sorting. Today: what to say when a child tells us, and teaching a lesson in two minutes." Part B holds the disclosure block, so book the counselor for it.

6. Room setup, quick#

  • Tables in 4 to 5 team clusters of 5 to 6 chairs.
  • One open wall for the Line-Up.
  • Reset Corner with a chair, water and a sign.
  • Scoreboard with team names.
  • Parking Lot sticky-note wall.
  • A table with the pickle trophy and stickers.
  • Music and a chime.

7. Troubleshooting (short)#

7. Troubleshooting (short)
Problem Do this
Counselor cannot attend Keep the counselor on the phone and next door; if no one is available, skip Segment 6 and use a discussion of the H6 card, and book the role-play for later
A teacher shares a real incident Thank, pause, move privately, state the honest limit on secrecy, do not probe, safety partner steps in, follow up within 24 hours (Half-Day Guide Section 8)
Someone wants to demonstrate an attack "We teach recognition and response, not attacks."
Skeptic: "This is not my job" "Your role is recognition and response: notice, listen, reassure, report, hand off."
Low energy Move Simon Says earlier; switch the music
Too many people Duplicate role-play spaces; two safety partners; teams of 6
Very few people Teams of 3; run the Derby with 8 cards in pairs
