Hands-On Hackers (the good kind) (ages 8-10)#

Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell

How to use this file: Print the pages you need. Cut cards along the table rows (each row is one card) or paste each card onto an index card. Print cards on cardstock and laminate if you can. Teacher keys are marked "Teacher key - do not hand out." Every scenario is invented and generic. No brand names, no real people.

R-0: Posters (print one of each and tape to the wall)#

Poster 1: Pause - Check - Tell#

HACKLES UP? (hand brushed up the back of your neck) Something feels off? That uh-oh feeling is a gut check. False alarms are fine.
PAUSE. (flat palm) Stop. Hands off. Take a breath.
CHECK. (magnifier fingers) Does it feel off? Look for SPOT: Secrets, Pressure, Offer too good, Trick to move you.
TELL. (hand on chest) Tell a trusted adult. Keep climbing the Ladder until someone helps.
You are never in trouble for telling. It is never your fault.
Illustration note Hackle, a baby hedgehog with pale-yellow, tangerine-tipped quills standing up, one paw up like a stop sign, one holding a magnifying glass, and a speech bubble that says "I need help."

Poster 2: The Help Ladder#

RUNG 3: REPORT IT. Use the report or block button (with a grown-up), or tell the counselor, principal, or a helpline.
RUNG 2: TELL A TRUSTED ADULT. Parent or guardian, teacher, counselor, coach, relative, neighbor.
RUNG 1: TELL A FRIEND YOU TRUST. "Will you come with me to tell a grown-up?"
If a rung does not help, KEEP CLIMBING.
Illustration note a friendly ladder against a bright wall with the squad climbing it and Hackle at the top.

Poster 3: SPOT#

Poster 3: SPOT
Letter Sign Kid words
S Secrets asked Wants a password, code, address, or says "don't tell."
P Pressure "Hurry!" "Last chance!" "Your account will be deleted!"
O Offer too good Free coins, a surprise prize, an easy win.
T Trick to move you Click this link, switch apps, buy a gift card.

Poster 4: Red Flags in Game Chat#

Poster 4: Red Flags in Game Chat
Flag Sounds like
Switch apps "Let's talk somewhere more private."
Secrets "Don't tell your parents."
Gifts "I gave you coins, so now you owe me."
Pressure or guilt "If you were a real friend..."
Personal questions Where you live, your school, who is home.
Pictures or camera Asks for a picture of you, or to turn on a camera.

Exit script: "Not doing that. Bye." Then BLOCK. REPORT. TELL.

Poster 5: The Four Moves#

Poster 5: The Four Moves
Move What it means
Say It Calmly say "That's not cool" (only if you feel safe).
Stand With Check on the person, include them, be a friend.
Keep It Do not pass it on or like it. Keep the message to show an adult. (Never keep or forward private body pictures. Tell right away.)
Tell It Tell a trusted adult. Use report and block with a grown-up.

Poster 6: Source, Sense, Second Look#

Poster 6: Source, Sense, Second Look
Check Ask yourself
Source Who made or sent this?
Sense Does it make sense, or does it rush me?
Second look Can I check another way, or ask a grown-up?

"I can't tell, I'm asking a grown-up" is a great answer.

Poster 7: OOPS#

Poster 7: OOPS
Letter Step
O Oh, I'm okay. Breathe. No blame.
O Off and out. Stop clicking. Close it.
P People. Tell a trusted adult: "I made an oops and I need help."
S Secure it. With a grown-up, change passwords and check settings.

R-1: The Help Ladder#

R-1.1 Ladder Scenario Cards (12 cards; print one set per group)#

R-1.1 Ladder Scenario Cards (12 cards; print one set per group)
# Card
1 A pop-up in a game says you won a prize and asks for your address. You get a funny feeling. Who do you tell, and what is your first sentence?
2 Someone in a game chat asked you to keep a secret from your family. Which rung do you try first? What do you say?
3 You clicked a link and a strange page opened. You feel worried. What do you say to a grown-up?
4 A classmate sent you a mean message. You feel upset and embarrassed. Who can help, and what will you say?
5 You saw a video you did not mean to see and it made you uncomfortable. How do you tell?
6 A friend told you they got a message that scared them, and asked you not to tell. What do you do?
7 You shared a password with a friend and now your account looks different. What is your first sentence?
8 A player asked where you live. You did not answer. Who do you tell?
9 Someone made an account pretending to be you. Which rung do you try?
10 You are worried about a friend who seems sad because of things online. What can you do?
11 You told a grown-up, but they were too busy to listen. What is your next move?
12 You made a mistake and you feel really embarrassed. What do you say?

Teacher key (do not hand out)#

For every card: any trusted adult is a good answer, and the first sentence can be any of the three starters. Cards 6 and 10: Rung 1 friend care, then bring the friend to a trusted adult (Rung 2). Card 11: keep climbing to a different adult (a teacher, counselor or another family member). Cards 4, 9, 5: Rung 2 plus Rung 3 (report or block with a grown-up). Card 12: emphasize "I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something."

R-1.2 Sentence strips (cut apart)#

R-1.2 Sentence strips (cut apart)
Strip
"I need help with something online."
"Something happened and I'm not sure what to do."
"I'm not in trouble. I just need to tell you something."
"Will you come with me to tell a grown-up?" (for a friend)
Good Grown-up Reply: "Thank you for telling me. It is not your fault. Let's figure it out together."

R-1.3 Family Card and Reply Slip (Lesson 1)#

Family Card: The Help Ladder. Today your child practiced telling a trusted adult when something online feels wrong. The most helpful thing you can say is: "Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble." Ask tonight: "Who are your five trusted people? What would you say first?" Stay calm even if the story is upsetting; kids stop telling when adults react with anger.
Spanish-ready line (please have a fluent speaker check it) [VERIFY]: "Gracias por contarme. No estas en problemas."

R-1.3 Family Card and Reply Slip (Lesson 1)
Reply slip (cut and return)
Student name (optional): __ We talked about our trusted people. One thing we learned: ______

R-2: Passphrase Lab#

R-2.1 Passphrase Word List (36 words)#

How to use: roll two dice. The first die is the ROW. The second die is the COLUMN. Write down the word. Roll three times for a practice passphrase. No dice? Use number cards 1-6 or a spinner.

R-2.1 Passphrase Word List (36 words)
Row / Column 1 2 3 4 5 6
1 apple bridge cloud drum eagle forest
2 garden harbor island jungle kite lantern
3 meadow nickel orbit pepper quilt river
4 saddle tunnel umbrella violin walnut yarn
5 zebra anchor button candle dragon engine
6 feather glacier hammock igloo jigsaw kettle

Reminder (print on the sheet): Practice only! Never use a classroom practice passphrase for a real account. Never share a real passphrase with anyone except your parent or guardian.

Our pretend Guess-Machine makes 1,000 guesses every second. (Real computers can be much faster, and real passphrases use bigger word lists.)

R-4: Cipher Wheel and Codes#

R-4.1 The Cipher Wheel (two ways to make it)#

Option A: Paper-plate or paper circle wheel#

Materials: two paper circles (one large, about 18 cm across; one small, about 12 cm across), a brass fastener, a pencil.

  1. Pin the small circle on top of the large circle at the centers.
  2. Divide each circle into 26 equal wedges. (Fold in half, in half again, and so on, or use a ruler: each wedge is about 13.8 degrees.)
  3. On the OUTER ring of the big circle, write the alphabet A to Z around the edge in order.
  4. On the INNER (small) circle, write A to Z around the edge in order, so each inner letter is just below an outer letter.
  5. Turn the inner circle to set a key. For key 3, turn the inner circle until the inner D sits under the outer A.
  6. To ENCODE: find your message letter on the OUTER ring and write the letter touching it on the INNER ring.
  7. To DECODE: find the code letter on the INNER ring and write the outer letter.

Option B: Cipher Strips (no brass fastener needed)#

Print the strips below. Cut out the TOP strip (fixed alphabet). Print the BOTTOM strip twice and tape the two copies end to end to make a 52-letter sliding strip.

R-9: Family Code Word#

R-9.1 Call Script Cards (8 cards; pairs role-play)#

Caller reads the card. Kid says: "What's our code word?" If the Caller cannot answer, the Kid says: "I'm going to hang up and call you back on the number I know."

R-9.1 Call Script Cards (8 cards; pairs role-play)
# Caller reads
1 "Hi, it's your uncle! I'm stuck and need you to send me a gift card number right away. Don't tell your mom."
2 "I'm a friend of your dad. He asked me to pick you up from school today. Don't tell anyone."
3 "Hi, it's Grandma on a new number. Send me your passphrase for the family account."
4 "Hello, this is the bank. We need your parent's card number to keep your account safe."
5 "Hey cousin, I crashed my bike and need money sent right now. Don't tell Dad."
6 "This is a teacher. I'm in the parking lot. Come out now by yourself, don't tell the office."
7 "I'm your game friend. My mom said I can't talk on the game, so text this number secretly."
8 "Your mom asked me to take your tablet to be fixed. Hand it to me and don't tell her."

Teacher key (do not hand out)#

All 8 are tests of the plan: ask for the code word, then use the callback rule, and tell a trusted adult. Cards 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 include a secrecy red flag; 3 and 4 ask for passwords or card numbers; 7 asks to switch to a private channel.

R-9.2 Our Family Code Word Plan (take-home card)#

Our Family Code Word Plan (do NOT write the real code word here)

  3. These adults are allowed to pick me up: ______
  4. If I forget the word, I will: ______
  5. We practiced once. [ ]
  6. Each home I live in can have its own plan. [ ]

R-10: Oops Recovery#

R-10.1 OOPS Step Card (cut out and keep)#

OOPS Recovery Card
Oh, I'm okay. Breathe. Mistakes happen. I am not in trouble.
Off and out. Stop clicking. Close it. Do not reply.
People. Tell a trusted adult: "I made an oops and I need help."
Secure it. With a grown-up, change passwords and check settings.
Illustration note Hackle holding a big band-aid and a thumbs up.

R-10.2 Oops Scenario Cards (12 cards)#

For each: What is the first sentence you will say to a grown-up? Which OOPS steps will you use?

R-10.2 Oops Scenario Cards (12 cards)
# Card
1 You clicked a strange link in a message and a weird page opened.
2 You typed your password into a page after a pop-up told you to.
3 You told a friend your password and now someone else seems to be using your account.
4 You downloaded a "free cheat" for a game and now the device acts strange.
5 A pop-up says "Your tablet has a virus! Call this number now!"
6 You accidentally sent a message to the wrong group.
7 You used a grown-up's card to buy coins without asking.
8 You saw something scary or yucky online by accident.
9 You answered a chat from someone you did not know and told them your school name.
10 You posted a picture you now wish you had not posted.
11 You told someone online a secret and now they want more.
12 You tried to hide an oops yesterday and now it feels worse.

Teacher key (do not hand out)#

Teacher key (do not hand out), table 5
# Notes for discussion
1 Off and out, People. Grown-up checks the device.
2 Off and out, People, Secure it (change that password with a grown-up).
3 People, Secure it. No blame to the friend or you.
4 Off and out, People. Grown-up decides about scanning or removing it.
5 Do NOT call the number. Off and out, People.
6 People (ask a grown-up how to delete or say sorry), Oh I'm okay.
7 People (tell the grown-up; they can contact the store or seller).
8 Oh I'm okay, Off and out, People. Seeing something yucky is never your fault.
9 Off and out, People; use the Ladder and the Red Flags exit script.
10 People; a grown-up can help with privacy settings or taking it down.
11 OFF AND OUT, PEOPLE right away. Secrets with someone online are a red flag. Do not send anything more.
12 Oh I'm okay. Telling late is still telling: "I made an oops yesterday and I need help."

R-10.3 Family Card and Reply Slip (Lesson 10)#

Family Card: Oops Recovery. Your child learned a four-step plan: Oh I'm okay, Off and out, People, Secure it. The most powerful thing you can do is respond calmly when your child tells you about a mistake: "Thank you for telling me. Let's fix it together." Please avoid taking away devices as a response to telling.
