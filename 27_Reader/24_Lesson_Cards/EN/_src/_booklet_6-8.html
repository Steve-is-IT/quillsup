    • If a student tells you something: quick reference

Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell.

One page for every lesson. Read a card in two minutes, then teach it in fifteen: Hook 2, Activity 8, Discuss 3, Takeaway and exit ticket 2.

Pacing planner#

Suggested pacing: ten weeks, one 15-minute lesson per week. Teach Lessons 6, 7 and 8 Tuesday to Thursday, not last period, in that order, with at least two school days between them and never paired with another lesson on the same day.

Sensitive lesson timeline#

  • 3 weeks before. Confirm which lessons are sensitive, check your policy on notice and opt-out, confirm counselor availability.
  • 2 weeks before. Send the family pre-notice with the lesson overview and a review option.
  • 1 week before. Offer a preview. Collect opt-out requests. Arrange alternates.
  • 2 days before. Send the counselor heads-up message. Confirm coverage.
  • Day of. Calm opening and closing scripts. Counselor on call. Offer the ask-the-counselor option.
  • 24 hours after. Debrief. Check in with anyone who was upset. Document any disclosure.
  • 1 week after. Send the follow-up note with the take-home card.

Notes for this band#

  • Plus lessons are optional extras (Florida and any school that wants them); outside Florida use one at a time as needed.
  • Compressed: two lessons a week over 5 weeks (1+2, 3+4, 5+9, 10); keep 6, 7, 8 on separate days.

Lesson 1: Help Ladder: Real Practice#

If a student discloses

Counselor heads-up recommended. Disclosure rehearsal. Tell your counselor which day you teach it.

Big idea: Telling someone is a skill. Skills get easier with practice, and the first adult you tell is not the only adult you can tell.

Materials#

Workbook CF-1 and My Help Ladder card; R1 sets (Barrier Cards per group; Tell-3, Listener and Bumpy Response Cards per triad).

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Cut the R1 sets (Barrier Cards per group; three cards per triad).
  • Checkbox: Post the Help Ladder poster: yourself, a friend who comes with you, a trusted adult, a reporting channel.
  • Checkbox: Read the disclosure box below; expect something real.

Hook (2 min)#

Hackles up? That is the feeling that something is off. Trust it. "Welcome to Case Files. For ten weeks you are analysts: you read short files about made-up people, find the pattern, and work out the best next move. Nobody ever has to share anything personal. Silently, think of a time you wanted to tell someone about a problem and held back. People hold back because telling feels hard, however small the problem. So today we practice telling, like a free throw."

Activity (8 min): "Barrier Busters and Tell-3"#

  1. (1 min) Read CF-1. Jordan, twelve, gets a private game message: "Want to chat somewhere more private? Also, don't tell anyone, okay?" Jordan worries: "They'll take my tablet away."
  2. (2 min) Barrier Busters. Groups pick the two Barrier Cards that fit Jordan and write one true sentence that makes telling easier.
  3. (4 min) Tell-3 in triads (Teller, Listener, Observer): "I need your help with something online." One sentence about it. "Can you help me figure out what to do?" Listener: "Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble." Rotate every 80 seconds. In Round 3 the Listener is Bumpy and the Teller uses the Next-Rung Rule: "That was not the help I needed. I am going to tell someone else."
  4. (1 min) What happens next. The adult listens, may bring in other helpers, and promises: "I will help, and I will not blame you." Start the My Help Ladder card.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Why did Jordan almost not tell? Which barrier felt most real?"
  2. "What did the Listener say or do that made the Teller feel safer?"
  3. "What if the first person you tell does not help?" (Next-Rung Rule.)

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Pause, Check, Tell. Today was all about Tell. It is the strongest move in every case file, and it is never your fault. If the first person does not help, tell the next one."

Hand signal: Hackles Up (hand brushed up the neck, optional), then Pause, Check, Tell. Post it: "First adult did not help? Next rung."

Exit ticket#

Name one role you could tell and the first sentence you would say.

Fun Boost#

Best Reply Challenge. Each triad draws a Bumpy Response Card and writes the calmest, most believable Teller reply in 60 seconds. Applaud believable, not funny.

Level-Up#

Small-Stuff Rep. This week, practice Tell-3 on something tiny and true (a forgotten form). Tick "I did a rep" on a slip.

Differentiate#

Offer sentence starters and the Observer role for two rounds; drawing the Ladder is fine.

Family card#

Ask: "If something felt weird online, who would you tell first?" Say: "Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble."

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing."
  • "This is not your fault, and you are not in trouble."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "Are you sure?" or "Why didn't you tell me sooner?"
  • "I won't tell anyone."
  • Asking to see messages, photos or accounts.

First 10 Minutes: see the Disclosure Guide. Then: tell your counselor or designated person right away. Follow your school policy and your state's reporting law.

Lesson 2: Account Defense#

Big idea: One reused password is a key that opens many doors. Unique passwords plus a second lock turn a break-in into a dead end.

Materials#

R2 set per group (6 Door Cards, Master Key, 6 Unique Keys, 2 Second Lock Cards, Leak Card, Tally Sheet); Workbook CF-2.

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Bag one R2 set per group.
  • Checkbox: Know the round answers: six doors open, one, four.
  • Checkbox: Read CF-2 once so you can tell it plainly.

Hook (2 min)#

It is never the target's fault when a group piles on."

Activity (8 min): "The Escalation Meter"#

  1. (1 min) Read CF-9. In a class chat, someone posts a goofy photo of Tess with a mean caption. Laughing faces. "Let's all stop talking to Tess." Someone screenshots the chat into another group. Tess leaves. Amir saw it all and did nothing.
  2. (5 min) Escalation Meter. ANNOYING: rude, not harming. HURTING: repeated meanness, exclusion, humiliation. TELL NOW: threats, hate, sexual pictures or pressure, self-harm talk, private information spread. Groups place 12 cards and write the first move: ignore, support privately, say something kind, save evidence, report, tell an adult.
  3. (1 min) Save, do not spread. Save words, usernames and times, never the hurtful image, and never forward it. A private or sexual picture of anyone under 18: do not save or forward; tell an adult at once.
  4. (1 min) Upstander phrases. "Not cool. Let's stop." "I am leaving this chat." "Hey, are you okay?" (private message) "I will go with you to tell someone."

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "What was the first moment someone could have changed the direction?"
  2. "What is the difference between annoying and hurting? Who decides?"
  3. "How can a screenshot help, and how can it harm?"

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Pause before you add to a pile-on. Check where it sits on the meter. Tell an adult when it is hurting or worse. You do not have to fix it alone. It is never the target's fault, and a small kind move from you can change a chat."

Hand signal: Hackles Up (hand brushed up the neck, optional), then Pause, Check, Tell. Post it: "Annoying: ignore. Hurting: speak up. Tell-now: tell an adult."

Exit ticket#

"Write one sentence you could say or send if a group chat started to pile on someone."

Fun Boost#

Human Meter. Post ANNOYING, HURTING and TELL NOW on three walls. Read a card; students walk to a wall and may switch after a neighbor's reason.

Level-Up#

Chat Agreement. Write a five-norm agreement for a friend group, with what happens when a norm is broken, and pitch it in 30 seconds.

Differentiate#

Give sentence frames for upstander phrases, eight cards, and picture icons for the signs.

Family card#

Ask: "If a chat ever got mean, what would you do? Who would you tell?" Reassure your child they will not lose their phone for telling you.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing."
  • "This is not your fault, and you are not in trouble."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "Just ignore it" or "Are you sure?"
  • "I won't tell anyone."
  • Asking to see messages, photos or accounts.

First 10 Minutes: see the Disclosure Guide. Then: tell your counselor or designated person right away. Follow your school policy and your state's reporting law.

Lesson 10: Built to Keep You Scrolling#

Big idea: Apps are designed by people to keep your attention. When you can spot the design tricks, you can choose your own limits on purpose, without shame.

Materials#

R10 mock feed screens (ScrollSpot is fictional) per pair, Design Feature Cards per group, Workbook CF-10 and My Limits plan, My Help Ladder card (final version).

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Copy R10 screens per pair; one Design Feature set per group.
  • Checkbox: Find students' My Help Ladder cards from Lesson 1.
  • Checkbox: Know your counselor and 988 (call or text).

Hook (2 min)#

Hackles up? Notice how an app makes you feel when you want to stop and it will not let you. It is never your fault. "Quick check, no talking. Have you ever opened an app for a minute and looked up half an hour later? That is no personal failure. Many apps are built by teams whose job is to keep your attention. That is persuasive design. Today you are a design detective. Screens are not bad. You should be the one choosing. And if you ever feel hopeless or stuck, talk to a trusted adult. This lesson is about choices, with no shame."

Activity (8 min): "Design Detective"#

  1. (1 min) Read CF-10. Eli, twelve, has a streak with a friend. His phone buzzes: "Do not lose your streak!" The feed never ends. One late night he cannot stop and wonders if something is wrong with him. Nothing is wrong with Eli. The app was designed to make stopping hard.
  2. (4 min) Design Detective in pairs. Circle each feature on the ScrollSpot screens and match it to a card: endless scroll, autoplay, streaks, notifications and badges, like counts, variable rewards. Write what it wants me to do and one way to take back control.
  3. (2 min) My Limits plan. Two changes (a notification off, a device bedtime, a time limit) and one replacement habit.
  4. (1 min) Update the My Help Ladder card with names and roles, including 988 and text HOME to 741741.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Which design feature is hardest to resist? Why was it designed that way?"
  2. "What is the difference between choosing an app and being pulled by one?"
  3. "How do you know when screen time is hurting your sleep, mood or friendships? Who could you talk to?"

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Pause when you notice you are scrolling on autopilot. Check which design feature is pulling you. Tell a trusted adult if screen time is making you feel worse. It is never your fault if apps are hard to put down. You can still choose."

Hand signal: Hackles Up (hand brushed up the neck, optional), then Pause, Check, Tell. Post it: "It was built to be hard to stop. You still get to choose."

Exit ticket#

"Write one limit you chose and one person you could ask for help sticking to it."

Fun Boost#

Redesign Sprint. Pairs get 3 minutes to redesign one ScrollSpot feature so it is easier to put down (a stopping point, a "you are caught up" message). Gallery vote.

Level-Up#

Seven-Day Experiment. Try one limit for a week and note how sleep and mood felt. Private.

Differentiate#

Give a labeled screen with features pre-circled and a menu of limits.

Family card#

Ask: "Which app is hardest for you to put down? What limit could we try together?" If your child seems low, the counselor can help; 988 is available by call or text.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble."
  • "This is not your fault."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "You are addicted" or "Just put the phone down."
  • "Everyone feels like that."
  • "I won't tell anyone." Do not diagnose.

Lesson P1: Work, Pressure and Promises Online#

REVIEW STATUS. REQUIRES REVIEW BY A TRAFFICKING-PREVENTION SPECIALIST BEFORE CLASSROOM USE. Written 2026-10-05, not yet reviewed by anyone outside the author.

COUNSELOR HEADS-UP FIRST + OPT-OUT PLAN. Specialist and counselor review done. Pre-notice and opt-out slip out 5 school days ahead; opt-outs do "Job Scams" (RP1-ALT) with a supervising adult. Teach mid-week, after Lessons 1 and 6.

Subtitle: Recognising trafficking and exploitation

Big idea: Some people use tricks, pressure and control to make others work for their benefit. Online it can start with an offer that is too good or a "friend" who asks for secrecy. Spot the flags, say no, and tell. It is never your fault.

Materials#

RP1 Offer Cards (six per group), Flag Strips, Control Signs card, Reporting Quick Card, Pass cards.

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Reporting Quick Card (RP1) in hand, with your state's routes.
  • Checkbox: Opt-out space ready. Show no real image, ad or profile; ask for no personal story.

Hook (2 min)#

Hackles up? When an offer online feels too good, or someone says "keep this between us," trust that feeling. "Dani is a made-up kid, nobody shares anything personal, and your Pass card works at any time. Human trafficking is when someone uses tricks, pressure or threats to control another person and make them work, or be used, for someone else's gain. It is a crime. We focus on early warning signs, because early is when you can still walk away and telling helps most."

Activity (8 min): "Offer Check"#

  1. (1 min) Read CF-P1. Dani, thirteen, gets a "talent scout" message: "You could make real money. Don't tell anyone yet. We just need to chat on a different app." Dani feels flattered and uneasy. Listen to the uneasy feeling.
  2. (5 min) Offer Check. Groups sort six made-up Offer Cards GREEN, YELLOW or RED and circle flags: SECRET, TOO GOOD, MOVE (to another app, or alone), OWE (a favor that becomes a debt), CUT OFF (from family or friends), TAKES (money, phone, documents). Two cards are fine. Ask: who is this, what do they want, who else knows? The Control Signs card describes a person being controlled and never something they did wrong.
  3. (2 min) Triads (Speaker, Listener, Observer; nobody plays the scout): "I need your help with something online. A friend was offered money by someone they do not know and told not to tell anyone. Can you help me figure out what to do?" Then read the Reporting Quick Card.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Why might someone ignore the uneasy feeling?"
  2. "Why is 'keep it a secret' a flag? What is a safe secret?" (A surprise party versus a secret that gets someone hurt.)
  3. "What if someone already said yes?" (Still tell. It is not your fault.)

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Pause before you answer an offer that is secret, too good, or pushing you to move. Check it with someone. Tell a trusted adult. It is never your fault, and never too late."

Hand signal: Hackles Up (hand brushed up the neck, optional), then Pause, Check, Tell. Post it: "A real offer can stand being talked about."

Exit ticket#

"Write one flag from today and one adult you could tell."

Fun Boost#

Silent Gallery (calm; no scoring). Groups walk silently to another table to see where groups agreed or differed.

Level-Up#

Helper Poster. With counselor approval, a school poster with the six flags and hotline numbers, with no pictures of people.

Differentiate#

Three Offer Cards, a Tell-3 sentence frame, a buddy.

Family card#

Ask: "If someone online offered you something and said not to tell anyone, who would you tell?" Hotlines: 1-855-FLA-SAFE; 1-800-962-2873; danger: 911.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing."
  • "This is not your fault. The people who use tricks are responsible."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "Why did you go along with it?" or "Are you sure?"
  • "I won't tell anyone."
  • Contacting the person or the family.

First 10 Minutes: see the Disclosure Guide. Then: report immediately (Florida Abuse or Human Trafficking Hotline per district procedure); tell your counselor or designated person; follow your school policy and your state's reporting law. Tell the family only when the agency or an administrator advises.

Lesson P2: Scroll Science#

REVIEW STATUS. REQUIRES REVIEW BY A SCHOOL COUNSELOR OR CLINICIAN BEFORE CLASSROOM USE. Written 2026-10-05, not yet reviewed by anyone outside the author.

COUNSELOR HEADS-UP FIRST + OPT-OUT PLAN. Counselor told 5 school days ahead (Appendix B); know your self-harm protocol and 988. Family note required. No formal opt-out, but one is honored (alternate: Lesson 10 My Limits plan). Teach mid-week.

Subtitle: How feeds and notifications pull on you, and how to take the wheel

Big idea: Apps are built to hold attention, and heavy use can affect sleep, mood and friendships for some people. Research is still sorting out how much. When use is hard to stop or leaves you feeling worse, try a reset and talk to someone.

Materials#

RP2 Evidence Cards (six per group), Signal Cards and three-lane mat, Reset Menu per student, My Help Ladder card.

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Print the cards and mat per group; a Reset Menu per student.
  • Checkbox: Ask no one about their own screen time or mood. Name no apps. Give no self-harm methods or suicide statistics.

Hook (2 min)#

Hackles up? Notice how you feel after time on a screen: tired, restless, left out, or fine. That feeling is information, never a failure. "Ever picked up a phone for a minute and looked up half an hour later? Today is about why, and what we know. Some of what you hear online is solid, some is still being studied, and some is hype. Social media can be fun and useful, and for some people at some times it makes sleep, mood and friendships harder. We are noticing and choosing, with no blame."

Activity (8 min): "Solid, Still Studied, or Hype?"#

  1. (1 min) Read CF-P2. Pia, twelve, scrolls in bed "for a few minutes." Last night it was two hours. Pia is tired and keeps checking the phone, even in class. Pia is not lazy or weak: apps are built to be hard to put down.
  2. (3 min) Evidence Cards. Groups place six cards on SOLID, STILL STUDIED or HYPE. Debrief with reasons from the Answer Key. Only a doctor or counselor can tell whether use has become a health problem.
  3. (2 min) Signals. Lanes: TRY A RESET, TALK TO SOMEONE, GET HELP TODAY. Place four Signal Cards and name a helper. A character who feels hopeless or wants to hurt themselves always means tell an adult today.
  4. (2 min) Reset Menu. Circle two resets and add one trusted adult to the My Help Ladder card. Point to 988.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Which signal would you notice first? Which would be easiest to ignore?"
  2. "Why can comparing yourself with others' posts feel worse?"
  3. "What can a friend say that helps without judging?" ("I noticed you seem tired. Want to take a break together?")

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Pause when you notice a signal: tired, down, left out or stuck. Check what is pulling you, and try one reset. Tell a trusted adult if it keeps happening or you feel hopeless. It is never your fault."

Hand signal: Hackles Up (hand brushed up the neck, optional), then Pause, Check, Tell. Post it: "You still get to choose, and you do not have to choose alone."

Exit ticket#

"Write one reset you chose and one person who could help you stick to it."

Fun Boost#

Evidence Card Walk (calm; no scoring). Groups swap sorted cards, check silently whether they agree, then say one reason aloud.

Level-Up#

Seven-Day Experiment. Try one reset for a week and note sleep and mood. Private.

Differentiate#

Four Evidence Cards, three Signal Cards, sentence starters.

Family card#

Ask: "What feels good about your apps, and what feels hard?" If your child seems very down, the counselor can help; 988 by call or text.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble, and it is not your fault."
  • "I'm glad you told me. You do not have to handle this alone."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "You are addicted." Do not diagnose.
  • "Just put the phone down." "Everyone feels like that."
  • "I won't tell anyone."

First 10 Minutes: see the Disclosure Guide. Then: any hint of self-harm or hopelessness: stay with the student, follow your district protocol, call or text 988. Tell your counselor or designated person the same day; follow your school policy and your state's reporting law.

Lesson P3: Fact, Fiction or Fake?#

REVIEW STATUS. Educator review before use. Made-up claims only. A teacher, librarian or media specialist should read it once. Check district policy on political or news content [VERIFY].

COUNSELOR HEADS-UP FIRST + OPT-OUT PLAN. A short heads-up is recommended: a student may disclose being targeted by a rumor. No formal opt-out is expected; district policy governs [VERIFY].

Subtitle: Check before you share

Big idea: False or misleading posts spread fast because people pass them on. A few quick checks before you share can stop them. If something harmful is going around, tell an adult and report it in the app.

Materials#

RP3 Forward Chain slips (six per row), Claim Cards (six per class; each group works three), Check Strip per student.

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Write one made-up message on each chain slip.
  • Checkbox: Split the six Claim Cards so groups cover all six.

Hook (2 min)#

Hackles up? When a post makes you mad, scared or in a hurry to share, notice that feeling. "Share this now!" is a sign to pause. "Quick game. I whisper a made-up sentence to the first person in each row: 'The cafeteria is giving out free pizza coupons if you tap the link.' Pass it quietly; then we compare the last version with the first. (It changed.) Online, messages change too, reach far more people, faster, and often nobody checks. That is misinformation: false or misleading information, sometimes shared by people who mean no harm."

Activity (8 min): "Claim Check"#

  1. (1 min) Read CF-P3. Tavi, twelve, sees "SCHOOL CLOSED TOMORROW because of a danger. Tell everyone!" with a scary photo. Friends are forwarding it. Tavi's thumb hovers over "share." Write the Check Strip: WHO made it? PROOF? WHO ELSE says so? FEELING: is it trying to scare, anger or rush me?
  2. (4 min) Claim Check. Each group takes three made-up Claim Cards (a closing rumor, a miracle cure, a "private message" screenshot, a confident chatbot answer, a chart whose bars start at 90, an old flood photo "from today"). Name the first question and a decision: SHARE, CHECK FIRST, DO NOT SHARE or TELL AN ADULT.
  3. (2 min) Debrief by card with the Answer Key. Redraw the chart from zero.
  4. (1 min) Share Pause rule: "If I cannot say who made it and who else says so, I do not pass it on." Rumors about a person, or asks for money: report it and tell an adult.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Why do false posts spread faster when they make us angry or scared?"
  2. "Why might someone with good intentions share something false?"
  3. "What can you do if a friend shares something false?" (A private message, never a pile-on.)

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Pause before you share. Check who made it, the proof and who else says so. Tell an adult if it is hurting someone. It is never your fault if you shared something false by mistake. Fix it by telling people and not passing it on."

Hand signal: Hackles Up (hand brushed up the neck, optional), then Pause, Check, Tell. Post it: "Who made it? What is the proof? Who else says so?"

Exit ticket#

"Write one Check Strip question you will ask before sharing and one person you could check with."

Fun Boost#

Fact Detective Relay. Each group has 60 seconds per card to name the first question and the decision. Thumbs-up for a good reason, not for speed.

Level-Up#

Family Four Questions. Try the Check Strip questions on one post with a parent or caregiver; bring one sentence.

Differentiate#

Two Claim Cards, sentence starters ("I would check ___ because ___"), the Check Strip as a labeled card.

Family card#

Try the four questions on one post together. If a post is hurting someone or asking for money or personal information, report it and tell a trusted adult.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing."
  • "This is not your fault, and you are not in trouble."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "Why did you share it?" or "Are you sure?"
  • "I won't tell anyone."
  • Asking to see messages, photos or accounts.

If a student tells you something: quick reference#

First 10 Minutes, from the Disclosure and Incident Guide, Section 2. Your school's child-protection policy and your state's reporting law come first.

If a student tells you something: quick reference
Do Do not
Stay calm. Soften your voice. Keep your face neutral. Show shock, disgust, anger or panic.
Move to a private place that is still visible to other adults (door open, or a glass window). Be alone behind a closed door with a child.
Say: "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble." Say: "Why did you...?" "How could you...?" "You should have..."
Listen. Use open prompts: "Tell me more." "What happened next?" Ask leading questions or press for detail.
Be honest: "I can't keep this a secret, because I need to get you help. I will tell you who I tell." Promise secrecy. Promise outcomes you cannot control.
Write down the child's exact words afterward. Investigate, confront the other person, or contact the alleged offender.
Report personally to CPS or law enforcement per law and policy. Assume telling the principal satisfies your duty.
If a device holds images: do not look, copy, forward or screenshot. Secure it per policy. Ask the child to show you the images, or look yourself "to check."
If the child may harm themselves: stay with them, follow your school's crisis protocol, call the counselor or an administrator at once, and use 988. Leave a child at risk alone.

Evidence in three cases#

  1. Words and records (comments, threats, usernames, links, dates, scam messages): screenshots and saved copies are fine. Keep them private; give them to a trusted adult; never post or forward them.
  2. Intimate or sexual pictures of anyone under 18, including an AI or edited fake: do not screenshot, save, forward or show anyone, including you. Save only the surrounding words. The adult uses the platform's report tool and, where right, NCMEC or law enforcement.
  3. Not sure what kind it is? Do not capture it. Tell a trusted adult first. Telling matters more than saving.

Plus lessons and sensitive topics#

  • Possible trafficking or exploitation (6-8 P1; 9-12 P1): do not investigate, do not ask for images, do not contact the person or the family, do not promise secrecy. Report immediately. Florida: Abuse Hotline 1-800-962-2873 or Human Trafficking Hotline 1-855-FLA-SAFE per district procedure; elsewhere your state's child abuse line. Tell the family only when the agency or an administrator advises.
  • Low mood, hopelessness, compulsive use (6-8 L10 and P2; 9-12 P2 and P3): do not diagnose; do not say "you are addicted," "just put the phone down" or "everyone feels like that." Any hint of self-harm: stay with the student, follow your district protocol, bring in the counselor, call or text 988.
  • Bullying and cyberbullying (6-8 L9; 9-12 P4): follow your district's bullying and harassment policy; know who takes the report at your school.
