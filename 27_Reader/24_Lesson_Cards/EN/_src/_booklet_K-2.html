Trust your hackles. Pause · Check · Tell.

Hackles · a free kit · quillsup.org. © 2026 Competence Collective. Lesson text CC BY-NC 4.0. Hackles name, logos and art: all rights reserved.

One page for every lesson. Read a card in two minutes, then teach it in fifteen: Hook 2, Activity 8, Discuss 3, Takeaway and exit ticket 2.

Pacing planner#

Standard pacing: 10 weeks, one 15-minute lesson per week. Do Lesson 1 first, always: every other lesson assumes children know their Tell-Someone Team.

Pacing planner
Week Lesson Counselor Notes
1 1. My Tell-Someone Team Lesson 1 first, always
2 2. Secret Passwords
3 3. Pause and Ask
4 4. The Oops Button
5 5. Private vs. Public
6 6. Online Friends vs. Real Friends Heads-up recommended Teach before Lesson 9
7 7. Kind Clicks
8 8. Real or Pretend? Family code word at home
9 9. My Body Is Mine Heads-up first + opt-out plan Family notice a week ahead; counselor co-teach if required
10 10. Screen Balance Certificate ceremony

Sensitive lesson timeline#

  • 3 weeks before. Confirm which lessons are sensitive, check your policy on notice and opt-out, confirm counselor availability.
  • 2 weeks before. Send the family pre-notice with the lesson overview and a review option.
  • 1 week before. Offer a preview. Collect opt-out requests. Arrange alternates.
  • 2 days before. Send the counselor heads-up message. Confirm coverage.
  • Day of. Calm opening and closing scripts. Counselor on call. Offer the ask-the-counselor option.
  • 24 hours after. Debrief. Check in with anyone who was upset. Document any disclosure.
  • 1 week after. Send the follow-up note with the take-home card.

Notes for this band#

  • Fast track: 5 weeks, two lessons a week (Mon and Thu). Keep Lessons 1 and 4 first.
  • Revisit: spend 2 minutes at the start of each lesson on the chant and a quick review.
  • Family loop: send the family card home after each lesson and collect reply slips.

Lesson 1: My Tell-Someone Team#

Big idea: I have grown-ups who help me, and I know the words to ask them: "I need help."

Materials#

Hand-trace sheet, crayons, Hackles poster and character cards, Hackle puppet, chart paper "Our Helpers at School", pocket cards.

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Copy the hand sheets and pre-cut the pocket cards.
  • Checkbox: Title the chart paper "Our Helpers at School."
  • Checkbox: Know your designated person's name and room.

Hook (2 min)#

Hold Hackle. Speak slowly and warmly. "Friends, sit in our circle. This is Hackle. Hackle's spikes stand up whenever something feels funny. Let's all be Hackle! Crouch small and calm: Hackles Down. Something feels funny... POP! Spring up with wiggly fingers on your head. (Twice, bigger the second time.) That tingly uh-oh feeling is your hackles. When mine go up, I Pause, I Check, and if I need help, I Tell. (Show Hackles Up: hand brushed up the back of the neck.) A false alarm is always okay. And it is never your fault: you will never be in trouble for asking for help. Say it with me: "Never in trouble for asking for help!""

Activity (8 min): "My Helper Hand"#

  1. (1 min) Show the poster. A trusted grown-up listens, helps and keeps you safe.
  2. (1 min) Name school helpers on the chart: teacher, counselor, nurse, principal, bus driver. "You can always tell any grown-up at school."
  3. (3 min) Fill the Helper Hand. Three fingers, one trusted grown-up each (draw it or ask for help writing). No one at home? "Let's put your teacher and the counselor on your hand."
  4. (2 min) Practice in pairs: "I need help." Partner: "Thank you for telling me. I will help." Switch.
  5. (1 min) "Keep telling." Busy grown-up? Tap the next finger.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "What do you say when you need help?" (I need help.)
  2. "What if the first grown-up is too busy?" (Keep telling. Go to the next helper.)
  3. "Can you be in trouble for asking for help?" (Never.)

Takeaway (2 min)#

"I have helpers, and I keep telling until someone helps."

Hand signal: Pause (palm). Check (eyes). Tell (hand on heart, then reach out). Tap five fingertips: "Tell... tell... tell... until someone helps!"

Exit ticket#

Circle the people who can be helpers; color a star by a favorite. Each child should name 3; follow up in a small group if fewer.

Fun Boost#

Spike Pop (30 seconds). Crouch small (Hackles Down), whisper "Something feels funny...", then POP up with wiggling fingers on your head. Freeze in a Pause Palm and breathe.

Level-Up#

Helper Spotter. Spot three school helpers on the way to lunch and wave.

Differentiate#

Pre-draw faces on the fingers; pointing is fine. Never ask a child to name a grown-up at home in front of the class.

Family card#

Ask: "Who are the three people on your Tell-Someone Team?" Say: "You will never be in trouble for telling me something."

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me."
  • "I believe you. It is not your fault."
  • "I will get help from someone whose job is to keep kids safe."

Do not say:

  • "I promise I won't tell anyone."
  • "Why didn't you tell me sooner?" or "Are you sure?"
  • Any leading question ("Did he touch you?").

First 10 Minutes: see the Disclosure Guide. Then: tell your counselor or designated person right away. Follow your school policy and your state's reporting law.

Lesson 2: Secret Passwords#

Big idea: A password is a secret key. I keep it private and make it silly-strong.

Materials#

Treasure chest (box) with a key, three word-card piles (Color/Feeling, Animal/Thing, Food/Silly), Activity Book page, crayons.

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Put the key in the chest.
  • Checkbox: Set the three word-card piles at each table.
  • Checkbox: Copy the Lesson 2 page.

Hook (2 min)#

Hold up the treasure chest and the key. "Look at this treasure chest. Only the key opens it. If I leave the key lying around, anyone could take my treasure. So I keep my key private. A password is a secret key for things on a computer or tablet. Only you and your grown-up at home may know it. Omar's friend asked, "What's your password? I want to play your game." What do you think Omar did? (Take two answers.) Omar said, "Passwords are private," and put up his Pause Palm. That is the Pause in Pause, Check, Tell. Today we make a silly, strong password, just for pretend.""

Activity (8 min): "Silly Three-Word Passwords"#

  1. (1 min) Silly and long. Three silly words are hard to guess and easy to remember: "purple pickle bus." Say it together.
  2. (1 min) What NOT to use: your name, your birthday, "1234."
  3. (3 min) Build one. Pick a card from each pile, say the words, draw them as three little pictures. "This is pretend. We make a real one with our grown-up at home."
  4. (2 min) Passwords are private game. A sneaky asker (a friend, a game chat, "I'm from the game company"). Each time: Pause Palm, "Passwords are private. I'll tell my grown-up."
  5. (1 min) Lock the chest. "Click! Locked."

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Who can know your password?" (Me and my grown-up at home.)
  2. "What makes a password strong?" (Long, silly, three words.)
  3. "What if you told someone by accident?" (Tell your grown-up. We can change it. Not in trouble!)

Takeaway (2 min)#

"A password is a secret key. I keep it private. If someone asks, I Pause and tell a grown-up."

Hand signal: Pause (palm) when someone asks for your password. Tell your grown-up.

Exit ticket#

Circle the pictures where the password is safe (showing a parent, a locked chest, the child pausing). Revisit with any child who circles the whisper-to-a-friend picture.

Fun Boost#

Walk the Password (30 seconds). Act out your three silly words in three big moves, then freeze and lock with a "click!"

Level-Up#

Spiral check: "Who on my Helper Hand can I tell?" Grade 2: add a fourth silly word.

Differentiate#

Offer 3 pre-picked cards; Kindergarten uses two words. Do not assume every child has a device.

Family card#

Make a silly three-word password together and tell your child: "Passwords are private. Only you and I know it."

Plate Dance (30 seconds). Call a wedge; everyone does it for 10 seconds: Move, People, Quiet, Screen (freeze like a robot). End with a wobbly one-foot "balance!"

Level-Up#

Family Break. Pick one Break Menu idea to do with a grown-up this week.

Differentiate#

Use three feelings faces and four Break Menu cards. Do not judge screen time at home; everyone's day is different.

Family card#

Ask: "How does your body feel after a screen? What break would you choose?" If anything online worries them, they can tell you.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me."
  • "I believe you. It is not your fault."
  • "I will get help from someone whose job is to keep kids safe."

Do not say:

  • "I promise I won't tell anyone."
  • "Why didn't you tell me sooner?" or "Are you sure?"
  • Any leading question ("Did he touch you?").

First 10 Minutes: see the Disclosure Guide. Then: tell your counselor or designated person right away. Follow your school policy and your state's reporting law.

If a student tells you something: quick reference#

First 10 Minutes, from the Disclosure and Incident Guide, Section 2. Your school's child-protection policy and your state's reporting law come first.

If a student tells you something: quick reference
Do Do not
Stay calm. Soften your voice. Keep your face neutral. Show shock, disgust, anger or panic.
Move to a private place that is still visible to other adults (door open, or a glass window). Be alone behind a closed door with a child.
Say: "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. You are not in trouble." Say: "Why did you...?" "How could you...?" "You should have..."
Listen. Use open prompts: "Tell me more." "What happened next?" Ask leading questions or press for detail.
Be honest: "I can't keep this a secret, because I need to get you help. I will tell you who I tell." Promise secrecy. Promise outcomes you cannot control.
Write down the child's exact words afterward. Investigate, confront the other person, or contact the alleged offender.
Report personally to CPS or law enforcement per law and policy. Assume telling the principal satisfies your duty.
If a device holds images: do not look, copy, forward or screenshot. Secure it per policy. Ask the child to show you the images, or look yourself "to check."
If the child may harm themselves: stay with them, follow your school's crisis protocol, call the counselor or an administrator at once, and use 988. Leave a child at risk alone.

  • Who I report to, and how fast: ____
  • Counselor on call and phone: ____
