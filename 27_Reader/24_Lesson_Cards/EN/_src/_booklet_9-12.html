Pacing planner#

Standard pacing: one 15-minute lesson a week for 10 weeks. Lesson 8 follows Lessons 5 and 7 so students already have the language to help a friend. Never teach Lessons 5 and 7 on the same day, on a major exam day, or before a long weekend without counselor input.

Sensitive lesson timeline#

  • 3 weeks before. Confirm which lessons are sensitive, check your policy on notice and opt-out, confirm counselor availability.
  • 2 weeks before. Send the family pre-notice with the lesson overview and a review option.
  • 1 week before. Offer a preview. Collect opt-out requests. Arrange alternates.
  • 2 days before. Send the counselor heads-up message. Confirm coverage.
  • Day of. Calm opening and closing scripts. Counselor on call. Offer the ask-the-counselor option.
  • 24 hours after. Debrief. Check in with anyone who was upset. Document any disclosure.
  • 1 week after. Send the follow-up note with the take-home card.

Notes for this band#

  • Minimum path (5 sessions): Lessons 1, 2, 3, 5, 10. Keep the counselor and family steps for Lesson 5.
  • Plus lessons are optional extras (Florida and any school that wants them); outside Florida use one at a time as needed.

Lesson 1: Threat-Model Yourself#

Big idea: Security means deciding what matters to you, what could realistically go wrong, and choosing a few protections that fit your life. Specific beats scared.

Materials#

R1 Asset Cards and Threat Cards (one set per group of 3 to 4), Workbook W1 per student, pens, a 2x2 grid on the board (Unlikely / Likely across, Small harm / Big harm up).

Prep in 5 minutes#

  • Checkbox: Print and cut about 8 R1 sets.
  • Checkbox: Draw the 2x2 grid and the four questions: Matters? Wrong? How bad / how likely? Smallest fix?
  • Checkbox: Listen during the lesson for a card choice that sounds like a real situation; note it for a private check-in.

Hook (2 min)#

Say it word for word. "Raise a hand if you have ever locked your phone, locked a bike, or chosen who gets to see a story. That is security, and you already do it. You lock your bike more carefully than your water bottle, and you tell different people different things. Today we make that instinct deliberate. Security professionals call it a threat model, and it is four questions: What do I have that matters? What could go wrong? How bad and how likely? What is the smallest thing I can do about it? Be specific, and skip the paranoia."

Activity (8 min): "Assets, Threats, Protections"#

  1. (1 min) Pick five assets. Each student privately circles five things that matter to them on W1 (email, social account, photos, location and routine, money and game items, reputation, group chats, phone, health information, AI chat history). "Pick what matters to you."
  2. (3 min) Group threat sort. Place each Threat Card (account takeover, scam message, lost device, oversharing friend, data broker or leak, someone who has your password, AI fake, pressure or blackmail, platform breach, location leak) on the 2x2 by likelihood and harm. "There is no answer key. Defend your placement in one sentence."
  3. (3 min) Protections. Each student picks their top two threats and writes the smallest protection for each (a passkey for email, precise location off for two apps, a callback rule with family). It has to fit your actual life.
  4. (1 min) One-line share. Each group names one threat from the "likely and big harm" corner and one protection.

Discuss (3 min)#

  1. "Which threat did your group argue about most? What changed someone's mind?"
  2. "Why might two people in this room have totally different top threats?"
  3. "When is 'do nothing' a reasonable answer to a threat?"

Takeaway (2 min)#

"Security is about knowing what to Pause over, what to Check, and who to Tell. Your threat model tells you where to aim them. On your Defense Plan page write: 'The thing I most want to protect is _, and my first move is _.'"

Hand signal: Hackles up? Your threat model maps where yours should go up (hand brushed up the neck, optional). Post it: "Specific beats scared."

Exit ticket#

"Name one asset you decided to protect first, and one thing you will do about it this week." No names; tally the answers.

Fun Boost#

Move-a-Card Challenge. After the sort, a neighboring group may move one card with a one-sentence argument; the original group accepts or defends. Points for reasoning, not winning.

Level-Up#

Pre-Mortem. "It is six months from now and my top asset got compromised. What most likely happened, and what is the cheapest thing that would have stopped it?" Three lines.

Differentiate#

Give a pre-filled example worksheet (a fictional teen, "Rowan") to reshape, and the starter "The thing I most want to protect is ___ because ___."

Family card#

Ask: "What is one thing you want to protect first?" You do not need to be technical. Curiosity helps more than rules.

If a student tells you something#

Say:

  • "Thank you for telling me. You did the right thing."
  • "This is not your fault, and you are not in trouble."
  • "I can't keep this secret. I need to bring in people who can help, and I will tell you who."

Do not say:

  • "Why did you...?" or "How could you...?"
  • "I won't tell anyone."
  • "You should have told someone sooner."

Evidence in three cases#

  1. Words and records (comments, threats, usernames, links, dates, scam messages): screenshots and saved copies are fine. Keep them private; give them to a trusted adult; never post or forward them.
  2. Intimate or sexual pictures of anyone under 18, including an AI or edited fake: do not screenshot, save, forward or show anyone, including you. Save only the surrounding words. The adult uses the platform's report tool and, where right, NCMEC or law enforcement.
  3. Not sure what kind it is? Do not capture it. Tell a trusted adult first. Telling matters more than saving.

Plus lessons and sensitive topics#

  • Possible trafficking or exploitation (6-8 P1; 9-12 P1): do not investigate, do not ask for images, do not contact the person or the family, do not promise secrecy. Report immediately. Florida: Abuse Hotline 1-800-962-2873 or Human Trafficking Hotline 1-855-FLA-SAFE per district procedure; elsewhere your state's child abuse line. Tell the family only when the agency or an administrator advises.
  • Low mood, hopelessness, compulsive use (6-8 L10 and P2; 9-12 P2 and P3): do not diagnose; do not say "you are addicted," "just put the phone down" or "everyone feels like that." Any hint of self-harm: stay with the student, follow your district protocol, bring in the counselor, call or text 988.
  • Bullying and cyberbullying (6-8 L9; 9-12 P4): follow your district's bullying and harassment policy; know who takes the report at your school.
