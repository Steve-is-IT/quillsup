Grades 9-12Ages 14-18

Your Digital Defense Plan

Ten lessons for students aged 14 to 18. Students build their own threat model, make their own risk decisions and teach each other. Attack methods are left out.

Help-seeking is the central skill. Every lesson says it in plain words: it is never your fault, and telling is not getting in trouble.

This page is for the grown-up. Children do not need to visit this site.

03A three-step start

Teach it this week

  1. Read two things first

    Read the Teacher Primer (about 10 minutes) and the Disclosure and Incident Guide. Check your school's mandatory-reporting policy.

  2. Get Lesson 1 ready

    Read the card for Lesson 1. Give your counselor and administrator the lesson list and the dates of Lessons 5, 7 and 8, and confirm your reporting route. Send the family letter.

  3. Teach Lesson 1

    Teach Lesson 1, Threat-Model Yourself. It takes 15 minutes. Introduce the group agreements and the Question Box.

After that, teach one lesson a week for 10 weeks. Each Teacher Guide has its own pacing guide, and that one wins.

05A note for the adult who teaches

Sensitive lessons and the counselor heads-up

Teens will sometimes tell a teacher something serious, and these lessons make that likelier. Read the disclosure section of the Teacher Guide before Lesson 1. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit marks these lessons as sensitive.

Sensitive lessons, grades 9-12
LessonWhy it is sensitive
Lesson 5. Sextortion and Image AbuseSextortion, legal context
Lesson 7. Coercion in Relationships OnlineDigital control, consent
Lesson 8. Be the HelperFriends in crisis, self-harm
Plus P1. Recruitment OnlineHuman trafficking and exploitation, recruitment, debt and control. Specialist review required. Family pre-notice, opt-out and alternate ("Spot the Scam")
Plus P2. Mind, Mood, and the ScrollMental health, self-image, hopelessness vignette. Counselor or clinician review required. Family note; opt-out honored; alternate (Two-Week Experiment with the counselor)

Still pending. The sextortion and coercion lessons (Lessons 5, 7 and 8) have not yet had a trauma-informed review by a school counselor and a child-safety expert.

Teachers and counselors still report under their own policy and state law. Hackles does not change that.

Before you teach one of these lessons

  1. Give your counselor and administrator the lesson list and the dates of Lessons 5, 7 and 8. The Counselor Heads-Up and Opt-Out Toolkit has the message.
  2. Confirm your mandatory reporting route: who to call, what form, what timeframe.
  3. Confirm your opt-out process for Lessons 5 and 7, and where opted-out students will be supervised.
  4. Send the parent pre-notices at least 5 school days before the lessons. [Confirm your district's notice requirements with district counsel]
  5. Do not teach Lessons 5 and 7 on the same day, on a day with a major exam, or on a Friday afternoon before a long weekend, without counselor input.
  6. Read the Disclosure and Incident Guide.
  7. Hold Plus Lesson P1 until a trafficking-prevention specialist has reviewed it, and Plus Lesson P2 until a counselor or clinician has.

06The family piece

For families of ages 14 to 18

A family card comes home with each lesson. Families get a pre-notice before the sensitive lessons and can ask to opt out.

Three questions to try at home

  1. What would your digital defense plan look like if you had to protect your most important accounts?
  2. What is one thing you would like adults to understand better about your online life?
  3. If I made a mistake in how I reacted to something online, how would you want me to respond?

These come from the ten conversation starters for ages 14-18 in the Family Engagement Pack. The pack also has the letters, the family code word guide and a page on what to say if your child tells you something scary.

If your child shares a mistake, begin with “Thank you for telling me.”

If a child is in danger nowCall your local emergency number. This site cannot respond to emergencies.
